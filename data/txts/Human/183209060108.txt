JOSEPH HARDING. I am a milkman, and live in New North-road, St. Leonard, Shoreditch. The prisoner lodged with me for five months.
   HANNAH HARDING. I am the prosecutor's wife.    GEORGE DEHANE. I am a Policeman. On the 9th of August I was in New North-road; the prisoner came to me - he said he had committed a robbery, and wished to be taken into custody, for he had been at Bristol, and he could get nothing to do, and would rather be transported than remain in England - he said he was sorry for what he had done; that he had robbed Mr. Harding - he told me the amount, and when he had done it.

 Second London Jury, before Mr. Justice Patteson.
 1985.  PHOEBE JAMES, alias    MARY POWEL, was indicted for that she, on the 13th of March , feloniously did utter, dispose of, and put off a certain forged will and testament of one Benjamin Norrington , well knowing it to be forged, with intent to defraud Jemima Norrington and others.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating her intent to be to defraud other persons.
   JEMIMA NORRINGTON. I am step-mother to Benjamin Norrington, who went to sea on the 10th of February, 1831 - his father was not living when he went; he left a paper with me - I took it to Mr. Thackray (looking at it) this is it; I saw Benjamin Norrington write it; he went out in the Baretto, Junior, which returned on the 4th of March, and shortly after I heard of his death; I took the paper to Mr. Clarkson's, and tried to prove it as his will, but they would not let me; after the ship returned, two men brought me another paper; I did not go with them to Doctors'-commons.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What has become of the paper the two men brought? A. Mr. Thackray has it; they did not leave it with me, it was never in my possession; (produced) - this is Norrington's hand-writing; he had been a seaman for about seven years - he was twenty-four years old; he had no sweetheart to my knowlege, but he may have had one.
 The documents being read, were as follows - the first was without a date."I, B. Norrington, leave my mother my will, wages, goods and chattels, provided death calls me. - B. Norrington." The second (brought by the two men) was a regular will, bequeathing all his goods and effects to  Sarah Norrington, Thomas Nevell, and Samuel Blake, and his wages to his mother, residing at Low Layton, Essex.
   SAMUEL MAYNARD. I am a clerk to Mr. Scurlock, a proctor. The prisoner came to our office on the 18th of March, and brought this will (looking at it); he had a man with her; I cannot say whether the prisoner or the man gave it to me; the man paid me for the probate - he gave me the name of Tye; the prisoner gave the name of Mary Powell, and she administered to the will; the probate was afterwards granted to her as the sole executrix - there is no written affidavit; the oath is administered by the surrogate; it is, that she was the sole executrix named in the will, that the effects were under 20l., and that she believed the paper contained the last will and testament of the deceased.
 The will was here put in and read; it was dated the 8th of February, 1831, signed  B.  Norrington, his mark; and bequeathing all his property to Mary Powell, his intended wife.
   JOHN CLIFFORD PETRIE. I am clerk to Mr. Read, ship-broker to the Baretto, Junior, which arrived on the 4th of March, having sailed about the 8th of February, 1831; the prisoner came about the 18th of March, and applied for the money of Benjamin Norrington; she did not produce a paper at that time; I told her to call after twenty-one days, when the ship would be paid off; she called two or three days after the twenty-one days, and produced a parchment, but I did not look at it; she was told to call again, which she did, and I then looked at the probate of the will which she produced, and seeing it purported to have the mark of Benjamin Norrington; I told her he could write his own name, and produced the ship's articles, which I had seen him sign; she said that was Benjamin Norrington's mark; she was told that we could not pay her on that account, and we did not pay her; she went away, and the next application was made by Mr. Howard, a solicitor of Mincing-lane - the application was from Mr. Styles, his clerk - he came alone; when the prisoner applied to me there was always a man with her - I do not know who he was.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you not know that sometimes seamen, when they can write, will make their mark? A. They do sometimes.
 COURT. Q. Is that a frequent thing? A. Not very; it is generally the case with those who cannot write very well; I have seen it done once or twice - I do not call this good writing.
   JOHN STYLES. I am clerk to Mr. Howard, of Mincing-lane. The prisoner applied to me on or about the 3rd of April to make application to the owners of the Baretto, Jun. for the payment of 15l. odd - I applied at Mr. Read's office, where I saw two gentlemen; I did not get the money- the prisoner instructed me to apply for her as the executrix of Norrington, and brought with her the probate, which was returned to her.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not she appear anxious that you should proceed hostile for the recovery of the money? A. Yes; she insisted on it as her right; I saw no appearance of fear or guilt.
