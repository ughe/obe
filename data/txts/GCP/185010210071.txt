JAMES Brett (City policeman, 18). I apprehended the prisoner on
31st Aug., in Huntingdon-street, Kingsland-road-be was in bed- I told him
be must get up and dress himself, and come with me in custody, on the
charge of obtaining 108. from Mr. Abbot by means of a forged check ; I said,
"I suppose you know something of it?"-he said, “I shall answer no questions"
I searched the room, and in a box, which was locked, I found three sove-
reigns wrapped in a piece of paper-I said, “How do you account for the
possession of this money?"-he said, “That is not your business " I said,
14 I shall take it, believing it to be part of the money that was got on this

account of it.

MR. PARRY called the following Witresses for the Defence.
George Bryant. I am a coffee-house keeper, of 39, High-street, Blooms-
bury. I have known the prisoner twelve or fourteen years he has borne
the best of characters--I know his hand-writing I believe this check not to
be his hand-writing.
Bloom
Cross-examined by Mr. ROBINSON. Q. Have you seen that check before !
4. I saw it about ten minutes ago, at the table, before Mr. Parry began his
address--the style of the writing, and shape of the letters is totally different
to his--it is about two months since I have seen the prisoner--the last time
I saw him, I gave him some accounts to make out, and then saw his writing
-- I frequently gave him jobs, making out bills and commissions-(looking at
a book)-I should say this was his writing; but I generally saw him write in
pen and ink, I cannot judge so well in pencil-I believe he left the Bankruptey
Court about twelve months ago in consequence of a death; since then I have
given him jobs occasionally, and I recommended him to a friend who gave
him commissions as a town traveller, and a very responsible situation it was.
JAMES MAJOR. I am a draper, and live at 3, Cambridge-road, Mile End.
I have known the prisoner about twenty-five years he has always borne an
exceedingly honest and honourable character-I have seen his hand-writing
several times- I lived with him in two situations. I do not think the writing
on this check is bold enough for his; I do not believe it to be his writing. Vi
Cross-examined. Q. What do you mean by boldness of hand-writing!

not mean to say there is not a single letter like his, but taking the average of
the writing, it is not like his-I never saw him write like this he is a plain
legible writer-I never saw any variety in his hand-writing-I first saw this
check just now, the Counsel showed it me before he made his speech the
