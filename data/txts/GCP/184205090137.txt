Eliza MARSH. I am in the employ of Messrs. Smith and Co., of St. Paul's
Churchyard - I have been there seventeen years—the younger prisoner has
been in the house about six years--I remember the 18th of March, I saw
him there at breakfast-time that morning, and engaged there during that
day, attending to the entry of goods--he was there during the whole day
-I can answer for him till six o'clock, ten time-he remained there from
breakfast till tea time we have a porter named Harniman, who was sent out
with goods that day-about one o'clock I saw Clark give him several parcels
to take to the West-end of the town, and 1s. to pay for his dinner-it was
just before dinner time, which is usually one o'clock, but we did not dine
that day till near two, and Clark dined with us.

Q. Was there anything particular that attracted yoar attention to that
day? A. Several things, since I have referred to the book-one was Mrs.
Shaw, a customer, who was there a long time in the morning, and he
assisted a good deal in serving her-she came about ten in the morning,

goods she bought-Mr. Clark gave them down at the counter to the clerk
-it was a considerably larger order than usual for her.
