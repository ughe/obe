288. ANDREW WILLIAM BARR, CHARLES BREWER, and
SAMUEL RALFS, unlawfully conspiring, with others, to cheat and defraud
Julius Samuel Kerie of his moneys.
Messrs. CHAMBERS, BALLANTINE, and HUDDLESTON conducted the
Prosecution.
Julius SAMUEL KERIE. I am a native of St. Christopher, in the West
Indies. I arrived in London, having gone to America, on 14th or 15th
Juneon 15th July I went to Cremorne-gardens-I was smoking a cím
there, and the prisoner Barr came up to me I saw no one with him-he
asked me to give him a light for his cigar-I did so, and we got into conver.
sation--we walked about the gardens, and eventually got to where the fire-
works were I am not exactly certain that I told him in the gardens where
had come from, but I did tell him we remained in the gardens an hour, or
an hour and a half, and left together at the time the gardens closed, I think
Dear eleven o'clock-I intended to have come to town by the omnibus, but
Barr said he was going by the boat, and there was a balloon going from Vaux.
hall, and I went with him-we landed at Vauxhall-bridge or Waterloo
bridge: I think it was at Waterloo-bridge--no one spoke to him on board the
boat when we landed, my cigar was out-I said I should like another cigar
-Barr said he knew exactly where I could get a very good one, and he took
me to Outhwaite's, a cigar-shop in Little Russell-street-we had talked about
places we had stopped at, and he told me he had come from Jamaica, and he
was managing estates out there for a cousin of his-when we got to Outh-
waite's, he said we West Indians were fond of a cup of coffee, would I join
with him we had some coffee, and while we were drinking it two men came
in, whom I now know by the names of Lewis and Carter-they then went by
the names of Stevens and Harrington- they did not appear to know Barr-
after they had been in a little while, they were discussing some point of
whist-there was some bet, apparently, on the point-they referred to Bart
and me for our opinion-we had some discussion on the point, and one of
them proposed that a pack of cards should be brought in, that the point might
be more clearly exemplified—a pack of cards was brought in by the man 8
the bar, on the application of either Lewis or Carter the point was then dis-
cussed again, and then we commenced playing for coffee and cigars—I do not
know who proposed it, but it was proposed to play for coffee and cigars-
then began to play for a shilling-Barr was my partner, he being my friend,
and Lewis and Carter were friends - I know the game of whist- have
played it-I took up a band that I considered a very winning band, a hand
that appeared impossible to lose I had four honours and the two bighest
trumps, nine and ten-it was a beautiful leading hand- I had, I think, the
ace of spades- I had no diamonds at all in my hand-hearts were trump

hand, Lewis said, "If any one would give me six to one, I should not mind
betting him on the odd trick" -I thought I would give him the odds, as
certain of winning it-I said any amount that he would bet I would
