DAVIS, George (38, Freneb polisher), feloniously attempting to
murder Henry Franks.
Mr. Cohen prosecuted; Mr. Tully-Christie defended (at the request
of the Court).
Henry Franks, 76, South Road, Hackney, cabinet maker's foreman.
On August 31, just before 1 p.m., I was in Grove Road, when I saw
pr.soner; I had known him for 17 years. He had previously
threatened me, as I tried to avo d him. As I was in Old Ford Road
I heard him running up behind me. I spoke to Burgess, who was a
stranger to me. Prisoner came up and said to me, "I want a private
interview with you "; I declined to speak to him. He grasped me by
the throat with one hand; with the other he pulled a knife from his
pocket and made a plunge at me. I wrestled with him and threw him
to the ground. The knife did not reach me.
Cross-examined. I believe prisoner has had a personal animosity
against me for some years because I have refused to give him employ-
ment; there had been no open quarrel.
FREDERICK BURGEss said that as he happened to be passing he heard
prisoner say to Franks that he wanted a private conversation, and saw
him pull out the knife and attempt to stab prosecutor.
Police-constable WILLIAM TAYLOR, 325 J. I saw prisoner in Lauris-
ton Road, and asked him to go with me to Franks's factory; he said,
"I will kill both the bastards"; he was looking at the man who had
fetched me, Edward Smith. When charged prisoner said, "My in-
tention was to do the pair in; I went into 20 or 30 shops yesterday in
the New Cut to buy a shooter, but as I had not got ten bob for a license
I could not get one."
WILLIAM RATTENBURY. I was walking along Old Ford Road when I
heard & seream of " murder." I saw prisoner and prosecutor on the
ground, the former with a knife in his hand, the point upwards. I ran
over and took the knife from prisoner's hand.
Verdict, Guilty.
Prisoner confessed to a conviction of felony, at this Court, on Sep-
tember 5, 1911.
