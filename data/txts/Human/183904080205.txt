   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  How long was that after the first pawning? A.  The following Monday—I believe the first was on the Friday—I said, "You had better leave me this piece to make up for the one you left," and he made a jeer at me—I said nothing about his paying me the money for the piece he had left, and taking it back again—some articles may be worth nearly the money we lend at the time, but at the end of the year may not be worth half the money—in taking an article like flannel, we estimate the value as if it would remain in out hands for twelve months, and then probably it may not have a good sale—we get fifteen per cent on the money lent—flannel at the end of the year would be much less likely to sell than silver and other things—it is liable to moth and other things—this flannel would fetch more if sold in a shop than at our sale—I think this might fetch 1 s . 6 d . a yard at the sale.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Are you obliged to sell these things by auction at the end of a year? A.
