Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Is your father here to-day? A.  No—this prosecution is with his consent—quite so—he has not said he had rather have paid 402. than the prisoner should be prosecuted—he said he would rather pay 40 l . than he should be transported—I have not had any particular quarrel with the prisoner—I do not remember that I quarrelled with him the day he was taken into custody—I will not swear I did not—it was on Thursday evening, the 26th, he was apprehended—I had put the marked money into the desk on the Wednesday week previous—it was always the custom to leave the key of that desk on the mantel-shelf when I went to work, before my father was up—it was not left unlocked—the money re mained untouched for a week—all the family and the maid servant had access to the room where the key was—I was not playing at a game called cock-shy  with the prisoner on the day he was taken—I saw him and George Munns playing at it in my father's yard—I do not remember quarrelling with him then—I did not play with him at all that day—I did the day before—at least I was playing with Munns, and he wished to play with us— cock-shy  is a stick stuck in the ground with a piece of money on the top of it to throw at—I see you understand it—I had some shillings the day before, but none passed between us—I changed some sixpences with him, but nothing else—I do not think any passed between Munns and me—my father desired me to send for a policeman—he was at home at the time.
   Q.  Had you paid the prisoner any silver between the time of your de positing the money in the desk, and your causing him to be taken into custody? A.  Yes, 11 s . 6 d .—most of that was in sixpences and four-penny pieces—I cannot tell what the rest was—I cannot say whether there were any shillings—I paid it him on the Sunday morning previous to his being taken—I will swear I did not pay him as many as eleven shillings—I had not marked any other money before—it was about eleven o'clock on Thursday night, that I found the marked money gone from the desk—it was about one o'clock in the afternoon that the prisoner and Munns were playing at cock-shy  —the prisoner lost two shillings to me the day before, and two shillings to Munns—he continued to play with Munns and I left them—I did not go after that, and play at skittles at the Druid's Head—I had been there on the Monday before with Munns, and the prisoner wished to go to stick up the skittles—I scarcely ever played before, and should not then had it not been for my cousin Munns being in town—I am not in the habit of frequenting skittle-grounds—I will swear I did not play at cock-shy  with the prisoner on the Thursday that he was taken—I wished the prisoner to go about his business, because I wished the business to be done—there were some sheep to be killed, and I wished him to go and do it—( my father was at home at the time )—he said he wished to have a game first—I told him to go and kill the sheep—nothing else passed—we did not quarrel—I think they left off playing in my presence—it was about three o'clock in the afternoon—not so late as five or six o'clock—the prisoner came to live with my father through a friend—he was not engaged because I was in the habit of playing at skittles and cock-shy , and would not attend to the business.
