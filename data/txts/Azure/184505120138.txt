GIBBS, Mayor.
1160. MARTHA EVANS was indicted for stealing 1 shawl, value
21. IOS. ; I umbrella, 9s.; I collar, Gd.; 1 bonnet, Is.; and 1 apron, 4d.;
the goods of Lydia Perry, her mistress.
LYDIA PERRY. I live in Lisson-grove—the prisoner was in my service.
On the 27th of April I found some duplicates between her bed and the
sacking—she had then left me—I went with the policeman to her lodging
in Park-street, and found this umbrella, which is .mine, and these other
things.
Prisoner. I gave her Is. for the umbrella soon after I went to her
service. Witness. It is false—I did not sell it her—this bonnet was mine,
but I gave it to my sister.
JAMES PORTER ( police-constable D 55.) I found the bonnet, the collar,
and the apron at No. 69, Park-street—the shawl has not been found—the
prosecutrix found the umbrella.
NOT GUILTY.
1161. MARTHA EVANS was again indicted for stealing 1 shirt,
value Is. ; and 1 sheet, Is. ; the goods of Lydia Perry, her mistress.
LYDIA PERRY. I found the duplicates of this shirt and sheet under the
bed—the sheet is mine, and the shirt was in my care—I had made it for
my lodger.
Prisoner. I was in the habit ofpledging things in my mistress's name.
Witness. Yes, but not these articles.
THOMAS AYRES. I am a pawnbroker. I produce this sheet, which was
pawned with me, but I do not know by whom—this is the duplicate
that was given for it.
GEORGE WAREHAM. I am a pawnbroker. I took in this shirt, but I
do not know of whom—I gave this duplicate for it.
Prisoner. I was ordered to pledge things, and knowing she was nota
prudent character, I gave her notice to leave.
NOT Gvrr,TY.
1162. SOPHIA BEVAN was indicted for stealing 1 pillow, value
2 blankets, 88. ; 1 umbrella, 3s. 6d.; 1 flat-iron, Is. ; and 1 petticoat,
Is. 6d. ; the goods of Mary Ann Bird, her mistress.
MARY ANN BIRD. I live in Duke's-row, New-road. The prisoner was
in my service, and left on Friday week without notice—I missed one
blanket the night before, and taxed her with it—she said I should have it
the next day—I afterwards missed these other articles—they are mine.
WILLIAM FRANCIS BEZANT. I am a pawnbroker. This pillow and pet'
ticoat were pawned by the prisoner.
Prisoner. They were pawned before, for my mistress—I pawned a dress
Of my own.
MARY ANN BIRD re-examined. I have sent her to pawn things, but I
always sent a note—I did not authorize her to take any things—she had
not a dress fit to pawn.
GUILTY. Aged 53. —Confined Three Months.
