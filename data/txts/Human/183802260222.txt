   MR. PHILLIPS to THOMAS HEIGHAM . Q.  Was the prisoner present when his box was searched? A.  No he was not—I found it open. CHARLES STEWART WARDEN. I am superintendent of the watch at Woolwich. On the night of Tuesday, the 6th of February, I received from Mr. Heigham 20 s . in silver, four half-crowns, and ten shillings—I marked them, and delivered to my son one of the marked half-crowns and two shillings—I gave him directions what to do with them—I hare since seen one produced by John Alsford—it is one that I marked on the even ing of the 6th of February. CHARLES GORDON WARDEN. I am the son of the last witness, and am living with my father at Woolwich. About ten o'clock, on the night of the 6th of February, I went to Mr. Machines shop, and took two shillings and one half-crown, marked money—I asked for a Dutch cheese and a bottle of blacking—I gave the two shillings and half-crown to the prisoner—he gave me 6 d . change. THOMAS HEIGHAM re-examined.  On the morning after the evening when I searched the box the third time I found one of the marked half-crowns—and on the evening of the 6th I searched the till, and fond the nine shillings, between ten and eleven o'clock, after Warden's son had been.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  What day did you first search the box? A.  On the 6th, at half-past eleven o'clock in the morning—that was Tuesday—I found 27 s . in half-crowns, shillings, and sixpences—there were more than two half-crowns—I did not count the number—I will not swear there were not more than half-a-dozen—the fact is, that I was so agitated at finding so much money, that I did not stop to count the number of them—I found the box open—there was no key in it. JOHN ALSFORD. I am a constable of Woolwich. I took the prisoner on the morning of Wednesday, the 7th of February—I searched his box, and found 3 l . 15 s ., all in silver—there were two separate papers screwed up, and in one of them there were two half-crowns and one shilling—one half-crown was marked—this is it—( producing one. )
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you take the prisoner up stairs to see his own box searched? A.  I did not—I went up in his absence, and searched it—I did not ask him how much money there was before I went up—I was before the Magistrate—I came down, after searching his box, and the prisoner was given into my custody—I had the money at the time—I did not ask him then if he knew how much money there was in his box, nor till I conveyed him to the station-house—I did there, and he said, "3 l . 16 s ."—I found 3 l . 15 s . CHARLES STEWART WARDEN re-examined.  This is one of the half-crowns I marked. NOT GUILTY.
