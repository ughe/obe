821. CHARLES COX, ANN COHEN, and HENRY SADLER, bur-
glary in the dwelling house of the President, Vice-President, Treasurer, and
Governors of Magdalen Hospital, at St. George's-the-Martyr, Southwark,
and stealing therein 3 shirts, 20 yards of linen, 2 petticoats, qnd other articles,
value 31. 15'. 3d. ; their property : 2nd COUNT receiving the same.
MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution.
MARIA GREEN. I am matron of the Magdalen Hospital, in tbe Black-
friars-road. On the night of 7th June, I had charge of ward No.
the door was closed at half past 12 0'clock at night, it was not locked—I
got up at ten minutes past 6 0'clock next morning, and found that door
locked outside; I had to force it open—when I got into the day ward, I
found the cupboard drawers broken open; I missed three or four shirts
partly made, two petticoats, and a night shirt—the wristband of a shirt was
marked Smaile," and the gusset of a shirt S "—they bad been sent
there to be made—two of the windows in the garden were found open, they
had been closed the night before, they are about three feet from the garden—
on the following Monday a constable called, and brought me these three pieces
of linen (produced) which were in my charge the night before.
WILLIAM ROMAIN (police sergeant, L 14). On Sunday morning 12th
June, I was at the Tower station-house when the prisoners were brought
in—I went to No. 5, Granby-street, and there was no one there then—I
found three new shirts, which are not identified, and these strips of linen with
a name in pencil on them ( produced), they have been in my custody ever
since—I do not know whether any of the prisoners lived there.
ALFRED SPICE ( policeman, V 47). I produce a crowbar found under
the bed at No. 5, Granby-street, in which Cox and Cohen were sleeping,
I saw Blackburn find it there—Sadler was sleeping in another bed, this erow-
bar corresponds with the marks on a piece of wood produced by a gentleman
from the Magdalen, who is not here to-day ; be was yesterday, it was taken
off the door of the cupboard.
Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH. Q. This house is a common lodging
house, is it not? A. I suppose so; there are a large quantity of thieves in
the same street, but I am a stranger there.
RIGBY WARR KENT. I am in the employ of Mr. Dickenson, a pawn-

Turner.
Cross-examined. Q. Has she often pawned these before ? A. Yes; she

JAMES PALMER. I am in the employ of Mr. Dempster, a pawnbroker, of
