CHARLES BOND. I am a poulterer, and live at 3, Church-street, Dept-
ford. On this night I was at my father's house in the Creek-road, and about
twenty minutes past twelve o'clock I heard a cry of "Murder!" I ran out, and
saw the prisoner kneeling on Towner and striking him-he made a kick at
him, but instead of kicking him he kicked me-I immediately pulled the prisoner
off-he ran up the road, calling" Police !"-I ran after him, caught him, and
with assistance kept him till the police came-- he knocked me down and tore
my coat-he said if he had his pricker, or dagger, or sticker, he would put
an end to his b-y life-I think he said sticker-Towner had been drinking
a little, but knew what he was about.
Prisoner. Q. Did you see me kneel on him? A. Yes; and your hands
were on his waistcoat-pocket when I pulled you off-I was never convicted,
and was never a witness on any occasion-I am in work at the present time
-I get 58. 6d. a day, and lose money by coming here.
WILLIAM WELTON (police-sergeant, R 40). I heard a cry of “Murder!"
and “ Police !" went in the direction, and found the prisoner in the act of
running away-he had his arms up, as if he was going to make a start-
I saw Towner bleeding, and all over mud and dirt-he pointed out the
prisoner, and said he had assaulted and attempted to rob him-I said he must
go with me-he was very obstreperous going along, and I was obliged to get
a man and the prosecutor to assist me--the prisoner kicked the prosecutor as
we went along, and said he should like to kill the b-r, and said, "If I live
to get out of the station I shall mark you; I shall serve you out"-Icon-
sider Towner was perfectly sober, but he was so injured that he had the
appearance of being not so.
Prisoner. Q. What did I say? A. That you should like to kill him-
I suppose you meant Towner and not yourself-you had had some drink,
but knew what you were about-the prosecutor was so weak from the injuries
that he was led home, passed on from one policeman to another on their beats.
Prisoner's Defence. I was going through the streets to my quarters, and
met the prosecutor, and asked where I could get some drink; he said, "Go to
hell, you b y soldier !" "Shove on, you red-coated fool!" and used words of
a most exciting nature, my temper could not resist it, and I turned back and
struck him till he fell; when he was down, he cried “Murder!" and
“Robbery!" and that excited my temper; he accuses me because I gave him
the blow he deserved.
