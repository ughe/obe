   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Did you ever see him drunk before that night? A.  Never; I have had opportunities of seeing him before, of an evening—there were twenty or thirty men on the drink on this Easter holiday—they were not at work that week—I was at the police-court, but was not called—I went to know whether there was any truth in the case—I understood that the stabbing had been in the other street. SAMUEL BARRACLOUGH. I am a boiler-maker, of Pomeroy-street, Hatcham—I work at the factory—I was with the prisoner on 11th April, in the evening, at a public-house in the Bath-road, and saw there the witness who has been examined here to-day, and another woman, Coombs—they were in the side bar of the public-house—the prisoner and I were drinking in the front bar—they beckoned to us to go to them—we did so, and they asked us for a drop of gin—the prisoner paid for half a pint of gin and peppermint, and a couple of biscuits—the prosecutrix and the female had some words, and went out—the prisoner followed her, and caught hold of her arm—she wrenched hold of his hand, said he had hurt her, and struck him—he took no notice, but took it in good part, and struck her again—she struck him on the face, in the jaw—she came inside, and he came after her—she struck at him again—he said, "Drop that"—she struck him again, and he said, "If you don't be quiet, I shall smack your face"—she struck him again, and he smacked her face—she was not covered with blood—shehad a slight scratch on her cheek, but I perceived no blood—we came out to go home, to get ready for work in the morning—they were outside, and said that they would give the prisoner in charge to the first policeman they came to—we went down Pomeroy-street, and met a policeman—she said, "I give that man in charge to you"—he said "What for?" she said, "For smacking my face" he took his lamp, looked at her face, and there was a scratch on it—the prisoner said, "She struck me several times, didn't she, Sam? " I said, "Yes"—he had his light there, and Baid, "Where did he strike you?" she said, "Here"—he could see no mark, but collared the prisoner by his handkerchief, and said, "You come with me," holding him with one hand, and the light with the other—the prisoner said, "Let me go; if you want me to go with you, I will go quietly"—the policeman let him go, and we all went away together to the station—I wanted to go in, but they would not allow me, and policeman 161 and the other went in together—I did not hear the prisoner utter any threat at that time, or strike Luscombe any blow—I was near enough to have seen him do so—I continued near them until they reached the station at Peckham—I am positively sure no blow was struck by the prisoner—I have known him since February—he has been quite mild since I have known him—he has not been accustomed to fight with people.
