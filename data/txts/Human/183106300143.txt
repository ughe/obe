I had not time to answer before the prisoner ran up to him- she had passed me with the prosecutor; I cannot say whether she could hear what he said to me, as she was behind me - he had left her and come up to me; she came up and gave the snatch - he took her by the hand and pushed her off the pavement; then she came and made a snatch at his cravat.

 Q. Did she not, in strong terms, deny indignantly that she had any intention of robbing him? A. She certainly said she was no thief - she appeared angry; a piece of his shirt was torn off.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q.Was that the part where his pin was? A.Exactly.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am a Policeman. I was not called up on this occasion - there was no call till I got to Mr. Maccallum - he then directly gave the prisoner into my charge for stealing a diamond pin.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you the Policeman the prosecutor was dissatisfied with? A. I am - I do not know that I gave him any reason to be dissatisfied; I think he acted with rather unnecessary violence - as I was going up Rathbone-place I saw the prosecutor and prisoner, and hearing an altercation between them, I stopped, and saw the prosecutor push her into the road - he directly came on the pavement; I passed them, and he gave her into my custody - it was a clear still morning; I came up directly after the push, and he exclaimed, "She has stolen a diamond pin;" and gave her in charge - I saw him push her, and was near enough for her to see me, if she had looked; I was in my dress, and on duty.
   MATTHEW PATTISSON. I am a Policeman. I was at the station - the prisoner, prosecutor, and Duffield came in; I called Duffield, and she described the spot to me - I immediately went there, found the pin, and brought it back to Mr. Maccallum - I have the shirt where it was torn from the breast.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Maccallum followed me some distance, and asked my address, which I refused; he repeated the question - I asked why he put the question to me; he said he was very anxious to know, that he might call next day - I told him I was so situated I could not tell him; he then behaved very ill to me, indeed, turned away to another woman, and spoke to her - I turned round, took him by the coat or sleeve, and said, "You behave very unlike a gentleman," and believe I said, "You are a blackguard;" he then pushed me to the other side of the road - I thought he would have killed me, he threw me with such force into the road, and at the impulse of the moment I attempted to slap his face, or scratch it - if I could have got at it I would have done it; I declare I never saw his pin till I saw it at the station-house - he took hold of me, pulled me to the station-house, I did not know what for, but thought it was for the assault; when I got there I wept bitterly; he said, "I charge this woman with stealing a pin;" I was astonished - I was not at all anxious for his property, or any body else's; I want nothing but what is right.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1515.  THOMAS GREEN and JOHN GRANT were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of George Joseph Ford , from his person.
   GEORGE JOSEPH FORD. On the 12th of June, about eight o'clock in the evening, I was in Mitre-passage, Hatton-garden, with my wife on my arm, and felt a tug at my outside coat pocket - I felt a shoving against the wall, and in a moment Grant and another man passed; I recollect seeing him behind me in Hatton-garden - I missed my handkerchief; I went to Grant, and said, "Give me my handkerchief;" he said, "I have not got your handkerchief," and swore - I then saw Green run away; he almost knocked my wife down in running; I followed, calling Stop thief! a gentleman came up - I asked him to hold him while I went after the other, and at the bottom of the court two lads said a fellow had run up there with a handkerchief; I went up - it is no thoroughfare, and Green came out; I called him, and said, "Give me my handkerchief;" he said, "I have not got it" - his coat came open, and it fell out; I got a Policeman, who took him - there was a third man, who got away.
   EDWARD GONOUD. I am a Policeman. I received them in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Grant's Defence. I know nothing of this young man.
 G. J. FORD re-examined. They were all three abreast in Hatton-garden, and when I felt them at me Green was exactly behind me - he turned round, and ran.
 GREEN -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 GRANT -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Three Months.
 1516.  RICHARD FRITH was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , 2 shirts, value 20s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 5s., the goods of the Honourable Augustus Barrington.
   WILLIAM HAWES. I am servant to the Honourable Augustus Barrington, who was lodging in Half Moon-street, Piccadilly- the prisoner was footman to Mr. Malon, the landlord. On Monday, the 27th of June, I missed from the drawers two shirts and a pair of white silk stockings - the prisoner had left on the Friday previous; I suspected him, met him in Shepherd's-market about six o'clock next day, and asked if he took them- I said it would be better to give me the tickets of the shirts, than have them taken from him; he then gave me some duplicates - I went to the pawnbroker's, and found the shirts.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You cannot tell whether Mr. Barrington might not have made him a present of them? A. I did not know them certainly.
 COURT. Q. I suppose if Mr. Barrington gave his shirts away he would give them to you? A. Yes.
