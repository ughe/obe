JAMES WELLS. I am a labourer, and live at No. 3, George-street,
Camberwell-road, at the back of the George public-house. On Monday
morning, the 5th of May, or the middle of Sunday night, I was awoke by
the springing of a rattle-I got up and went out to see what was the
matter-the policeman said, would I spring his raule-I went and sprang
it for other policemen to come-I then returned back to the George, and
saw a pair of shoes on the bar--these are them-I knew them to be the
prisoners directly I have known hion about five months, to the best of
my recoliection-he slept in the loft of Mr. Bullock's stable, in Waterloo-
street-about a quarter to five o'clock that same morning I went to that
stable-he was not there-he was to come to work with me-he did not
come during that day- I did not see him until five o'clock that evening,
at the Waterloo Arms-he had an old pair of shoes on then-I said, “ Bili,
where are your prads," meaning shoes-be made no answer-I know
the shoes produced - they are the shoes the prisoner gave to the shoemaker,
or whatever he calls himself, to have an under-lay put on the right shoe,
and the upper-leather of the left was to be sewn up-I was present when
he gave them to the shoemaker in the public-house on the Saturday week
before the robbery-I saw before the Magistrate the shoes the prisoner
had on then-they were not the shoes I had seen him give to the shoe-
maker- I have seen him wearing these shoes every day- I am quite
certain they are his shoes.
Cross-eramined. Q. You swear these are the shoes that were given to
the shoemaker in the public-house! 4. Yes-1 never got into any
scrape but once, and that was nine years ago, about a few potatoes which
I got from Mr. Tibbald's ground, a farmer at Peckham-I had to pay a
fine for it-malleting means fighting- I live in a very respectable house-
the landlady is a widow-I do not know ber name I keep the house
-I am married - I was in bed when I heard the rattle spring-I went to
the stable where the prisoner sleeps about a quarter to five to call him to
come to work—that was my object, nothing else- there was no arrange-
ient between us-I had then given information to the police that these
shoes belonged to the prisoner- I did not tell the police then that I
would show them where the prisoner was- I did about a quarter or twenty
minutes before five-I told Hopper so-he and I went there together, and
he was not there I went with Hopper to have him apprehended if he had
been there.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Was he to have worked with you that day? A. Yes,
Mr. Rofy, my master, told me so I made sure he would be at the stable
at that time—the prisoner had worked for Mr. Rofy before, and was to do
so that Monday.
