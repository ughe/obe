Humphreys. Q.  Is it not a common brothel? A.  No, it is a house of accommodation, but we do not let in children, as you with to insinuate.
   COURT. Q.  Did you ever see Pheasant at your house? A.  No—I do not know him, nor can I say what means he can have of knowing how our house is conducted.
   Pheasant. Q.  The last time I was at your place, was I not robbed there? A.  I never saw your face. THOMAS TWIG. I am a brush-maker, and keep a coffee-house, at No.1, Lower Serle's-place, Temple-bar. On the 11th of June last, I was at West-minister-hall, and saw Humphreys there—understood that he was going to get some fresh bills of indictment—he asked me if I was coming to settle with him about the indictments, that if he had 10 l . from me, and 5 l . each from Wilson and Lane, he would squash  the indictment, and if I did not come into those terms, he would ruin me—I told him I would not give him anything, for I did not keep a house in the parish—I did not keep or have a house in the parish at that time, nor had I done so for these four years—I keep no house jointly with Wilson and Lane, and never did, or ever partook in the profits of any house in which they were interested.
   Humphreys. Q.  How long is it since you were indicted by the parish of St. Martin's for no. 23, Rose-street, where you now live, and where you slept last night? A.  Four years ago—my wife does not sit and take the money there—it is not a common brothel. JOHN WORMINGTON. will you go into the Sessions-house, and see what is the reason that Humphreys is not come out?"—that had reference to the indictment at the Session—about a week after that, I saw him by the Victoria, he asked me if I had seen Humphreys—I said I had not—he said "He has called on me"—I said, "Have you got paid for anything you have done—he said, "No, I have not"—I said, "I think he has led me and you into a shocking trouble"—he said, if I could arrange things in this matter, there was a party at Convent-garden who was indicted (he did not mention any name), and if I could go to that party, and get them to meet him, very likely he could remunerate me something—I did not do so—before I went before the Grand Jury at the Westminster Sessions, I saw Humphreys in the street, near the York Hotel, in the Waterloo-road—that was the first time I ever saw him—we came into communication, and he asked me some questions—he said, "Well, you know Davis?"—I said, "I have known the man as a neighbour for years"—after a little conversation he asked me if I knew Shepherd-street—I said, "Shepheard-street, where?"—he said, "Oxford-street"—I said, "I have known it for forty years, I lived near there"—he said, "Well, there is a chance of getting some money in Shepherd-street"—I said, "In what way?"—he said, "There is a man there who has offended me very much, named Wooldridge, and I mean to indict him"—I said, "I would rather not have anything to do with it"—he asked me to meet him on the
