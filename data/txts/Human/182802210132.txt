SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, FEBRUARY 27.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 734.  BRIDGET RYAN and ELIZABETH MARLOW were indicted for stealing, on the 21st of January , 1 watch, value 20s.; 1 watch-key, value 20s.; 1 watchchain, value 1s.; and 1 watch guard, value 1s., the goods of John King , from his person.
 JOHN KING. On the 21st of January, as I was going home, at two o'clock in the morning, I saw two men ill using the prisoner Ryan; she asked me for protection; I went with her to her own room; I went to bed, and put my watch under the pillow, and in the morning, when I was putting my stockings on, I saw her take my watch and boots and go out, I said "Give me my watch;" she said I should have it; I could not follow, as I was undressed. Marlow had burst the door open, and asked Ryan if she wanted any gin; she fetched the gin, and, in half an hour, she took the things out of the room. I took Ryan at her own room next morning; she said she was very sorry she had committed the robbery - I am sure she said "robbery." I told the Magistrate so.
   JOSEPH HALL. On the 21st of January, Marlow pawned this watch with me, about nine o'clock in the morning; she said she brought it from a friend who was at her house - the prosecutor came in that morning very tipsy.
 RYAN'S Defence. I was letting a friend out; the prosecutor came and spoke to me; he went up; he said he had been with a woman before, and she had robbed him; and in the morning, he said he would thank me to go and get eight shillings on his watch, and then he got me to pawn his ring.
 JOHN KING. I did not; I had plenty of silver.
 MARLOW'S Defence. I saw him give her the watch to pawn, and she gave it to me to get ten shillings on it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 735.  GEORGE DIXON was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of February , 2 cwt. of lead, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Skelton , and fixed to a building of his.
 THOMAS SKELTON. I lost this lead from a house, which I let, in  Church-lane, St. George's; I know it was all safe on the roof on the 1st of February.
   THOMAS SCOTT. I am tenant of this house; the lead was all safe on the 1st of February, as I was on the roof: on the 2d of February it was all cut ready to be taken away - a neighbour came, about seven o'clock that evening, and said there was a man on the top of my house; I went up and found a parcel of lead, loose, on the top of the dormer; it was not rolled up, but cut off, and in about half an hour I again heard there was somebody on the house - I went up with my son, and found another piece cut off, and saw it matched, and it fitted exactly.
   WILLIAM HAY. I live at No. 64, Church-lane, two doors from Scott. On the 2d of February, I came from Hackney, and heard some lead was gone: Scott said he thought there was somebody on the house then; I went up and saw the prisoner on the roof, I followed him over the tops of the houses; he got in at a window, and was taken on the first floor of No. 5, Church-lane - it was then near eight o'clock.
   OWEN SULLIVAN. I am a watchman. I was at Scott's house, and heard an alarm; I went and took the prisoner, on the first floor of No. 5; I heard something drop from the him, which proved to be this knife.
   JOHN JAMES. I am a sailor, and lodge in Rosemary-lane, with the prisoner. On Saturday evening, the 2d of February, he left me about 6 o'clock - I know the knife produced to be his; I have often borrowed it.
   WILLIAM SUMMERS. I am an officer. The prisoner was given into my charge; I found nothing upon him - I have fitted the lead; it corresponded exactly with the dormer.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had no money to pay for a lodging and went there to sleep.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 736.  SAMUEL PHILLIPS, alias    ABRAHAM ABRAHAMS, and CHARLES alias    COLLEY BENJAMIN were indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 2 sovereigns and one 5l. Bank note, the property of Edmund Watling , their master; against the Statute.
 The prisoners having been convicted at Clerkenwell of obtaining the property in question by conspiracy, the case was not proceeded with.
     NOT GUILTY.
 623.  CHARLES ROSSER was indicted for feloniously receiving 2 bars of iron, value 5s. 6d., the goods of our Lord   the King , knowing the same to have been stolen.
 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE WALKER. I am a carpenter employed by the Surveyor of Hyde-park. Part of the iron railing was taken away; I found some of it at Mr. Fox's, of Knightsbridge, about one o'clock on the 23d of January.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Is there anything particular about that railing? A, I know it by putting it up.
   JAMES SHEDDEN. I am surveyor of Hyde-park. I went to Fox's house, on the 24th of January, and found these two iron bars, which were taken from the outlet at Hyde-park; and in consequence of what passed, I went to the prisoner's house, in North-street, Knightsbridge - he was out; I sent an officer, who brought him in about five minutes; I asked him how he became possessed of the iron, which was at Fox's; he hesitated, and said he bought them honestly; I asked where, he said of two boys; I said, "Did you know them;" he hesitated, and then said "I don't know;" his wife said "Yes, Rosser, you do know; you had them from young Griffiths;" he said "Yes it was him and another person he did not know - I asked where Griffith's lived; he made no answer, but his wife said, "Just by, in Cross-street, over a stable:" we went in search, but could not find him; I asked the prisoner what he gave for it; he said 1d. for 3lbs., which would be 1s. 10 1/2d. - they are worth 17s. 4d.
 Cross-examined. Q. As old iron, would it be worth more than he gave? A. It was not old iron; 17s. 4d. is the new price; he went with me to look for Griffiths - the prisoner keeps an old iron shop.
