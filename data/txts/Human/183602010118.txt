I can assure you, Sir Edward, I never had the re motest idea that ever I should be nominated amongst begging-letter wri ters; nor did I for a moment except the reply in answer to my unfortu nate affliction, from so meritorius an individual as yourself.— HENRY STEELE , late 100th regiment.—To General the Honourable Sir Ed ward Paget.—No. 138, Oxford-street, London; 28th of September, 1835.—P.  (No. 5.)—Sir Edward Paget has to acknowledge the receipt of Mr. Steele's letter of yesterday's date, in which he has favoured him with his address, and given him references of so circumstantial a kind, that he came no longer doubt the authenticity of the statement made in his letters of the 17th and 23rd instant.
