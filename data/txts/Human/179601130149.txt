   WILLIAM SAUNDERS sworn.
 Examined by Mr. Gurney. Q. I belong to Mr.  Richard Gearing's barge.
 Q. Did you receive any hides from Mr. Alston? - A. One hundred and nine and fifty-two on the 1st of December.
 Q. Did Mr. Alston come at any time afterwards? - A. Yes; he asked me, if I had all the hides; I looked, and found one was taken out; it was taken out of the fifty-two; only fifty-one remained.
 Prisoner's defence. About half after five o'clock, I had done my work; I went to make water, and found this hide in Thames-street, and kept it till the Tuesday following; and then I went to this porter, at the Saracen's-head, to take it to market to sell it for me; he called at Mr. Cork's, in Leadenhall-market, and was stopped; I met a young man that worked in the deal way, and he told me, that two ships were to land at Gravesend; I went, and there was no such thing; I came back to Deptford as fast as I could; coming to London, I found a young man, he gave me work; and on Monday, about two o'clock, the constable came to take me.
 Court. Q. Was there any body present, that saw you find it? - A. No; it was about six o'clock, on Christmas Eve.(The prisoner called two witnesses, who gave him a good character).

 150.  WILLIAM MORRIS was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 1st of Novenber , a silver skewer, value 18s. and a silver table-spoon, value 10s. the property of Joseph Barnes.
   JOSEPH BARNES sworn.
 I live in  Water-lane, Tower-street; I am a victualler: About six weeks ago, I missed a tablespoon and a silver skewer; I take an account, once a week, of the plate in my house; and I found these articles deficient: the prisoner used frequently to come to the house, with his master, Mr. Dickson, an attorney, in Mincing-lane; I employed Mr. Dickson, in whatever I had to do in the law line; the prisoner had access to my house; he used to go into the kitchen, where the plate and things were; I had not seen the prisoner for a considerable time, it might be a month or six weeks, when I missed these articles.
 Q. Could you be a month missing these articles? - A. They could not have been taken till within a week, without my missing them; I missed them the latter end of November, or beginning of December.
 Q. Where did you see this spoon again, and when? - A. On the 16th of January, in my own counting-house; Mr. Murl, partner with Mr. Cox, sent the spoon, in two pieces, and the skewer, in three; Mr. Bennet is here, who belongs to Mr. Murl.
 Q. When did you last see him, before you missed the things? - A. In the course of that week.
 Q. Then you now correct yourself? - A. Yes; if I said otherwise I was mistaken.
 Q. When Mr. Murl produced them, was any body with him? - A. Yes; a clerk of Mr. Dickson's.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Knapp. Q. This spoon and the skewer were lost, as you imagine, or, at least, might be lost out of your kitchen? - A. I only suppose so; I did not see them taken away.
 Q. You keep a tavern in Water-lane, Tower-street? - A. Yes.
 Q. Of course you have a good deal of company resort to your house? - A. Yes.
 Q. And a vast number of persons have access to your kitchen, as well as Mr. Dickson and his servant? - A. Yes; of course.
 Q. And, of course, in the way of your business, as a tavern-keeper, your plate must lie in that kitchen liable to the inroads of any body that thinks fit to come? - A. Yes.
 Q. The prisoner had been in your house in the course of the week preceding the time of your missing them? - A. Yes.
 Q. A great many persons might have been in your kitchen in the course of that week? - A. Yes.
 Q. In the beginning of December, or the beginning of November, you missed them, after rumaging, and the property was not found till six weeks after? - A. No.
 Q. Have you seen Mr. Murl, who brought the property to you to-day? - A. No.
 Q. Have you seen the clerk who came with Mr. Murl, with the spoons and the skewer, to-day? - A. No; I have not.
