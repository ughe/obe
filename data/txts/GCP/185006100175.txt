Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. When you went up into the
Joft, where was Ward ? A. Down-stairs with the cart-I followed Rayment
through the stable, up the ladder into the loft-I had the conversation with
Rayment up-stairs-Ward did not bear what I said to Rayment in the first
place, but he heard the conversation afterwards-it was about twenty minutes
before eight in the morning when they left the wharf-I bad been watching
from six-I and Garforth did not keep together all the way, but we never lost
sight of the load-we were not more than from twelve to twenty yards from
it at any time I saw nothing of Skelton after Ward had started—I did not
go into any shop--Garforth was with me the whole way--he never was more
than a dozen yards from me I did not make any bote of what passed at the
time, or since I saw my depositions at the police-court, and I have seen
them since at Mr. Bennett's office at Farnival's-inn-I did not go there to see
them-I heard them read over there they were read, to see whether they
were correct--they had been taken down before at the police-court-they
were read to me there was asked if they were correct-- signed them-
what was read at Mr. Bennett's was merely a copy they were read to me, to
see if the copy was correct- I had not the depositions—they were quite
correct-I did not ask to have them read--I have not seen them since, nor a
copy of them-it was not for the purpose of refreshing my memory that they
were read, I did not require it.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL, Q. When you were before the Magi-
strate you could not recollect what Rayment had said ? A. Yes, I did ; there
is my statement of what I said-Garforth did not remind me of what the
statement was- I did not tarn to Garforth and ask him what Rayment had
said I had no conversation with Garforth about what Rayment had said I
only went once to Mr. Bennett's office-I was told by bis brother that he was
the solicitor, and if I were going that way, I should call in as I passed-I did
not go by appointment-my beat is in various parts of Lambeth-I am at
times a plain-elothes man-it was from five to seven days before the Sessions
that I went to Mr. Bennett's it was after I had been before the Magistrate
the last time the depositions were only onee read over at Mr. Bennett's
Garforth went with me-- he happened to be passing that way-we did not
talk about it as we went along, to my knowledge will swear we did not-
I never lost sight of the cart did not notice how many times it turned-1
