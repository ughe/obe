 1672.  ANN MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 bonnet, value 2s., the goods of John Cox.
   SARAH COX. I am the wife of John Cox, and live at Lisson-grove; my husband worked for the prisoner. On the 17th of August I locked up my house and took the key; I returned in half an hour, and missed this bonnet out of a box - I found the prisoner selling it, and asked how she came to do so, when I had been so good to her child.
   HENRY RICHARD BUCKERIDGE. I took her in charge. The person, who was buying the hat, has run away.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never saw the hat till I saw it in her hands.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1673.  CORNELIUS McCOY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 handkerchief value 4s., the goods of John Humphries.
 JOHN HUMPHRIES. My son had been bathing in the canal, on the 30th of July - he had a handkerchief when he went out, and returned without it.
   WILLIAM HUMPHRIES. I went to bathe. I left my father's handkerchief in my hat, by the side of the Regent's-canal, near Primrose-hill- my cousin called me out, and said the prisoner had robbed me.
   JOHN ADDERLEY. I was by the side of the canal, and saw the prisoner take the handkerchief out of the hat, and ran away - some other boys were about - I saw him fold it up and give it to some one - he stood still, and I gave an alarm.
   JOHN HURWORTH. I was on the opposite bank, and saw the prisoner get out of the canal naked, and take the handkerchief; I did not see what he did with it; he ran among about a dozen others, some of whom were naked - I secured him; he was then dressed - he said he had taken it, and given it to some person, whose name I do not remember - he afterwards refused to tell the name - I am certain he is the boy.
   JOSEPH KINGSTON. I am an officer, and received him in charge; he said he had given the handkerchief to another person, but would not tell me who; he said, at the watch-house, that he would rather be lagged for seven years, than he would split.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 1674.  SAMUEL MESFIELD was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 coat, value 5s., the goods of George Pritchard.
 GEORGE PRITCHARD. On the 31st of July I was bathing near  Maiden-lane-bridge, and lost my coat from the bank - I saw the prisoner put it on and walk away - I got out, and called Stop thief! he pulled it off and ran away - I saw him in custody.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. What time was this? A. About eight o'clock; he had no other coat on.
   SAMUEL STEVENS. I apprehended him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been bathing in the morning, and lost my coat; and in the evening I went and took this. GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1675.  WILLIAM LILLYWHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 2 rings, value 8s.; 6 tea spoons, value 10s., and 1 pair of sugar tongs, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Burchall; and ANN LILLYWHITE was indicted for feloniously receiving the same.
   ALICE BURCHALL. I am the wife of Thomas Burchall, a plasterer; we were in possession of a house, No. 42, York-terrace. On the 9th of September the prisoner, William, was working there as a painter; and on that day I missed this plate, which I had seen safe on the 4th, in a chest in the kitchen; the key was left in it.
 Prisoner WILLIAM LILLYWHITE. Q. Was nobody else in the house? A. There were two plumbers, but they were in my presence all the time - I went out for five minutes and left the prisoner there.
   JOHN LIMBRIC. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner on the 9th of September; he denied the charge; Burchall said there had been nobody else in the house, and that when she went out he shut the door and kept her out; I went to No. 13, Crown-street, where he lived, and found his wife, who afterwards produced some duplicates - I asked if she had nothing else - she said No - I saw something in her hand, and found a purse, containing the duplicate of two rings - she said her husband gave them to her.
   THOMAS BLACKMAN. I am a pawnbroker. On the 7th of September the female prisoner pawned the rings.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 WILLIAM LILLYWHITE's Defence. There were various workmen in the place; one was there an hour and a half; I can prove we have had the rings two years.
 WILLIAM LILLYWHITE -    GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 ANN LILLYWHITE -    NOT GUILTY.
 1676.  SARAH MOORE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 2 sheets, value 5s., the goods of William Williams , in a lodging-room.
   MARY ANN WILLIAMS. I am the wife of William Williams. I let a room to the prisoner and her husband the beginning of July last; her husband absconded a a week before she left the room - I went in and found the sheets were gone, which I had let with the lodging.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned these sheets with me.
   THOMAS DAGNALL. I am an officer. I took up the prisoner - she said she had pawned the sheets, but wished her husband to know nothing about them.
