 859. GEORGE ATTERTON was indicted for stealing on the   3rd of December , 1 glazier's diamond, value 30 s  ., the goods of William Walker ; and WILLIAM JOHNSON for feloniously receiving the same, well know ing it to be stolen, against the Statute, &c. WILLIAM WALKER. I am a painter and glazier, and live in Gravel-lane. The prisoner Atterton is the son of a gardener, employed at Mr. Druces's, Denmark-hill, Camberwell—I was at work there and the other prisoner came backwards and forwards—I saw him there—I missed the diamond on the 23rd of December—it was taken from a place on the premises which I used as a shop—I had seen the prisoner on the premises coming backwards and forwards, and he had access to the property—this is it—it is worth 30 s .—I have had it six years. SAMUEL CHAPMAN. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Lock's-fields. This diamond was pawned with me—I do not know who by. JOHN CALLIGAN. I am a policeman. I received information on the 10th of February, from Atterton's brother and another boy, who came to me—I went with them to Johnson's and asked him what he had done with the diamond which he had bought—he asked me what diamond—I said the one he bought of Atterton—he said he never bought one at all—I said there was a boy there could prove he had bought it; and the boy said, "You did buy it: you gave him 6 d . and a pennyworth of apples for it"—I asked him where it was—he said in pledge—I asked where—he said at Mr. Chap man's—I asked him where the duplicate was—he said he had given his mother the duplicate to put away with the rest—the mother went and got it—I took him into custody—Johnson said he pawned it for 4 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  Did you say the boy's mother gave you the duplicate? A.  No; his brother brought me the duplicate, but he got it from the mother—I do not know that she was in the habit of send ing him to pawn. NOT GUILTY
