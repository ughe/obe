Re-examined. It is true that my name was Elizabeth Bowen Kendal-
I did not know when these persons came who and what they were-they
ask d me who was the mother of this baby Verch, and ne of them
said "It is Miss Addiscott's own child we have been told," and I said
"What, Sir!”-that is why I gave my Christian name; if I had told
them who I was I should not have heard what they had to ray to me, and
I thought it a cruel thing to impute that to my child—it was Mr. Pilcher
who said that my daughter had had an illegitimate child, and he has said
to others that Kate Veroh was my daughter's illegitimate child-I thought
it was cruel; there is not a blemish on her character-I have never known
anything against her virtue-I can defy the world as to her character-
my husband is still in the Customs-we have been married 35 years-our
children are all grown up, four are living- I have told the truth--the
policeman who was present on boxing night with Mrs. Pearson is, I
believe, one of the Greenwieh policemen-he had his uniform 01-I have
not seen him since; it was because Mrs. Pearson was dancing about that
I say she bad something to drink-her conduct was most disgraceful, no
decent woman would have acted as she did.
By the Court. When my daughter established this home she con-
Bulted me about it, and she continued to do so during the time she had
the children, but I never interfered with her money matters, or any thing
of that kind - I do not know who Mr. J. Williams is; I never heard of
him, I think somebody audited the accounts, but I do not know him-
the laundry was established at the commencement of the home, and there
is a washhouse with a copper where the clothes were Washed--that was
used for the purpose of employing widows, or if big girls came to the
home I can tell you of widows who washed there, but I know them more
by their Christian names than their surnames--I know Ellen Swales and
Mrs. Swales washed ladies' clothes there, but it was not continued long-
the laundry existed about six months in 1878, I think, or not so long-it
was found a useful occupation; it was given up owing to my daughter's
ill health, she could not take so much upon her to do--Mr. Clark has &
letter from a lady named Blundell, and several other ladies sent washing,
