Cross-examined by MR. COOPER. Q.  Had you known Marson before? A.  No—he was the principal speaker—he met me there alone—I think it is very likely. considering the circumstances, knowing that it was done under the Kennington Union, that I should have sent the materials, supposing Day had taken no part; but the guarantee was an additional security—in the conversation in the Builders' Arms, Marson said, "Mr. Day, some persons have been here inquiring the value of your land, what you let it at per house," referring to this very land—be said, "About your land here"—I cannot swear that the word "here" was used, but he referred to that land—I have heard that Day has banked, but rather disreputably—this letter is my writing ( looking at one )—in this I applied to Day for the money—I know that he lived at Elt✗—the solicitor who sits by you, called once on me—I did not on that occasion agree to wait for the money—I said I had been out of town, or I should have prosecuted Day before—-the solicitor said the money would be paid very likely that day—I said I would not wait longer than that day. ROBERT STIMPSON. I am carman to Mr. Doulton. On 26th Aug. I car ried a load of fifty pipes from my master's to Victoria-road—I saw a sewer going on—I asked for Mr. Marson—he was not there—I went further, and inquired, but he was not there—I brought the pipes back to Victoria-road—I saw Mr. Marson there—he said to me, "Where have you been? you have been to the wrong place"—I said, "No"—I delivered the pipes at Victoria-road—Marson received them, and signed my bill, which was handed up here last Session—I heard something, and I communicated it to my master.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were there men working there? A.  Yes, at the sewer in Victoria-road, and the other sewer too. ROBERT COCKBURN. I am a stone-mason, at Marlborough-road, Chelsea. On 26th or 27th Aug. I was asked if I knew any one who would by some pipes—I took fifty pipes from Victoria-road, where they were making a drain, to Mr. Thirst, in Cadogan-street, Chelsea—4 l . 19 s . 6 d . was paid for them—Marson got two men to help me load—I saw the prisoner about the 15th Sept., I think—I told him I had heard that he and Marson had received some pipes under false pretences at a potter's some where at Lambeth, and I in tended to go on to Marson and ascertain what it was—he said, "If you go there you will transport me, and think of my wife and child, what are they to do?"—I said, "You did not think of me"—I went on to Marson's, found Mr. Doulton, and went there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you known Marson before? A.  Yes, he was a joiner and builder—I had known Day a few months—Marson told me Day was an independent gentleman, living at Eltham—he brought him to my house, and said, "This is the gentleman that is going to advance me some money towards building"—when I spoke to Mr. Day, he did not say I was wrong—he flew in a passion, and said, "If you go there you will transport me"—I did not speak to policeman—I went to Mr. Doulton's—he said he was busy, and could, not attend to it; when he could he would send me a letter and let me know.
   MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  When did Marson bring Day to you? A.  About June of July. EDWARD THIRST. I am a builder. On 27th Aug.
