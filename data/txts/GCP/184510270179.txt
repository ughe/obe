GEORGE CASELEY. I saw the prosecutor on Whit-Monday- he was
not married that day to anybody at Paddington church.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you related to the prosecutor ? 4, No-I
have known him about three months I was helping him all that day I
have no sister-I am not in any way connected with the family.
Prosecutor. My name is Patrick Henry-I am called Patrick Henry,
since the day I was at the office-I went by that name, because they
said it was necessary before that I used generally to be called Henry-I
was confirmed in 1827-before that I was called Patrick only, and after
that I was generally called Henry-my mother used always to call me
Patrick Henry-I generally call myself Patrick Henry, but before I went
to the Police-office, Henry, generally--my mother always called me Patrick
Henry after my confirmation-I was confirmed by the name of Henry only
- I did not give up the name of Patrick-the bishop called me Henry, by
my desire.
Jory. Q. At the time of your marriage what name did you sign? 4.
I was married to my first wife--sbe is now dead-I signed Henry, not
Patrick- I was married by bands, and called Henry-I never had any
other wife when the gentleman spoke, I thought he might allude to this
party, whom I know nothing about-she has no claim to me at all on any
solemn occasion, such as being sworn, I call myself Patrick Henry-(the
signature to the certificate found on the prisoner was Patrick Henry Smith.)
Not GUILTY.
2154. BRIDGET SMITH was again indicted for feloniously attempt
ing to administer to Catherine Smith a large quantity of oxalic acid, with
intent to murder her.
Thomas HYETT. On Friday, the 26th of Sept., I saw the prisoner in
Church-street, Cornwall-road-she gave me a brown paper parcel, and
told me to take it to No. 3, Pier's Cooperage, to tell Mrs. Smith to get
tea ready, and she would come-I took the parcel, and delivered it to
Mrs. Smith-I am sure the prisoner is the person.
CATHERINE SMITH. On Friday, the 26th of Sept., I received a parcel
from Hyett-it contained lump and moist sugar, and a little tea in another
paper-I took it and showed it afterwards to my son, and to Mrs, Pem-
berton-1 gave it to my son in the same state as Hyett had given it
to me.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. Did you expect that evening, after
wbat Hyett told you, that some woman would come and drink tea with
you? A. No-he told me a woman was coming to drink tea, but he
did not mention her-I expected a woman to come to tea, from what he
said, but not the prisoner-I could not expect anybody to come till
heard what the boy said he said a woman was coming to tea, and to have
the kettle boiling.
Court. Q. When he said that, did you think anybody would come?
4. No-I said to myself, “I don't know who sent it;" nor did I expect
the tea from anybody.
Elizabeth PEMBERTON. On the 26th of Sept., I went to Mrs. Smith's
-she gave her son & parcel in my presence-I saw him take it to Mr.
Handley's in the state it was in.
