Yes; I found them waiting for me—the mother was very far advanced in pregnancy, and has been delivered in gaol—the whole place presented a scene of destitu tion, but I have witnessed many worse—they had furniture, and bread and butter—she was very thankful to me for the mutton—I found it on the fire next day—she told me they were in a horrible state of starvation—I offered to attend her—a child having mesenteric disease gets emaciated, and very frequently the lungs get diseased—consumptive people get thin—I did not know the child had disease of the lungs till the post mortem  examination, at which I was not present—the mother seemed as intent and anxious as mothers usually are—I have seen the girl in the street—she was very healthy—I know that consumptive people eat voraciously. THOMAS JACKMAN. I am house-surgeon to St. Bartholomew's Hospital. The child was brought there on 27th Jan.—I made a post mortem  examination—the cause of death was a burn—the body was in a very emaciated state; the lungs were affected with inflammation of the lower lobes to a considerable extent, otherwise the child was healthy; there were some recent bruises on the head and legs—there was a bruise on both eyebrows, which could not be produced by a child falling on the floor—there were about three bruises on each leg, corresponding with each other, as if the two legs were struck at the tame time—there was no disease about the child to account for the emaciated state in which I found it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been admitted as a surgeon? A.  About twelve months—I pay for my place at the hospital, I have no salary—I cannot always tell by inspection what disease has or has not existed.
