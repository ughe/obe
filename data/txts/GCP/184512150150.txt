JOHNSON, Mayor.
THOMAS LEEDHAM ROBINSON. I am a wine-merchant and a banker.at
Croydon-I do not know the prisoner, but I paid some one 141. 25. Od
on the 29th March, and he made an entry in my book- this is it.
Cross-examined. Q. Whether he had called on you several times and not
seen you, you cannot possibly tell ? A. Not at all.
WILLIAM HOLMES DIVERS. I am a wine and spirit merchant at Yarmouth
--I paid the prisoner on the 17th April 131. 16s. 6d., by a cheque on Gurney's
bank.
Cross-examined. Q. You paid him 131. 38.? A. Yes, the rest was dis-
count-I had known him two years- I do not recollect the day of the week
on which he received this money-I never heard of his having been thrown
from his horse.
Henry NEALE. I live at Cheltenham, and am a china dealer. On the
31st August I paid the prisoner 4l. 43.-the account was 41. 138.-- he gave
me a receipt.
Not Guilty.

347. THOMAS APPLEFORD was indicted for stealing 2 shirts, value
38.; 1 pair of shoes, 118.; 2 pair of stockings, 2s.; and 2 handkerchiefs, 18.;
the goods of George Holdaway.
ELIZABETH GILLHAM. I live in Hampshire-I sent some articles to
Holdaway, the carrier, on the 23rd Nov.--they were lost-these are them.
GEORGE HOLDAWAY. I am a carrier. On the 24th Nov. I received some
goods from the Vauxhall-station, packed up in a bundle I did not know
what the bundle contained they were directed for Edward Gillham-that
direction is lost-I took it from the rest of the parcels, and left it in the
stable at Nine-elms-it was directed to Camberwell-we did not take it--we
expected the lad to call for it-when I went to look for it in the stable it
was gone-I asked the prisoner if he knew where the bundle was-he said
he had never seen it- I told him he must have seen it—he was employed by
me, and had access to the stable-I left him, and returned in balf an hour-
then found he had changed his shirt-I asked what he had done with the
shirt he had on-he said, "I will tell you the truth: I did have the bundle"
-I asked where it was-he pointed to a box in the stable, and said it was
there I went to the box and found these things, which Mrs. Gillham has
identified.
Guilty. Aged 21.-Confined Six Months.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
348. SARAH HOARE was indicted for stealing 35 gross of matches, value
148.: Alb. of wax, 1s. 6d.; and 37 boxes, 2s. : the goods of Richard Bell
and another, her masters; and WILLIAM HOARE, for feloniously receiv-
ing the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
SAMUEL ALEXANDER BELL. I live at Wandsworth: I am a patent wax
and match manufacturer, in partnership with Richard Bell; the prisoner
Sarah Hoare was in our service. There was property taken away from our
premises, this identifies with ours, and was never sold-here is part of it with
the name on, and part with the name off - this is our property-we never seld
it her, nor gave her any right to take it.
