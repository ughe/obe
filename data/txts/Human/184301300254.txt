Cross-examined by MR. HEATON . Q.  How long have you known the prisoner? A.  Since the 3rd of Sept.—I have paid my rent twice to him—I did not see Mr. Judd daily—sometimes he was not there for two or three days together—sometimes not from one week to another—he has another shop at some distance—I have lived in the house with my husband since the 6th of May—we were on friendly terms with the prisoner—I did not ask him to get me this stand at the cost price—my son-in-law sent me to buy a double washhand-stand—I do not know that the prisoner ordered it of the maker for me, nor that 4 s . is the maker's price for it, and the ordinary charge 6 s .—the prisoner did not say he had got this from one of the persons who made for them, on purpose for me—when I asked him he said he had not got one at that price in the house—he did not say he would get one—it might be a week or a fortnight before I got it—I did not tell him that 4 s . was as much as I could afford—I do not recollect that he said he would get me one—it was not for myself, and of course I took no more trouble about it—I did not ask him to get one made for me—he never said he would get the person who made for Mr. Judd to make one for me, or anything of the kind—there was no other person present when I paid for it—I did not see the stand in the shop, nor see it brought up to my room—it was painted, and looked quite new and clean—I do not know whether it looked as if it had come from the maker—I know that a stand would acquire a darker hue by remaining in the shop—it did not look as if it had been in a broker's shop. SOPHIA MARIA UNDERWOOD. I live in Fore-street, Cripplegate. On the 5th of Dec. I bought at Mr. Judd's shop, twelve yards of carpeting at 2 s ., 2 d . a yard, and a hearth-rug for 12 s . 6 d .—my daughter was with me—to the best of my belief the prisoner is the man I paid—he gave me this card ( pro  ducing it) as a bill—the things came to 1 l . 18 s . 6 d .
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not Mr. Judd present? A.  The prisoner showed us a hearth-rug, which was 12 s . 6 d .—we said we would rather have a darker, and he brought us one which he said was 14 s . 6 d .—I said I would give him 12 s . 6 d . for that—Mr. Judd came into the shop, and the prisoner showed him the mark on the back, and he then left the shop—I said, "If they won't take 12 s . 6 d . for it, I won't have it," and the prisoner rolled it up, and we had it—after the 14 s . 6 d . rug had been shown to us, the prisoner and Mr. Judd spoke together—we had agreed for the carpet before—I did not hear the prisoner ask Mr. Judd whether he would take 12 s . 6 d ., nor tell Mr. Judd the quantity of carpet we had bought—it was then rolled up—it was a Kidderminster carpet—I did not hear the prisoner say there were twelve yards of it at 2 s . 2 d . a yard, or ask whether he might let the rug go at 12 s . 6 d .—he rolled it up, and then Mr. Judd left the shop.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Had you been long in the shop? A.  No, I was only there about a quarter of an hour—Mr. Judd came in at a side door. ELIZA ANN UNDERWOOD. I went with my mother to Mr. Judd's shop, on the 5th of Dec.—I saw her pay for the carpet and rug—I think the prisoner is the man she paid 1 l . 18 s . 6 d . to—Mr. Judd came in while the bargain was going on—he staid a very short time—he left us together.
