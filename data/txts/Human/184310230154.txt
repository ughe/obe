 3010. ALFRED CURTIS was indicted for feloniously receiving, of an evil-disposed person, on the   29th of Sept ., 1 printed book, value 5 s  .; the goods of Thomas Longman and others, well knowing them to be stolen: against the Statute, &c.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM LONGMAN. I have three partners—Thomas Longman is one—we live in Paternoster-row—I have every reason to believe this book is our property—on the 22nd of Sept., I ordered two out often of these books, which we had, to be marked—between the 22nd and 29th two of them were sold, one went to Bristol and the other to Liverpool—when this book was found in the prisoner's house, we had only seven left, therefore one was missing—I have got the one from Bristol back again—that is marked, and the one found at the prisoner's is marked.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  The one you sent to Bristol was sold? A.  Yes; there is no difference between that and the one found at the prisoner's. WILLIAM SHARP. I am in the prosecutor's employ. On the 22nd of Sept. we had ten books of "Lalla Rookh"—I marked two of them—when the prisoner was apprehended, we had only seven of them in our stock, which were all unmarked—one of the marked ones had been sold to a person at Bris tol—this is that identical copy.
   COURT . Q.  Was any note taken that it was a marked copy, when it was sold? A.  No, but the two marked ones were the only ones that were accessible to be sold—on the Wednesday, before the prisoner was apprehend ed, we had not one of the two marked ones in the stock—they were both gone—on the Thursday, we wanted to sell another copy to go to Liverpool, and we sent one not marked.
   MR. CLARKSON  here withdrew from the prosecution. NOT GUILTY.
