To Mr. David. I have never been to Stammers's house when Mr.
and Mrs. Calcutt were there. As to whether 1905 was the heyday of
the prosperity of the Calcutts, I do not understand those big words.
LILY MATTHEWS, 8, Bartlett's Buildings, Holborn Circus. I used to
be domestic servant in the employment of the Calcutts. I recognise
Trots and Gilder as having called at Grafton Street. Grimes also
used to come. I have seen Gilder there very often.
HAROLD TEwson, pawnbroker and jeweller, 101 and 103, Burdett
Road, said Caloutt had been a customer of his for some years, and he
remembered selling him an 18-carat gold albert chain on March 27,
1905, for 12 guineas. He believed the chain produced to be the one.

Mrs. SARAX CATHERINA Jones, 121, Mile End Road, baby out-

CHARLES HARRY HARRIS, 115, Lower Road, Rotherhithe, manager
for the executors of Mr. Greaves, jeweller, Mile End Road. I know
Caloutt and I have known Hirst very well for about 15 or 16 years,
My house is about 150 yards from the "Three Crowns"; I went
there almost daily. I know nearly all the prisoners. When Caleutt!
bought things he paid either by cash or by cheque. When he did
not pay at once I entered the things in a book. Cash transactions
would be entered in the daily sale book. (The witness spoke to the
following articles as having been bought by Calcutt: A diamond
stud, £12, March 25, 1904; a china mirror, £5 156., on June 28;

18-carat gold-mounted umbrella, £3; on November 9, 1905, gold
cigar case, £20 15s.; on December 23, 1905, a diamond pin, £10
(formerly belonged to Marquis of Anglesey); on May 29, 1906,
15-carat gold masonic charm, £2 10s.; on December 20, 1906, .
lamb's wool travelling rug, £2 15s.). On January 15, 1907, Hirst
ordered a gold watch with an inscription : "Presented to Richard
