   COURT. Q.  I suppose you cannot speak to the identify of the handker chief, farther than saying it was the same pattern? A.  No. SOPHIA CRESSY. I am the prosecutrix's mother, and live with her. The prisoner lodged at her house previous to 31st Oct.—for the last three months he has conversed with me respecting his marrying my daughter—I told him he had better not think anything of her, as she had a large family—he spoke of marrying her several times—I saw him on Friday, 29th Oct.—there was a quarrel about the tea not being ready when he came home in the evening—there were a great many words, and, being persuaded by the police, we turned him out of doors, and would not let him rest in the dwelling that night—on Saturday morning he came to the window, and asked for his victuals—I gave him the box he had his victuals in, and he took what he had, out of the box at the window—I slept in my daughter's room on the Sunday night, and was awoke by the falling of a foot-board—I afterwards, when the light was brought, saw the state in which her throat was—I picked up a handker chief which laid by the bedside, and laid it on the bed—I am sure that hand kerchief was not in the room on the previous night—I have seen the prisoner wearing a handkerchief of the same colour as that, and have washed it several times while he lodged in the house—there is no particular mark about it—to the best of my knowledge it is the same—he had the same sort of handker chief on Saturday morning—when I found the handkerchief, I had a strong suspicion, the instant I saw it, that it was the prisoner's—I was the first that went down stairs—I went to the back door—it was open, and there was a ladder placed, so that a person could get through the window of the room where the witness Howe used to sleep—the ladder belonged to Mr. Soames, a farmer, living near—I saw a pair of boots which were found in the privy—they were the prisoner's boots—a woman of the name of Adams showed them to me—they are the same that the policeman, James M'Colloch, has the custody of—I did not fasten up the house the night before. JOSEPH HOWE. I am a labourer, and live in the prosecutrix's house.
