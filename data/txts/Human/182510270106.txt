 Re-examined. Q. Was there any limit set to the quantity of beer? A. No; if it was a wet job it was more.
   JAMES HAMPDEN. I am a coal-porter. I have often worked for Mr. Leslie, with other men, since 1821 - we had our beer, for which he either sent the money by his boy or his wife - I do not know a time when the money was not sent - I have heard Mr. Leslie come in and say, "Is all settled?" and he said to Mr. Smith, "Don't you trust them black chaps on my account; if you do I will never pay."
   GILBERT LEE DOWSE. I work at the West India Docks. I did live with Mr. Leslie, and carried out coals for him. When the barges came in - the men used to have so much beer, and I used to take money to pay for it - I was at his house from the latter end of 1821, till Easter 1822 - I never knew, in all that time, a barge come in without the beer being regularly paid for - I never heard Mr. Smith or his wife say that Mr. Leslie owed them any thing.
   JOHN TINGAY. I am a carpenter. I was never the bearer of any message to Mr. Leslie; but I went to Mr. Smith's to get a pint of porter - I think about October, 1823 - Mr. Smith and Mr. Leslie were there, and a few words arose - Mr. Smith asked Leslie if he owed him any thing - Mr. Leslie said "You do; a five of coals, and labour done by horse and cart;" they had a few words, and Mr. Smith told him, if he would call on him in a few days he would pay him; he did not say "Why do you ask me for such a small sum when you owe me money."
 Cross-examined. Q. Was Baker or Johnson there? A. No, I did not hear of any scandal about my coals.
 MR. SMITH re-examined. Q. Do you know Robert Ball? A. Yes, I never sent him with any message to Mr. Ford, or to Mr. Leslie - I had some conversation with Ball about this trial; it was before last sessions - he said it is put off till next sessions; and I said he has put me to so much trouble and expense he ought to be transported. I never said if Ford would pay the expence I would not go on with the indictment. I never spoke to Mr. Gomersall on the subject - I do not know him by name. I have seen this gentleman before, but I did not say to him, on the 8th of August, that if the money was paid, or they would come forward like men, I would not go on with the prosecution, nor that had not old Ford been so d - d impudent, I should not have indicted Leslie at all - this gentleman came to me from Whitecross-street - my daughter was not there - during part of this time she was at school - she served in the bar a year and a half - she has been a witness for me. I do not know that my son was out walking last night - I saw him in bed yesterday about eleven o'clock.
 Cross-examined. Q. You have heard Barlow, Ralph, and the others, examined; and you know they were labourers employed by Leslie - is it true that the beer sent for them down to the water-side was paid for by them either at the time or on the evening of the same day? A. No. never, that I declare - it was always ordered to be chalked up.
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. Were you always at home? A. No, I was generally at home, and sometimes chalked it up myself; I never knew beer to be paid for that was ordered for the bargeman - at least what I drew and sent for them was not paid for.
   ROBERT BALL. Q. Do you know Mr. Smith. A. I do; I had conversation with him; I heard him say he wished to God that somebody would speak to Mr. Ford, and if he would pay the expenses, he would drop the action.
   EDWARD FRANCIS GOMERSLLA. I am a wax-chandler. On the 8th of May I was at Mr. Smith's house; I had some conversation with him - he said, had not old Ford been so d - d impudent to him, he would not have indicted Leslie at all.
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. Where is your shop? A. I am in the employ of Thorp and Burch, in Jewry-street, and did not go there from Whitecross-street - I went from my lodging in Fore-street - no one sent me - but I went to ask the particulars of Mr. Leslie's affairs, as an old friend, without asking Leslie's leave - I went at ten o'clock at night - there were two gentlemen there, but they retired, and Mr. Smith brought me a glass of gin and water - I asked him if he knew Leslie? he said, Yes - he shut the door and sat down - I said, "This is an unpleasant business between you and Leslie," he said, "Yes it is; what is to be done? - it is their own fault - if they will pay me my money, I will let him out of prison." Leslie was then in Whitecross-street. - I did not tell Leslie or his friends what had passed, but I spoke to his attorney in a few days, and the attorney told me it was necessary for me to be here, and say what he had told me - when I told the attorney I had got that answer, he did not make any overtures to settle.
 Re-examined. Q. Were you ever in White Cross-street yourself? A. No, never.
 HENRY SMITH Re-examined. Q. When was the first item in this account? A. It is for beer sold in 1821, 3 l. 15 s. 4 1/2 d., which Mr. Leslie had a great deal of for the bricklayers, while they were building his house, before he began to be a coal-merchant.
 Three witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Confined Six Months.
