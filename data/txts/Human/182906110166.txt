   TIMOTHY BYRCUMORE. I produce this knife, which was given me by Mears; the prisoner came into the watch-house without a shirt, and looked like a madman.
 WILLIAM JAMESON. I went to the watch-house, and found the prisoner in one of the cells: he began by stating that Perrys' wife had called him a rogue, and a rascal, said his mother was a wh-e, and he was a bastard - I said "You had better compose yourself; where is your shirt?" he said they tore it off: I went and consulted about searching him - when I went back to him, he said he had done it, and he should be hanged next Monday week; in going from the watch-house he met Perry, and said to him."Jack, I have killed your wife; I shall be hanged tomorrow week;" he sent me word afterwards that he had a present to make me, if I would come and see him hanged - he afterwards begged me to go for a shirt for him; I went to his sister-in-law - she said she could not get out, as her two children were had with the measles, and I took it him in the evening; he stated that Mrs. Perry was the cause of his not marrying a girl he had kept company with some time.
 Cross-examined. Q.What took you to the watch-house? A. I go there for letters and orders for the Commissioners; I am street-keeper - I did not think any harm of speaking to the prisoner.
 LEAH GOODING re-examined. I was stabbed in my left breast, and a little on my arm; he did keep company with a young woman - I know they were asked in church, and I persuaded her to marry him.
 EDWARD PARKER. I am a porter, and live in Tottenham-court-road. I have known the prisoner six years - he bears a humane character; he got a hurt, and has not been well since - for the last three months he has been drinking more like a madman than any thing else, and when he drinks he is overcome by it; he has, complained of his head and side.
 JOHN CAMPBELL. I am a watchman, and have known the prisoner five years. I and my fellow servant got to the watch-house just before him, and the prosecutrix - I heard her say Perry was with that d-d villain Waters - she ran to him, and struck him repeatedly; he thrust her away with his hand and foot.
 EDWARD TALBOT. I am a watchman. I was going to the watch-house, and saw the prosecutrix strike the prisoner several times; and as she passed me she struck Perry, and said, "You are not a man, for not taking my part."

 1373.  GEORGE YOUNG was indicted for feloniously assaulting Mary Grant , on the 11th of April , putting her in fear, and taking from her person, and against her will, 1 pocket-book, value 1s.; 1 sovereign, 4 half-crowns, 3 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the property of Alexander Grant.
   MARY GRANT. I am the wife of Alexander Grant; the prisoner lodged above me; Mr. King holds the house, and I lodged there - the prisoner had lodged there a quarter of a year, and is a painter. On the 11th of April, about five o'clock, or a little after, I returned home with a pocketbook and 1/2lb. of tea, tied in a white handkerchief - there was a sovereign, seven half-crowns, some shillings, and 6d. in halfpence, in the pocket-book; the prisoner was at the door, and he began in his foolish way, as he does when he is tipsy - I said, "Let me in;" he played tricks and molested me in the passage, and on the stairs he began violence; it continued till he got up to the landing-place: he then said, "I will have none of your b-y soup; I will have what you have got here;" he began to beat me, and snatched it by violence from me - he had not beaten me before that, only pulled me about; the handkerchief came untied, but I held it, and received more violence than I should have; the door opened, and I threw them on the bed - he got the pocket-book, but the tea went on the bed- I then swooned away: I said nothing more that night. but next morning I went to his room, about five o'clock - he did not appear sober then; I asked him to give me the pocket-book and papers; he said, "I don't recollect, she is not at home; but if you want a few shillings, I can lend them to you;" he cohabited with a woman of the name of Parker - I never got my book or money: he followed me down, and gave me three blows on the back of my neck, he said, "Where is your b-y pocket-book now?" and mentioned such words as I cannot repeat.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1374.  MARY MOODY was indicted for feloniously assaulting John Henrickson , on the 25th of May , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 3 sovereigns, his monies. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1375.  JOSEPH BYRON was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of May , at St. Anne, 2 pins, value 4s.; 1 brooch, value 4s.; 3 coats, value 6l.; 4 pairs of trousers, value 2l.; 1 handkerchief, value 4s.; 1 box, value 5s.; 1 waistcoat, value 12s.; 1 shirt, value 5s.; 1 pocket-book, value 1s.; 5 sovereigns, and 1 half-sovereign, the property of William Hunt , in his dwelling-house.
 JANE HUNT. I am the wife of William Hunt - he lives at  No. 22, Phoebe-street, Limehouse, in the parish of St. Anne. The prisoner lodged at my house, with two other young men - he came on the 13th of May; he was there two or three nights; the then said he was going to Yorkshire; some of this property was in drawers, and some in a trunk - he slept there on Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday nights. On the Friday evening I put my pocketbook into the trunk, in the room in which he slept, and on the Saturday morning I missed it, when I went to get the money - he had left that morning, and said he was going to Yorkshire, by the steamer, on the Monday morning; he had paid me, but I thought he was coming again on the Saturday night and Sunday, but he did not return: I have only seen the coat since, which had been in the trunk - I lost two pins, a brooch, three coats, four pairs of trousers, and various other articles, and five sovereigns; the value of the whole was 10l. - I have only seen one coat.
