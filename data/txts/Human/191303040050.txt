BEFORE MR. JUSTICE BANKES .
 (Monday, March 10.) LOUFER  , Morris (50, tobacconist) and LOUFER  , Samuel (16, tobacconist), both wilful murder of Morris Kovalsky , both coroner's inquisition for the like offence, both wilful murder of Cornelius King, both coroner's inquisition for the like offence, both feloniously setting fire to a certain dwelling-house, Morris Kovalsky and others being then therein, and with intent to injure and defraud.
 Prisoners were tried on the indictment and inquisition as to the murder of Morris Kovalsky.
 Mr. Muir and Mr. Graham-Campbell prosecuted; Mr. H. S. Q. Henriques defended Morris Loufer; Mr. P. M. Burton defended Samuel Loufer.
 Detective DAVID CHRISTOPHER, H Division. On January 28 I went to 254, Commercial Road, E., and found in the first floor front room the photograph produced.
 Detective-sergeant JOHN BOUSTRED and Police-constable PERCY ATTERSALL produced and proved a number of photographs and plans. SAMUEL ISAACS. 254, Commercial Road is my property. Morris Loufer has had the place on a weekly tenancy for four years, rent two guineas, I paying rates. He had never spoken to me about leaving.
 Cross-examined. If he could have got a premium for his business I would not have been surprised at his giving me notice. He was a satisfactory tenant. I do not recollect that on one of the counters there was a pipe-lighter with a tube affixed to the gas bracket. ABRAHAM GOLDSTEIN, rabbi and sexton of the synagogue to which Morris Loufer was attached. There is a committee to help our mem bers in matters of insurance. We usually recommend the Alliance Company. The form produced (Exhibit 4) is a proposal form in the name of Morris Loufer for a fire insurance policy of £400 (£150 furni ture, £200 stock, and £50 fixtures). It was passed by our committee on December 11, 1911. Exhibit 6 is a proposal form passed on Octo ber 15, 1912, for £400 (£200 furniture, £200 stock and fixtures). EDWARD DAVIS, accountants' department, Alliance Insurance Com pany, identified Exhibits 4 and 6, and produced the policy (Exhibit 93) issued under the second proposal.
 Cross-examined. Before issuing the policy we should inspect the premises to see what the fire risk is; but should not have the insured property valued. FREDERICK P. LINN, manager of Farrow's Bank, 253, Whitechapel Road, produced copy of Morris Loufer's account. It was opened on May 20, 1912, and closed on December 17, 1912. Exhibit 9 is a cheque presented on January 24 last, for £8 15s., in favour of the Farmville Tobacco Company. It was dishonoured. ISIDORE AARONS, traveller to the Farmville Company. We had various transactions with Morris Loufer, commencing in 1911. In October, 1912, his account began to get in arrear. There was a balance due of about £8, and we instructed our solicitors.
