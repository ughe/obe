   Before Mr. Justice Littledale,
 1453. CATHERINE DENNY was indicted for unlawfully, malici ously, and feloniously, assaulting Bridget Santry , on the   15th of March , and cutting and wounding her on the head, the left side of her face, and left hand, with intent to maim and disable her—2nd COUNT  , stating her intent to be to do her some grievous bodily harm. BRIDGET SANTRY. I am the wife of Dennis Santry, and live at Rother hithe, in the same house as the prisoner, and Margaret Mulchannaugh. About half-past seven o'clock in the morning of the 15th of March I was lying on my bed—I heard the prisoner outside at the foot of the stairs, calling me by an expression not fit to mention—in the course of ten mi nutes I heard her again addressing me with the same kind of expression as before—I went down stairs, and knocked at her door, and told her I wanted to speak to her—the second time I spoke she opened the door, and let me in—I told her she deserved a good hiding  for her filthy language to me—she told me to hit her—I said I did not feel myself able to do it, but if I was, she deserved it for her abuse to me—she took bold of my hair with her left hand, took her right hand from behind her back, and I felt her stab me on the left side of my head with a knife, but I did not see the knife till after Mrs. Mulcbannaugh came up and took it out of her hand—I was standing up at the time—directly I saw the blood over me, I got so weak I fell down directly—I had two wounds and a scratch—I put up my left hand in my defence, and it cut through my fingers—I felt pain from the cuts, and was very unwell indeed—I was attended by a surgeon for ten days—the blood-vessel then burst out again, and I sent for the doctor, who is attending me still.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Who went down stairs with you to the prisoner's room? A.  No one—I am sure of that—her husband was up stairs in my room, and my husband, and Mrs. Mulchannaugh also—we had half a pint of gin between five of us—it was half-past seven o'clock in the morning—I had not been at work—I was on my bed—I got up when the prisoner came to abuse me—she opened her room door to me the second time I knocked—she was alone in her room—she did not receive any injury in the scuffle, that I know of—she had none from me—there was no one but me there—I saw no blood on her—I have had no money from her or her husband about this, but the husband when I first came to the house, warned me not to give her any rent—I have had a sovereign from him since this, and gave it to the surgeon—he has promised me another sove reign—this was the prisoner's house—I was a lodger—she was not chop ping wood in her room, that I saw—there was none thrown at her—the policeman came in about half an hour.
