 BROOK BRYDGES PARBLY. I am a  colonel in the East India Company's service. I was at the King's Arms garden, attending the meeting, as I live in the parish, in Brompton-square; Lord Teynham was in the chair - I had a purse of this sort and colour, with two sovereigns and some loose silver in it - I missed it when the officer spoke to me; I believe this is my purse - I recollect the cheers being proposed; I had before that had my hand on my side, but when Mr. Hume was leaving the meeting, I lifted my hand, and took off my hat - by that means my pocket was exposed.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was your coat buttoned? A. Yes; I cannot swear to the purse - Hutchins insisted that it was his from the first; there was a great crowd.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you observe any communication between the prisoners? A. No- I could not swear to any of them.
 JURY to  THOMAS MADDEN. Q. Did the prisoners go away together? A. They were close together when they quitted, and went at the same time.
 Gascoyne. I was not near the door; I was in the crowd - another person took me. Witness. I took him and Hutchins near the door; there was some crowd there - I called a person to assist me; I called to a special-constable to take Jones - they were all going away together: there were several speeches after Mr. Hume left.
 Hutchins. The purse is mine - I have had it three or four months; I bought in the Strand, where you can buy fifty every day - this officer did not take it from me; another person took it out of my pocket, and gave it to him- I said it was mine, and that it had 2l. 13s. 6d. in it. -Witness. He had his hand in his pocket, grasping the purse, and I took it; I said, "If it is yours, you can tell what is in it - how many sovereigns are there?" he said three; I said, "How much silver?" he said about 14s. - he guessed the silver right within 6d.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you sure he did not say two sovereigns? A. He said two, after he saw the money counted, but at first he said three.

 1288.  JULIUS PICARD was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of April , 1 guitar, value 4l., the goods of Angelo Bernedetto Ventura.
 The prisoner being a native of France, had the evidence explained by an interpreter.
   ANGELO BERNEDETTO VENTURA. I live in Cirencester-place, and am a professor of music. Three or four days before the beginning of last month I became acquainted with the prisoner at the appartments of Mr. Burger, when he invited me to take a little play - he applied to me to lend him a guitar, which I did several times; at the beginning of April he asked me to lend him one for a night- he was not to pay for it; I did not want him to take it then, as I wanted to clean it, but he took it from a table; he was to return it the day afterwards - two days afterwards he invited me to go to his house in Bath-place; he came in a horse and gig; Mr. Crutz came with him; he saw a very beautiful guitar at my house, worth ten guineas- he said he wished to show it to his Baroness, as she would want to take a lesson very likely, and he invited me to go to Bath-place; I went there to tea - he gave his address " Baron Dillon, No. 13, Bath-place, New-road;" I went, and took another guitar with me; he wished me to fetch my elegant guitar; two or three days afterwards I found the guitar I had first lent him at a pawnbroker's, in St. Martin's-lane, and two other instruments - the prisoner came to me that day; I told him I had found where he had pawned my instruments, and if he did not return them I would have him arrested; he then gave me up the duplicates of these two guitars, and a harp - this is the one I first lent him.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You teach music, and deal in musical instruments? A. Yes; he had all three of the guitars in his possession - I leat him one, and these other two were sold; I had not sold him the three, to he paid for in three months; I had not asked him to return the one I left him - I went to a great many pawnbrokers, because I suspected I had got with a set of swindlers; I did stand once where the prisoner now does, but I went out with very great honour; you scolded my wife very much - I was charged with bigamy.
   WILLIAM WILLIAMS. I am shopman to a pawnbroker in St. Martin's-lane. I have a guitar, which was pawned for 1l. 10s., but not by the prisoner - this is the duplicate.
 PROSECUTOR. This is one he gave me.
 Prisoner's Defence. He sold me the three instruments before two witnesses.
     GUILTY. - Aged 49. - Judgment Respited.
 1289.  JOHN MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of May , 1 handkerchief, value 2s, 6d., the goods of James Cumming , from his person.
 JAMES CUMMING. I am a  surgeon, and live at Tavistock-place. On the evening of the 2nd of May I was in Upper King-street, Bloomsbury; I heard a cry of Stop thief! missed my handkerchief, and saw the prisoner pursued by Mr. Rix - he was secured with my handkerchief in his possession, which he said I had dropped.
 WILLIAM RIX. I am a plumber. I was in Upper King-street, and saw the prisoner and another person; I saw the handkerchief taken from the pocket of a gentleman, who, I believe, was the prosecutor, the prisoner was within a yard of the person who took it; and the other person gave it to the prisoner, who put it into his breast pocket - I cried Stop thief! and the prisoner ran into a mews, I took him there; I had lost sight of him, but I know he is the person.
 WILLIAM JAMES. (Police-constable E 17). I received prisoner, and found this handkerchief on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Six Months.
