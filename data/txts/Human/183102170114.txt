 631.  WILLIAM DOBSON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of February , I handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of John Walter Walker.
 JOHN WALTER WALKER. I live at Park-house, Turnham-green, Middlesex. On the evening of the 5th of February I was walking with my wife, in  Drury-lane; a constable gave me information - I put my hand to my pocket, and missed my handkerchief - I have since seen it produced by Dowding, and to the best of my belief it was mine, but I cannot swear to it, because it was lent me by a friend; I had one in my pocket, and when the officer called my attention to it it was gone.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you had considerable difficulty in saying that it was the one you had lost? A. Yes, as it had been so short a time in my possession, I could not conscientiously swear to it; I stated so to the Magistrate in the first place - the officer had been examined then; he then added that he saw the man take it from my pocket, having before said he found it - I said I could not swear to it, and the Magistrate was about to discharge the prisoner; the officer then said there was no occasion to discharge him, for he saw him take it from my pocket - the officer had not been examined first; I know Dowding was sworn - I do not know whether Groom was.
 COURT. Q. You were not aware your handkerchief was lost till your attention was called to it; you then missed it, and before the Magistrate you expressed considerable doubt about it? A. No; I said I could tell it by a stain which I recognized, and I pointed out that stain, but I said I would not swear to it.
   GEORGE DOWDING. I live at No. 15, Rose-street, Long-acre, and am a constable. A little after seven o'clock in the evening, on the 5th of February, I was in Drury-lane, near Long-acre; I saw the prosecutor walking with a lady, and I saw the prisoner, whom I had not known before, in company with another person - i saw the prisoner touch the gentleman's pocket several times, and at last he put his hand into his right-hand coat pocket, and took out a silk hankderchief; he ran across towards Groom, and I called to him to stop him - the prisoner threw the handkerchief into the road; I took it up, and told the prosecutor he had lost his handkerchief - he identified it as his property.
 Cross-examined. Q. He knew it at once? A. Yes, he had no doubt of its being his property; he identified it both to me, and at the office - he pointed out a stain, and swore he had no doubt of it, but the fact is, the gentleman did not wish to prosecute, as there was so much trouble attending it.
 Q. Now, which is the truth - did he swear to it or did he not? A. He did swear to it to the best of my knowledge, and I believe that was taken down; I think I was examined after the prosecutor - I saw the prisoner make two or three attempts at the prosecutor's pocket; I was across the road - I saw him for five minutes; I am an officer - I am serving in my own right this year; I served for five years as a substitute - I am a tailor; it all helps to bring in a crust to support my family - I am both a master and a journeyman tailor; I work as journeyman for Mr. Stringer, in Long-acre; I generally get about 10l. for acting as a subsitute - I had a good many cases here last year, but I only had my expences in one case at one Session.
   JOHN GROOM. I am a constable. On the evening of the 5th of February I was in Drury-lane; I saw the prisoner, and another person with him, following a lady and this gentleman - Dowding called out "Groom;" and I saw the prisoner running from the lady and gentleman with a handkerchief in his hand - he dropped the handkerchief, and I told Dowding to take it up, and to run after the lady and gentleman; I took the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you show the gentleman the handkerchief, and did he say it was his? A. Yes, he had no doubt of it; and at the office he said it was one he had borrowed of a friend - I am in no business; I did not see any more persons about the place but the prisoner and his companion; I had known the prisoner before - his clothes had no pockets - I am sure the prosecutor said he had no doubt it wasthe same handkerchief.
 Prisoner. I have the same clothes on now; I have pockets in them.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined One Year.
 632.  THOMAS FRANCIS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , I handkerchief, value 6d., and 1 half-sovereign, the property of David Walker.
   SARAH WALKER. I am the wife of David Walker; we live in Collingwood-street, Ratcliff; the prisoner lodged there for seven months. On the 17th of January I sent him with a newspaper to a neighbouring public-house, and he did not return; soon after he was gone I missed a half-sovereign from a cupboard in a room, which I had seen safe half an hour or twenty minutes before -I in general keep the room locked, but having no one in the house but the prisoner I had not locked it that day -I do not recollect that he had been in that room; I had not sent him there - I never saw him again till the officer brought him, which was more than a week afterwards; I asked how he could have a heart to rob a woman who had been so kind to him; he said he put the half-sovereign into his pocket and lost it, and he had been to Gravesend to try to get a ship, but could not - I missed a handkerchief, and found a duplicate of it.
   GEORGE BLAKE. I am a Police-constable. On the 25th of January, in consequence of information, I took the prisoner at Cock-hill; I asked if he knew any thing of Mrs. Walker - he said Yes; I asked if he knew any thing of the half-sovereign - he said Yes, that he had not changed lit but lost it, and that he had just returned from Gravesend.
   WILLIAM LANCE. I am a pawnbroker. I have a cotton handkerchief pawned by the prisoner, on the 17th of January, for 3d.; it is worth about 6d.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I took the half-sovereign, put it into my pocket and lost it; I went to Gravesend - I had only 1½d. on the handkerchief.
 GUILTY. Aged 25.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutrix and Jury.
