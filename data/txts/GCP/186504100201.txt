The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : I did not strike him at
all, he was drunk and incapable ; when I first saw him he was lying in the
centre of the rond, near Brixton Church ; I saw him as far as the “Horns,"
he said I was a good sort of fellow, and he would reward me with a glass of
ale; I said I did not want any; he was reeling along the road. When we
got to Kennington-road he wanted me to leave him, but I said I would
see him further; he said, “Take care of my watch, I live at 3, Hercules
buildings," he tried to give me the watch, but we both fell together, and
then it was broken from the chain ; I put it into my pocket, intending
to return it the next day.

490. THOMAS STANTON (17), Stealing a purse and 108., the property
of Mary Westbrook, from her person.
MR. Wood conducted the Prosecution, and MR. TAYLOR the Defence.
MARY WESTBROOK. I am a widow, living at No. 20, Vine-terrace, Lam-
beth-on Saturday night, 1st April, about half past 9, I was in the Waterloo
road with a basket full of potatoes, and some grooery, on my arm--the prisoner
came to my side, and made an attempt to snatch a purse out of my left hand
which was underneath the basket-he gave a second pull, and I saw his face
distinctly—there was no lamp-post there, there was light enough to enable
me to see his features-he then ran up Granville-street--there was a 08.
piece and 38. in my purse he took the purse and the money-I took my
things home, and then went down Tower-street, and gave information to the
police-I described the man-I saw him again on Monday morning, at
Tower-street station, amongst several more, and I picked him out-I said,
"I feel quite sure be is the man, if there were 100 people there "-I feel
quite sure of him now.
