 993.  JAMES GREGORY HOPKINS was indicted for embezzling 19l. 10s. and 2l. 2s., which he had received on account of James Reynolds , his master.
 The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 994.  BENJAMIN BLACK was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of March , 1 bottle, value 1d., and 3 half pints of wine, value 3s., the goods of George Holt, his master.
 GEORGE HOLT. I live in  Threadneedle-street, and am in the wine trade. The prisoner was my cellarman. On the 14th of March I told him I should not want him next morning, but he came, and had a glass of rum; I had found a bottle of port in a particular place the day before, and marked it with a diamond - after he had the rum he went into the yard and took this bottle - I went after him, and found it in his possession.
   JOHN RAWLINS. I am an officer, and received him in charge.
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and stated that he had taken the wine for his wife, who was indisposed.
 GUILTY. Aged 40. Strongly Recommended to Mercy. - Confined 1 Month.
 995.  JAMES STOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 fowl, value 2s., the goods of Robert Turle , and 1 shirt, value 1s., the goods of George Salles.
 ROBERT TURLE. I  keep the  Three Tuns public-house, Bridgewater-gardens. On the 25th of February I lost this fowl - it was found on the prisoner, dead, but it was warm and bleeding; he appeared the worse for liquor.
 GEORGE SALLES. I live with Turle. My shirt was in the warehouse; I secured the prisoner there, with it, and the fowl in his hat.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much intoxicated.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined One Month.
 996.  PETER JORDAN, WILLIAM LIVERMORE, CHARLES BISSENT, and JOSEPH PURDOE, were indicted for stealing, on the 2d of April , 1 clock, value 5s., and 1 looking-glass, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Funnell.
   SUSANNAH FUNNELL. I am the wife of Thomas Funnell, a printer- we live in Green Arbour-square. On the 2d of April we had a raffle at our house for a shawl - there were seventeen or eighteen people in the room; a cat was thrown into the room; I saw Purdoe come in, and the light was put out.
 Q. Do not you know that all the four prisoners were there? A. No - I never said so - I never saw the others, to my knowledge - the raffle began between ten and eleven o'clock - it was 6d. a number - Purdoe came in smoking a pipe - the cat was thrown and the light put out - I had a clock and glass in the room - I got a light in a few minutes, and then my son detained a young man, and took him to the watch-house - I did not know that man - I followed him to the watch-house - it was one of the prisoners - I cannot say which.
   THOMAS FUNNELL. I am son of the last witness; I came into the room before the candle was put out - Purdoe, Livermore, and Jordan were in the room - I did not notice Bissent - there were about twenty there in all - I cannot say who put the light out - the cat was thrown at it - some things were broken on the mantle-piece, and the clock was gone in the scuffle - I do not know who took it - I saw it again on the Thursday - the looking-glass was brought to the watch-house by somebody - Livermore was there then, and charged with a riot, as nothing was missed when he was taken - I saw some people run across the court, and heard a cry of "Stop thief!"
   AMELIA BARLOW. I was at this raffle. I did not see any of the prisoners there - I went out, returned, and then four or five young men came in; one of them was very tipsy - I moved from him near the door, and then one of them put the light out - they came out, and the tipsy man, who was Jordan, dropped this glass by the door as he ran out - I took it up, and ran across the court, crying "Stop thief!" The constable came up, and asked what was the matter?
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. How long were you in the room? A. About half an hour; twelve or fourteen persons were there; I was not there when the first light was put out, it was done twice.
   WILLIAM STALLIN. I am a watchman. I was called to take charge of Livermore, for destroying the things and making a riot; I took him to the watch-house, and while there it was said the thieves had returned to the door; I went, and Jordan was given into my charge for bringing the looking-glass out; he went a few yards with me; we were then surrounded by a mob - I was struck, and he was rescued; Bissent and Purdoe were among them - I took Bissent and gave him to another watchman, and went to look for Purdoe, whom I knew before - he had struck me a violent blow in the bowels; Jordan was brought in by another watchman; I said, "Here's the one who was taken from me" - he said he had not seen me before - he was sober.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Who charged Livermore with riot? A. Funnell's son - I went to the room and saw glasses and things scattered about.
   THOMAS ANDERSON. I am a watchman. At eleven o'clock on this night, I was near Saracen's Head, Snowhill, heard a cry of Stop thief! and saw Jordan running from Green Arbour-square - I stopped him - he said, "Let go of my clothes, and take my arm: I will go with you" - a strong party came round and cried out, "Do you want to murder the man?" Jordan then struck me in the eye - I took him to the watch-house.
 SAMUEL HOBSON. I am an officer, and have the glass - the prosecutrix brought the clock to me afterwards - somebody had delivered it up - Jordan appeared sober.
