Q. Pray do you know Miss Phæbe Blackwell! A. I think I had
never seen her to the best of my recollection, before about the autumn when
we were acting for her and her brother as Stanley's executors--I never re-
presented her to Miss Constable to be lame, and living in Derbyshire-I will
swear that most positively, nor any thing to that effect-I never described her
to Miss Constable in my life, because Miss Constable was there with her some-
times, very often at Ely-place---I will swear that on my introducing the
deed to be signed by Miss Constable, she did not say, “Mr. Boys, stop here,
what has Miss Blackwell's name to do with me! I do not know her as I
stated before, Miss Constable in the first instance wished to convey the sur-
plus of this property to the prisoner alone--she wished not to have the pro-
perty conveyed to Miss Blackwell-she at first objected to Miss Blackwell's
name being in the deed-I did not tell her that she was so nixed up with
Stanley that it could not be prepared in any other way-I told her that she
was mixed up in this way, that they were joint executor and executrix under
the will--that they were jointly interested under the will, and that it would
be improper to make a conveyance to the prisoner alone--the instructions to
counsel to prepare the draft goes on that principle, and I always kept her
interest, and the prisoner's in view, for that very reason.
