   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Where is the house you searched? A.  At Peck ham, Rye, about six miles from the Office. WASTELL ARROWSMITH re-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  Is there not a certain sum of the public money which the official clerks in the office have access to and may use? A.  For official purpose—I cannot tell whethers there is any authority for using it—it has been a practice ever since I was in the office—it is necessary—the money is entrusted to the junior—he is answerable for it to the post-master—it is allowed to him for official par poses, by the superintending President—it is 40 l ., I believe at present—it used to be 35 l . when I was junior—it is used for the purpose of returning over-charges on letter and other official purpose—there are letter charged to him, coming by the mails from France and other places—he is to pay the money with which he is changed on these mornings, on a certain day—three days in a week, Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.
   Q.  Is any part of that money lent to the junior clerks? A.  It has been, though that is at his own risk—he is answerable for it—I have myself bor rowed some of that money, for my own purpose—it is borrowed by the senior as well as by the junior—Mr. Barnes himself has borrowed it.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  How often does the junior clerk account for the money in his possession? A.  How often does the junior clerk accounted for once a week—it is given by one junior to his successor—we get the return of the overcharges once a week, and always keep up the sum—that been no things at all to do with called upon through the tunnel.
 (The prisoner being called upon for his defence, contended that by the 5th and 6th William IV, caput 25, sects. 8, 17, 28, and 24, he was authorised to exercise a discretionary power in the mode of transmitting letters—that on the night in question several addition mails required to be made up, which caused an immense pressure of business, and induced him to render his assistance in those inferior departments which his duty not require—that on retiring to bed, after every lock in the office was secured, he ob  served that two single letters were left, out, which were of no consequences, there being no postage on them, they being directed for ordinary seaman—he placed them in his pocket, for security, till the morning—that if the money, stated by the witnesses as paid to him, ever came into his hands, he must have forwarded it with letters to the Inland-office—he further stated, that the junior clerks had an interest in his removal, as they would thereby be advanced—and contended that it must be evident he should never have had the folly or madness to risk his situation of 500 l . per annum, after twenty-seven years service, as well as his character and the interests of his wife and family, for so paltry a sum.
