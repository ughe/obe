 754.  JOHN MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of January , 1 lamp, value 12s., the goods of George Ingram.
   ROBERT DIXON. I am journeyman to Mr. Ingram, a coach-maker, of Great Queen-street, Lincoln's Inn-fields. He lost from his warehouse, a pair of lamps, on Monday, the 23rd of January - they had just been japanned.
   THOMAS DELANY. I am a Policeman. I was on duty on the 23rd of January, between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, in Short's-gardens, Drury-lane; I saw the prisoner running up Short's-gardens, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I stopped him, and found nothing on him; I asked how he came to run if he was not guilty - he said he thought the officer saw him take the lamp.
   CHARLES WILKINS. I am a Policeman. On the afternoon of the 23rd of January, between three and four o'clock, I was in Drury-lane, and saw the prisoner at the corner of Queen-street, with this lamp - he was carrying it under his arm; he ran away, and I followed him - he threw it down in Short's-gardens; I never lost sight of him; the japan was wet on the lamp.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had met a man named Wilson, in Queen-street, who gave him the lamp as a pattern to make one for him.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined Three Months.
 755.  JOSEPH PRIESTLY was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of February , 24 pennies and 36 halfpennies, the monies of Sarah Gurney , his mistress.
   SARAH GURNEY. I am a widow, and keep the Old King's Arms, Gray's Inn-lane. On the 27th of January I sent the prisoner, with 3s. 6d. in copper, to Mr. Bennett; he returned, and I asked if he had paid the money - he said Yes; I afterwards saw Bennett, and in consequence of what he said I spoke to the prisoner - he said he had not paid the money, that he had got into bad company and lost it - he ran away directly; he had been eleven months with me, and had been a good boy for about nine months.
   HENRY BENNETT. I live in Upper North-place, Gray's Inn-lane. On the 27th of January I served the prisoner with some ribbon, on the account of Mrs. Gurney, which came to 3s. 6d. - he never came to pay me for it.
   JOHN BURTON. I was an officer. I took the prisoner on the 3rd of February; he begged I would not take him, and in going along he said his clothes and handkerchief, which were in his box, would settle it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 756.  ISABELLA DONOVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 1 cap, value 3s. 6d., the goods of Joseph Allen.
   ANN LOUISA ALLEN. I am the wife of Joseph Allen. I keep a milliner's shop in Norton-falgate; the prisoner came there on the 17th of January, alone - I was standing in the back parlour; I saw her take up a cap out of the window, and a blue handkerchief - I went into the shop, and when I saw her I knew her very well, and thought it was of no use to speak to her then - she dropped the cap, as I suppose, between herself and the counter; I went into the parlour again, and told my girls to watch - they gave me information; I went out when they said, "Now, Ma'am;" I found her with a cap, which she had taken - he came to buy nothing, but in the same manner as she had come before - she desired me to put a cap by for her, and not to mix it.
   CAROLINE LITTLE. I was in the parlour - Mrs. Allen came into the parlour and told us to watch, and I saw the prisoner drop the cap; then she stopped down and put something into her bosom.
   HENRY DEPIES. I am an officer, and have the cap, which I saw Mrs. Allen take from her; I heard her say,"You have robbed me, and I know you have the property;" she took this cap from her bosom - she was searched in my presence, and another new cap was found on her, and half a yard of lace; she had a sovereign and three duplicates.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought it about a fortnight before, of a young lady who was in her shop, for half a crown; I am a  mantua-maker- there are plenty of caps like that.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. - Transported for Seven Years.
 757.  WILLIAM BERRY, JOHN BERRY, WILLIAM TAYLOR, and BENJAMIN CATLING were indicted for stealing, on the 6th of February , 16 bushels of wheat, value 5l., the goods of William Boards.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM BOARDS. I live at Edmonton, and am a farmer. John Wheeler fastened my barn on the night of the 6th of February, and next morning he told me it was broken open - I examined, and found part of the roof had been untiled, sufficient for two to have got in; I missed some wheat from the bulk - I thought about two quarters; I found a hat in the barn, which I believe is here - I gave notice to Camp, the officer, and went with him to Taylor's, at a place called Journeyman's-green, on the following day - I think Wheeler went with me; Catling lives next door to Taylor - I found at Taylor's a bed-tick filled with wheat in the chaff, and the bed clothes on it - the bed appeared made; I think there were about six bushels in the tick, and I found about five pecks in a sack in the house -I have compared the wheat I found there with that in my barn, and I believe it to be part of the same wheat; I found at Catling's about five pecks of wheat, from which the chaff had been blown, and some chaff was buried by the side of the house.
