John Brown. I am landlord of No. 18, South-street. I let the pri-
soner in on the night of the 6th of April-I believe he stopped there till
about half-past five in the morning, when I heard a cab drive away.
HENRY LITTLEBOY. I drive a cab. On the morning of the 7th of April
I was in Paddington-street--the prisoner fetched me from there and or-
dered me to drive to Charing-cross, but in going along he said to Whit-
comb-street-he had a bed, a large box, and a small one, and a hat-box-
I took them up at No. 18, Sonth-street, and drove down to Sheppard's
livery stables-he took all the things out there--I left him in the street
with them near the livery stables.
JAMES OSBORNE. I drive a cab. I was on the stand near the Union
Club-honse, on the morning of the 7th of April, between a quarter and
twenty minutes to six, I was called to drive to Whitcomb-street, which I
did, and saw the prisoner with a bundle, a bed, a large trunk, a small box, and
a woman's reticule basket-the box appeared quite full of things I took
the trunk on the dickey, the other things were put into the cab the ordered
me to drive to the Spotted Horse, in the Strand-I said I did not know
the Spotted Horse, I knew the Spotted Dog-he said that was all the same
-I drove him there, assisted him in taking off the luggage, which was put
in front of the bar it is a booking-office--I saw him pay eight-pence for
booking.

Strand. On Thursday the 7th of April, the prisoner booked a quantity of
things there, and gave the name of Stanley-be said nothing more about
them-he went away in a very few minutes, came back again about ten or
half-past with a female, and went into the coffee-room with her-I cannot
say how long they staid they went away together and took all the
things with them I believe they went in a cab, but I did not see two
THOMAS SAYLES. I was waiter at the Spotted Dog on Thursday, the
7th of April. I remember a man coming there in the morning the pri-
soner is that man-he bad a female with him-they came into the tap-room
-- he had a beef steak in his hand, and asked me to cook it for him--I said
I had a very bad fire and could not-he said, "I don't mind waiting, I am
not in a hurry, if you will do it for me; I am very hungry, I have been
travelling all night by the railway "-I asked him what railway he came
by—he said he did not know-in a minute or two after he said, "I came
from Bristol "-I cooked the steak--they both sat down and ate it--they
were very thick in conversation together all the conversation I noticed was,
he made a reply to the female, “That b-ch won't trouble you any more"
-I am quite sure I heard the prisoner say that to the woman I saw him
go for a cab--some things were put into the cab and some outside they
were all taken away-I did not see them start-he kept frequently going
in and outside the door-I suppose he went in and out seven or eight times
while the steak was cooking, and while it was being eaten he went out
twice-he was in the house about half-an-hour, and went out as much as
eight times during that time.
