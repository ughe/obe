   Prisoner. Q.  Are you not in the habit of paying money to the signature of persons who you do not know, on receiving a letter of advise from one of your clients, that is, if Colonel Carruthers had written a note? A.  We do certainly pay money on receiving a letter of advice—I have no knowledge of you as having been a client of our house for some years.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was a cadet at the Military College with Colonel Carruthers and a brother of his in 1818 and 1819; I met his brother at a levee of the Horse Guards with other officers; we dined together, and he borrowed from me 15 l ., promising to repay me; I never thought of it or heard from him since; latterly I have been obliged to live by my pen, and have published several works by subscription, which have been supported chiefly by military officers, who were cadets; I saw an account of the arrival of Colonel Car ruthers, and of his regiment receiving colours from another old college friend of mine, Lord Fredrick Fitzclarence; I wrote to the Colonel, telling him how circumstances were changed with me, mentioning the loan to his brother, and asking him to take a copy of my book, and that if I did not hear from him in ten days I should take the liberty of drawing on Cox and Greenwood on his account, for 2 l . 10 s ., the amount of my book, and he would then perhaps see what he could do for me as to his brother's debt; receiving no answer I did as the prosecutor has stated; I have perhaps acted foolishly, but I had not the slightest intention of cheating Mr. Selwood, or doing wrong to any one. GUILTY . Aged 52.— Confined One Month .
