H. E. HERMAN (Ro-examined). A clerk in our office served the writ-
Mrs. Levy appeared by Lewis and Co.-they have delivered state-
ment of defence, but we have not replied to it.
ESTHER LEVY. I let the prisoner the house at 14, Upper East Smith-
field—this is my handwriting (referring to the agreement) ---the prisoner
paid me 1501. on the 30th May, I think-he was quite a stranger to me
-the bill was in the window that the premises were to let, and he
seemed anxious to have it I did not suggest we should go to a lawyer,
but I took him to Mr. Hind, of Cannon Street, estate agent, whom I
knew, for the purpose of having the agreenient prepared—the prisoner
had two or three interviews with me-he first came in to make & pur-
chase, and he said he had been after a place where he would have to
pay 1201. for the goodwill and 1001. a year-I said my house was cheaper,
and he was agreeable to take it—that was all that passed—the next
time he came was about the business, and I let him the house and shop
for nine years at 45l. a year-he paid me by a note out of his own pocket,
and had no one to represent him-I have a brother—the prisoner did not
buy my furniture-I sold him the agreement for nine years—I took my
furniture away-when he came he said he should not open the house he
had been trying to let it, and bad advertised it at 751. and 1507. a year;
80 he could not have thought it & very bad bargain—he said he had &
niece who would come to attend to it, but there was some death in the
family and she could not come-& barman took the furniture away my
brother did not go there-he is living at the Robin Hood public-house-
