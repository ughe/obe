ELLIS, Mayor.
Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. I attended at the police-court-I was
not called I found a mattress in the back room of the ground floor, not
in the front room-I saw no tools or trestles for putting mattresses on-
the house was searched-there was a quantity of shavings-& search was
made amongst the shavings but nothing was found.
EDWARD REDWOOD. I am an auctioneer at Walworth-I let the
house, 19, Warple Way, to Jones, about the beginning of November,
1881-I did not ask his Christian Dame-I collected the rent every week
at the house—not more than twice from Jones- I think I took it from
the mattress maker, Grey-the rent, 88. 6d. a week, was paid regularly.
Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN. A young man was with Jones
when he came to take the house-the young man did not give his name
-I never heard it-Jones said “Have you a house in Warple Way?"
I said " Yes". he said "What is the rent?"-I said 88. 6d. Der
week”_"How many rooms are there ?"-I think I told him-he said
he would go and see it, and if it suited him-he was away two hours or
less; then he said he would take it—the young man was still with him
-I cannot say I have seen the young man since-the police did not show
me the man they detained-I let the house to Jones—the entry in my
rent-book is to Jones I cannot remember how the 88. 6d. was paid; I
might have given change-I am positive Jones paid it.
Cross-examined by Mr. RIBTON. I have seen palliasses-I cannot say
they were made there-I saw shavings and the frame they are
stuffed in every time I went-I saw no tools-I may have seen three
palliasses outside when I called for the rent-Gray has asked me for
orders-I never saw any straw there I have a doubt as to whether
palliasses were made there.
Ro-examined. I saw no women sewing with needles and thread, nor
anything of the kind—the first name in the rent-book may have been a
former tenant; after that it is always Jones.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I have seen everything that has been produced,
moulds, ladles, jars, bottles, &c., &c.—here is a double mould with
obverse and reverse sides for half-sovereigns of 1877, two counterfeit
half-sovereigns from the mould of 1878; nine double moulds, obverse
and reverse sides of a half-crown of 1819, 1835, and 1846, of which the
six good half-crowns found on Jones are the patterns—there are 101
counterfeit balf-crowns made from these moulds-five double moulds,
obverse and reverse sides of florins of 1858, 1859, and 1872, and the
five good forins in the purse found on Jonos are the patterns from which
these are made-there are forty-two bad florins from the moulds of 1858
and 1872; not one from 1859-the five treble moulds for shillings 1844,
1860, 1863, 1865, and 1866; the five good shillings found in Jones's
purse are the patterns for these moulds, with the exception of 1863-
there are nineteen complete moulds, and altogether, with those broken,
100 moulds--moulds may be used a great many times before they break
--then there are four batteries with everything complete, two good
florins which are used in the batteries for the purpose of facing the
coins, heaps of paper used for wrapping up counterfeit coin, and a lamp for
colouring them after they come from the battery-they are blackened and
afterwards rubbed-all these articles produced are used in the manu.
facture of coin on a very large and extensive scale.
