 1759. JAMES SMITH was indicted for that Henry Harland, the younger , being in the dwelling-bouse of John Harland , did steal 1 watch, value 4 l  .; and 1 coat, 2 l  . 10 l  .; the goods of Richard Harland : 1 mantle, 1 l  .; 1 shawl, 1 l  .; the goods of John Harland : 1 waistcoat, 5 s  .; 1 belt, 2 s  .; 3 scarfs, 6 s  .; 1 shawl, 2 s  . 6 d  .; and 3 shirt-collars, 6 d  .: and having committed the said felony, did feloniously and burglariously break out of the same dwelling-house: that the prisoner did incite him the felony aforesaid to do and commit, and felo niously received the same goods, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   MR. RYLAHD conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD HARLAND. My father's name is John Harland—I have a brother named John, and another named Henry—we all live with my father, in Union-row, Old Kent-road. My brother Henry went to bed on the night of the 5th of July—in the morning I was awoke, and he was gone—he had slept in the same bed with me—I missed a watch and a coat—I took a deal of pains to find Henry—I saw him again about ten days after, about the Wednesday week—he was then in custody—the policeman produced the articles to me which I had missed.
   COURT . Q.  What time did you go to bed that night? A.  Between eleven and twelve o'clock—all was then shut up safe—our house is in the parish of St. Mary, Newington—the watch was worth 4 l ., and the cost 1 l . 10 s .—there were other things gone belonging to my father and mother, to the amount of 7 l . or 8 l . PRISCILLA HARLAND. I am the wife of John Harland. On the morning of the 6th of July I missed a great-coat of my husband's, a mantle and shawl of my own, and various articles belonging to my son John. JOHN HARLAND. I am a son of the last witness. On the morning of the 6th of July I missed three coats, four or five pairs of trowsers, five waistcoats, and some other things—I saw a handkerchief produced by Kerney—I had not missed it, but I afterwards found I had missed such a thing—I went down to Brighton, and I saw my brother's watch and a coat at Mr. Lewis's, the pawnbroker—I shut up our house safely on Sunday night, the 5th of July, about half-past eleven o'clock.
