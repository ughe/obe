Cross-examined by MR RIBTON. Q. What time did the two women
come? A. About 3 o'clock in the afternoon ; I did not see the men outside
--they all came at 6 o'clock. I was to give 61. or 71. for the chests, they
would have been worth about 117. if they had contained tea half chest
contains about 40 lbs.; tea is worth about 10d. a pound without the duty
they made out that they had not paid any duty on this, that it was per
quisites-I did not think it was smuggled, but I thought it came out of the
Government office, in Cutler-street, and that it was perquisites; and seeing
Mr. Nash's name, threw me off my guard—I could buy sweepings, duty
paid, at 28. per pound—the chests would be worth 51. each—the prisoners
asked me 8. for them at first, but they were to take 71. -Mr. Nash is a
grocer and tea dealer_this was the first irregular transaction I have had
until I parted with my money, I thought that the prisoners came by the tea
honestly; and then they tried everything to prevent my opening it; they,
said, “You have no occasion to be afraid, we can leave it tilf Monday morn.
ing; and if you fear anything, we will throw it down and open it before
your face"-I said I should like to see it ; 'but being called away, as it was
Saturday night, I got rather Austrated-1 cannot find the paper Mr. Nash
sent-if the prisoners had not been so respectably introduced, I should have
had nothing to say to them, but turned them out at once it was really in
consequence of that paper that I entered into négociations with them-I
am sure I should not have given the 21. if I had not seen that piece of paper,
for I had not seen the prisoners before.
