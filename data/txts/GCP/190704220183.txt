cannot give the date of the interview when I first introduced this sub-
jeet this notice; it was after the contract. When I told him I
had made an arrangement with Lewis Hill to charge for a quantity
at coal which was never to be delivered my acquaintance with him was
very slight. Baird was described by Lewis Hill as an unknown
quantity; Hill did not quite know what manner of man he was; I
suppose he had not been able to "get at" him. I did not know
wbether Baird was an honest man or not until I went to him. As to
it being a fair presumption that he was honest, you must believe
everybody honest until you find them otherwise. From what I had
been told by Crump and Lewis Hill I did not think it was a risky
thing to tell the whole thing to Baird, an unknown quantity. If
Baird had been an honest man when I went to him with the tale it!
would have blown the whole thing out of the water. Baird accepted
the terms without discussion. My conversation with him about the
2d. a ton took place about the end of the year. The contract imposed
ne obligation to put the coal into sacks, but for years it had been
the custom to do so. As regards creating dust, I do not know that
there is much difference between carrying in sacks and in bulk. It
largely depends upon the quality of coal. If you are carrying hard
ocal you carry very little dust, but if it is soft coal you make
abundance of it. Down to July, 1905, all the steam coal I had
delivered was English, and there was no question as between English
and Welsh. It was in June, 1905, that we first commenced to deliver
Welsh coal at the infirmary. It was in June that Baird had been
making experiments with various kinds of coal, and I remember sup-
plying a number of sample wagons. After the Guardians once com-
menced to use Welsh coal they did not cease to use it. It was after
the experiments that I had the conversation with Baird. I am under
this impression that I mentioned this matter at the Police Court. I
certainly mentioned it to Inspector Kane when he took my statement.
I gave Baird no additional advantage for the risk he was going to
run in taking English instead of Welsh coal. The only date that I
can remember on which I made a payment to Baird was Christmas
Eve, 1905, but I say that I made payments to Baird on each Christmas
Evo after I had made the arrangement with him. I certainly have a
very distinct recollection of making him & payment on Christmas
Eve, 1905. In 1903 I had paid him two or three small amounts on
account, and I made up the balance of the £10 on Christmas Eve.
I do not remember saying at the Police Court on January 3 "I first
made a payment to Baird at Ohristmas, 1903, in gold, all at once."
The first payment was made at Baird's office in the main blook of
the infirmary buildings, near the boiler-house. I may have given
