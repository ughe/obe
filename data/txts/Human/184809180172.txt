   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Is the prisoner your sister?. A.  Yes; she always behaved with the greatest kindness to my mother. MARTHA FOSTER. I am nine years' old, and live with my parents, at 10, Upper Marsh—I know the prisoner—on Wednesday, 30th Aug., about half past four, I saw the old lady in the back-yard of Mr. Hemmings' house—she lived in the back parlour there—my sister was with me—I saw the prisons by her back-door—she was talking with the old lady, who was sitting on basket—I did not hear what they said—the prisoner was in one yard and the old lady in another—there was a small fence between them—I was in No. 10, where the prisoner was—after they had been talking some time, I saw the prisoner pick up half a brick from the stones, and throw it at her mother—it hit her at the back of the head—she had just got off the seat, and was coming towards the paling—she was stooping down when the brick struck her—her head was all over blood—she then went into the passage, and the prisoner met her there—I heard a noise in the passage.
