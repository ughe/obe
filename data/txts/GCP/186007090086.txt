COURT. Q. Then, although he sent up three bills of Webb's, he debited
himself only with 2002. instead of 3007. A. Yes when we receive
remittances it is taken into account as so much cash-we do not par-
ticularize the bills, and it is only at the end of the journey that he gives me
this book as a true account of his receipts, therefore I have no opportunity
of comparing it with the account that he sends, or with the amount-having
paid three bills and only giving credit for two, he actually owes 1001. less
to us than it appears he does owe by the ledger.
Q. On your book there is a record which must inevitably lead to some
explanation about the 1001.? A. There would have been if we had looked
at all these particular books and compared them when he returned from his
journey, but it was never our practice to do 80—this account was given to me
six weeks after the bills came into my possession, thereforeit was never compared.
MR. SLEIGH. Q. Have you been in the practice of comparing carefully
that book with the accounts ? A. Never--he charges himself by this book
with having received in bills 2,4181.-he actually did receive in bills 25l.
188., and he takes credit to himself in remittances for 2,4181., so that he
keeps 100%. in cash which he receives from our people, and puts it into his
pocket-if we had suspected any fraud of that sort we could have looked
into the books and found it out, but we never suspected him of robbing us.
