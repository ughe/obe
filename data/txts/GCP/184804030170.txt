JOHN BENNETT. I am a smith, and have worked for the prisoner six or
ten months. I left on Saturday week-he said he could not afford to give
me so much wages as I had been having -- last January a man came with six
pairs of tongs, and some tools, in a kettle-there were some swedges and a
punch-I did not hear what passed-he left them there-about three weeks
afterwards the prisoner told me the Railway Company were looking after
some tools, and said, “You must hide those things belonging to the Com-
piny, for I don't want them found at my house-his son took them into
the back yard, by his orders, and buried them for three or four days, till the
search was over-two or three days afterwards the prisoner and his son
brought six pairs of tongs into the shop, and told me to knock the marks out
of them-I knocked the marks out of five-he said they would do, and one
pair was made into something else two pairs had a star, and one pair "M."
on them, and another pair a claw—this sledge-hammer was brought last
September--there is no mark on it-the Company sent for me last Sunday
week, and then sent for the prisoner.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you first give information ? A. A person
came to me on Sunday morning, after I had left prisoner-1 did not go to
any of the railway officers-1 saw the Company's mark on the swedge several
times- I was not angry with the prisoner for not giving me more wages-1
was a little annoyed-1 did not say, "I will do for you for this, Master
Etherington"-no angry words passed between us- I was not in liquor
when discharged—it is an open shop.
HENRY HOLTON (policeman, P 104.) I went with Bonner to the prisoners
- he came in as we had the first pair of tongs-I asked where the other fivo
pairs were that had the Company's mark knocked off them-he said “They
are all here that I know anything about" - we found three pairs more in the
coal trough-there appeared to have been a mark knocked off them-you can
see the remains of it; here is “M." on one.
CHARLES FREELAND. I am a smith, employed by the Railway Company
at New Cross-there is a separate mark for the tools of each forge-the mark
of my forge is a star, another forge is “M."-there are several tools about
marked with a claw, that do not belong to any particular forge-on two of
these tools I find traces of a star in the place where we mark our tools
several pair of tongs have been lost from my forge--the prisoner's shop is
about half a mile from there.
Cross-examined. Q. Is not the star a common mark in the tradel A.
There are other stars, but not like mine- I cannot tell what star has been
effaced.
