 1035. JAMES BARNES was indicted for embezzling and stealing, on the   2nd of March , the sum of 6 s  . 4 d  ., 4 d  ., and 2 s  . 6 d  ., which he had re ceived by virtue of his employment in his Majesty's Post Office.—11 other COUNTS  , varying the manner of laying the charge.
   MESSRS. SHEPHERD, ADOLPHUS , and SCARLETT conducted the prosecution . WASTELL ARROWSMITH. I am a clerk in the Foreign Office of the Post Office; the prisoner was one of the Presidents of that office, and had the control over the inferior officers. I received instructions respecting the prisoner, which made my attention to him more exact, and my memory more accurate—letters received at the receiving-houses, in different parts of the town, for inland and foreign delivery, are made up in bags for the evening, to he delivered at the Post Office—it often happens that Colonial letters are put into the foreign instead of the inland bags—( the evi  dence given by the witness in the former case, page 1016, as to the routine of business at the Post Office, was here read to him, which he confirmed.)—when letters come by mistake into the Foreign Office, they are sent to the Inland Office, by a tunnel, with the money—the money ought not to come into the hands of the President—he ought to send the letters by a messenger to the bag-clerk—that is the usual course of the office—the prisoner had been in the habit of coming himself, in stead of sending a messenger, to get the money—I had been spoken to to pay particular attention to Mr. Barnes—on Wednesday evening, the 2nd of March, I was on duty as window-clerk—I came on duty between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, and remained on duty all day till half-past seven—the prisoner came to me, about a quarter before five o'clock, with three letters, which he said were to go
