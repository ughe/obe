   Price's Defence.  We both came out of the shop together, and met a woman, who touched Miles on the shoulder, and asked if she would buy a dress for 9 s .—she gave her 7 s . 6 d ., and she thought she would pawn it for a little money.
   Miles's Defence.  A woman came and tapped me on the shoulder, and wanted me to buy the dress—I asked her what it was—she showed it to me—she wanted more money than she could pawn it for, he was going into the country—I had but 7 s . 6 d ., which I gave her for it. JOHN MANNING. I produce a certificate of Price's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—I was present when she was tried, on the 10th of April, 1839—she is the person—( read. )
