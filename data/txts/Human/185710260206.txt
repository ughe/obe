 1139. HENRY CHENERY was indicted for embezzlement.
   MR. ROBINSON . conducted the Prosecution. HERBERT STURMEY. I am secretary and clerk to the trustees of the Borough Market. We have, under our Act of Parliament, to collect the tolls from persons coming to the market—up to 1855, we had been in the habit of letting the tolls—at that time the trustees took them into their own hands, and the prisoner was appointed collector of the tolls—he went on acting as collector for the next twelve months—it was his duty to enter the tolls as he received them, in a book, and from week to week to produce that book to a local committee, and at the end of the month to produce that book to me, in order that I might enter the proceeds in the cash book of the trustees, and produce the book at the monthly meeting of the trustees, held on the first Friday in every month—it was his duty to pay the money into the bank weekly, and to produce that book also to the local committee, to show that the money had been paid in—this is the book in which he made the entries of money received, and this is the bankers' book—the prisoner commenced collecting in June, 1856, and he went on collecting till June 24th, 1857. HENRY MONK. I am a fruit salesman. I was in the habit of paying the prisoner the tolls for the fruit brought into the Borough Market. I recol lect paying him the amounts of these three papers—I was not in the habit of taking receipts, but merely a cross on the bills—I paid him the amounts of these three bills, 25th Aug., 1 l . 2 s . 7 d .; 1st Sept., 1 l . 10 s . 6 d .; and 11th Sept., 1 l . 12 s . 0½ d ., amounting to 4 l . 5 s . 1½ d .—I do not know the date; it was near 27th Sept.—I have seen the prisoner since June, 1857—he requested me to destroy some of those bills—I cannot say when it was; it was several times.
   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Have you known him many years? A.  Yes; he was beadle of the market fifteen years, and collector of the tools—he held both situations—there are a great many persons in that market—he has to collect from a great number of persons.
