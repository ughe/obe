 1435. ELIZA THOMPSON and JOSEPH ROBIN ON , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. DAWSON conducted the Prosecution. ELIZA COX. On 22nd April, about seven in the evening, Thompson came to my mistress's shop for a halfpenny-worth of thread—I served her—she gave me a fourpenny-piece—I put it into the till, and gave her 3 1/2 d . change—she went away—there were half-crowns and shillings in the till, but no small money—I am sure there was no fourpenny-piece—after she was gone Troson came in—I went to the till and gave him the fourpenny-piece I re-ceived from Thompson—I identify it by a mark on it—no one bad been is the shop from the time Thompson came till Troson came JOHN LOCK. On 22nd April, between six and seven in the evening, Thompson came to my shop for a pennyworth of tea—my wife served her—a man in private clothes at the door beckoned to me—Thompson gave my wife a fourpenny-piece—she laid it on the counter—I went round and took it up—it was bad—I said so—I bit it—Thompson said she did not know it, or something to that effect—soon afterwards the officer brought Robinson in.
   MRS. LOCK.  I served Thompson—what my husband has stated is true. THOMAS TROSON( policeman, M  337.)
