I expected to receive these bottles from Mr. Townsend: I had charged him with them, and upon their reaching me, I should have given him credit for them; they were worth 15s.
   JOHN SMITH. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner; I did not make him any promise or threat, but he said he had not taken more than twenty dozen, and he had done it to support his father.
 Prisoner's Defence. On the 23rd of August, Mr. Hughes' son said to me, "James, we are very short of bottles, do you know where there are any owing;" I said Yes, at Mr. Townsend's - he said, "Go and fetch them;" I went, and got them - I did not see my master; he was denied, as he was afraid of being arrested for debt - I gave the bottles to his son; and said he had better put them down - he said, "I shall not; come in and have a game at marbles."
 MR. HUGHES. He had left me on the 2nd of August.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Seven Years.
 228.  EDWARD BAILEY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of December , 1 hat, value 5s., the goods of Edward Jeffs.
   EDWARD JEFFS. I am a hatter, and live in New-street, Covent-garden. I had a hat in my window for sale on the 8th of December, about nine o'clock in the evening; I had seen it about half-past eight - about nine o'clock I heard a noise in the window, and heard something fall inside the shop; on hearing that, I looked and missed the hat - I looked out and saw the prisoner about twenty yards from the door, carrying the hat in his hand before him; he turned his head round, saw me, and ran away -I followed him, and he dropped the hat; he was stopped before I lost sight of him - this is the hat, it is worth five shillings.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going home - this gentleman called Stop thief! and took me.
   PATRICK ROENAYNE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner: this is the hat I received from Mr. Jeffs.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - One Month and Whipped.

 230.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of December , 1 pair of trousers, value 12s., the goods of William Alleston.
 WILLIAM ALLESTON. I live in  Duke's-row, Pimlico. On the 11th of December I saw the prisoner in the custody of an officer, with these trousers, which were taken from the cill of my door.
   JOSEPH BURTENSHAW. I am an officer. I was on duty on the 11th of December, and saw the prisoner and another lad running down Brewer-street, Pimlico - I followed them across Vauxhall-road into Wilton-road, where I overtook the prisoner with these trousers; I asked what he had got - he said a pair of trousers, that he was going further on with them; I asked how he came by them; he said the lad who was running on before had given them to him, and he was going to a field to look at them - I first saw him about twenty yards from the prosecutor's.
 Prisoner. Q. Who had the trousers when you first saw us? A. I cannot say.
 Prisoner. He did not ask where I was going with them - I was informed I should not be tried this Session.

 231.  THOMAS KEYS was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of December , 1 sovereign and 17s., the monies of Colin Campbell , from his person.
 COLIN CAMPBELL. I am a  mariner. On the 4th of December, I was in liquor in the early part of the day -Lawson was with me; we were together, and between eight and nine o'clock at night, I went to a public-house, remained there for an hour, and fell asleep - when I awoke, I found myself quite sober; the prisoner came in afterwards with another man - I had no conversation with him; they were both strangers to me - I said I had occasion to cross the water, and being unacquainted with the road, asked Lawson to show me the way to Execution-dock to cross the water; the prisoner heard this - I and Lawson went off together, about half-past ten o'clock in the evening; I had in my trousers pocket, a sovereign and 21s. 6d. in silver - there was a half-crown among it; I paid for two pints of beer in the public-house, and put the money into my pocket, and am sure it was safe - I had my hand on Lawson's shoulder, talking to him as we went along; the prisoner came up behind me suddenly. put his hand round me, and into my trousers pocket - he took out my money; a half-crown and shilling fell on the ground - I was stooping to pick it up, and the man who was with him came forward to push me over the money, but I got it up, and sung out for the Police; the prisoner ran away, and called to the other, "Come along Tom;" they were both out of sight in a moment - I had seen them both in the public-house, and swear positively to the prisoner; I felt in my pocket, and all the money was gone, except the half-crown and shilling which I picked up - the prisoner was taken last Monday week.
   THOMAS LEWIS. I am a Policeman. I was on duty in Back-lane, Shadwell- the prosecutor called me; I went up, but the men were gone; the prosecutor told me his story.
   ANDREW LAWSON. I am a blacksmith. The prosecutor said he would give me a pint of beer to show him the way to Execution-dock; I did not know him before- the prisoner came in with another man while we were having the beer - they sat down by us; they heard the prosecutor ask me which way we were to go, and they followed us; the prosecutor had his hand on my shoulder - the two men followed us in Back-lane, and the prisoner laid hold of him by the arm, and some money fell on the ground; the other man stooped down, picked something up, and called after the other man, Tom; they both ran away - I had seen the prisoner selling fish in the streets before - I swear he is the man.
