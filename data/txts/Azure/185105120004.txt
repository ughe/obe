1039. GEORGE BAILEY, stealing 2 shawls, and other goods, value
ll. 148. ; the goods of Samuel Clark.
SAMUEL CLARK. I am a bricklayer. The prisoner was brought to my
house on Saturday, 5th April, as a lodger, by another tradesman—I had two
boxes standing one on another, under the window in my bedroom, containing
my own wearing apparel, and my wife's—l went to the boxes on Tuesday, and
found the hasp broken off one of them, and I missed a waistcoat, and two
shawls—I gave him in charge on Tuesday evening—I first saw him in the
house on Tuesday evening, between eight and nine o'clock—he was then
sitting nearly facing the bedstead that I lie on—he had half a bed there that
night—I saw him go to bed on the Tuesday evening—I did not see bim again
till Wednesday evening—I do not know that the box was safe on the Tues-
day evening, between four and five—I found it had been opened on Tuesday
evening—we never tried the box—we saw the nails had been pulled out and
placed in again—I missed the articles on Tuesday morning—I found the
things were gone about three in the afternoon—I saw the box safe betweep
four and five—I vas in the hospital on Sunday morning—I came out on
Tuesday, and then a lodger's shirt was missing—I cannot say that I saw
the box safe myself—Wednesday was the only time I examined the box.
SAMUEL RAISSON (policeman, B 212). took the prisoner—I told him
he was charged with stealing this shirt—I did not know of anything else at
that time—be did not make any reply.
WILLIAM HEATH. I sell greens. I know the prisoner by sight—he came
to my place, 31, Queen-street, and brought these two shawls—I bought
them of him—I afterwards gave them to the prosecutor.
SAMUEL STRAFFORD. I am a sawyer. On Sunday evening, 6th April.
between eight and nine o'clock, I saw the prisoner at the Fox and Hounds, at
Chelsea—he offered this handkerchief for sale—-l bought it of him, and gave
it up to the prosecutor.
THOMAS FLOWERS. I am a coal-heaver. On that Sunday evening, the
prisoner came into the Duke's Head—he asked me a shilling for this waist-
coat—I gave it him—I afterwards gave it up to the prosecutor.
SAMUEL CLARK re-examined. I can swear these are my things. and
before they were lost they were in my box in my room.
Prisoner. It was on the 1st April I lodged at his house; I slept in that
room, and his wife slept in the same room ; this shirt belongs to me.
NOT GUILTY.
(The Jury statcd that they could not rely upon the prosecutor's evidence.)
