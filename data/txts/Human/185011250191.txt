   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you known the prisoner? A.  Some years—he has been in the habit of dealing at our house, buying and selling—I never heard anything wrong of him—he has been allowed to select goods in our shop—he has sold such goods as he might buy at sales.
   MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  What articles has he sold at your shop? A.  Goods of various descriptions—we are gas-fitters—I rather think we have bought taps of him before, with other goods; not very recently—my master is in the miscellaneous way, he purchases other things—the prisoner lives some where close by us—he said he purchased these taps at a sale-room; I am not certain whether he said Jones's. JOSEPH HONNOR. I am a porter in the employ of Mr. Jones, in Lei cester-street, Leicester-square, an auctioneer—there is no other auctioner of that name in Leicester-square—I have been in his employ four years—I do not recollect seeing the prisoner there—the regular days for our sales are Wednesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays—I cannot undertake to say the prisoner has not been there—I recollect Monday 4th Nov.—there was no sale there that evening nor on that day—there has been no taps like these sold at Mr. Jones's—there was no sale on Tuesday 5th Nov., nor any taps sold of this description—there was a sale on Wednesday 6th— —there were no taps of this description sold in that week, nor the week before—I was present at all the sales all the time—about twelve months ago we had an ironmonger's stock sold—we sold some taps, but not twelve dozen.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You cannot swear that taps might not have been sold without your knowledge? A.  Not in the sale—they might have been sold in the sale-room, that is done at times—persons come to the sale with lots and sell them without their being in the catalogue—persons attending other sales knowing that dealers are in our room, come in the afternoon and sell them by private contract in the room—the sale-rooms are open other days for men to bring goods in—the articles are on view on Wednesday morning—the rooms are open on Monday and Tuesday for taking in articles—articles might not on these days be sold by private contract—we begin on Monday night to make the catalogue, and we allow no one to enter except to bring goods—as many as half-a-dozen persons might be there—I cannot undertake to say that goods might not be sold by private contract—brokers and persons of that sort meet at our doors and do business—Mr. Thomas's auction-rooms are in the square—their sales are on Mondays and Thursdays.
   MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  Were you at Mr. Jones's rooms on that Monday? A.  Yes; I live there—I am always there—except on auction days no persons are allowed to come there to traffic—they are not allowed to be in the room to make a sale—if two dealers meet there to make a sale, they would be told to go down-stairs and settle it—I was there on the Tuesday—the sale commences on Wednesday at six o'clock in the evening. EDWARD JONES re-examined.  I am the only person who occupies this house—I keep the key of the outer-door and of the room where the pro secutor's goods are—I pay no rent nor taxes—I live on part of my master's premises—the family never sleep there—the prisoner did come to Doulton.
