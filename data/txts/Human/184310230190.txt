 3072. GEORGE HENRY HAISELL was indicted for forging and utter ing, on the   20th of Feb ., a certain bill of exchange for the payment of the sum of 37 l  . 12 s  ., with intent to defraud Elizabeth Hatton .
   MESSRS. CLARKSON and CARTEEN conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS CHARLES SIRRELL. I am a gold-refiner, and live in Barbican. The prisoner frequently called on me—I could not undertake to say he brought this bill with him, as he was in the habit of coming himself, and of sending a young man who is since dead—I discounted this bill ( looking at it ) a day or two after it was drawn—I could not undertake to say whether I paid the money to the prisoner, or to the person be sent the bill by, but it was on the faith of his note from the firm, on the faith that he had authority from the firm to draw it—if the prisoner did not bring it, a person named Woolley did—this is my signature to these depositions—( looking at them )—this bill, marked No. 1, is for 37 l . 12 s .—it purports to be drawn by procuration of Elizabeth Hatton and Co.—I discounted it—I do not remember the day, except by the bill-book—I would not undertake to say that the prisoner brought me this bill—he brought me one bill, and two were brought to me by Woolley, to the best of my recollection and belief—I never said that to the best of my belief it was the prisoner—I could not say whether it was No. 1, 2, or 3, he brought. ELIZABETH HATTON. I carry on the business of a chemist and Prussian blue maker, in Elder-street, Spitalfields—it was formerly the business of my son—he has been dead three years.
