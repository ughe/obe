THOMAS FLAUN (KR 23). I was at the Bow station when Brown and
Nash were brought in at the Victoria Park station Nash said, “ God
blind me, Tom, I didn't have the watch, but I know who did"—that was
during the time they had gone out to get some others to put with them,
for identification-I saw Nash change his hard felt hat and put on a
cap; I did not raise any objection-I have seen the prisoners together
frequently during the nine years I have been attached to the station,
JAMES TURTLE. I am a medical man-on the 7th June I examined
the prosecutor at about 10 p.m.-he was sober, but very excited-he
was quite able to walk about, and I understood what he said there was
a severe contusion on the back of his right hand, and I bandaged it-it
was an injury such as would be caused by the butt end of a whip.
Cross-examined by MR. BURNIE. It was & severe contusion, but not
serious to life, if you mean that I did not attend the Police-court-some
solicitor attended on me, and I told him what I know about it-I should
say, from the prosecutor's condition, he had been drinking I should
imagine that the effect of a man being struck on the hand so as to cause
this bruise, and losing his watch after a long drive, would be to sober
him.
Witness for Nash and Broton.
JAMES CRAWFORD. I am a greengrocer, of Maplin Street, Mile End
Road-I known Nash and Brown-I beard they were taken into custody
-I saw them the Sunday before that at my yard, between 8 and 9 p.m.
the Victory public-house is about a quarter of an hour from my place-
they came about taking a stable, and said they had been about buying &
pony—they remained with me for an hour the Prince of Wales
publio-house is near my place-they left me about 9.30—that was the
Sunday before I heard they were locked up.
Cross-examined by Mr. HUTTON. I have known Nash and Brown
for years-Brown used to work for me when I was in business-I am
costermongering now-I first heard they were locked up on Wednesday
night about eleven o'clock-it was the 7th they came down about the
stables-I went to the Police-court-I knew they were innocent I was
never called-Brown asked me to come here to-day; he sent his niece
he is my nephew.
Re-examined. I was only one day before the Magistrate.
GUILTY. Nash then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction on January 6th,
1890, in the name of George Bennett. WEAVER and BROWN-Twelve
Months' Hard Labour. NASH-Fiftoon Months' Hard Labour.
