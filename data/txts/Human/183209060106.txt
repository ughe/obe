 1978.  JOHN CASHMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 3 saucepans, value 9s., and 1 fryingpan, value 1s., the goods of Henry Benjamin.
   MARY CARTY. I live at Mr. Benjamin's, on Saffronhill- I left these articles safe in his yard. On the morning of the 24th of July, I was awoke by my master, who asked me if they were my mistress' property; I said Yes - there are palings all round the yard, and these things had been on the shelf, and on the washing-stool.
 Prisoner. Q. Does not the door of the yard open into a court? A. Yes, I cannot say whether it was bolted.
   RICHARD VERYAN. About twenty minutes after five o'clock that morning, I was getting up; I heard a little rumbling in the court - I looked out, and saw the prisoner get over the palings of the prosecutor's yard; I saw him take one iron saucepan off a shelf - I called a Policeman, who came and took him.
   JOHN WILLIAM TURNER(Police-constable G 218). I went and took the prisoner in a house opposite the prosecutor's back door; he had some soap and some tins, which were taken out of the yard.
   JOHN SMITH(Police-constable G 33). I found the three saucepans and the frying-pan in the passage; when the officer took the prisoner, I found this piece of soap in his pocket, which had been in one of the saucepans.
   MARY CARTY. I lost a piece of soap, and I believe this is it, and the paper of pins.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been at a raffle till half-past four o'clock in the morning; I then went home with a young woman to Bridgewater-gardens; at half-past five I came to this court, where I had lived two or three years ago - I went into this yard to get some water to wash me, and took some out of one of these saucepans -I could not take these three saucepans and the frying-pan away.
     NOT GUILTY.
