 215. THOMAS DORRINGTON was indicted for embezzlement. JOHN MARLOW. I am a Peckham carrier. I took the prisoner into my service about a month ago—on the morning of the 9th of November I sent him with a basket of plants to Mr. Reid, at Hanover-park, Peckham—he was to receive 1 l . 10 s . 6 d .—he came back in about half an hour, but did not give me any money—I sent him with another basket, and told him to make haste home, but he absconded altogether—I had employed him before to receive money several times. JAMES REID. I received a basket of plants from the prisoner on the 9th, and paid him 1 l . 10 s . 6 d . for his master. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months; Two Weeks Solitary.
 216. BENJAMIN MORRIS was indicated for stealing, on the 2nd of November, 1 pail value 1 s  .; 12lbs. weight of fat, value 4 s  .; 1 gut, value 6 d  .; and 3 gallons of blood, value 6 d  .; the goods of Frederick Brown , his master. FREDERICK BROWN. I am a butcher, and live in Lambeth-walk. I employed the prisoner occasionally to kill for me—on the evening of the 1st of November he came to work, and on the 2nd of again—on Monday. I missed 12lbs. weight of fat and a pail—he was standing at a green-shop opposite—I called him over, and said, "Where is the rest of this fat?"—he said, "I do not know, indeed, master"—I said, "Do you go and fetch it, and then I will not say any more about it"—he said, "I know nothing about it"—I pulled the other fat open, and said, "You see it is not all here"—he said, "No, it is not"—I said, "Go and fetch it"—he said he knew nothing about it, and gave him in charge—we went to his house, and there I found my pail, with the fat in his cupboard—he said he did not know how it came there.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  When you went to this house was the policeman with you? A.  Yes—the prisoner might have had the pail, he had it once, I believe, to fetch some grains in, he then asked for it—I have a person in my employ named Foulkes—the prisoner never said in my presence that he had the pail lent to him for a temporary purpose—he never bought skins and offal of me.
   COURT . Q.  Have you any doubt that the fat belonged to you? A.  Not at all. JOHN JEFFERIS. I am a pork-butcher. The prisoner called on me on the 2nd of November—I asked if he was going to kill any thing—he said he was—I asked him to let me have a set of guts and a pail of blood, and about eight o'clock he sent the blood down, and brought the gut himself, but I was not present—I went to Mr. Brown—he came and saw this blood and gut. FREDERICK BROWN. I missed the gut, and went there and found it.
   MR. DOANE called JOSEPH LAWFORD. I live at No. 16, Mansion House-street, Bermond sey. I saw the prosecutor foreman—he told me that he had lent Morris the pail himself, and that Mr. Brown knew it. FREDERICK BROWN. It is an entire falsehood—I went to Jefferis house, and saw some blood and some gut—the gut was missing from my slaughter-house. GUILTY. Aged 39.— Confined Six Months; Three Weeks Solitary.
