—Mr. Seeley asked me to see Mr. Fenton—Mrs. Charlton came down on the Monday, and I was examined on the Wednesday—Mrs. Charlton paid my expenses—Mrs. Seeley was not sober on 4th July—I think I said in the other Court that I was to help Mrs. Charlton move the palliasse into her house—what I said was "Mrs. Seeley told me to help Mrs. Charlton move the mattress downstairs," but Mrs. Charlton was in the room and told me to help Mr. Charlton down with it and over the wall and up Mrs. Charlton's staircase; I won't swear I said that but I did it—I cannot say whether Mrs. Seeley saw the palliasse in the back garden—she was not upstairs all the time—I said "Mrs. Seeley did not see the mattress go over the wall," that is true—Mrs. Seeley went to bed intoxicated that night, but she first went round the house to see if all was safe—I do not think she hardly knew what she was doing at the time of the removal—I do not think I have seen Mrs. Seeley go to Mrs. Charlton's; yes, she went that night, but she did not go usually at night—I did not say about the things being given Mrs. Charlton for moving before, because I forgot it.
   Re-examined by MR. W. SLEIGH . I said before the Magistrate, "When the goods were being removed from Hornsey Park Road, the defendants Charlton and Clayton were there—Mrs. Seeley told me she had given some goods to the Charltons—while we were in the front room in the Euston Road she said "I think it was very unkind of Mrs. Charlton not coming to help put the things straight, as I have given her a bedstead, a palliasse, a chest of drawers, and a washstand"—I helped Mrs. Charlton to move the palliasse downstairs in Mrs. Seeley's house, over the wall and up Mrs. Charlton's staircase—Mrs. Seeley was upstairs while the palliasse was being taken down—she was in the room while it was taken from it—she told me to help Mrs. Charlton to take it down—this was on the Wednesday afternoon. CHARLES WARD. I am a carman, and work for Mr. Denman,. of Camden Town—I recollect moving some thing from Hornsey Park Road, and Mrs. Seeley, Mrs. Charlton, and the girl waiting for me—Mrs. Seeley said Clay ton was her husband.
   Cross-examined by MR. MATHEWS . I have not been examined before to-day—I had a "speen" paper four weeks ago—I have never told anybody what I was coming to say.
   Re-examined.  I gave ray proof to the solicitors.
   By THE COURT . I was going to take down the washstand, and Mrs. Seeley said "You cannot manage that yourself, my husband will help you"—Clayton was the only man there.
   The prisoners received good characters.
   EMILY CHARLTON  — GUILTY — Nine Months' Imprisonment.
   EDWARD CHARLTON and EDWARD CLAYTON  — NOT GUILTY.
