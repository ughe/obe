in consequence of a quarrel between the mother and brother and her the
prisoner himself sent for me then-I had never seen him before he was not
a patient of mine--he exhibited on that occasion affection and interest about
his wife--she was then very excited, rushing out of bed, throwing herself on
her knees to her mother, and begging her to leave the house--she exhibited
no want of affection towards her husband-I believe the mother said they had
been so often quarrelling, and the brother also, and he said he would not
leave the house, being determined to protect her, but the whole time she was
begging them to leave-it is decidedly my belief, having examined the wound,
that it was inflicted by her own hands.

Arthur John Vasey. I am the son of the prisoner's wife, and live with
my aunt, at Shaftesbury-terrace-I recollect the Thursday night before this
Sunday I slept in the room with the prisoner and my mother-he came
home at one o'clock at night I went to bed in the dark in the back room
he and my mother were quarrelling in the front room I heard a fall in the
front room, and I thought it was my mother falling they afterwards came
into the back room where I was, and came to bed-I slept in the same bed
with them--there was a rushlight burning-I saw him get out of bed and
sauff it with a pair of scissors, and he put the scissors to his own throat, and
said he should do for her.I afterwards saw my mother lying on the floor.
