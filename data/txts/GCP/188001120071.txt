ALBERT ALLEN. I carry on business at 6, St. Swithin's Lane, as a general
commission merchant and shipping agent-it is part of my business to make
advances on goods-- I made the acquairtance of Frank Wilton and Co. in
1878, by making advances on bills of lading for colonial produco-I was
introduced to them by Mr. Penny, a chemical broker, and I called on them
in Mark Lane-my first advance on woollen goods was on 6th May-I
advanced them money on different pledgings from that time to the time
of their stoppage-within the last four months before they stopped I advanced
from 4,0007, to 5,0001. on the invoice value of the goods, 6,0001.- the
invoices were sent with the goods; they were principally lying at South-
gate's, some at J. and T. Bailey's—I have sold the goods, but have not yet
received the whole of the money-I shall lose about 8001. I am not up
in the woollen trade, but I believe they are determined by fashion- I have
made a very bad bargain--my principal dealings as to the pledgings were
with Wilton; I have never to my knowledge seen Dunderdale with reference
to them-I know him by sight, but I have never treated with him for an
advance-I only remember one instance of his being present when I was
negotiating an advance with Wilton, that was I think on 8th September,
but that was not about the pledging; Wilton wanted me to advance some
money to renew a bill that Messrs. Walker would renew, it had nothing to
do with these goods-on 21st August I advanced 5001., that was a renewal
-on 28th August I advanced 2007. on goods that came from Crossland's
on 2nd September 5941. ou goods from Judd and Son, Robshaw, Thackeray,
and Ward and Son--on 13th September 7507. on goods from Benn, Staplos
ton, and C., Liddell and Brierly, C. and J. Hurst, and Hall and Dyson -
these (produced) are documents which I received from Wilton and Co.-my
commission on the advances was from 3 to 5 per cent.--in round numbers,
since May, if I had all that was due to me, it would be about 3771.- I did not
hold any capital at Wilton's disposal on 27th August, 1879--when I made
the advances I received a memorandum, and the original invoices and the
transfer orders, and on the receipt of those documents and the goods I gave
my cheque.
Cross-examined by Mr. WRIGHT. I have been in business in the City
for some time, and have had frequent dealings in advancing money on goods
consigned to merchants of high standing and respectability--it is no un
usual thing for merchants to require temporary advances on goods consigned
to them for sale ; I believe it is very common-I never imagined that there
Was anything wrong about the dealing with these goods, if I had I should
have ceased to have any dealings of the kind—I frequently advanced money
on the goods that were sent to Paris before the loans were repaid-the
arrangement was, that on the arrival of the goods in Paris the bills they
gave me would be taken up--they were to be met through my bank
in no case were the goods given up first—the dealings I had with this
firm in no way practically differed from the dealings I had with mer-
chants of the highest respectability-I knew nothing of the value of the
woollen goods, except from the invoices—all the loans were for short
umes-generally speaking the goods were redeemed.
