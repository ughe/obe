They demanded money; I gave them what money I had, and should have given them more, if I had had it in my pocket. When my master came home he asked me how they came there; I told him. There is a person here, who lodges in the house, that saw the men in the shop.
 For the Prisoner.
   SARAH PHILLIPS sworn.
 I lodge in the house where the prisoner lives. On the 7th of June, between eleven and twelve o'clock, while I was in my chamber, I heard a noise, I came down stairs, and saw five or six men in the house with blue cockades; they insisted upon the prisoner buying these things; she refused to buy them. They told her, if she did not buy them they would set fire to the house, because they were papists.
 Is the man of the house a catholick? - Yes, he is a Flemish man.
 Did she buy the things? - I went up stairs to look after my children; when I came down stairs again I saw the woman give them money, but I do not know how much.
 Prisoner. I have somebody here to prove my master is ill of the gout.
   JOHN GRAY sworn.
 I am an apothecary in Bartholomew-Close, I attend the prisoner's master, he was ill of a fever; the fever is now gone off, and he has a white flux.
 The prisoner called  John Slaide and - Majorby, who gave her a good character.
     NOT GUILTY.

 412.  ELIZABETH TIMMINGS was indicted for stealing five china dishes, value 20 s. five china plates, value 5 s. a linen sheet, value 8 s. a linen napkin, value 10 d. a pair of leather gloves, value 1 s. and a muslin cap, value 2 s. the property of the Right Honourable William  Earl of Mansfield, June 6th.
   JOHN DIXON sworn.
 I found the things mentioned in the indictment (producing them) in Timmings's house; we had an information that a great many things were carried to that house; we found her packing some of them up; we took her into custody; when she was at the office she said she had had them a great many years; she said the sheet was given her by an aunt of her's in Ireland; she said she bought some of the china at Bristol, and had had the things in general a great many years; that they were her own property; that she took the sheet to Ireland, and brought it back with her.
 Cross Examination.
 This confession was taken down in writing, was it not? - I believe not.
 Did not she tell you that  Ann Charlotte, Mary Gardener, and Esther Kelly, brought these things there? - No.
 You took every thing you could lay your hands on, and her own things too? - Yes; I do not know that she had so much of her own.
 Was there not an application made to the court to deliver her her things. You never heard that Charlotte, Gardener, and Kelly, had given her the things? If they had given her the things, why did not she say so? they were in the house at the time.
 Have they not been charged with this offence? - No.
 You never heard her charge these three people with bringing the things? No.
   JAMES HYDE sworn.
 I belong to the office in Litchfield-street.
 Was you at Timmings's at any time? - Yes; I am not positive to the time; we searched the back room, and found a parcel of linen and some china. I took her into custody. I think she said they were her own things, and that she had them from some relation.
