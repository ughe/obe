HENRY ST. JOHN BULLEN. I am a surgeon. On 1st Sept, my atten
tion was called to the deceased, who was then in the reception ward at
Lambeth workhouse-- he was able to stand upright, and was conscious and
rational, and able to reply to my questions—I found him tremulons, as if
from a habit of hard drinking-he drew my attention to the condition of
his chest-I fonnd the fore part of it very much swollen, enlarged, and
bruised, so that I could not feel the bones underneath-I had him put to
bed in the sick ward-on 3rd Sept., I found symptoms of an abscess on the
right side of the chest, which I opened, and there was a discharge of about
a pint of very fetid matter-that appeared to relieve him-he appeared
better the next day-on 6th Sept., he was much worse, and from that time
till the 13th he continued declining—on the 13th, his state was one of
imminent danger I told him so at that time, and he said that he felt that
he was sinking and could not recover-in consequence of that I directed &
police Magistrate to be sent for, and the deceased made a statement to him
in my presence-no longer time elapsed between my telling him he was
in imminent danger, and his making the statement, than was necessary to
sed for the Magistrate after that the deceased continued gradually to
sink till the 20th, when he died on the day after his death I made an
examination of the body, and took some notes of it the head was carefully
shaved over the upper portion of the scalp-I found two contusions between
the dura mater and the brain-I uncovered the skull, which I found solid
and unbroken--the membrane covering the brain was oongested, and adhe-

chest and found the large sinns of an abscess, extending from the right breast
across the breast bone to the left breast about eight inches in length it
