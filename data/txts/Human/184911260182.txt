183. WILLIAM PAYNE , stealing 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  .; the goods of John William Wyatt , from his person.
   MR. DAWSON conducted the Prosecution. JOHN WILLIAM WYATT . I am a clerk in the Provincial Bank of Ireland, Old Broad-street. On 3 rd  Nov. I was in the Borough, about half-past five o'clock—I had left the bank, about five minutes past five—I had my pocket-handkerchief then—in High-street, in the Borough, I felt a person take it—I turned round and saw two persons running—I could not identify them—the prisoner is about the size of one of them—they were about a middling height; a person spoke to me—I afterwards saw the prisoner at the police-court—I have not seen my handkerchief since.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q. IS  not this the first time you have said anything about two persons running away? A.  I think not—(Me depo-sition being referred to did, not contain it )—I must have omitted it, 1 did not think much of it.
   MR. DAWSON . Q.  Where was Arkwright? A.  He appeared to me to be looking in one of the shops—the persons were running towards London Bridge, and were about 100 yards behind me. JAMES ARKWRIGHT. I live in Wilderness-street, Long-lane, and am a currier. On Saturday afternoon, 3 rd  Nov., I saw Wyatt in the Borough, from a quarter to half-past five—I had seen the prisoner follow two or three gentlemen before—then Wyatt and his friend came past—the prisoner followed them for about a minute—he then took the handkerchief and ran away—I ran about twenty yards after him, but found I could not overtake him—I turned and went to the prosecutor, knowing the prisoner well before. Cross-examined. Q.  How many times have you been a witness? A.  I have had fourteen or fifteen convictions—if I see persons do wrong I think it is my duty to have them brought up—I do not know that it is better pay than being a currier—I do not know that there is any pay—I have got the same as others—I had 3 s . a day in the other Court. NOT GUILTY.
 184. JOHN GREEN , stealing 2 trowels, value 4 s  .; the goods of Edwin James Embleton .
   MR. M. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution. EDWIN JAMES EMBLETON. I am a plasterer, of Peacock-street; I know these two trowels, they are mine. On 5 th  Nov. they were safe between one and two o'clock, and when I returned from dinner I missed them.
   Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLETT . Q.  How do you know this one? A.  By wearing the shank off; and I had a letter put on the back—by constant wear it is very nearly worn out, and by my driving the shank up in the handle—I know this other one by having it in constant use.
   RICHARD  Moss ( policeman, P  330). I saw the prisoner at eight o'clock at night, at the corner of Lock's-fields, with a small bag under his arm—I asked him what he had got; he said, "Two trowels"—I asked where he got them; he said it was all right—I said I did not know that—I took him to the light, and he had got six trowels; these are two of them—he said he had bought them of a man who was gone to Australia, he did not know his name, nor where he had lived. GUILTY.* Aged 41.— Confined Eighteen Months .
