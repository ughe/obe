I get my living by selling fancy goods at White-chapel—I sold a pair of anti-macassars, and received this in payment—I was going to a wake, and did not wish any body to see how much I had got, as if they did, they would wish me to spend it all, and therefore I went into the shop with it—I did not know it was bad.  540. JOSEPH POWELL ( ), Unlawfully having counterfeit coin in his possession, with intent to utter it.
   MESSRS. CLERK and POLAND conducted the Prosecution. JAMES BICKNELL( Policeman, M  276). On 12th April, between 6 and 7 in the evening, I was on duty in Thornton-street, Southwark, and saw the prisoner there very drunk—I took him in custody; he kicked me very violently, and wanted to get his hands loose—I took him to the station with some diffi culty, though I had a sergeant to assist me—I searched him at the station, and found in his left-hand waistcoat pocket 9 s . wrapped separately in fine paper, and in his right waistcoat pocket, five florins in similar paper—I took off his coat and waistcoat, and found in his left waistcoat pocket another counterfeit shilling loose, which I had not noticed before—in his trousers pocket I found a purse and 3 l ., and 5 d . in copper, good money, loose—he gave his address 25, Weston-street—that was false; but he was drunk—I gave the money to Sergeant Butler.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Did you see him first in a public-house? A.  Outside the door—he had a scar on his forehead; it was bleeding; the man who knocked him down did that in his own defence, I was told—the people were round him, but were not annoying him. CHARLES BUTLER( Policeman, M  16). I assisted in taking the prisoner to the station; he was very drunk—Bicknell gave me 5 florins, and the 9 s . were on the desk; these are them ( produced )—the prisoner first refused to give any address; he afterwards said I live at 25, New Weston-street—I went there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was he very much knocked about? A.  He had two cuts; a deep one over the left eye, and a slight one near the right eye—the skin was grazed off his nose—he could have walked to the court, I believe, next morning, but he said that his arms and legs were so knocked about that he could not, and as there was no one to own the money, I allowed him to have a cab—I do not know who had been fighting with him.
   MR. CLERK . Q.  Did you go to the Police Court with him next morning? A.  Not in the cab; I followed him there—he gave no account of how he got the money—he was quite sober then. WILLIAM WEBSTER. This is all bad money, these 5 florins are from one mould; these 5 s . of 1855 are from one mould; and these 5 s . of 1857 from another—when counterfeit money is taken out for sale, it is invariably wrapped up in this way to prevent its getting rubbed before it gets into circulation. GUILTY.— Confined Eighteen, Months .
