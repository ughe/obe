   Cross-examined by MR. ISAACS . I came up to London before I was appointed liquidator, on December 26th, on an order of the Court—there was a petition presented on the Record before the Court, and on that there was a conflicting opposition, and then the Court ordered me to go down to the bank and make some report—I came up here in order to inquire, and when appointed liquidator I employed Messrs. Clarke and Rawlins, the solicitors, to act for us in London—I had nothing to do with Messrs. Davidson and Syme, and I had no solicitors at all when I came up the first time—I first went to the bank on the 27th—it was closed on the day I came up, the 26th—I went there in the morning, and stated what I had come for—I saw Mr. F. J. Baker—I was informed that Mr. Beall was away at Brighton ill—I got a certificate from his doctor in the beginning of January—I had no reason to doubt that Mr. Beall was at Brighton ill—in January he came back—it was about January 23rd that he came back—in December, 1894, when I was in Scotland, I was told that the persons, that is, Mr. Glaskin or high officials in connection with the London Refuse Destructor, would be asked to meet me to explain what had been done and what was being done—Mr. Glaskin was the con sulting engineer in December—I did not see them; I was told that they were there, but I did not want to see them; I made no engagement with them—I understood about this balance-sheet of Messrs. Abbott; I knew that one of the assets which, at any rate, the Scottish Bank claimed to have were the shares in these Refuse Destructor companies—I was making inquiries in other quarters as to the balance-sheet—it did not occur to me that those were the best people to tell exactly what was being done with regard to it, as I wanted an independent witness—so far as these shares were concerned, there was no market value or Stock Exchange quotation—my reason for not seeing the people who were actually working the thing was that I wanted evidence outside the directors of the London and Scottish Bank—I never did, in fact, see either Mr. Glaskin or Mr. Cheesewright—I saw Mr. Cooper about it at his office, and I saw Mr. Wain about it—although the certificate did not come into my hands, I satisfied myself as to the title of the Scottish Bank to the £16,500 British; that is to say, that the Scottish Bank was entitled to the shares, although they had not got the certificate in their hands—ultimately I got all the books and papers except a shareholders' ledger—I know-a shareholders' ledger was kept, and I am almost sure I must have seen one iu December; when I came and took possession it was not there—so far as the ledger is concerned, it would be a register of ordiuary shares—the shareholders' ledger was of this importance, that I could" not tell who were in arrear, it is only to ascertain who had not paid the calls; I got £23—I had to get my clerk to make up a complete statement of the calls, and I found several inarrear—I came up later on in January, and employed Messrs.
