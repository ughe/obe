WILLIAM KRITCHELL( Policeman K  97). The prosecutor came to me on 30th July, before the prisoner was arrested on this charge, and said "Have you got Isaac Webb in custody?"—I said "Yes"—he said "Did you search his premises about some watches?"—I said "I did, and found nothing relating to any property"—he said "How can I identify him?"—I said "It is not my place to tell you how you can identify him"—he said he believed he could identify him—I said "Oh" afterwards he said "I have been to his place, 66, Ocean Street, and I gave his wife 1 s ., since that I have given her 5 s ., for the purpose of going to Chelmsford to see him"—I said "I don't think you had any perfect right to go near the place after you had given information to the police."
   Cross-examined.  He didn't ask me by what means he could see him to identify him—the prisoner was in custody on another charge.
   The prisoner in his defence stated that he had never been near the place, and knew nothing of the watches. JOHN GRINDON( Re-examined ). I saw the prisoner in the dock at Chelmsford Assizes with four or five others. NOT GUILTY.
 890. WILLIAM WAKEFIELD  (32), Feloniously marrying Amy Genisis , his wife being then alive.
   MR. WARBURTON Prosecuted; MR. GEOGHEGAN Defended. SARAH RADFORD. I live at 1A, Craven Street, City Road, and am a costermonger, and the mother of Jane Radford—I was present when she married the prisoner at St. Mary's Church, Charterhouse, on 12th October, 1874—the ceremony was performed by a clergyman in the usual way—they signed their names in the vestry afterwards. AMY GENISIS. I met the prisoner in Old Street, St. Luke's, and was married to him five weeks afterwards at St. Thomas's Church, Bethnal Green, on 13th April, 1885—he has since ill-used me very much, knocked me about, and ill-treated me.
   Cross-examined.  I did not instruct a solicitor to prosecute. ROBERT DAY. I got the certificates, and now produce them. ( These were certificates of a marriage at St. Mary's, Charterhouse, on October, 12th, 1874, between William Wakefield, bachelor, and Jane Radford; and the other of a marriage at St. Thomas's, Bethnal Green, 13th April, 1885, between William Wakefield and Amy Genisis. )
   Cross-examined.  I did not instruct Mr. Hadley to prosecute—the first wife is still alive—she was here yesterday—I have not seen her to-day. SARAH RADFORD( Re-examined ). My daughter is alive—about a year and a half ago the prisoner came to my place and asked where my daughter was—I said I did not know—he said if I found her they would be happy and comfortable together, and next day he came round and they went home and into my bed.
   MR. GEOGHEGAN did not address the Jury. GUILTY.— Four Months' Hard Labour.
