I am the wife of William Ballard, who was formerlys policeman, and live in Chapel-place. On Saturday the 21st of May, in co. sequence of an arrangement, I went to the prisoner's shop in the London road—I bought a cap there, and said I wished to have a baby's'-l said I wished to make a present to a little boy of a cap, to go out of doors, and wanted a very pretty one made"—the prisoner asked if I should like one of cheni velvet—he showed me a bit of ribbon of the colour and I ordered him to make one by Wednesday—I deposited 1 s . 6 d  —be said he had not got one by him, but would have one made for me—on Wed nesday the 23rd I went to the shop and received this cap—( producing it )—I paid 3 s .—I did not see him the day I got the cap—I went the next day, and saw him, and said the cap was too large—I wanted a smaller one—he said he would make me one, and would take this back if it did not suit me—I said the child was small, I would keep it, and he might make me another of the same colour—he said he had not sufficient vel vet—he bought but a small piece in the market, and if he had to buy more the cap might not be so cheap.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  You did not get the second cap? A No—I went to the shop because I was told to do so by my hus band—I was by myself when he bid me—I did not see any one else-I had no communication with the prosecutor about this cap—I did not give the measure—I told the child's age—I had taken this cap home and tried it on—I did not return it because I thought it was cheap, and I could give it to a child—there was no other reason for my wanting another—my husband told me not to take it back—I live in an ordinary sized house-we keep baths. JANE TOYNE. I live in Lemon-street, Gravel-lane, and am in the em ploy of Muller and Simpson. On Monday, the 23rd of May, I saw the prisoner at Mr. Muller's warehouse—he wished me to make a cap of the chain velvet—he intended purchasing a yard from Mr. Muller's—on the Wednesday he gave me the velvet—I made it up, and sent it to his house—this is it.
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  How long have you worked for Muller and Simpson? A.  About seventeen weeks—I work at home-I never saw the prisoner till I went to work for them—he was employed there cutting out—I do not know whether he brought his own materials from his shop and cut them out there—I once had a silk velvet cap of him at Mr. Muller's—he gave me the materials at Mr. Muller's warehouse to make it—he must have brought them from home—I once fetched a piece of cloth from the prisoner's to make a cap for the prosecutors—I never fetched any thing else—no one was present when he told me he should want me to make up a cap—I made no secret of it—I made it at my own house—I sent it to his own shop—I did not show it to any one.
