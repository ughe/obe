CHARLOTTE SPARVILLB. I am single and live at 23, Spencer Road, Sands
End, Fulham-on Tuesday night, 28th April, about 12 o'clook, when I
had gone to bed, the prisoner knocked at the door, a lodger opened the
door and let her in, and I opened the parlour door--she had a baby with
her which I knew as Mrs. Sorivener's-h asked to be allowed to lie down
in my empty room and I agreed-I asked her why she brought the baby
outshe said because she loved it-I asked her why she came, she said
that something unpleasant had happened at home-I laid a blanket down
in the baok room and she and the child slept there that night and she took
breakfast with me the next morning--the child was still with her and she
seemed very fond of it-about 12 or 1 o'olook, I cannot say positively, I
asked her if she was going home-she said she had not made her mind up
-nothing further passed but she left, taking the child with herishe did
not write any letter at my house-she did not say anything about Mr.
Serivener-1 did not see her again until she was in oustody.
Cross-examined. She did not say when she came at 12 o'olook that she
had no money, she never mentioned money to me, she merely said that she
thought she should have had a oup of tea.
ANN IRELAND. I am a widow, of 20, Lawrenoe Street, Chelsea, half-an-
hour's walk from Sparville's-or Wednesday, 29th April, about 8.15 p.m., I
was in Fulham Road on my way home from work, and saw the prisoner with
her grandehild in her arms-I knew who she was, and said 44 It is a cold
night"-she said 46 Yes said 44 What a pity you have brought the baby
out suh a cold night as this"-she kissed the baby and said he is all right
-we walked along as faras my place, close to here home, and she asked me
if she might come in and sit down for awhile (I mean close to Mr. and Mrs.
Serivener's house I live a few doors off)-he came in and talked, but
nothing particular was said-I went out to get a pint of ale for my supper,
and asked her if she could drink a glass of ale-ghe said 44 Yes" and when
I went out I met Mrs. Serivener, and made a statement to her-when I got
back I gave the prisoner a glass of ale and a bit of bread and cheese and I
said 44 Mrs. Stewart, I hear you have not been home all night; get up and
leave my place, and take the child home to his mother"sh said The
boy is all right, bless his heart, he loves his granny, and his granny loves
im"-when I mentioned her not being home all night she made no remark
except that she knew that, and she had been over at Fulham-it was

did not see her again till she was in custody.
