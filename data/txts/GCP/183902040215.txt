Mr. Bopkin. Q. Why, when asked by your master about the person
who employed you, did you not give his name and a description of him?
A. I had no particular reason at first-he had been a companion of mine
in Thames-street-I did not know whether it was right or not-he had
been a man out of work, and when I bad a job by which I could get a
shilling, he went with me
Q. He had befriended you before? A. Yes, I had known him for years
it is many years ago that I lived with Bailey-I have been between
seven and eight years with Hill, and was with Bailey some years before
I was not taken before any Magistrate about the silk-I might have gone
to work for Bailey afterwards if I had thonght proper--when my wife died I
had some money coming from my club, and when these things happen they
collect a trifle round-I kept a little money back till I got that --master
stopped that out of my wages. I told him how it happened. 2
