-I continued as chief cashier up to the liquidation of the bank, and at
the end of 1894, or January, 1895, I remember seeing Mr. Aitcheson,
when I handed over to him £1 Os. 8d., which was the cash in hand at the
time-in the bank premises there was a safe upstairs, and one down-Mr.
F. J. Baker kept the keys of the safe-Mr. Beall kept the duplicate keys
of the top safe; the other one downstairs we used to keep the books and
letters in, and all manner of things-Beall was there nearly every day-
I used to take orders from him—there were directors' meetings, some
times once a week and sometimes once a fortnight-Mr. Baker kept the
minute-book and the records of what went on there-I remember Mr.
Baker going up to Scotland ; in December, I think-after his return I
remember his making some inquiries about some papers and books-I had
seen Mr. Beall going to the safe while he was away-my back was to
him, but I could see him take some sort of papers-he took them in the
board room-I never saw them afterwards I see in this cash-book No. 2
(Produced) that I debit myseif with the receipt of a cheque for £200:
"To cheque Beall and Co. account, £200"-on the other side I find an
entry with regard to the same cheque : "To cheque Beall and Co. account,
£200, establishment expenses"-in the establishment expenses account I
find entered a cheque for £200, credited to the account of March 1st,
1894-in Beali and Co.'s account I find there, under the date of February
28th, £200 credited to Mr. Beall's account-I should say that £200 is the
same sum that is entered in the second of these books.
