Cross-eramined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. You were very tired when you met
the women? A. Yes, rather-I should not have gone home with them if
they had not stopped me I was anxious to get a place to rest-that was one
reason why I went home with them-that also accounts for my going on the
bed so soon-I had been in the room about three minutes when Jones took
my watch-I had never seen them before I did not see Jones again till
Friday at the station-Flynn was with her then when I went into the house I
had 23. 4fd. in my pocket-I gave 4 d. for gin, and 1s. to Jones—the other
shilling remained in my pocket-I had been walking about the streets at
Westminster about an hour-I did not speak to any other females, some
might have spoken to me, but I did not take any notice- no other female
spoke to me during the hour I was walking about this is the guard that was
round my neck-Jones broke it when she took the watch from me -I am quite
sure Flynn is the other woman.
WILLIAM NOWLAN (policeman, B 56). On Tuesday morning, 18th July,
I beard a cry of Police !" in St. Ann-street-I went towards the place, and
sw Doyle in the act of running through St. John's-buildings into St. Ann's
Jane, Old Pye-street, from the direction of St. Ann's-court-I ran after him,
and lost sight of him-I had known him for three or four years I saw the
prosecutor, and went with him to 20, St. Ann's-court, and found Doyle's
hat, about a yard and a half from the door-I know it to be his hat; there is
crape on it-I afterwards showed it to Doyle-he said it was his; that on the
morning before this occurrence he had given it to Polly Sale's old man the
prisoner Jones went by the name of Sale--I returned back with Preston into
ŚL Ann's-street, and when we came to the end of St. Jobn's-buildings
Haycock was standing against the court-Preston pointed him out to me, and
I took him into custody-I know that Jones occupied the front-room second-
door at 20, St. Anne-street.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Does Doyle live in the house 2
Q. No, he lives about 150 yards off, in St. John's-buildings, in the same
street-I have seen the man whom Polly Sale associates with he appears
to be a coal-heaver-Doyle had no hat on when I pursued him—the prose-
cutor appeared perfectly sober--the hat was brought to the station, and is
there now-Doyle did not claim it-he wore another when he was appre-
bended at four o'clock the same morning.

morning, 18th July, about half-past ten o'clock, at the George and Ball
public-house, at the corner of St. Ann's-street, close to where the robbery was
committed there were several others with him-I told him be was charged
with others in stealing a watch from a man named Preston, that night-he at
first said he knew nothing at all about it, and on the way to the station he
said he would not suffer for other persons on the day following, after he had
been before the Magistrate, he called me to the cell door, and said he wanted
to speak to me I went, and he said that on the morning of the robbery,
