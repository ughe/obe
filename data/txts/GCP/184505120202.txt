JOHN DAVIS. I keep the Boyton Arms beer-shop, near the canal in
New Church-road, Camberwell. The church bell-ringers practise the art
of bell-ringing--we have a peal of seventeen bells for their practice-iti
a society held in our club-room--I saw the prisoner at my house-I canner
say what time he came there-I saw him at half-past eleven o'clock, whes
he came down stairs to go away, and I saw him some short time before.
between ten and o'clock I should think, sitting down with two others-he
made a remark to me, which is the reason why I know him-he was in the
club-room where the bells were-it is open on Sunday evenings--I wa
before the Magistrate-inspector Gifford sent the officers to know whether
what the prisoner had stated as to being in my house was correct, and as !
identified him they did not swear me.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Was the prisoner in the habit of frequenting your
beer-shop? A. No-I never saw him there but once before there were
very likely twenty or thirty persons in the room that night-I am not
aware that there is anybody else here from my house - I did not see the
prisoner after half past eleven o'clock-I should say it was rather more
than that-my house is about half a mile from the George, I suppose, but
I never saw the house to my knowledge.
-- LESTER. I am a bricklayer. I was at the Boyton Arms on
the Sunday night of the robbery-I saw the prisoner there at half-past
cight o'clock-he remained there till half-past eleven-I then left--I let
the prisoner outside the door along with two more.
MR. PAYNE, Q. Do you often go to the Boyton Arms? A. I do
generally on a Sunday-I am one of the bell-ringers - I only saw the pri-
soner there once, and that was that night-I knew him before-I know
this was on Sunday night, the 4th of May, a fortnight last Sunday-be
asked me to drink twice-I did not see him after half-past eleven o'clock
-I was not examined before the Magistrate.
JAMES HISTEY. I was examined before the Magistrate last Tuesday
fortnight, I was at the Boyton Arms with the prisoner on the Sunday
night when this burglary is said to have been committed—(I heard of his
being taken up on the Tuesday)--he remained there from eight o'clock till
half-past eleven-I was with him all that time--we then went to the
Anchor and Hope, and from there to the Elephant and Castle, to get some
coffee-- we got some-I was with the prisoner till half-past two on the
Monday morning--I never saw him wear such a cap as this he wores
blue cap-this is not my cap-I do not know whose it is--I never saw it
before to my knowledge.
MR. PAYNE. Q. He did not generally wear such a cap ? A. No, he
never wore such a cap since I knew him-I had a bed to go to, and so had
he-I cannot say why I did not go home-he gave me no reason for not
going home I left him at half-past two at the Elephant and Castle with
Barton-they were drinking coffee-we were not doing anything besides
drinking coffee-only standing against the Elephant and Castle--we did
not get there till a few minutes before one-we had been drinking at the
Boyton Arms from eight till half-past eleven--we had ale and beer-

--that is not a great way from the George--the bottom of George-street
