 790. WILLIAM SWAINE was indicted , on the   12th of Jan ., for stealing 1 ass, price 12 s  ., the property of Henry Boughton . THOMAS BENT( police-constable N  95.) On the 12th of Jan. I saw the prisoner on Clapham-common—he went up to three donkeys, drove them a little way across the common, drove two back, then got a stick and drove the other on alone about a quarter of a mile—he saw another policeman in uni form on the other side of the common, and then turned the donkey into the furze—Iwatched him for a quarter of an hour hiding himself behind the trees—I then asked what he was doing with the donkey—he said Harry Bowden had lost it, and asked him if he found it to drive it there—I asked where he brought it from—he said from Clapham-common—I asked him to show me the donkey—he took me into the furze and showed me another—I said that was not it—I showed him the right one—he said, "Harry Bowden told me to drive it there."
   Prisoner.  I saw the donkey in Mr. Cubit's park; I knew it would be impounded, and knowing the owner I drove it into the furze, and was going to drive it home. HENRY BOUGHTON. I am a labourer, and live in Acre-lane, Clapham—I kept an ass at Mr. Parson's, in Acre-lane, near Clapham-common—I had not desired the prisoner to drive it—they are liable to be impounded if astray—if they get into Mr. Cubit's park they are always impounded—that is in Clap-ham-park—I know the prisoner, and should not object to bis keeping it from going there—I had seen it about an hour before. THOMAS BENT re-examined.  It was nearly a mile from Mr. Cubit's park when he first drove it—he was driving it away from home—it was feeding on the road, not going towards Mr. Cubit's park. NOT GUILTY.
 791. JOHN KURNER was indicted for stealing 6 lbs.  weight of mutton, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Brooks , his master; and ELIZABETH KURNER for feloniously receiving the same, knowing it to be stolen; to which
   JOHN KURNER  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 25.— Received a good character.
   Recommended to mercy— Confined One Month . JOHN MENNIE( police-constable V  70.) On the morning of the 14th of Jan. I saw the female prisoner go to Mr. Brooks, the butcher's shop in Britannia-place, Wandsworth-road, and wait at the door two or three minutes—the male prisoner, who I believe is her son, gave her something, which she put under her cloak, and went away—I followed, and asked what she had got—she said, "Nothing"—I said, "I know you have"—she then said it was a breast of mutton she had bought of Mr. Brooks—I saw there were two, and took her back—she afterwards said, "For God's sake let me go, I will tell you the truth; I have an afflicted husband at home, and my son has given it to me"
   ELIZABETH KURNER  — NOT GUILTY.
