WILLIAM CLARKE. I live at 211, Cater Street, Peokham, and am &
pensioner in the Royal Marines on Monday, the 4th October, I went up
to the Greenwich Pension Office to draw my pension-I took with me
78. 5d. and drew from the Pension Office 21. 106. Bd., making 21. 178, 10d.
-I afterwards went into a publio-house in Greenwich; I cannot tell the
name--I had 2d. worth of bread and cheese and ld. worth of beer-I
went into another public house first and had a glass of 4d. ale and 2d.
worth of bread and cheese-I was by myself—I then went into the Cape
of Good Hope in King Street, Deptford—I was alone and it was about 3
o'olook in the afternoon; the prisoner was in the bar when I went in and
some conversation ensued between us about joining Her Majesty's service
-paid for spot of ale which we were drinking in front of the bar
the prisoner suggested that we had better go into the tap-room and sit
down"--we went in together and another pensioner was there, who
remained about twenty minuteswhen we were alone I drank some of the
beer and very shortly afterwards became quite insensible—when I recovered
the prisoner was cutting my right-hand trousers pocket out with a knife,
I could not recognise the knife---that is my knife; white bandle, two blades,
and a little bit broken out of the handle--this is the cut pocket--the
prisoner ran out, I told the landlord—I have not seen any of my money
since-I got my knife again from the police.
