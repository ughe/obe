   Cross-examined. Q . Was the boy a strumous  or scorbutic subject? A.  Not that I am aware of—I cannot tell either one way or the other—if there is a purulent discharge from the ear, that must arise from some disease either within the ear, or deeper—irritative fever is sometimes accompanied by deli rium—I only perceived that he was light—headed on one occasion, five or six days before his death, but I heard that he had been delirious in the night—I gave a very small quantity of soothing medicine, not so much to subdue the irritation as from the great disturbance of the bowels—he was suffering from profuse diarrhoea—that would not, if it did not act as intended, produce wan dering, not in the small doses I gave it, and combined with other medi cines—it will do so sometimes, but the medicine did act as I intended—it was only administered on one or two occasions—I cannot say that the mark behind the ear was produced by a blow—the gangrene covered a space of four inches in length and two in breadth—all the symptoms were those of inflammation.
   MR. PLATT . Q.  Might not discharge from the ear take place from a common cold? A.  Yes, it is frequently the case with children. JOHN SWAN FLOWERS. I am a surgeon at Camberwell—I assisted Mr. Thomas in the post mortem  examination—I have heard his evidence, and concur with him so far as this, that I consider the boy died from irritative or hectic fever, caused by an abscess which had formed on the brain—apart from what we have heard as to a blow, it might have arisen from natural causes, from an abscess generated in the system, without external violence, particu larly in a strumous  habit of body, or one of delicate formation—if there was a predisposition, a blow would accelerate it—in my opinion disease was ex isting prior, probably, to the receipt of any blow, and that was indicated by the discharge from the ear—if he had received a blow, and this had been a consequence, I should have anticipated more disturbance of the head, bis intellect would not have been so good as it was, and there would have been vomiting—he ought to have been kept perfectly quiet—if the abscess was caused by a blow, it must have been a very violent one, and in a very bad state of constitution—I should not think a blow from the hand of a boy like the prisoner would produce it.
