Q. Have you never said that you authorized the prisoner to sell the gig ?
4. Never-I think on two occasions I may have told him I wanted to sell it
-I did not tell him to find me a customer- I said I intended to sell it, and
then he spoke about 31. 108., and I said the axle alone was worth that I did
not say it was worth that without the old body--the springs were good, and
there were new shafts-Beckwith called at my house on the 22nd of Dec.
for some of the prisoner's clothes-I at first reſused to give them to him-1
did not tell Beckwith on that occasion that I had certainly authorized the
prisoner to sell the gig, but never intended to give it him.
CHARLES CATER. I live in Montague-mews, and am coachman to Mr.
Mills, of Bryanstone-square. I met the prisoner about the 7th or 8th of
Dec., in Circuit-street, New-road-he said he had a gig for sale, and wished
me to call and look at it, I thought no more of it till the 12th of Dec., when
Harris called on me and said he knew of a gig for sale-I went with him to the
stables, and the gig was pulled out for me to look at-I examined it-the
prisoner said he wanted 51. for it-I said it was more than I would give-I
offered 41. for it-he refused it, and said I should bave it for 41 10s.--I offered
him 41. 58., and was coming out of the mews-he called me back and said I
should have it--I went to the Cleveland Arms, and bought it for 41. 58.
-I have laid out 51. 108. on it-the lining is the same, but the apron is
different.
Cross-examined. Q. What should you have thought if you had been
asked 15l. for it? 4. If I had examined it thoroughly I would not have
given 41. for it-I have offered it for 51. to two respectable tradesmen.

BENJAMIN BECKWITH. I am a gentleman's coachman, and live in Great
Barlow-street. On the 22nd of Dec. I went to Mr. Warren to ask for the
prisoner's clothes--he told me he certainly had authorized the prisoner to sell
the gig, but he did not mean to give it him-he said he was very glad to get
it out of his warehouse, for fear of the moths getting to his new carriages, and
he had sent it to his coach-house.
