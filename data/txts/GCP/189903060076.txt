JOHN WALLACE (The prisoner). At about 4.30 on the day we were
Arrested, I went to the pier head entrance, walked through the dock, and
round to the north side of Victoria Dock, and went on board and got.
something to eat, and came back to see if I could get a night's lodging on
a barge--that was about 6 o'clock, and about 6.45 I was standing at the
corner of the shed, and a policeman asked me what I was doing-I saidi
I had come to find a place to sleep-that was a little after 7-I saw Bar-
ber coming along-I knew bim pretty well-we walked across for a quarter
of an hour or twenty minutes.No. 20 shed is where the New Zealand
Company unload their ships-that was empty, as the ship had been un-
loaded the day before we were arrested, and the last bit of cargo had been
taken out the week before-what could any man wish to break into an
empty shed for and would a man go with a man like Barber, with a
pair of clogs on his feet?; you can hear him in the daytime, let alone the
nights
Cross-examined. I was standing by the general office about five minutes.
talking to Kelloe-he came there after me, and then Barber—no other
man was there-Barber said good night to us and walked between No.
20 and 22 sheds to get on to the quay--we were a few yards behind him,
and we caught up to him and talked to him-we were standing about a
couple of yards from the shed door-the police were seen for twenty
minutes before they arrested us-Kelloe said, "There is a policeman
there," and I said, “Don't let him see you, or he will kick you out of the
doek ? -that was just before they arrested us—I did not say, "Look
out, here are the police "-I was asking whether a boat was coming off,
.and about some private matters- I had my hand in my poeket, and was
singing at the time and that big fellow rushed round the corner, and gave
me a whack with a truncheon, and I said, “There is no need for you to
do that"-I was there ten minutes, and Kelloe was in a barge smoking--as.
you walk along the road you can hardly help hearing noises, because there
is always wood burning, and a man might think it was the fence breaking
-I know nothing about the breaking of the fence-I heard a sound, but
did not know whether it was burning or breaking; that was half an hour
before I was arrested.
