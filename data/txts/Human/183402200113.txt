Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known Flynn? A. Seven or eight months - I live next door to him - I do not remember the prisoner living there - Flynn's son has lived there - my husband is dead - it is about three months since I saw a man named Maquire - he is in Hertford gaol - he is no relation of mine - he has called at my house - he never passed as my husband - I never lived with him as his wife - he did say when he came out of the country he would marry me, if I wished it.
 MR. PHILLIPS to TIMOTHY FLYNN. Q. Were you ever in custody for an assault? A. Not to my knowledge - this policeman came into my house, as he was told I had some liquor there - he and I fought and beat one another - I was taken to the House of Correction, and was there two months - but I forgot that.
   MARMADUKE LOVELL(police-constable G 144). I went to see for Patrick Flynn, as the prisoner told me he had committed felony, but I could not find him - I disturbed the old man - he got up, and opened the door - I took the prisoner the next night.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you find the breeches? A. No: Flynn got up, and was a good while opening the door for me - I saw a woman there - she asked what I wanted of her son - they were both awake when I went down - Flynn went with me, and shut the door - the prisoner was there - she went out with me; and he said, "Ah, Carroty Kit, is it you?" I did not stop to hear the door shut.(The prisoner put in a written defence, protesting her innocence; and stating that she had induced the policeman to go to the prosecutor's house, suspecting his son who had deserted her was to be found there.)
   JOHN CONNOLLY. I have known Flynn two years - I should be very sorry to believe him on his oath - he has perjured himself once or twice in what he has stated here.
   THOMAS GRANT(police-constable H 76). I have known him more than three years - I would not give him credit - he swore when he saw the prisoner going up the street, that he would transport her if he could - I have heard him say that fifty times.
 COURT to  TIMOTHY FLYNN. Q. Did you say you would transport her if you could? A. Yes.
     NOT GUILTY.
 523.  GEORGE ROWLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of February , 18 pair of shoes, value 2l. 15s., the goods of Walter Pitman , his master; and ELIZABETH CLARK for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   WILLIAM DAMAN(police-constable, G 161). I took the prisoner Clark, and found at her house thirteen pair of shoes - I showed them to Mr. Pitman, and while I was doing that Rowland came in - Mr. Pitman showed the shoes to him, and asked if he knew them - he said he did, and they were Mr. Pitman's property - I then asked Rowland if he recollected my speaking to him in Castle-street, about a fortnight before - he said he did not - I then asked him if he did not go to No. 5, Friendly-place - he hesitated some time, and then said he did - that was where Clark lives, and where I found these shoes - he said he went there to see Mrs. Townsend, which is the name Clark went by.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe it is a bad house? A. Yes.
   GEORGE AVERY(police-constable, G 175). I went with Daman, and found these shoes - Rowland said he did not recollect seeing me in the street.
 WALTER PITMAN. The prisoner Rowland was in my employ as a  confidential shopman- these shoes were brought to my shop by the two officers - I looked over them, and identified them - when Rowland came in I called him round and said, "This is neither retail nor shoplifting, this is wholesale work; whose property is this?" - he said, "It is yours, Sir" - I traced some other shoes to the pawnbroker from duplicates we found in Clark's box - here are eighteen pair - they are all mine.
 Cross-examined. Q. You keep several shops? A. Yes- I think I have some recollection of seeing Clark at my shop.
   CHARLES WALTER. I am a pawnbroker - I have one pair of woman's shoes pawned in the name of Ann Pett - one pair in the name of Elizabeth Pett, and one pair in the name of Elizabeth Townsend, by the prisoner Clark.
   JOHN PAWLEY. I am a pawnbroker - I have one pair of shoes pawned by Clark.
     NOT GUILTY.
 524.  GEORGE ROWLAND was again indicted for stealing, on the 11th of February , 1 pair of leather boot fronts, value 2s. 6d.; and eight ounces of leather, value 1s. 2d. the goods of Walter Pitman , his master.
   GEORGE AVERY(police-constable). I found at Clark's a large leather travelling trunk, and some property in it - I took Rowland at No. 4, Denmark-court, Golden-lane - I found on him a key, which opened a tin box, but I only found some copper money in that - I found some soles and a pair of leather boot fronts, in a hat-box in his room - he directed me to the house, and said he lived in the front room, one pair - I there found a woman who answered to the name of Mrs. Rowland.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. The prisoner was not there? A. No; he was at the station .
   WALTER PITMAN. This leather is mine, and is worth about 4s. - the other I have no mark on - the prisoner had no business to take any to his own house .
 Cross-examined . Q. Is he a workman ? A. No; I can swear to these boot fronts - they are my own cutting ; and I can swear to this piece by a stain on it.
 Prisoner's Defence . I bought that front and blocked it myself .(  Elizabeth Avery, and Joseph James, gave the prisoner a good character.) (See page 255.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
