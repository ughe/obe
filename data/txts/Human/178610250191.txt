 How soon did he go off? - In about twenty minutes, or it might be less.
 How came you to let him go off with your watch? - I thought he would give it me again; he lodged in Little Moore's-yard; the same place that I do; I went home the night I lost my watch, but he did not come that night; I found him at home, and in bed; I asked him for my watch he had taken out of my pocket; he said, he had no such thing; I asked him twice whether he would deliver up the watch he had taken from me; he said, he did not know any thing at all about it; I took him to the watch-house; I never got my watch again.
 Do you say you was not in liquor? - No, I was very bad in my head.
   MARY SILCOCK sworn.
 I have known the prisoner eight years since the Westminster association, he asked me a little trifle for rent; I went to him and asked him to settle it; he told me he would be with me on Saturday night or Sunday; on the Sunday evening he came between four and five; he was very much toxicated in liquor; says he, I cannot pay you, then says he, I am doing very well, I have got my watch out of pawn; and he had a silver watch in his hand; I knew him eight years ago to have a watch; he went away; I thought this was the prisoner's watch; I never knew the prisoner guilty of any improper action; he is a hard working industrious man.
 Prisoner. I knew nothing at all about the matter till I was challenged about it on Monday night to my knowledge; I have not had a watch for some weeks; I have worked hard upon the spot seven or eight years; I never saw any watch; if I had been guilty of such a thing as this, many favourable opportunities would have happened; I had no watch to my knowledge.
     NOT GUILTY.

GEORGE FLATHER was indicted for feloniously assaulting Barnard Holbrook , Esq . on the King's highway, on the 19th of September , and putting him in corporal fear and danger of his life, and feloniously taking from his person and against his will, one guinea, value 1 l. 1 s. and five shillings in monies numbered, his property.
   BARNARD HOLBROOK, Esq. sworn.
 I live at the Grove-house, in the parish of Ealing; on the 19th of September last, I was robbed, about a quarter or half after seven in the evening; I was coming from London, between the Princess Amelia's at  Gunnersbury-house and Little Ealing; a man passed my carriage and rode up to my servant, and ordered him to stop; mine is a post chariot and the box takes off; the servant was on the horse; he immediately stopped; at the same instant another man came up on the other side of the carriage, and one or both of them said, down with your glasses immediately; I put down my glass, and I believe my wife put down the glass on her side also; the man on my side asked me for my money; I immediately gave him all I had in my pocket, which was not less than five shillings, nor more than seven shillings; I took notice the man stroked it over his hand, says he, you have more; I told him, I had not, upon my honour; Sir, says he, you have a watch, give me your watch; I had dropped my watch down into the pocket of the carriage; I immediately jumped out of the carriage; almost under the horse's nose; the man backed his horse, and the other man called out from the other side of the carriage, Sir, get into your carriage immediately, or by God, I will blow your brains out, over and over, and over again; I cannot swear to the persons of either of the men, excepting that the voice of the man at the bar is exceedingly like one of the men; I sent my servant to Bow street the next morning to acquaint them of the robbery, and they sent for me about five or six days
