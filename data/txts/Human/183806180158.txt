 1614. CATHERINE CONNOR was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of April , 1 gown, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Sarah Wilsden . SARAH WILSDEN. I am servant to Mrs. Stevens, of Charlton. I put my gown in the garden, six or seven weeks ago—I do not know how it was lost—this is it—( looking at one. ) JOHN EVERDEN. I am a pawnbroker. On the 27th of April the prisoner pawned this gown with me—I afterwards received information, and when she came again to our house, I had her taken.
   Prisoners Defence.  I met a woman in Deptford Broadway, and bought the gown of her. NOT GUILTY.
 1615. DAVID SHERMAN was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 5 cricket-balls, value 5 s  .; 1 set of skittles, value 4 d  .; 2 rings, value 4 d  .; and 6 keys, value 6 d  .; the goods of a person unknown.
   AARONOUTRED . I am an officer. On the 6th of June, about six o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner, and two other lads, in the Kent-road, going to London—I found these five cricket-balls, and other things, on the prisoner—I took him to the station, and said I would go and see if his father lived at Dockhead, as he had told me he did—he said, "It is no use, I have no father there, the balls were stolen by two other boys, and I was in their company"—I asked him how he came by the skittles—he said, "I stole them myself"—I said, "How did you come by the keys?"—he said, "I turned the tail of a gentleman's coat up, and took them."
   Prisoner's Defence.  A chap  with me found the keys, he had no pocket, and asked me to take care of them—I did not say I lifted up the pocket of a gentleman, and took them. NOT GUILTY.
