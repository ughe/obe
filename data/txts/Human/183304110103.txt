 Q. Did you never tell him to go to Kid, and if Kid would give you a sovereign, you would turn this question just which way he thought proper, as there was no one there besides an old coachman and yourself? A. No, never in my life.
   THOMAS WILD. I live at Noel-house, Kensington. I was on the stop of that house, coming out, on the 18th; I saw Hancock's wife come by; I saw the two omnibuses going at a smart trol - I then saw Kid's omnibus in the road, and the hind wheel passed over William Hancock; I had known him for many years.
 Q.If the horses were not galloping, only trotting, how did it happen that the wheels went over Hancock? A.He was crossing the road, and the wheels went over him; I hallooed out, his wife sereamed, and the prisoner stopped - I assisted in getting Hancock into the coach.
 Cross-examined. Q.Are you a coachman? A. Yes, and have been so for twenty-six years; the prisoner's omnibus was not galloping at all - I did not see him whip the horses; I know, by experience, that when horses get to a piece of fresh gravel, they make an additional exertion to get over it - there was some fresh gravel at this place; the prisoner did his utmost to stop his horses - I did not hear him use the expressions which have been stated; I did not hear a sancy word out of his mouth - Mr. Kid got down and gave what assistance he could; I have no notion of seeing either of these witnesses there at all, but I spoke to Randall after the accident; I knew the deceased - he was a very old man, and I think he was rather deaf.
 COURT. Q. Did you know Mr. Kid before this accident? A. No; when I assisted in taking up the deceased, he said, "Wild, this has done me - I am done;" I said, "Bill, I hope not" - he did not mention a word about the driving so quick; I do not suppose the omnibus had got half a dozen yards from where the man laid before it stopped - I did not see the man knocked down - I did not see him till the hind wheel was going over him; he was rather on this side of the middle of the road, I suppose sixty yards from the side; I suppose the prisoner's omnibus was six or seven yards from the other; I do not know the prisoner's attorney - I have never been examined by him; I believe I have never given an account of this to him - I do not see any body in Court to whom I gave the account.
 Q. Upon the oath you have taken, have you not been examined by the attorney for the prisoner? A. I have seen this gentleman, and told him what I had to say; Gook, the officer, sent me word to attend, but I believe the attorney for the defendant sent for me to attend the Coroner.
   OSWALD BEAL COOPER. I am a surgeon at St. George's hospital. I saw a person who went by the name of William Hancock brought in there; a number of persons came with him - he had received a very severe compound fracture of the left leg, a simple fracture of the right leg, and a fracture of the collar-bone; he died in consequence of the wounds and bruises he had received - I had a conversation with him respecting the accident; I could not point out any of the persons who came with him- he came about eleven o'clock in the morning, in a hackney-coach; he lived till the following Monday, nearly a week; there were no others brought there that day wounded as I have described, but the deceased; nor any person with limbs broken - I was the person who attended there that week for accidents; the deceased decribed to me how the accident happened, and complained of his sufferings - he was perfectly sensible; he was in the accident ward below, which we call the Oxford ward.
   ROBERT BAILEY. I took the deceased to the Oxford ward.
   PETER PARSONS. I saw this accident - I was thirty or forty yards off when it happened; I was speaking to a young man named Randall - I saw the omnibuses galloping, Mr. Kid's particularly - the prisoner was whipping right and left; the poor man was crossing the road, and the near wheel knocked him down.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you bound over by the Coroner? A. I gave my oath before the Coroner, and he ordered me to attend at Queen-square.
   WALTER RANDALL. I was with in thirty or forty yards when it occurred; I saw the man at work on the road - the prisoner drove on the off side; the wheel knocked him down and went over him.
 Prisoner's Defence. It was an accident - I was not galloping, my horses were trotting at a fair pace; what these men have sworn is false.
   JAMES KID. I am the prisoner's master; he has been three or four months in my employ - I was on the steps of the omnibus at the time; one of the horses was not galloping, I could not see the other - the prisoner stopped instantly, got down and ordered a coach, and said he would pay for it; he was in tears the moment he got off the box- both the prisoner and I called the coach; I have a horse-keeper, whose business it is to put curbs on the horses, but he had not done so that day, which made them require more strength to pull them in.
 COURT. Q.What deodand was put upon the omnibus? A.20l.; I attended at the hospital, and saw the deceased several times - I did not see him when dead; the prisoner was not crossing to pass the omnibus which was in front of him, but there was some loose granite on the road, which was a little worn in the centre, and I conjecture he went there for that reason - we were between forty and fifty yards behind the other omnibus at the time.
