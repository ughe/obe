ELLEN COOPER. I am the wife of Joseph Cooper, tobacconist, of 274,
Lower Road, Rotherhithe, which is about fifty houses away from Mrs,
Heldon's I was in the shop on December 16th, when the prisoner came
in about eight, and asked for a packet of "Woodbine " cigarettes-he
put down a shilling, which I tested and found to be bad- I told him 80-
he said, “Give it me back," I said," No, I shall not give it to you back."
- he said, " There is'another one,” and threw down another shilling-he
asked me for his change-he did not mind losing the bad one-I said, “ No,
you wait a minute "-I sent for a policeman, who came-I gave him the
bad shilling.
Cross-examined. When you opened the door I thought you were a
regular customer and that made me look at you all the more.
GEORGE BURGESS (M. R. 11.) I was called to the last witness's shop on
December 16th-I saw the prisoner there—the lady told me he had given
her a bad shilling-the prisoner said to me,"How do I know it is a bad one!"
I gave her a shilling, I don't know if it is the same one "-he said to the
lady "I will give you another one for it "--Mrs. Cooper then gave the bad
one to me--I searched the prisoner, and found Jd. on him besides the lid,
which the lady had given him as change for the good one-he was taken
to the station and detained, and later on identified he was then charged
- he said in reply, "I never went into this lady's shop," meaning Mrs,
Holden's.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am Inspector of Coins at His Majesty's
Mint-this shilling is a bad one-it is a very good imitation.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : "I had only been in
one shop, Mrs. Cooper's."
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he went into the tobacoonist's
and tendered a shilling, but did not know whether the one produced was the
one he tendered or not; and that as to the other case he knew nothing. Guilty.
Eight convictions of felony and four summary convictions were proved
against him. Twelve months' hard labour.
