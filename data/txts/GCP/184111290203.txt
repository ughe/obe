disclosed what I knew of these robberies-in consequence of which I was
taken to Holywell-street by inspector M'Lean, and Evans the warehouse-
man-a parcel was made up like the one I had carried to the prisoner-Evans
carried it, I saw Evans go into the prisoner's house I went in first—the
prisoner was not in-he came in shortly after, and I pointed him out to
Mr. Evans- I was in the shop-I said, “This is the person I sold them
to"- I did not hear the prisoner make any observation-I never said in
the prisoner's presence what I had sold to him-he was taken in custody
-I come from Newgate to give this account to-day.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You are an honest boy in your
own eyes, I suppose? A. No-I am speaking the truth-I consider
myself a boy of truth-I have told a falsehood- I mean I have told the
truth to-day-this is not the first time I have told it-I had some trans-
action with Mr. Coronel-when he questioned me in the warehouse I did
not tell the truth-I did not tell a word of truth before Mr. Coronel—it
was on Thursday night I kept him dodging me from shop to shop-I gave
him the slip by going out at the back-door-I knew the house, and had been
there before I went in at the door and told Mr. Coronel to wait for me, in-
tending to let him remain in the street while I slipped through the back door
-I am not acquainted with many shops at which I can get out at the back
-I saw Mr. Coronel at my master's the night I was taken-I knew him-I
denied ever having seen him in my life--that was the first falsehood I told
about him-every word I said about Mr. Coronel was untrue when I went
to sell these things I told the prisoner the truth-1 did not tell him I was
a warehouseman-I never represented that to anybody-I did not tell the
prisoner these were remnants and perquisites which were to be divided
among the warehousemen-I could not tell it with truth-I never said 80
to anybody else—I said so to Mr. Coronel-there was not a word of truth
in it—did it to make him buy the goods, to make him think me honest.
Q. Knowing at the time that there was hardly a greater rogue going at
large than yourself? A. Yes, that is the truth-the first things I stole
were those I sold to the prisoner-as I got into Holywell-street, the thought
first flashed across me I am very sorry now that I sold him things stolen
from my master-I was sorry when I reflected on it-it was about a week
after I sold to the prisoner that I tried to sell to Coronel - I tried to sell
twelve lengths of figured velvet-I offered them for sale at a shop in New-
ington--they bought them-I went into another shop before that-no
other-the shop where I gave Mr. Coronel the slip is in St. Martin's-lane
I had often been there for my master-I never attempted to sell any
