I got up on Satur day morning, the 2nd of April, at a little after six o'clock—I was the first down—when I got down stairs I found the hail door open, which open into a front yard—they had broken into the parlour and gone out that way—I am certain I fastened the parlour shutters the night before—I missed a tea-spoon out of the store-room, and also the things my master has spoken of—there were several marks of dirt on the window sill and the carpet, as if some body had got in there. JAMES BARRETT. I live in London. I went to my cousin's the pro secutor, on a visit, on Sunday, the 27th of March—I only staid there for the duty—I left a silk umbrella there, which I have since sea at Lambeth-street. THOMAS GEORGE SIZER. I am in the employ of Lawrence Kennedy, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Shadwell. I have an umbrella which was pawned by a female named Sarah Johnson, for 8 s .—I knew her before—I do not recollect her by any other name—it was on Saturday, and I think in the afternoon. BENJAMIN PRITCHARD. I am connected with the police. I went to the prosecutor's house, after the prisoner was taken into custody, to examine the shutter of his house, and brought away a part of it, which I now produce—I compared it with the crow-bar, and it tallies with the marks on it. THOMAS SHELSWELL. On Wednesday, the 11th of April, I went to Elbow-lane, Shadwell, and found the prisoner there; and in a hole in the ceiling of his room, I found this crow-bar, and other implements—I shewed the crow-bar to Pritchard—I found, on the bed, the duplicate of an umbrella, pawned for 8 s ., at Kennedy's, No. 184, High-street, Shad well, on the 2nd of April, in the name of Sarah Johnson—I asked him whose property it was—he said it belonged to him.
