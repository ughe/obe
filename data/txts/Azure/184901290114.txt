WILLIAM HALLOWS. I live at Dockhead—the prisoner was a customer Of
mine. On 20th July, 1846, he brought me this Bill of Exchange for '251..
he wanted me to cash it—I gave him cash and goods for it—I paid away the
bill, it was returned, dishonoured—l went to the prisoner and told him—he
gave me 71., and afterwards 41.—he said he would make up the remainder as
Soon as he could—he said I had no occasion to apply to Mr. Nokes, the
acceptor—I afterward. applied to the prisoner for the rest.

father-in-law had been served with a copy of a writ, I discovered that be had

JOHN NOEE8. I keep the Green Man, at Plumstead. The prisoner is

writing it is—I did not know anything about it till it became due, which I

with it—I gave no one authority to write my name to it.
Cross-examined. Q. You do not write so well as this? A. Not now—I
cannot say whose writing it is—I believe it to be the prisoner's—he married

member calling on him about the time this bill purports to be drawn, that be
