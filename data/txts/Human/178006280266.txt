- I knew Burn very well, I do not know that I saw Price before.
 Did not you know he lived in your neighbourhood? - No; he may have been in my house, but I do not know it.
 Was not you very much frightened? - To be sure I was.
 You only saw him as he passed by you, and you was very much frightened? - I saw him before at Mr. Lynch's, that night at about twelve o'clock. A gentleman desired me to give them a gallon of beer for them not to burn the goods there. I knew him again when he came into my house.
 Might not you be mistaken in the person, when you say you saw him come up the second person? - I was with him in all the rooms of the house; I am sure he is the person.
 Did you first inform against him? - I did not know it till I was sent for.
 Are you sure the three persons at the bar are the persons of whom you have been speaking? - I am sure.
   CHARLES LEE sworn.
 I lodged at Mr. Bradbury's, in Golden-lane; I was there at the time the mob came. I took particular notice of two persons.
 Which two? - Those two (pointing to Price and Burn.)  Thompson I know nothing of. I saw the mob come to Mr. Lynch's, the next door but one; I heard some of them, but I do not know which, say, the next shall be Mr. Bradbury's. I went to Mr. Bradbury's and said, for God's sake! remove what goods you can. I got some acquaintances to help me away with my goods; they had done at about a half after one, then they came to Mr. Bradbury's house; I begged and prayed of them, when they came to Mr. Bradbury's house, and I hung out a blue ribband and told them there was 10 Popery. I brought down a large church-bible I had; I went into the street and held it up and said, This is my religion; here is no Popery; for God's sake do not pull the house down; they made a ring and swore me to my religion; there were pails and barrels full of beer in the street, and they drank very freely; then they went into the house and had liquor at the bar; then a tall lamp-lighter went upon the stairs and said, Blast you, come along, and let us go up stairs and see what is there; the mob followed him. I went up stairs, I suppose I was the third who went up; when they came up the first pair of stairs they saw the bed-steads, the beds were removed. They said Blast their eyes, there is Popery here, else they would not have moved; then they began to break the windows; then they went from the one-pair-of-stairs into the two-pair-of stairs to my room; when they were in my room there was only a case of a chest of drawers left; they went to stamp out the partitions of the case of the chest of drawers; and then they broke the windows and part of the window frames. Price and Burn were into my room and in every room of the house assisting in doing the mischief. I stopped their hands several times and said, they did not know the consequence; they had bludgeons in their hands; there was a door between Mr. Bradbury's lodging room and mine, they burst that open and broke down part of the partition; they they proceeded into the garret; the tall lamp-lighter began breaking the windows in the garret, and then looked out at the window, pulled off his hat, and huzza'd, No Popery! There was a box locked, they broke it open; there was a fiddle in it; there were eight hands, I suppose, in the box at a time. Bradbury took up the fiddle and music books; they broke the garret windows. Then they came down into the club-room, and broke the chairs, and threw the things out of the windows into the street.
 Are you positive to Price and Burn being there? - Yes; they were in the house from the top to the bottom of it. I went away with my wife and children.
