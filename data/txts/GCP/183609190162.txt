JEMIMA ELIZABETH SCRIVEN. I live with my father, who keeps
chandler's shop in Grosvenor-place, Commercial-road. On the 27th all
gust the prisoner came, and bought half an ounce of tobacco, which came
to d.- he gave me a shilling- I looked at it very much, expecting it
bad-it looked as if it had been in the fire, from the colour-I suspected
he had been in with bad money twice before, and I had turned him away
was not quite certain of it-I gave him 11d. in change-he returned me 1
farthing for a pipe-I put the shilling in the corner of the till, and after
was gone I took it out-I was quite satisfied it was bad-I showed its
the person who came down stairs--I marked it, and wrapped it up in paper
and kept it in a box by itself till the 3rd of September, when the prisone
came again for a penny candle, and gave me a sixpence-my mother stond
near the door, and when I went to the till, as if to get him change, I would
to my mother, “Shut the door"-I then said to the prisoner, “You are the
young man who gave me a bad shilling on Saturday night last"-he will
he did not know he bad given me a bad shilling-I said it was him, I wa
certain, and if he would give me a good one for it I would let him go
said he would go and get one, but he had not got one with him, and would
leave his jacket-I went to get a policeman, and gave Brown the shilling and
sixpence, and he returned them to me- I took them to the station-house,
and afterwards saw the Inspector deliver them to Brown-I am sure they
are the same.
THOMAS BROWN. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner, and
received the shilling and sixpence from the Inspector-I saw Scriven give
them to him.
MR, FIELD. These are both counterfeit.
GUILTY. Aged 18.-Confined One Year.
