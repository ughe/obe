1223. SARAH READ was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April,
1 shirt, value 48.; and I flannel-shirt, value 28.; the goods of John
Matthew and another : and JANE MELON, for feloniously receiving the
same, well knowing them to have been stoler; against the Statute, &c.
James FREDERICK SYMons. I am in the service of John and Benjamin
Matthew, pawnbrokers, in Liquorpond-street. In the afternoon of the
5th of April, the prisoner Read came to our shop and pawned an apron-
about an hour afterwards a policeman came and produced a linen shirt and
a flannel shirt, the property of my employers, which I had seen banging
up in the shop close by where Read stood, about a minute before she
came in-she came alone-I did not miss them till the policeman pro-
duced them.
Read. I found them on Eyre Street-bill, and there is mud on the shirt,
where I trod on it. Witness. I do not observe any mud on it.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you observe any one watch-
ing or waiting outside? 4. No.
GEORGE WATERLOO HOLLAND (police-constable G 23.) I took the pri-
soners into custody-in the station they were both sitting together,
shuffling about, and during the time I took Read to be searched by a fe-
male, one of them, I cannot say which, put this linen shirt on the table-
if I have said it was Melon, I cannot stick to it—there was no one there
but the prisoners to put it there the sergeant on duty asked Melon if it
was her property-she said no, she knew nothing about it-I was ordered
to take Read to the cell to be searched by a female-on coming back I
found this flannel-shirt under the seat on which they were sitting, which
put it there, I cannot say-when the sergeant asked Melon if the linen
shirt was hers, she said it was not, it was Řead's-Read said it was ners
the sergeant asked whether it was marked-Melon said it was her sister's
husband's shirt, and Read said it was hers-Read said it was not marked
-Melon said it was marked G. B.-the searcher is not here-it was the
flannel-shirt that Melon said she knew nothing about.
JAMES HARMAN. I am shopman to Mr. Watt, a pawnbroker, in Ex-
mouth-street. In the afternoon of the 5th of April the prisoners came to
our shop in company together-Melon offered this linen shirt in pledge,
but would not take what I offered on it--I observed a flannel under her
arm, rolled up, which I believe was a flannel shirt-I did not look at the
linen shirt enough to identify it again.
NOT GUILTY.
