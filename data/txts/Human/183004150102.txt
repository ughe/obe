 889.  EDWARD PRICE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Whitehead , from his person.
 THOMAS WHITEHEAD. I am a  servant. I was in Piccadilly on the 28th of February, in the evening, and had a pocket-handkerchief in my right-hand pocket; a person asked if I was aware I had lost my handkerchief, and pointed out the prisoner - I ran and took him, and found my handkerchief concealed under his waistcoat.
   THOMAS HEAD. I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief, and told the prosecutor of it, who took him.
   THOMAS TULL. I am an officer, and took him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did it from distress - the prosecutor had the handkerchief half out of his pocket; I said, "I shall have something to eat to night" - I thought I should go out of the country, and I wish to go; I told the prosecutor so.
   THOMAS TULL. He said he had had nothing to eat for twenty-four hours.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 890.  MARY MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of February , 1 cap, value 1s., and 1 necklace, value 1s., the goods of Thomas Davis , from the person of Mary Ann Davis.
 ANN DAVIS. I am the wife of  Thomas Davis, we live at Mr. Burrough's, in Houndsditch; my child's name is Mary Ann. On the 27th of February I sent her to a bakers in Banner-street, for a loaf; she had a cap and a necklace on - she was out an hour, or an hour and a half, and was brought home by a stranger without her cap and necklace.
   SARAH SPICER. I live in Oxford-court, Paul's-alley. I saw a dirty woman pass with a decent child in her hand; I looked over the curtain, and in a few minutes, I heard the child cry - I looked out and the child was crying for Kitty; I said, "Is that your sister?" she said, "No, a naughty woman has taken my mother's basket and bread, and my cap and beads" - I cannot swear to the woman; I asked the child what she had on, and she said, "A dirty cotton gown and a red handkerchief" - that was what the woman I saw had, I believe the prisoner is the woman; this was near a mile from the prosecutor's.
   MARY MOORE. I live in Chequer-alley, which is a half or a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's. I know the prisoner; but I cannot tell how she was dressed on the 27th of February - she lived in a lodging of mine, from the 10th of February till the 10th of March; I found a necklace and a cap in her room, and two baskets which were not owned: the prisoner is single.
   ROGER DIX MOORE. The prisoner left our apartment on the 10th of March, there was one week's rent due; she had given no notice - I went into the room on the 15th; I found the cap and necklace - I called my wife, who took them and gave to the officer.
   JOHN ATTERWELL. I heard of the child being robbed, and in two or three days I heard the prisoner was in Bridewell - I took the child, who said she was the woman; when her time was up, I took her before the Magistrate.
   ANN DAVIS. This is my cap and necklace.
 Prisoner's Defence. On the 9th of March, I was going for half a hundred of oranges; I had but 2s., I got them for 1s. 6d. - and had 6d. left; I saw some caps marked 3 1/2d. each - I bought one; I did not like the cap and I ripped it, and sewed it again - there was a young woman slept with me; I was making the bed and found these beads rolled up in this cap - I hung them up, and put the cap in the cupboard, and when the young woman came home, I asked her if she had left any thing there - she said Yes, the beads and cap; she gave me the cap and hung up the beads.
     NOT GUILTY.
