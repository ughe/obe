CHARLES Tenu. I am servant to Mr. Ravenbouse, of Queen's-build-
ings, Brompton. This glass was pawned for 6s. on the 19th of February,
between six o'clock and half-past by the prisoner, in the name of Adam.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you quite certain of him? 4. Quite-I
never saw him before-he was nearly twenty minutes in the shop.
- Kay. I am a policeman. I took him in charge at the pawn-
broker's-he at first denied having pawned it, but before he left the shop
he said he bought it of a man in Brompton for 88., and pawned it half an
hour afterwards, as lie was distressed.
Cross-examined. Q. Has there been any other person indicted about
this glass? A. No-a policeman of the V division came to Queen-square
to identify a person there, and he said he saw a person coming with a
glass similar to this in the direction from the prosecutor's on the evening

JANE Nutr. I am the prisoner's sister. I was with him on Friday
evening, the 19th of February, when he bought the glass of a man near
the Red Lion public-house at Brompton--the man had on a long dark
coat, that is all I recollect of him-my married sister, Harriet Sherwood,
was with me at the time-I do not know what became of it after he
bought it-we left him, and went to my sister's house at Kennington-
street, Knightsbridge-- he went away with the glass.
Court. Q. Tell us the exact time this happened ? A. It was about
six o'clock in the evening—it happened opposite the Crown and Sceptre
public-house, not a quarter of a mile from Mr. Ravenhouse's, the pawn-
broker-my brother works for Mr. Bigley, a market-gardener-he was
in place there at the time-he does not deal in glass-this did not take
long-the man who had the glass spoke first-he asked 10s, for it-my
brother would not buy it for a considerable time, at last he gave him 88.,
and then the man went away-the man was coming from towards Knights-
bridge-my brother did not pass by the name of Adam, I never heard him
called by that name--he pawned the glass for 68.-I heard that from him
- he did not inquire how the man got it, or why he was selling glass in
a public street-he had the appearance of a hawker-I cannot tell why my
brother should pawn it in the name of Adam-I did not know he had
pawned it in that same.
Jury. Q. Did you meet your brother in the street? 4. I came from
home with him.
- KAY re-examined. He said at the station there was no person
with him when he bought the glass.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Where was it you asked him if he bad any body
with him? 1. The inspector at the station told him he might say any
thing he thought proper, but what he said would be given in evidence
against him-he then asked whether he objected to say whether any body
was with him when he bought it-be said no, there was not, and he told
me in the station afterwards that nobody was with him--be said he had
bought it in the street at Brompton for 88.
(The prisoner's other sister being called, did not appear.)
GUILTY. Aged 22.-Confined Twelve Months.
