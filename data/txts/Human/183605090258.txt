JAMES HUTCHINGS. I am private watchman to Mr. Hunpherys: his name is John. I do not exactly know whether there is an a to his some or not to it. EDWARD THOROGOOD. I do not know Hutchings—I do not know what ward he is watchman to—I know a gentleman of the name of John Has phery—he is proprietor of Haye's Wharf, Tooley-street—I mean the Alderman—his wagons are over London-bridge continually.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Do not wagoners and carters often go on a wharf which they do not belong to? A.  I only know the what belongs to him by taking letters directed to him, to the hotel at the end of our bridge—I do not know what connexion the hotel has with the wharf. JAMES HUTCHINGS re-examined . My master is Mr. Alderman Humphery, and he owns Haye's wharf. On Sunday morning, about quarter past four o'clock, I came on shore from the tier of vessels on the wharf—my business in on board ship and on shore—I came from The Lord Glenlyon  —I found the prisoner concealed between some hogsheads of hams—which were lying on the cast side of Haye's Wharf—he was lying on his belly, and three hams were lying on his feet, between two and three yards from a hogshead which was broken open—he pretended to be intoxicated, but was not—I secured him of stealing these hams—he told me he only meant to take one—I said I should lock him up, and asked him what hogshead was broken open, and where the hams came from—he went and shewed me the hogshead that was broken open, and told me the hams came out of that—the hogshead was lying with the heads pressed out, and the hams strewed about—there ought to have been forty-eight hams in the hogshead, and there were forty-three—those at his feet seemed to correspond with those in the hogshead.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You say he was not drunk? A.
