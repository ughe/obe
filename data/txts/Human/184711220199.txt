SOPHIA CRESSY re-examined.  These are the prisoner's boots—I know them by the heels being broken out in several places, and by moving them about, the house from place to place when he has been gone to bed in the house—the policeman brought them to me before he took them to the field—I think I had them for an hour or two, but I cannot recollect—they were kept in the back kitchen during that time—I did not go with them to the field. JOHN DALLISON BROWN. I am a surgeon, at Walthamstow. On 1st Nov. I was called to the prosecutrix—I found a wound on the left side of her throat extending about three inches—the wound generally was superficial, with the exception of one part, where I presume the instrument had pene trated rather deeper through the muscles, and consequently had produced considerable hemorrhage—no doubt a sharp instrument such as a razor must have inflicted it—it was a clean cut—there was a considerable degree of ex haustion from loss of blood, so much so that for several days I considered her life in danger—that was attributable to the loss of blood, and to her being in an impoverished state of constitution, being a pauper—the wound was over a vital part—it had commenced by the windpipe, and had passed over the carotid artery—if it had wounded her there, immediate death would have followed. JAMES M'CULLOCH re-examined.  I found no footmarks leading from the house—the road comes close up to the house, and there would be no marks there—there were footmarks, but not similar to the prisoner's—there were some similar to Howe's shoes, but I could not tell exactly—they were not found until the 6th, and it was too late then to make any comparison.
