CHARLES MOORE. I am a beer shop keeper, in Bennett-street, Green-
wich. On Saturday, 20th Sept., about half past 9 o'clock, in consequence
of what my little girl said, I went across the road to the prisoner's house-
I went into the front parlour, there were three or four persons there I
said, "There is a fire here, let us have a light "--they had no candle, but
they had a fire-by that time constable 74 came in-I said, “Get me a
eandle, and we will see where this fire is "-I got a candle and went up
stairs into the front room-there was nothing at all in that room, with the
exception of a few rags lying on the further side of the room, towards the
front of the house-I said to the constable, "Here is no fire here" I went
across the room, and opened a cupboard doorI found two bricks had been
removed from the bottom part of the cupboard, and I saw the flames raging
behind the wall that parts the bedroom from the loft-the removal of those
bricks would enable a person to put rags, or anything, into the loft-I found
some rags there I had to cut the partition down and throw some pails of
water through to stay the fire, and then I went into the loft where the fire
Was-I found something like half a bushel of rags and paper, and a butter
firkin all burning together, that part which was towards the passage was
not burned, but all round towards the hole was burned-part of the joists of
the kitchen ceiling were burned--the flame was raging about two or three
feet high when I first saw it; the joists were burning a surveyor who has
seen the house has given me orders to put in two new joists, on account of
those being so burnt-if the fire had not been put out then, in my opinion
it would have spread in less than ten minutes, and the whole row of houses
would have been burnt down-amongst the rage there was some whitey
brown paper, and some pieces of blue cotton shirting- I believe they cor-
responded with some of the rags found in the room--I know the situation
of this loft; the only mode of reaching it from the prisoner's premises was
by the hole that was out through the brick wall of the cupboard-LI saw the
prisoner after the fire had been put out; I met her about twenty yards
from the house. she said, “Mr. Moore, they tell me my house is on fire" I
said, "No, don't frighten yourself, it is not on fire, it is put out;" and she
turned on her heel, and said, "Well, I will go and let them see if anybody
is there ;” and she went to the door--whether she attempted to knock at
the door, or whether she forced it open, I cannot say, but she was falling to
the ground, and policeman, No. 74, caught hold of her arm, and picked her
