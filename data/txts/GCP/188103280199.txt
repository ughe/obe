the station; he wanted to make a statement - I cautioned him, and said
I would take it down; it might be used against him in evidence-he
said, “Very well"-he then made this statement. (Read: "As soon as I
came home my wife accused me of being out whoremongering again,
which I denied, and then started putting up the shutters. There were
some cheese-boxes in my way, which annoyed me. I told her to move
the things out of the way, when she said, B- them; move them
yourself. I then made a rush at her, and attempted to strike her. We
went into the parlour and had a free fight. In the scuffle she took up
the knife and drawded it across my wrist, which was marked longways.
She got the knife from the bacon-board in the shop. In the struggle
she got her stab.") I read it over to him and he signed it-I looked at
his wrist, and noticed that there was a mark on it- I believe it was the
left wrist-it appeared to be a mark from finger-nails, a scratch-it was
fresh, but not bleeding then-I called Dr. Wilson's attention to
it next day-the prisoner also had some scratches on his right cheek,
apparently made with a finger-nail; they were fresh.
Cross-examined. I found the knife on the bacon slab there are two
steps leading from the parlour to the shop- I found the prosecutrix in
the sleeping-room; the prisoner was about speaking while we were
questioning her, and he was stopped by Dr. Wilson-he said “Hush"
--he thought the woman was dying—thie dress is a sort of black stuff,
the petticoat of flannel; there were no stays—the wound on the
prisoner's wrist was about six inches long, extending from the wrist up
the arm-I found some dinner knives in a drawer in the parlour ; none
of those were pointed.
JAMES VANOE YOUNG. I am assistant to Dr. Wilson-I was called by
Fotherby to see Mrs. Davies-I arrived about 1.10—I found a wound
about four inches above the loft groin, with the bowels protruding; I
sent for Dr. Wilson.
