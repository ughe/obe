247. THOMAS ELLIS was indicted for feloniously assaulting Richard
Berry, on the 21st of August, at St. Mary Newington, putting him in fear,
and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 5l.; 1
Watch-guard, value il.; 1 watch-chain, value 58.; and 1 watch-key,
value 6d.; his goods; and, at the time of the said robbery, striking
kicking and using other personal violence.
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution. ro
RICHARD BERRY. I am a wagoner, in the employ of Mr. Coome, of
Linfield, in Sussex. On the 21st of August I was with his wagon, going
from London, home--I started from the White Hart, in the Borough-I got
near the Elephant and Castle at half-past eleven o'clock at night, and had got
into the wagon--the prisoner called me out of the wagon-I got out, and
he begun to abuse me very much, and spit in my face twice, and asked me
how I liked it-I said, "I do not like it much, but I suppose I must put up
with it"-he then knocked me down-I had not said any thing to him-I
felt I could not stand up against him, nor had I any disposition to do so-
he knocked me down with his hand-whether he had any thing in it I can-
not say--my mate was at the horses' heads driving the wagon-there were
three men standing with the prisoner, very near him they stepped back on
the pavement as soon as I got out of the wagon--they remained there
did not want to have any thing to do with it--I am fifty-seven years
old—when I was knocked down I fell near the wheel-I was stunned by
the fall - I understand the three men who were with him lifted me up into
the wagon, but I was notin my senses—I came to my senses directly after, and
found my pocket was turned inside out-I had had no money there I found
my watch and guard weregone--I had had the watch in my hand, just before,

phant and Castle, that he called me out of the wagon--I had not sense
enough to make an alarm, but when I came to myself I told my mate to
