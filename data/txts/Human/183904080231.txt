 1445. HENRY PALMER and RICHARD HALL were indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and DOAN  I conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS HAWES WARREN . I keep a chandler's-shop, in Carrterbury-plaee, Old Kent-road. On the 16th of March, about four o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner Hall came into my shop-for one penny-worth of tobacco—he put down a bad shilling—I tried it, and it was bad—I bit it, and bent it, and gave it to him back—he went away—I directed two of my sons to follow him, and tell a policeman. THOMAS WARREN. I am the prosecutor's son. My father told me to go after Hall—my brother was with me—when Hall got as for as the Crown and Anchor public-house, he crossed the road, and then threw a tobacco pipe at Palmer, and another man, who got away—they walked on some distance, and then the other one went into a baker's shop—I went into the baker's—I did not see the prisoners taken.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you hear the other's name? A.  Smith was the name he gave at the station-house. ROBERT WARRBN. I went out with my brother to watch Hall—he went on towards the Crown and Anchor—I then saw Palmer, and the other man—they approached together, and went on talking about 200 yards, then the other left them, and went into a baker's shop, and the two prisoners went on on the other side of the way—when the little one came out of the baker's shop, he went on eating a roll—he went on the same side as the baker's—he did not join the prisoners again—my brother sent me on to follow Hall and Palmer—I did so, and saw three policemen—I told them something, and they went in pursuit of the prisoners—when the policeman had been spoken to, I saw Palmer look round, and then run—Hall ran after him—they ran in the same direction, and as they ran I saw Hall hold up his hand, and throw something over Mr. Cormack's garden-wall—there is a little bend in the wall there, and it was close at the bend that he threw it.
