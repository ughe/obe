deed ? A. I know no deed but the one the Thompsons had executed

prisoner proposed that it should be 8001.--Newhall said no, he thought two
sums of 4001. would be much better-I know of no account-there was no
memorandum, or any paper written, in my presence.
Q. Did you not just now say there was an account? 4. That is the ac-
count that you have ; that is the one I heard Mr. Newhall had spoken of
the other day, when he said he had an account for 1,2001.-when I was at
the prisoner's, the prisoner, Newhall, and myself were present, and alone--I saw
Newhall writing, but not on such an account as that; it was a small piece of
paper, which he took out of his pocket, the back of a letter, or something-
he was making memorandums, and certain sums were named-the prisoner
said it should be 8001., that would be sufficient-they first talked of 1,000.-
Newhall thought it would be too much, and then the prisoner said it should
be 8001.-Newhall said he thought two sums of 4001. each would do much
better-that was to make the deed I was going to give to the prisoner valid-
Newhall said that a deed without a consideration was of no use-what memo-
randum there was he made himself with his pencil, and Mrs. Blackwell ob
jected to it-she put her head just inside the room; she was not in the room,
but she called the prisoner out of the room-I heard her say something to
him, and he said, "Oh, no, you may trust him with anything," and she went
down stairs again-- I did not write at all, I will swear that-the memorandum
was made with a pencil, which Newhall took out of his own pocket.
Q. About how long were you arranging all this? 4. The arrangement,
whatever it was, was not more than an hour altogether; I dare say it was an
hour-Mr. Newball, in the prisoner's presence, went into the history of the
prisoner's uncle, and the prisoner sat quite dumb-he came to me next
evening, and I said I thought Mr. Newhall's conduct with respect to his uncle
was very extraordinary indeed-he said he thought so too; he never was 80
astonished at anything in his life; and he then again repeated that his uncle
had no funds, and he would have died in the workhouse if it had not been
for him.

number of every note that was paid it was in 50l. notes.
