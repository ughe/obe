   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Is Mr. Molineaux the superintendent? A.  Yes—I was about eight years old when I was at Maidstone about the gown—I was charged with stealing about five years ago—since that I have not been in custody, except for getting drank—I only saw Harman change one sovereign that night.
   COURT . Q.  You were charged with stealing about five years ago? A.  Yes, the young man with me was also taken—the Magistrate dismissed the charge. STEPHEN BROOKS( policeman. ) On the night of 6th Oct. I saw the witness in Child's-lane, Lewisham, with another female, who I did not know, at ten o'clock—she was on the way from the Horse and Groom to Marsden's. MARY EASTGATE. I live at the Ordnance Arms—I know Harman and Cook, and remember them having a quartern of gin at my house one evening, which they did not pay for—it was between nine and ten o'clock early in Oct.—I cannot say what day—I heard of this robbery a few days after.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was not Cook the person who asked for trust? A.  I think it was, but cannot say. MARY ANN FIGG. I live at the Swan, Blackheath-road. One Monday evening, at a quarter past eleven o'clock, I gave change for a sovereign to a person who was with Cook, and who I believe to be the female prisoner, but I have no knowledge of her, except serving her that time.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Ton know it was the other female took the change? A.  No, I know it was the prisoner—I can not positively say it was her—I believe so—I had seen Cook before—I did not suspect them to be women of the town—I do not know Carry  Hawkins.
   COURT . Q.  What makes you believe the prisoner to be the person? A.  I have no particular reason for saying so, only from what Cook said to me about a week or eight days after—I have no recollection of her features. LOUISA HOOPER. I am an apprentice to Mr. Clay, a draper, Broadway, Deptford. On the morning of the 5th of Oct. the female prisoner came to look at a cloak—she said she could not have it until her husband returned with the money—she then went away, and returned in about an hour with five or six sovereigns, which she gave to me and took the cloak to show her husband—she afterwards came back again and paid 2 l . 4 s . for the cloak—I gave her the remainder of the money—next day she came with the male prisoner and purchased a dress—she said she would call her husband to have his opinion upon it—he came, and said he was no judge—the amount of her bill that day was 19 s . 9 d .—I saw her again next day, and she bought a bonnet for 6 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Had you known her for any time? A.  I had seen her before—I believe she was a customer at the shop—I never served her but once before—she then gave me silver—the value of the article she then bought was 16 s . or 17 s  —I do not know of her having had other articles at our shop—I knew where she lived.
