He asked her some questions, and she said three ways two years: he asked her how long her husband had been on board, and she said two years: he asked how long he had been dead, and she said two years; and she said two years to another question: he said he believed she did not come honestly by it. Then I went to Gulliland in St. Catharine's, and he could not get it sold directly; Gulliland, Aickin, and I, went to the Ticket Office, and had it cast up - it was about 30 l. Some time afterwards Gulliland, the Prisoner, and I, went to a notary publick not a great way off, and who should come in while we were there but Mr. Tarver a merchant, and the bill of sale was made over to Mr. Tarver, (Aickin waited at the Woolpack in Birchin Lane.) After it was filled up, Mr. Tarver asked her to write her name, and she said she could not write, but she would set her mark; and Mr. Tarver said it is no matter whether you can write or no, for your mark will hang you as well as your hand writing. Gulliland, the Prisoner, and I, went into the Woolpack, Gulliland had a 20 l. note of Alderman Hankey's, which was payable to Mr. Tarver, and Gulliland brought the money; it was part in Portugal gold, and part in guineas; then Gulliland, the Prisoner, and I, went to the King's Arms Tavern in Lombard Street, and there he gave me the money, I think it was 15 l. or 16 l. - He said he knew the name as well as I, and that he had as much right to it as I, and he had three or four pound gratis besides 3 s. 6 d. in the pound, which he kept for the discount of the ticket; and I was not willing to make words about it, because I knew how it was, and the last day of January I put all of them in my information voluntarily before any body else, - I gave the Prisoner two guineas for her trouble, and I gave Aickin two guineas and a half; the Prisoner grumbled, and thought she had not enough, and I told her I would satisfy her, and I believe I gave her some silver to buy some fish.
 Prisoner. Oh you wicked rogue to draw so many poor innocent people in!
 Sherlock. You drew yourselves in, as well as I drew you in; I think I did very well to bring you all to justice, or else you would have brought me to it, I only catched you first. She has been with me several times since to ask me if I had any more business for her to do.
 Prisoner. Oh that vile man!

   Charles Alexander. There was a person came to prove this will, but I cannot say who it was, I cannot say it was the Prisoner.
   Francis Cuxon. This will was proved by one who stiled herself by the name of Mcdonald - I do not remember the Prisoner's coming to prove this will, but I remember her coming afterwards with another woman to prove a pretended will of another person's.

   Edward Bentham. The money was paid to Thomas Parr, as for Margaret the executrix, and I have marked this probate, as the probate for which I delivered out the ticket.
 Q. How came you to pay this, when the probate was under twenty pound?
 Bentham. The wages were not cast up, if they had I would not have given the ticket; we commonly do cast them up unless we are in a hurry.
 Prisoner. I never did any such thing in my life, and I never will again.
   Robert Brewer and Mary Clement gave her the character of an honest hard working woman.
 Q. to Clement. Did you ever see Aickin or Sherlock at her lodgings?
 Clement. She said she was to go and fetch Sherlock's child to nurse - about eight or nine months ago.
 Q. to Cuxon. Do you recollect that you ever saw the Prisoner at the bar, unless it was at that time that she came with the woman about proving the other pretended will?
 Cuxon. No; never before - it is about six months ago.
