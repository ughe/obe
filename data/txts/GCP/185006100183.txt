Mr. Cockle to Mr. Wiss. Q. Although the prisoner had only dealt
with you once before, yet you knew previously that he was in the same
business? A. Yes; I had known him as having been in that business about
six months--the amount of the order he gave on 31st Dec., was 591. 68.
the preceding order was 161. odd—I believe he paid that in cash in the
counting-house-when he said, "Send the invoice with them," I said, "I am
not the master, the goods must be paid for on delivery I had no master
there at that time-it was a regulation of my own-I had no authority to sell
to strangers, except on cash, without my master being there I had no
discretion-I believe 50 pounds of this was recovered from Mr. Weller--the
prisoner did not say that he had been disappointed by a Mr. Edwards, who
owed him 531. on a bill of exchange-on 1st Jan., I charged the prisoner with
having committed a crime, I sent for the policeman, and he refused to take
him in charge.
Q. Did you not say to the prisoner, in the presence of the policeman, after
some altercation about this, "Very well, mind, I will take care you shall have
the bundle of leather which we took this miorning sent back to you, and then
if you don't pay on Saturday you will have a writ?" . No the prisoner
sent out for some gin, and made two glasses of gin-and-water, one for him-
self and one for me, but I never touched it--the policeman drank the glass
that was made for me--the goods were sent on Tuesday 1st Jan. I went on
the day after I did not find the prisoner there. I did not see Mr. Weller
there any time during that week-I went about eight o'clock each morning
-the prisoner was not taken on this charge; he was in Whitecross-street
prison for debt before we could find him.
