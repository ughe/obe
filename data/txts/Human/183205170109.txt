 1367.  MARY BIGNEY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 1 quilt, value 4s.; 1 sheet, value 1s.; 1 flat-iron, value 6d.; 1 pair of bellows, value 10d.; 1 saucepan, value 2s., and 1 frying-pan, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Elizabeth Pearce.
   ELIZABETH PEARCE. I am single, and live in Payne's-buildings. On the 26th of April the prisoner came and hired a back room, furnished, at 3s. 6d. a week - she said that she had a husband; I gave her clean sheets, and a pillow-case; I saw her go out in about five minutes - I followed her to a pawnbroker's; she returned in a few minutes, and told my girl she was not coming back till the Friday, as her daughter was very ill - on the 28th of April she came again, and I gave her a washing-tub; she slept two nights in the room.
   RICHARD CHARLES MARSH. I live with a pawnbroker. I have a sheet and flat-iron, which were pawned by the prisoner on the 26th and 27th of April.
   SAMUEL THOMAS KNAPP. I have a pair of bellows, pawned with me on the 28th of April.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. My husband said he would give me the money to redeem them.
     GUILTY. Aged 44. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1368.  THOMAS BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of May , 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of Patrick Barry ; 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of Sarah Lee ; and 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of Robert Helling.
   PATRICK BARRY. I keep the  Old Bell, in Bond-street. This pot is mine - I do not know the prisoner.
   ANDREW VALLANCE(Police-constable L 174). I was at the corner of Bond-street, and saw the prisoner go down Duke-street, on the 7th of May, about five o'clock- I followed, and asked what he had got; he made me no answer; I took one pot in this bag, from under his right arm; in going along he took another another pot from his person, and at the watch-house he took this other pot, and put it under the settle.
 SARAH LEE. I  keep the  White Lion, in St. Alban's place. This pot is mine.
   ROBERT HELLING. I keep the  White Swan, in Great Portland-street. This pot is mine.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to see a gentleman in Bond-street, and in crossing I picked up the bag, which I saw laying down; I found there were two pint pots in it - I took it up; one pot fell from it - I put it into my pocket; I went on, and found another pot; I was turning back to take it home, or to find a pot-boy; the witness then laid hold of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 57. - Confined One Year.

 with intent to defraud  William Venables and James Walford.
 SECOND COUNT, for uttering the same.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   RICHARD VENABLES. My brother William, and another, carry on business as haberdashers and linendrapers, in Lamb's Conduit-street- I assist in the shop. On Tuesday, the 3rd of April, a person came and asked for cash for a bill; it was after the gas was lighted - between five and eight o'clock; I cannot be more particular as to the time - I was in the middle of the shop, No. 75, when he came in; we have more than one shop - I noticed his person, and am positive it was the prisoner; he presented this bill to me, and asked for change for it - I told him it was not customary to change bills for parties we were not acquainted with; he said his name was Boot, that he lived at No. 41, Guildford-street, and was well known in the neighbourhood - his appearance being respectable, and knowing that persons living in that street were respectable, I desired the boy Olney to give him the amount for the bill, and saw him do so - I saw him give him a pen, and saw the prisoner writing on the bill, but was not near enough to see what he wrote; he wished me good night as he went out - the bill was presented next day, and discovered to be forged; on the following Saturday I met the prisoner in Lansdowne-place, by the side of the Foundling - he was round the corner, nearest to Guildford-street (our shop is two doors from Theobald's-road); he was going towards Russell-square - he turned round, and looked at me; I immediately crossed, and overtook him at the corner of Brunswick-square - I asked if he would oblige me with his name and residence; he refused - I do not exactly recollect his words; I then took hold of his arm, and said I wished him to walk with me to Navarino House, Lamb's Conduit-street, immediately, respecting a bill which I had; he said he lived over the water, and wished me to go with him to a friend of his, in Henrietta-street, which is in the neighbourhood of Brunswick-square; I refused - he then told me if I did not loose him he would have a knock at me; he immediately struck me; a scuffle ensued; I eventually
