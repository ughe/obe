GEORGE JOHN YATES. I am a tobacco pipe maker, and live in Cornwall.
street, Mile End-road—the prisoner is my brother-in-law. On 16th May, about
half past I I o'clock, I was at my father's house, 25, West-street, and saw
the prisoner there—he was in liquor, but knew very well what he Was
—he was heaving stones at my father's door and shutters—I went Out to get
some beer for supper, and be began using abusive language, and throwing
stones at me—I came back with the beer, and more stones were thrown, and
at last one very large one was thrown, and my father went out to give
prisoner into custody—he ran away, and during the time we were going up
the street we met a policeman, and my father gave him in charge—I was with
my father, and was going to point him out, and during that time the prisoner
came back again, ran across the road, and said, I have you, you b—, take
that," and laughed—I was stabbed in the groin—my bowels were disarra*,
and I was Obliged to put my hand to keep them from coming out—I did not
see anything in his hand—after that he attempted to strike my head, but my
father struck bis hand down, and it came by my ear and inflicted a cut—I
called out to my father that he had a knife, but I could not halloo very loud,
and he did not bear me—I managed to get in doors, and found myself bleed.
ing very much—I bad not quarrelled with him—I have had a few words with
him, but nothing to any effect—I was taken to the London Hospital,
remained there seven weeks—I sometimes feel the effects of the wound in
the groin now—the wound on my head is healed—I believe I was great dan-
ger for some time while in the hospital—I was not able to come out at all
during the seven weeks—I can do a little work now, but I cannot do a hard
day's labour.
