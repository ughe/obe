First Session, 1870–71.
NEW COURT.-Monday, November 21, 1870.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
5. FREDERICK WRIGHT (32), PLEADED Guilty to unlawfully obtain-
ing two sewing machines, with intent to defraud, having been before con
victed of obtaining goods by false pretences-Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
And 6. THOMAS ROCHESTER (30), to two indictments for embezzling
sums amounting to 201. 153., of John Davis Gregory and others, his
masters, who recommended him to meroy-Six Months Imprisonment.
7. JOHN CHARLES GIBSON (39), was indicted for wilful and corrupt
perjury.
M RS. Besley, MONTAGU WILLIAMS, and COLLINS conducted the Prosecution;
and MR. HORACE Brown the Defence.
JOHN SURRIER TIVER. I am a clerk in the City of London Court-I
produce the minute-book of causes tried there-I have the original sum-
mons in Henry Rogers ». David King." on 20th October, for work done,
145. 8d.-I was not in Court-The record was here put in, which stated that
the cause was tried before JAMES ANDERSON, Esq., 0.0., deputy for ROBERT
MALCOLM KERR, Esq., Judge of the Court)- The plaintiff and defendant
both appeared, and judgment was given for the plaintiff for 14, 8d. and
costs, making 178. 8d.
Cross-exanuined. This is signed by T. J. Nelson - I was not
present when he signed it, I know that he is never in Court when the
cases are taken-I know this to be the minute-book-I do not write in it
-Mr. Grant makes the entries; he acts as registrar--he is a clerk in the
office-Mr. Commissioner Kerr is the Judge of the Court.
Re-examined. Q. Is this a book upon which you set 1 A. Yes
moneys are paid in and paid out on this book--there is no other record of
the proceedings--I have been there three years I take the book into
Court every morning, and take it out again after business is over, and
I then find in it an entry of the business of the day.
EDWARD PRATT. I am a clerk to Mr. Buohanan, of Basinghall Street,
solicitor to King & Son-on 20th October I was present when the case of
"Rogers 1. King" was heard-1 produce the original summons-Gibson was
called as a witness, I believe originally at the suggestion of the Judge-
the claim was for work done-Gibson was sworn, and I took notes of his
examination-Mr. Grant, who was sitting as chief clerk, made entries in
this book in my presence-Mr. Anderson, Q.C., presided over all the cases
while I was there.
Cross-examined. Mr. Buchanan did not call the prisoner, and examine
him as a witness for the defence - I do not think he called him, but
he examined him at the suggestion of the Judge, and the solicitor for the
plaintiff cross-examined him the defendant was cautioned at least a dozen
times-be repeated it so often that I did not take it down-I did not
make a selection from his evidence the gentleman who sat on the Bench
also took notes, but they were not so accurate as mine-the Judge said
that he had not been particular in taking notes (The witness notu were
here put in, by which it appeared that the defendant said: "I was foreman,
at least I considered myself as such; my wages were 21. 10x per week.")
