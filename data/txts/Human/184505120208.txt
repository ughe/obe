 1213. JANE TURNER was indicted for stealing 1 table-cover, value 4 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Askew . JOHN ASKEW. I carry on business at No. 110, Blackman-street, Bo rough. On the evening of the 18th of April I was standing on the stain, and saw the prisoner sitting by the side of my counter—she took a table-cover from the counter, folded it, and put it under her shawl—I came down and waited about two minutes—she was sitting by the side of the counter—I told my man to go for the policeman—she then took the cover from under her shawl, and said, "What, about this"—she said she was going to ask the price of it—she had not bought anything then.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  The person who was with her did buy something? A.  Yes, and it was higher in price than this table-cover, it was a hearth-rug, which came to about 5 s . or 6 s .—I cannot say what the prisoner's intention might be—she brought the cover out at once, as soon as I talked about the policeman—I believe her friends are very respectable—I believe it was her sister who was with her—the prisoner is not married, her sister is—I said, "These people don't want to buy a hearth-rug," and they had not bought one then, but after I sent for the policeman they bought one and paid for it—I think I have stated all that happened—I think the prisoner might be tempted to take this cover, from being placed in favourable circumstances for so doing, as the young men were out of the way, except the person who was serving the rug—after I had told the young man to go for the policeman, the prisoner said she wanted to ask the price of the cover—I told her, and she said it was too much—I was willing to sell it, but she would not pay the price.
   COURT . Q.  Do you mean that after you charged her with stealing it; she asked the price? A.  Yes, and I said, "You don't want to buy one;" but she persisted in asking the price—I said it was 6 s .—she said it was too much—it had cost me less than the rug did. NOT GUILTY.
