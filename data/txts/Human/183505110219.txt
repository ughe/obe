JAMES WILD( police-constable R  141.) I was on duty at Greenwich fair, on the 22nd of April, between two and three o'clock I saw the pri soners and another person in company—I watched them, and saw Key go behind a poor man, and take this cotton handkerchief out of his pocket, he passed it to Shearman, who put it in the flap of his trowsers—I told the man he had lost his handkerchief—he said he had—I told him to come with me—I took him about five yards, and took the two prisoners—I opened the flap of Shearman's trowsers—I gave the other one to the pro secutor, but he got away—the prosecutor followed me to the station, and gave his name as Daniel Mahony—he stated that the handkerchief was his.

 1355. CHARLES BABER was indicted for feloniously and burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Thomas Cox , at   St. Saviour's, Southwark , about the hour of twelve in the night of the   22nd of April , with intent to steal.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS COX. I live at No. 107, Union-street, St. Saviour's, South wark, and am a shoemaker—I have another establishment in the New-cut—the prisoner was formerly in my employ for six or seven weeks. On the 22nd of April, I met him with George Rogers, who had been in my employ—I went to a public-house with them—I went to one or two pub lic-houses with Rogers, and to one with the prisoner—I had something to drink, and was a little intoxicated—it might be about eight o'clock in the morning when I met the prisoner—I left the company of Rogers, went home, and went to bed between twelve and one o'clock, as near as possible—I do not think I was in the prisoner's company above an hour and a half, but I was with Rogers till about half-past twelve o'clock in the day time—I left him close by my own home—I was in bed at one o'clock, and slept through the day and the night; and about a quarter to one o'clock in the morning I was awoke by my dog barking—I was certain there must be somebody in the shop, by the barking of the dog which was in the shop—I was quite sober at this time—when I came down, I saw the prisoner in the shop—I had the gas burning—I gave an alarm backwards—I sung  out, "Murder! thieves!"—I could see nobody in the shop but the prisoner—I was quite sure he was there—I was not the first person who got into the shop—I called out backwards, seeing the prisoner in the shop—while I was calling out, Mrs. Cox jumped out of bed, and flew into the shop—we got the street door open, and gave an alarm there—I sent my boy, Jenkins, with some goods to the other shop, in the New-cut, next day, and on his return he made communication to me.
   COURT . Q.  When you saw the prisoner in the shop what became of him? A.  He made his escape through the same place as he had got in at—I found the shutter lying down in the middle of the pavement—I am sure it was the prisoner—he got out through a stall under my window, where I have two men working.
