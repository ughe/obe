 1760.  RICHARD DUTTON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , three iron locks, value 7s., and 22 squares of glass, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Dunstan , and fixed to a certain building of his.
   JOHN WALKER. I am agent to Mr. Thomas Dunstan - he has a house in Pleasant-row, Mile-and Old-town, Stepney; I was at that house on Tuesday, the 26th of August - it had been lately repaired, and was unoccupied; I afterwards received information, and went there - I found the sashes broken, the glass gone, and the locks gone off three doors; I did not find any of the articles - they had been previously taken care of; the glass was worth 1s. 6d. a square.
   GEORGE MASSEY. I am a watchman of Mile-end Oldtown. I was on duty in Pleasant-row on the 28th of August; at four o'clock in the morning I went up to the prosecutor's house, and saw a piece of flannel in the keyhole - I pushed it through, and then missed the lock; I called the other watchman, and he came - I had seen the place all right at three o'clock - the other watchman came, with a lamp-lighter, who put his ladder up and opened the window; he said, "There are thieves in the back part" - I went round, and found the prisoner and another man coming over the back fence; I sprung my rattle, and sung out, Stop thief! the prisoner was taken by a man going to his labour - I followed him, and had kept him in sight, oxcept just as he turned the corner; I took him to the watch-house, and found this chisel on him.
   JAMES FULLER. I heard a watchman's rattle on the 28th of August, opened the window and looked out; I saw the prisoner running, and Massey caught hold of him - I saw the prisoner throw something from his pocket.
 Prisoner. I do not suppose that witness saw me throw anything away. Witness. Yes, I did, and my brother went and picked them up - it was those two screws.
   ROBERT CHRISTIAN. On the 28th of August the prisoner was brought to the watch-house - I searched him, and found some matches and a short pipe on him; I went to the house, and found twenty-two squares of glass in the back room, and four sashes broken to pieces; I found the lock of the street door broken. and lying in the passage - it had been broken evidently by force from the outside - I received these two screws from Fuller; I compared them with the holes in the door where the lock had been taken off, and they fitted exactly.
 MR. WALKER. The key of the street door lock was in my possession, and is here now.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going to my work about half-past four o'clock that morning, when I was stopped by a man, and they took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1761.  THOMAS MOXEY was indicted for embezzlement.
   JOHN MILLS. I live in Edgware-road, and am a baker. The prisoner was in my employ for two or three months; he was to receive money - there was 14s. 10d. due to me from Mr. Wigley, of Star-street, for bread; there was 6s. 6d. due up to the 9th of August, and the difference up to the 15th; the prisoner brought 8s. 4 1/2d. to me, and stated that it was the remainder of that bill, but he had never accounted to me for the 6s. 6d. - here are two bills in the prisoner's hand-writting for Mr. Wigley, one for 6s. 6d., and the other for 8s. 4 1/2d.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Shew me those bills? A. These are them; I took up the prisoner on Saturday afternoon on the 23d of August, between four and five o'clock; he should settle every day - I have only settled with him on Saturday nights for the bread taken out on Saturday; I never to my knowledge settled any of Friday's bills with him on Saturday night; if I have, I have forgotten it; I will not swear that he has not settled the Thursday bills on the Saturday - he was the servant of my predecessor; he was about leaving me to go into the service of a person in that street - I took him because I knew no one else; I do not know that he had a considerable connexion in the neighbourhood - he was going into the service of Mr. Shaw; I owed him his week's wages, which were 15s., but he had monies standing on my books that I had lent him; I had paid him the Saturday night before - Mr. Wigley had been a customer from the time of my going there, and had been of my predecessor; I do not know that he had made a waistcoat for the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. When you apprehended the prisoner on that Saturday, was he told what it was for? A. Yes - I mentioned to him in the morning that he had been keeping his accounts very bad, and before he left me I should expect him to clear up his accounts - he said they were all right; he had had a sovereign of me, and had paid me money at different times - there was 3s. 4d. then left, and I owed him 15s; he had paid me the 8s. 4d., but said nothing about the 6s. 6d.
     NOT GUILTY.
