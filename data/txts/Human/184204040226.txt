   MR. PAYNE called the following witnesses. SARAH LEWIS. I am the daughter of Mr. and Mrs. Lewis, who are now at Manchester. The prisoner came to London from Bristol, the latter end of November, or the beginning of December—I was in the habit of seeing her from time to time—my son and his wife gave her a residence under their own roof, until she saw the father of the child—(she came to London with the expectation of seeing the father)—she resided with them till business was so bad they were obliged to leave London, and then the went to this room—during the time she was with my son, she behaved to the child with the greatest kindness, as far as I saw, and she was par ticularly partial to any one that would do any little thing for it—I never saw her at Easton-street, nor since she left my sons, until yesterday.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  How old was the child then? A.  I think about eighteen months—it was not in good health—it was a delicate baby—she behaved as a natural mother to it—she nursed it, gave it proper food, and did not leave it—she had no occasion to do so at that time—she seemed to understand how to treat it.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  She had no employment at that time? A.  No—if she was obliged to go out, there were persons in the house to take care of the child. SARAH LEWIS. I am the daughter of last witness. I saw the prisoner when she was living at my brother's, and slept with her and the child for upwards of a week—she was very kind to it—she used not to nurse it much because the doctor ordered it to lie on the floor on its back—I saw her when she was in Easton-street—I slept there from the Friday till the Monday—the child was ill—she said it was like that from three months old, that she put it out to nurse, and the people tied its arras down, and made that deformity in the arms—when I saw her in Easton-street she was very kind to it, the same as usual—she bad only been in the room two nights when I went to sleep with her.
   Q.  Can you account for her going out in the morning and leaving it all day? A.  She was obliged to, to get work, and she had but 5 s . a week—I pledged some things for her—from what I saw, she treated the child well—it was ill, and used to pine and cry all the time.
