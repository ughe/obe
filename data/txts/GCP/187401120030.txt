Cross-examined by the Prisoner. You might have said when you came in,

London with the expectation of getting some money-you showed me that
document directly after we had decided the amount-I asked you who you
were, and you showed me the card and the document at the same time-
you read parts of it to me-you were standing by the door when you read
it; it was rather dark, and you went to the door-you held it up to read
it, and I thought you were near-sighted from the way in which you read it
-you took out a pocket-book; you had a great many cards, and you gave
me one I went to the door to look at the ring, to get as much light as
possible we were not talking about lending money on the ring, but about
selling it-you asked me whether I would buy the ring-I asked you if you
had got anything to sell to make up the 101., and you said "I don't wish to
sell anything but the ring"-my father is a pawnbroker-I believe the ring
had W. H. S." inside-after I told you it was not worth 101. I went up to
show it to my father, to have his opinion upon it-you did not say “Will
you give me 101. and take the ring ?" and I did not say “You will sell the
ring, will you l-when my father came down he did not take the matter
