1413. ANN PRITCHARD was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of
March, 1 gown, value 68., the goods of John Spragg and another.
WILLIAM JAMES TAYLOR. I am shopman to Mr. John Spragg and
Richard Parry, pawnbrokers, at Rotherhithe. I hung this gown on the
door within the door-post on the 7th of March-I saw it safe between one
and two o'clock-the prisoner came into our shop between three and four
-the gown produced is ours- I did not see a woman named Hilbert there
that day--she might have been there.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What had the prisoner come to
pledge ? A. A piece of cotton for 4d.-I am quite sure she was there that
day-I have known her for some years-she has always borne a good cha-
racter-I missed the gown the next morning.
Joseph Newman Perkins. I am shopmnn to Mr. Perkins, a pawn-
broker, at Rotherhithe. I produce the gown pledged in the afternoon by
a person named Hilbert-the prisoner was with her.
Cross-examined. Q. Where was the prisoner at the time? 4. In the
shop-it frequently happens that two persons come together to pledge.
NOT GUILTY. aus

FRASER, were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of March, 3 half-sove-
reigns, 2 half-crowns, and 5 shillings, the monies of John Monks, from bis
person.
John Monks. I am a velvet-weaver, and live in Straits-mouth, Green-
wich. On Friday, the 4th of March, about ten o'clock in the evening, I
was in a public-house near London-bridge-I had four half-sovereings in
my pocket--I changed one, and put the change, which was two half-
crowns and five shillings, with the other three half-sovereigns in my right.
hand trowsers pocket, expecting to have to pay a few shillings on my road
to Deptford—I was going by the railway but it had just closed-I spoke
to the prisoner Dust, who is a cab-mau, or he to me, to take me he asked
me how far I was going-I said, to Greenwich, that I was going over
the way, and if he would come with me I would speak to him-he fol-
lowed me to the public-house, and I agreed with him to carry me to Dept-
ford-cross-he went out, came in again, and asked if I had any objection
to allow a young woman to ride part of the way with me--I said, "Why
if it is any benefit to you I have no objection"-he went in and out seve-
ral times, at last the cab was ready-I got in and was very glad to find no
one in it-he drove some little distance, and by a turning as I thought by
St. George's church, he pulled up all at once, in the dark and took in two
females-- I consider it was by St. George's church, but it was dark, and I
could not be on my oath as to the exact spot-St. George's church is in
the road, but he turned down into Kent-street-I had two coats on
clapped them over so that nobody could get at my pocket-the women came
in, sat one on each side of me, and put their hands about me I put their

so hard that I was in pain, and to get her hand away I was obliged to turn
