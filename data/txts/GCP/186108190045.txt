MR. LILLEY. Q. Could you tell with any certainty by merely counting
the money in the till? A. No—the prisoner returned about ten minutes
afterwards, and in consequence of the information Astley gave me, I gave
her in custody-I did not find the sovereign on the counter after I had
thrown it there.

her the shawl? A. No, he has left us there is no glass door at the back

when I threw the sovereign on the counter ; it might have been I have
never found sovereigns pitch on the edge and roll on the other side-I have
not almost persuaded myself into the belief that I put the sovereign into
the till-I said that I endeavoured to persuade myself so, but I did not
succeed-when the boy told me that he saw the prisoner pick it up I did

means; and being a customer, I did not believe she would be dishonest
enough to do 80-I did not even ask her for it-I had not paid her for the

she had a sovereign of my mistresses in her possession-my memory is not
in a haze in the slightest; what I have stated I believe to be the truth-1
believe she said that she had got a sovereign belonging to her husband sewni

the sovereign or not, as I know her as a customer-she prioed the shawl

unknown to her husband, and that her object was to buy a shawl with it
the young man left the service a fortnight ago, through illness; it had
nothing to do with this, I could find him-the prisoner's husband was
somewhere in the street while she was pledging the boots-he came in
before she left, and she gave him the halfpence-I do not remember his

is the sovereign ; but give me the shawl first"-about a quarter of an hour

which I took possession of, and would not give up-- she told me she knew
nothing whatever about my sovereign, when she said that she had sewn it
up in her dress to buy a shawl.
