GUILTY, Aged 36.— Death.
 1280. HENRY WILLIAM ALBERT was indicted for forging and utter ing an indorsement on a foreign bill of exchange, with intent to defraud James Butler M'Rae .
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. JAMES BUTLER M'RAE. I carry on business as a wholesale warehouse-man in Ave-Maria-lane, Ludgate-street, in partnership with my brother John. I know the prisoner; he calls himself a wine-merchant, and does a sort of agency business—he was indebted to us—he came, on the 20th of Dec., and offered me this bill of exchange—( looking at it )—he said he would take part goods and part cash for it—I told him if he would leave the bill, and call again, we would make inquiries about it—he said it was a very good bill, a bill as good as Rothschild's, as good as the Bank of England's—I saw him again on the 22nd of Dec—we had then made inquiries, and said we would take it—we gave him part goods, and a cheque for the remainder, which was 34 l . 5 s . 11 d .—I saw him put his name on the back of the cheque when he received it.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  You have known him some years? A.  About three years—we had bad dealings with him before—there is an old account standing now—I always understood him to be a German; he did not speak English very perfectly—after we were in possession of the bill, Mr. Wil kinson came to us—the prisoner brought a friend with him to talk about the bill, and I refered him to our solicitor—his friend promised to go to our solicitor the following morning—he said he could explain the matter—I accompanied Mr. Wilkinson to the prisoner's lodging, in Watling-street, one morning—I did not hear where he had removed to—I saw a servant, who said he had been gone out some minutes.
