Robert WILSON policeman, N 103.) On 21st March, about half-pat
one o'clock at night, I was on duty in Holloway-road, and met the last wit
ness-he had had a cut, and complained of being very much kicked and
bruised--there were twenty people or more about him - another officer wa
with me-we took two persons in charge-Empy, who was behind them, cried
out, "Now for a rescue ; now give it the b- b--s!”-be struck me.
I was knocked down, struck after I was down, and five or six of them
jumped on me-they burt my thumb, got my staff from me, and rescued the
prisoners- Empy was among them, and had stones in his hands--I WA
pelted--they struck S 290 with my truncheon-they pelted me for a long
time-I and several others were struck-two constables came up when I wa
down, and were the cause of my being got up again-the mob went towards
London, into Brand-street-I never got my staff back-I lost my hat-it was
afterwards found-I knew Pickett before.
Cross-examined by Mr. Platt.Q. Who were the two men given in
charge ? A. Two Irishmen-nothing was said about the prisoners till after
wards- I am sure Empy threw stones--he did not strike me till I got him in
charge--be then struck me over the shoulder.
Cross-examined by Mr. PARNELL.Q. Does Pickett live in that neigh
bourhood ! A. I believe he lives in Sutton-gardens, Chalk-road.
WILLIAM STEPHENS (policeman, N 434.) On 21st March, about hall-past
one o'clock, I was in the Holloway-road-Pickett pointed out a man to me
- I took him-the mob pelted me and rescued him the prisoners were there,
both throwing stones-the mob went into Brand-street-I did not see where
Empy went-Pickett went into a house on the left side I was in the mob
about ten minutes.
Cross-examined by Mr. Platt, Q. Had you ever seen Empy before!
4. No.
FREDERICK WILLIAM PRITCHARD (policeman, N 400.) I was in the Hal-
loway-road, and saw the mob—I cannot say whether I saw Empy-Pickett
was taking an active part, throwing stones-the mob cried out, "Down with
the police stone the b !" I took out my rattle, it was
instantly broken by some of the mob.
John DONOVAN (policeman.) On 21st March I was in the Holloway.road
I saw a mob, and two constables on their backs-Empy came towards me
with a stone in each hand, and aimed one at me---I did not see him throw it
for l got away in the mob-I saw him taken in a house in Brand-street
and have no doubt he is the person.

JOHN OAKSHOT. I am a surgeon, at Highgate. On 21st March,
between two and three o'clock in the morning, the officer Pickett was brought
to me--he had two wounds on the head and a cut on the eyelid, about an inch
and a half long, nearly through the lid, which I think was from a stone-- the
wound in the temple was an inch and a half long, it was through the skin to
the bone-- there is nothing beneath the skin there--I should say it ww
caused by the blow of a stick-it was very much contused and out-he was
confined to his bed two days-it was a fortnight before he ebuld do duty,
he could not wear his hat-the wounds were not dangerous--the stone need
not bave been thrown with foree-a slight blow would split the integumes
there.
