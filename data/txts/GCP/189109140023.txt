HENRY WHITTINGTON. I am second clerk at Westminster Police-court
-on 25th July I was present when Mary Walsh was charged with being
in unlawful possession of a hamper, a bottle of champagne, two bottles
of claret, a pencil-case, and other articles—this is the charge-sheet
(produced)-the prisoner was called as a witness, and sworn-Mr. De
Rutzen was the Magistrate-the prosecutor said that he was butler to the
last witness, and had never seen the prisoner before, and that the cham-
pagne and claret were the same as used by his master; that he had
charge of the wine, and that which was unused after dinner was kept in
a cellarette-she then interjeoted that he gave them to her--he continued,
"I did not give you the postcard produced; the address on it is my
lady's" -Mr. De Rutzen remanded her to July 29th, when the prisoner
gave further evidence- he said, "I made a mistake last Saturday, saying I.
