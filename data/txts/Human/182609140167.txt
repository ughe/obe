1759. PHILIP HARTLEY was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS PEARCE. I am a lamp-manufacturer, and live in Ludgate-street. The prisoner was my shopman, and collected money - he never paid me 25s. 6d. on account of Mr. Vansalles; on the 10th of August I asked if he had received it - he denied it, but said next morning, that he recollected having received it; he then put 25s. 6d. on the desk, but I would not touch it.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ANDREWS. Q. Does he keep a book? A. Yes - I have not got it here.
   HENRY SHARP. I am in the employ of Mr. Vansalles; I do not know the prisoner, but cannot say I paid him any money.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1760.  PHILIP HARTLEY was again indicted for the like offence.
   THOMAS PEARCE. On the morning of the 11th of August, when the prisoner was leaving my counting-house, I found a piece of paper in his hand-writing - I have not brought it; in consequence of that, I went to Stevens and Co., about 2l. 17s. 6d., and had the prisoner apprehended; he had never accounted to me for that sum; his duty was to give it me immediately he came home.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long had he been with you? A. Ten months; on the Monday after he was apprehended, Cape, who is in my employ, said he had a sum of Stevens and Wood's to offer me; he is the prisoner's brother-in-law; I have looked into his book; this sum is not entered; he has several small sums to collect - perhaps 2000l. a year.
   GEORGE TOBB. I am clerk to Messrs. Stevens and Co. On the 22d of July I paid 2l. 17s. 6d., and took a receipt from the person - he wrote it in my presence; I gave him two sovereigns and 17s. 6d. - it was about the middle of the day.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you a recollection of the person? A. No.
   CHRISTIAN ACOLT. I am a patrol. On the night of the 11th of August, Mr. Pearce gave the prisoner in charge.
 Mr. PEARCE. This receipt is the prisoner's writing. I swear he never paid me the money; I cannot say whether he paid me any money on the 22d of July.
 Prisoner's Defence. (Written.) The truth is, that having accidentally omitted to enter this sum, I did not like to acknowledge my inattention, and as Cape was going out to collect, I got him to pay the amount, as if he had received it.
   - CAPE . I am in Mr. Pearce's employ. On the evening the prisoner was taken, he gave me 2l. 17s. 6d. to take care of; he afterwards told me he had received it for Stevens - he gave it me after he was in custody; he never told me why he gave it me - I gave it to his sister; I told Mr. Pearce I had some money of Hartley's, and asked what I was to do with it - he had not told me not to give it him - I was in the room, he put it into my hands, and said, "Take care of it."
 Mr. PEARCE. I gave the prisoner 30s. a week; I find, by my cash-book that he paid me no money on the 22d; I never made an error in my entries; Cape occasionally collected for me.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined One Year.
 1761.  GEORGE FULLERTON was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July 6lbs. weight of cock-metal, value 3s., and 16ozs. of bell-metal, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Edmund Pontifex , and others his partners, his masters.
   FRANCIS YEOWELL. I am in the employ of Pontifex and Co. On the 29th of July I saw some cock-metal separated from the mass, and laid in a place, as I supposed, to be taken away - the prisoner was in their employ; when he was gone to dinner, a constable was sent for - he went after him, and he was taken - this parcel was found on him.
   JAMES WOOD. I am in partnership with Edmund Pontifex and another - we live in Shoe-lane; we have above a hundred men in our employ, and a great quantity of metal; the prisoner had 1l. a week.
   THOMAS GREEN. I am a constable. On the 29th of July I took the prisoner, just as he left the premises, and found on him this 7lbs of metal concealed in his trousers pockets, his frock-pockets, and about him; he did not account for how he got; he said it was through distress.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 1762.  FREDERICK SNELLING was indicted for embezzlement.
 MR. ANDREWS conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT GRAY. I am a coach-proprietor at the Boltin-Tun, Fleet-street. The prisoner was a clerk in my office, and received money from passengers, and from Eastland particularly; he did not account to me on, or after, the 19th of June, for fifty sovereigns received from Eastland.
 Cross-examined by ADOLPHUS. Q. He had settled with you for other monies? A. Yes; he was about leaving me - I had given him notice about a fortnight before; he enters in a book all the money he receives; there is none entered to Eastland after the 16th; Eastland is joint proprietor of the coach, and part of the loss would be his.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ANDREWS. Q. Was the prisoner your servant exclusively? A. Yes; the payments are made to me, and I divide the profits afterwards.
   WILLIAM EASTLAND. I am one of the proprietor's of the Tunbridge Wells coach. On the 19th of June I paid the prisoner 50l. in sovereigns and half-sovereigns; the proprietors settle at the end of the month.
 Cross-examined. Q. You drive and collect the money? A. Yes, and pay to Gray; I told the prisoner to make a memorandum of it, but he has not.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
