   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Why do you say far from it? A.  In appearance—I do not know what she had to live on, or to eat—I am a wine and spirit finer—I took my meals with a person named Hoare, who kept the first-floor, but I slept in the first-floor back-room alone—the child used to cry from the time the prisoner went out till she returned—I cannot say that it was constantly crying, but for five or six hours, and that continued for nine or ten weeks—I never spoke to the prisoner about it—I complained to the people of the house, how scandalous it was for the child to be so neglected, and at last went to the overseers of the parish—I did not like to interfere in it before, being a man, and I was not aware that the child was neglected so much as it was—she appeared flighty in her manner when she was singing up and down stairs—I have not heard that she was insane for three months about the time of the child's birth—I heard the man who passed as her husband state, that she was very bad at the time of her confinement. SARAH THRIFT. I am married, and life on the second-floor at No. 14, Easton-street, Clerkenwell, in the next room to the prisoner—she lodged there with the child for about nine or ten weeks—I have heard the child cry repeatedly—the prisoner used to go out between six and seven o'clock in the morning, and return about the same time in the evening, and some times went out again after that—I believe no one was left in the room with the child while she was out—I have tried about three times to look into the room when she has been going down stairs, to see whether there was any one there, but she always shut the door—she did not take the key with her when she only went down stairs—I did not open the door to look in—when she went away for the day she locked the door, and I had no means of looking to see what was the matter with the child—I never re member any person being left with the child when she went out—I never heard her speak kind to it, whenever she did speak she called it a little devil—that was when she has been going into the yard—it was a female child—I remember the parish-officers coming to the place—the child was in the room—after the first gentleman had been the prisoner came to my door, and asked if I knew any thing of a little girl that would take care of a child—I told her I did not—I afterwards went with the child to the workhouse.
