1766. MARY ANN VARLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 1 1/2lbs. of sugar, value 1s., and 3 shillings, the property of John Morgan Thomas, her master.
 JOHN MORGAN THOMAS. I am a  grocer, and live in High-street, Shadwell. The prisoner came every day to my house to char, and was paid weekly; I lost three shillings from my till - she had access to that once a week, generally on the Monday or Tuesday. when she cleaned the shop. On the 12th of August I watched my till, which contained 2l. worth of silver and some copper; I placed myself in a sngar hogshead and heard the prisoner go to the till - I heard the money move; there was another person in the hogshead with me, and he could see through a hole what was done; I then went out and accused her of taking money - she said she had not; I sent for a constable, who found on her 3s. in silver, 8d. in copper, two 1/2lbs. and three 1/2lbs. of sugar, which I believe to be mine.
   DAVID THOMAS. I was with my master and saw the prisoner go to the till, and put her hand into it; the money was afterwards found on her - the silver was marked.
   THOMAS AMES. I am a constable. I was sent for and found these three shillings on the prisoner, and this sugar.
   JOHN MORGAN THOMAS. These are the marked shillings which had been in the till, and these found on her, make up the right money which had been in the till.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 27. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 Before Mr. Justice Littledale.
 1767.  WILLIAM CHAPMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 1 pocket-book, value 4d., 3 sovereigns, 17 shillings, and two 20l. Bank notes, the property of Henry Towell , in his dwelling-house.
   MARY ANN TOWELL. I am the wife of Henry Towell - we live in John's-lane, Clerkenwell, and keep a cheesemonger's shop, which has an internal communication with the house. I have known the prisoner twenty years - he came to our shop on the 9th of August, a little after nine o'clock in the morning - he had been in the habit of coming there from time to time; on the 9th of August, I went to a drawer in the parlour at the back of our shop, and gave my husband some money out of a pocket-book - there is a door from the shop to the parlour - it is not a glass door; I do not know exactly what money there was when I went there, but I left two 20l. Bank notes, 3 sovereigns in gold, and 17s. in silver - I am certain that is what I left; there were some shillings among the 17s., and it was all in a small pocket-book; when I took the money out, the prisoner stood on the left hand side of me, and my husband on the right; I cannot exactly say how near he was to me, or that he saw what I was doing; I gave the money to my husband that I took out - he and the prisoner were to go to buy some goods for the shop- they went out together about half-past nine o'clock - he returned with my husband between ten and eleven; he soon went away then, but returned between five and six in the evening - he did not stay long then, but he came again between eight and nine in the evening, and went away a little after twelve; I do not remember his saying anything to me, but my husband was speaking to a man about making him a grate, in the yard, and the prisoner wished me to go and tell my husband not to let the man make the grate, because he could get one much cheaper, but I did not go; I saw the prisoner shut the door between the shop and parlour once or twice in the course of the evening, and he had shut it in the afternoon as well; in the course of the evening, I went to the drawer in the parlour to get change for a sovereign, that was a little after ten o'clock - the money was all right then - I saw the pocket-book there; I left the sovereign in the same drawer, but not in the pocket-book - I took the change for the sovereign out of a bit of rag in one corner of the drawer; I have a bacon-knife, which I saw the prisoner take into the parlour between eleven and twelve o'clock - he did not stay in the parlour above two minutes, if he did so long - the parlour door was then a little way shut, but not close; no one came into the shop but the customers - none of them went into the parlour; when the prisoner went away, he said nothing particular - he bid my husband good night - I do not know that he bid me good night; my husband shut the shop up between twelve and one o'clock; my husband had not been in the shop all the evening - after the shop was shut, I saw the bacon knife on a table close by the drawer in the back parlour; I went to unlock the drawer, and it was unlocked - I had left it locked; I did not observe any marks on it; I missed all this money - it was all gone; it was between twelve and one o'clock; the bit of rag with the rest of the money was safe - this was on Saturday night, we expected the prisoner the next day; he had promised to come, but did not.
