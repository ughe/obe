 203.  EDWARD RICHARD BURCH was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of October , 1 brooch, value 10s., the goods of Richard Clay.
   ELIZABETH LEATHERDALE. I am the wife of John Leatherdale. The prisoner was in my husband's employ; I placed a brooch in my back room, where the prisoner slept, and a gold pin, on the 18th of October - I missed them the next morning; I found the pin in the prisoner's bed; the brooch I could not find - the prisoner said he had not seen it; it belonged to Richard Clay - it had been repaired by a jeweller, and sent to me.
   JOHN EASTERBROOK(Police-serjeant K 25). I saw the prisoner on the night of the 25th of October - I watched him to a house in Turner-street, Ratcliff-highway; I went and searched him - I found on him the duplicate of the brooch; I asked where he got it - he said he found it a week before, but in going to the station he said he found it in some saw-dust at his master's; when he got to the station he told the inspector he found it in the cellar - he said he did not return because his master made so much bother.
   THOMAS EELY. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have a brooch, pawned on the 24th of October, by the prisoner; he was in a fustain frock - this is the duplicate I gave him.
 Prisoner. Q. You said you could not swear to me? A. Yes, but I have collected my thoughts, and can swear to you now.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. They take in several lodgers, and I used to get duplicates from the sailors, by giving them gin for them; Mrs. Leatherdale said she did not know whether she left it on the table or on my bed - they might have fallen on the floor; I found it in some saw-dust.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined One Month.

 204.  ARTHUR OWEN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 3 pairs of shoes, value 7s., the goods of Walter Pitman.
   GEORGE ROWLAND.I am in the service of Walter Pitman, a shoemaker. About two o'clock in the afternoon of the 9th of November, I saw the prisoner and another about the shop - I saw the prisoner take three pairs of shoes off the nail at the door, and hand them to his companion, who made off with them - I followed; his companion escaped, but I took the prisoner, without losing sight of him; he dropped a ticket, which had been attached to the shoes - I am positive of him.
   THOMAS BOLTWOOD. I received him in charge.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in Chiswell-street - two women called out that a man had run away with the shoes; I saw a man running from the shop with them, but lost sight of him; I turned back, and met the prosecutor - the Policeman actually saw the thief run by him.
   GEORGE ROWLAND. He turned round as if to strike me, and the ticket fell from his coat.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Three Months.
 205.  HENRY PATTERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of November , 1 sovereign, the money of John Gee.
   JOHN GEE. I am a servant to Mr. North, of High-street, Shoreditch. This sovereign was in a box in my bed-room; I missed three within about a fortnight - the prisoner slept in the same bed with me; I spoke to Mr. North, my master, about it, and he marked four sovereigns: on the 23rd of November, in the evening; I got up first in the morning, and went into the shop - I returned afterwards, and missed one sovereign; I always missed one at a time - I named it to my employer; he got an officer, and the sovereign was found in the prisoner's box, with the mark on it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How long was he in your master's employ? A. About two months; I never quarrelled with the prisoner - his principles were rather different to mine; I corrected him when he used to swear - we never went to blows; his box was kept in the same room as mine - I left the key in my box, as I always did, and after the money was marked I wished to ascertain how it went; I generally went to bed after the prisoner - I do not know where the key of his box was kept.
   WILLIAM GLOVE. I am an officer. I was sent for - I searched the prisoner's box in the bed-room, and found a sovereign; I asked him if he had any money - he said Yes, he had a sovereign; I asked where he kept it - he pointed to a part of the box, and said, "Here," and gave it me.
 Cross-examined. Q. He at once said he had it? A. Yes; I searched the box, and could not find it - he then pointed to a drawer in his box, and showed it to me.
   JOHN GEE. This is the sovereign which my master marked.
   ALEXANDER NORTH. In consequence of what Gee told me I marked four sovereigns; I did not see Gee put them into his box - I know this to be one of the sovereigns I marked.
 Cross-examined. Q. You were not present at any part of the transaction? A. I went up stairs with the Policeman; the prisoner took the key out of his pocket, and I think opened the box himself - he took out two trays; the Policeman began to search the bottom of the box - the prisoner said, "I have only one sovereign," and produced it; I believe it is his first offence.
 JURY. Q. Did they quarrel together? A. No, except on religious questions; the prosecutor appears very religious, and the prisoner quite different.
 GUILTY. Aged 19. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
