Flynn. Q. Did not a pint of milk come every day, one in the morning, and
again at 2 o'clock in the afternoon * X. It was a half-pint in the morning, I
never knew of any more.
COURT. Q. Can you say it was not a pint 1 A. It did not look like a
pint-I can't say whether any came at 3 o'clock-the man used to call for
the can at 2--I believe they had some milk on trust, and I heard
the milkman say he would not let it run-he did not leave milk after
that he only left it for a week or a fortnight, and then he would not give
her any more credit-the prisoner used to look after the child when she
was sober, but she was continually drunk, and then she neglected it one
day she went out and left the man at home, and he did not know what to
do with the children, and he was very much bewildered with his workshe
left him alone with the children on two occasions, and he did not know
what to do-he seemed good and kind to the children when he was there
he was fond of them, as far as I could see- he beat the little boy sometimes,

EDWARD HEARNE (Policeman G 247). On 5th October last I was called
to the prisoner's house by the last witness- I went up, and saw the baby-
it was cold and wet, covered over with a few dirty rags—there was a
feeding bottle by the side of it, with about a tablespoonful of sour milk in it
I waited there nearly two hours, when the female prisoner came in, intoxi-
cated-1 spoke to her about the condition of the child; she said the child
was fed well, and she always attended to it-I told her that was falseshe
went down stairs, and took the child out for a short time, and returned
home again-I sent for the parish authorities, who came and saw the child
I took the male prisoner into custody last Wednesday month I charged
him with the manslaughter of his child-be said he always gave the female
prisoner plenty of money-there was no one in the room when I went to
see the child-the boy was out in the street at play; the prisoner was
continually drunk Flynn. I did not see the policeman at sil; he never

GEORGH EUGENE YARROW. I am the medical officer of St. Luke's
Workhouse on 10th October a child was shown to me by one of the
nurses in the infirmary-it was very much emaciated, and had been much
neglected, and there were sores about it, from negleot-it weighed 6 lbs
it was about three months old - the weight of a healthy child of that age
should tell or 12 lbs. - it took its meals very ravenously at first-it gradu.
ally sank from exhaustion--it had brandy and water and milk, and other
stimulants; it sank gradually for a week, and then died on the 18th made a
post-mortem examination--in my opinion, the cause of death was exhaustion
from neglect and privation--the sores on the child indicated that it was
not changed sufficiently-the child being left wet and cold for hours would
produce what I saw-its organs were healthy--the sores would produce a
certain amount of exhaustion. Flynn. There were no sores about the baby
-I always attended it before I went out, and washed it properly I left
milk with it before I went out. Witres. They were sores that a woman
attending a child with ordinary cure would have seen - I am not quite sure
how long the child had been at the workhouse before I saw it-I saw it on
my first visit-it was suffering from want of food at that time.
