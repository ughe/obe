HENRY RONALD STEVENSON. I am a scrivener, of No. 3, Bond Court House,
Walbrook- I know the defendant--on 27th March, 1874, he called on me
and wanted to borrow some money-I let him have 4001. on three farms
Crowhill Farm, Poppy Lot Farm, and Shrubhill Farm, near Downham,
Norfolk-I asked him if he had any bill of sale or any encumbrance upon
his property-he said he had not-I advanced him 4001., and have the
cheque here-before I parted with it he signed the bill of sale and the
inventory; I produce them; it is to secure 4751 to be repaid by three instal-
ments on 27th of May, June, and July-he did not pay any of them; he
only made one payment of 2501. in September, about six months after the time
- he still owes me 2251.
Cross-examined. I went down to Norfolk; I was there nearly two days
with the defendant-I found him in the occupation of a large farm there!
I went over it with him and his son-I have no idea of the number of
acres, it was a very large place ; he told me it was near 2,000 acres alto
gether, but I have no means of judging at all of that-I saw a great quantity
of turf-I took a second bill of sale on 26th January, 1876; that was in
reality to secure this--this was unregistered the reason I took the other
was that on 23rd or 24th January, or perhaps the 25th, Mr. Mitchell tele-
graphed to me that he had got an execution in, and I went down with my
solicitor to see what it was about, and then the second bill of sale was taken
to secure this, because he thought he might have other executions put in
--when I went down there I looked very carefully round the farm I don't
know much of growing crops, but other things I know pretty well; I think
what I saw was worth between 4,0001. and 5,0001.- I did not hear till
went down the second time that he was engaged in a heavy law suit last
year; I read an account in the London papers that he had an arbitration
case with Canon Sparkes, of Ely, his landlord; I believe all this trouble was
occasioned by the failure of that law suit-I don't believe he would have
had any reason to call his creditors together but for the execution that was
put in by the clergyman.
Re-examined. I did not go to the meeting of creditors; I went to the
solicitor's office--my solicitor had a notice that he was a liquidating debtor
and could not meet his engagements-when I saw him on 25th January he
never told me anything about the 1121, he had obtained from Mr. Cochrane-
I never heard of his bill of sale till Mr. Cochrane sent a person round to
make inquiries—I heard of the execution on 24th or 25th January-it was
for 2111.
