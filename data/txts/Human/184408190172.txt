   COURT . Q.  What makes you say the prisoner did not go out? A.  I am sure he did not—we went straight in to him at the Ship, and as we were com ing out to go home, the policeman and the prosecutrix ran across—the police man said, "Is this the man"—she said, "Yes," and he collared him—my husband and I had not been in the tap-room—when we went back, we stood in front of the bar—the landlady was cross because the last pot of beer was not paid for—the prisoner was in the tap-room—the door was open, and we could see through—there were several men there.
   JURY . Q.  Are you intimate with Hawkins? A.  He has worked with my husband—the Ship is near the suspension-bridge—there is another public-house between them, called the Duke of Sussex—when we left the prosecu trix she went with the policeman towards the Broadway, and we went to the Ship.
   COURT . Q.  Is the Ship in the Broadway? A.  No; the Ship is on the left-hand side; it lies towards the water, just as you go to the toll-gate. WILLIAM TRIGG re-examined.  The prisoner ran by me about 100 yards from the Ship, and the prosecutrix came up to me about a dozen yards from the Ship. HENRY WALE. I am the toll-collector of Hammersmith-bridge. I know the prosecutrix. On the evening of the 5th of Aug. she came to the bridge, put her right hand in her bosom, pulled out a purse, or a bag, and paid me with a halfpenny—she was drunk—she did not speak to me—Mrs. Knight came running after her, and asked if there was a young person gone on the bridge—I said yes, and allowed her to go after her—the policeman came, and Knight, and Cook—when they went off the bridge they did not pay any toll, as I thought the policeman had got the prosecutrix in charge—she was in front of him—I saw her go on to the Broadway, which may be about 200 yards from the toll-gate—the Ship may be about 40 yards from the Broad way—about a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes afterwards, Mrs. Knight came and told me Newman was taken for robbing the prosecutrix, who went on the bridge—I knew Newman, and always knew him to be an honest per son—he belonged to the Ship formerly—I cannot say how long he has left—he lived at Mr. Chapman's before he went to the Ship.
   COURT . Q.  In conducting the prosecutrix from the bridge to the Broad way, would the policeman have to pass the Ship? A.  Yes, within ten or fifteen yards—near enough for a person standing in the door-way to see her. JOSEPH WALPOLE.
