ELIJAH TALBOT( Policeman M  263). On the morning of the 8th of August, at 11 o'clock, I was on duty at St. James's Road—the prisoner and another man passed me walking quickly—I followed the prisoner; he crossed the road and ran when he saw Gaertner, another constable, speak to me—I followed him to the corner of the Old Kent Road, and then being out of breath Gaertner followed—the prisoner was brought back—as we ran he passed 324, St. James's Road. JOHN GAERTNER( Detective M ). Talbot made a communication to me—I followed the prisoner who was running and arrested him in Peckham Park Road—I said "I am a police officer, what were you running for just now in St. James's Road?"—he replied "I have not been in the St. James's Road, neither have I been running"—I took him to Mrs. Goodhall's shop; she identified him as having been with another man who had attempted to pass a bad half-crown—I saw the prisoner pass 324—I produce seven counterfeit half-crowns given me by Mayhew; I showed them to Mrs. Goodhall, she picked out this one—it has signs of having been bent—it was then marked.
   Cross-examined.  I searched you at the station, there was nothing found on you—you told me that you did not know the other man, that he asked you to go and have a bottle of ginger-beer with him, haying met him close to the shop. HENRY OLIVER. I am a clicker, and live at 324, St. James's Road—I have a little garden in front of my house—about half-past 11 on the 8th of August I saw a packet under the portico; I picked it up, it con tained these three half-crowns wrapped up—on the grass I saw another packet, containing four half-crowns, also wrapped up in paper—I gave them to Mayhew. FREDERICK MAYHEW( Policeman M  261). Oliver made a communica tion to me, and handed me these seven half-crowns, which I took to the station and gave to Gaertner; four in one packet and three in another. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These seven half-crowns are all bad—the one that has been tested is not from the same mould as any of the others—there are no marks of the coins on the paper, which is like the professional paper used.
   The Prisoners Statement before the Magistrate. "It was not me, sir, it was the man that asked me to have the ginger-beer. I don't know who he was."
   The prisoner in his defence stated that he met the other man, who offered to treat him to some ginger-beer, and that he went into the shop with him, but that not liking the man's company after the affair about the half-crown, he started running at soon as he got outside the shop in order to get away from him. SARAH GOODHALL( Re-examined by the Court ). The other man tendered the half-crown, and asked for the bottle of ginger-beer. GUILTY*†— Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
