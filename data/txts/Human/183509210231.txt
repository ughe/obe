THOMAS CORNEY ESQ. I am Deputy of Broad-street ward. The Bank is situated there—I can hardly say that it is left to me to settle the times of the omnibuses there, but I generally do—the officers of the ward seem to be under my direction—I wrote a circular letter to the omnibus pro pritores in 1833, in consequence of the great confusion at the Bank, and the Governor of the Bank having written to me that he should put the Bank Act in force; I saw him, and told him I would enter upon it, and make some arrangement, which I did—I was instructed by the police committee to make some arrangements, that times might be set by the proprietors, and kept by the time-keepers—I invited the proprietors to meet me, to make some arrangements which I prescribed; one was, that no longer should any cabs  be allowed there, as we found a great many robberies took place, and there were thieves taking advantage and robbing the public—the officers were ordered to remove any persons of that description—a few of the par ties met me, but by no means half their number—Mr. Shillibeer was then in opposition with them, not subscribing properly to their funds—I could not get them to terms but by threatening to put the Bank Act in force—they said, if I could get the twenty-nine proprietors to agree to having fifty-nine omnibuses, they would be satisfied, if I pointed out an offi cer in whom the ward would have confidence—one was appointed, who was to pay no respect to any one, but to summon every person who did not keep his three minutes—that went on for some time, till Mr. Bray began to make a disturbance, and threatened that he would continue it—he said it to me, I belive—I summoned him before the police-com mittee—he said that he should not belong to their committee—he would run his omnibus and take time, and I had him summoned before the police-committee—he had not had time then, our officers being strictly
