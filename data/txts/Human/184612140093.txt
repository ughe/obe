JOSEPH RONO( police-constable. ) On the morning of the 9th of Dec. I was on duty, and about four o'clock I heard a cry of "Stop thief!"—I saw the prisoner running a minute or two after, and Hampton running after him—he came running towards me—I stopped him, but found nothing on him—he was sober—I consider he feigned drunkenness—when I got him to the station he was not drunk—he was told the charge was breaking a fan-light, and made no answer to it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  About twelve o'clock I met a person who was going to Birmingham next morning; I went and stopped drinking with him till half-past three, then went straight down Union-street to Price's night-house and had some gin and milk; as I passed the end of Wellington-street, I saw the policeman; I stopped at the end of Friar-street, considering whether I should go to my sister's or go home; I determined to go to my sister's; I crossed the road, tumbled against the door, and threw two pieces of glass down; the man called out; I thought I had broken a window which was over me.
