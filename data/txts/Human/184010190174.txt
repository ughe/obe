   Cross-examined by MR. CHANMBERS . Q.  Is that your estimate of the value, or what you hear from others? A.  Both. WILLIAM BENHAM TOMLINSON. I am a pawnbroker in Upper George-street, Bryanstone-square. I know both the prisoners—Wilson brought a suit of clothes to my shop, and put them down to my young man—I was standing alongside—he asked 2 l . 10 s . on the coat and trowsers—the young man asked him if he would take 2 l ., and asked him his name and address—I think he said, "Thomas Wilson, No. 40, Nutford-place"—I sent Richardson, my young man, to the house—he returned, and said, in Wilson's presence, that no such person was living or lodging there—Wilson then said, "If you don't like to take them in, give me them back, they are my own property"—I said, "I shall not give them up unless you give a better account of yourself, as you have given a false name and di rection"—he then went out—about fire minutes after, Lilly came with Wilson, and said, "D—it, what do you stop my things for? they are mine"—I said, unless they could give a better account of themselves, I should not restore them—on that they both went away—in the course of the day I sent the clothes down to the police-station in Marylebone-lane—Lilly came twice next day, and demanded the property—I said they were at the station-house, and if he went and gave a good account, they would restore them to him—he said he should not do it, and on the fol lowing day I was served with a notice of an action—I had examined the clothes—they were duffers,  made up for the express purpose of plunder—I understand jewellery—I should say these stones, in the articles produced, called emerald and rubies, may be manufactured for about 12 s .—they are spurious.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  The value of such articles as these are known in the trade? A.  No, they are not—the way these are passed off on us, is, they watch the opportunity for the master to be out of the way, and come at twilight to deceive the young men—I could tell the value of them in a moment—my shopmen could not tell so well, par ticularly if they came in the evening—this was in December, 1838.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You said in Wilson's pre sence, that he did not live there, what did he say about that? A.  He said "What is that to you? I have a right to state what I like"—he did not come afterwards, and tell me the articles were his brother's, or relation's—they said nothing about being relations—I had to pay 26 l . to compromise the action—I have not given up the clothes—I sold them afterwards for 35 s .—he made me pay eight guineas for them—the only error I committed, I found I ought to have given them both into custody, and not doing so, subjected me to the loss of the action—( looking at the seal produced to the prosecutor )—this gold seal is genuine, but it is loaded for the express pur pose of holding the stones—I should say it is a fair gold seal—Hall marked seals are not common—I should say gold in this state, at the low est calculation, is worth 43 s . or 45 s . an ounce—I see no solder in the handle—I believe it to be all gold, but I am not a manufacturer—I should say the ring or loop is all gold, and the handle also—it is not quite genuine, because that means pure gold.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  What is a standard gold seal of that size worth? A.  About six guineas.
