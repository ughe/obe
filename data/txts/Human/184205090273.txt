MARIA PARKER. I am single, and live at Austin's house. On the 5th of April, between three and four o'clock in the morning, before daybreak, I heard a knocking at the street-door—I knew Warren had got a key—Logden was stopping with her, I know, because I have seen her there—she was at home that night, because I heard her there—I did not see her—I could be positive it was her—I heard somebody in the room, and I bad' seen her on the stairs with Warren frequently—I cannot say she ever slept there—I saw her come down stairs next morning after ten, and spoke to her—after I heard the knocking at the door, I heard somebody get over the wall of the yard, and then heard somebody open the street-door within, and let somebody in—I could hear the footsteps of more than one person come in—I heard them go up stairs—I lodge in the parlour—Warren had the front room, over the parlour—I heard them walking about over my head—shortly after ten in the morning I saw the two male prisoners there—they came down stairs by themselves, and went out—I had never seen them before—the females were not with them—the shortest prisoner said, "Good morning"—I said, "Good morning"—they each bad a bundle, or ft coat, or jacket over their arm—my landlady called me up stairs about twenty minutes after that—I went up, and Logden was there—Mrs. Austin went to turn down the bed to see if the bed-clothes were right, and found these things there—I saw them found—next day we found a bag contain ing some skeleton-keys, in a drawer, which the constable has—there was all sorts of keys.
