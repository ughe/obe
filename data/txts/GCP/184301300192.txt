Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
778. THOMAS WHITEHEAD was indicted for stealing, on the 18th
of Jan., 2 trusses of hay, value 58., the goods of John Miller, his master; and
BENJAMIN SPICER, for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to
have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
John PARSFIELD. I live in Church-street, Lisson-grove, next door to Mr.
Miller, who deals in corn and hay-Whitehead has been in his employ for the
last month-on Wednesday evening, the 18th of Jan., I saw him take a truss
of clover bay out of Mr. Miller's barn, and put it down against Spicer's door,
who lives in Duke-mews, next door but one to Miller's stable-Spicer was
standing at his door at the time-I made my way towards them--they appeared
to be in conversation-I said to Whitehead," Tom, I think I shall go and get
a pint of beer"-Whitehead then made his way from Spicer's, and I could
neither see the clover, nor Spicer-I did not go to get any beer, but went and
stood at the corner-I then saw Whitehead come again out of the barn with a
truss of bent hay on his shoulder, which he took to Spicer's door-I then saw
Spicer bring the truss of bent hay from the door up into Earl-street-I saw
Spicer's man with the truss of clover hay on his back- I went to Mr. Miller's
shopman, and told him what I had seen.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did you see Spicer at the door?
A. No, I do not know where he was then I was standing at the corner of
Duke's-mews, and in about five or six minutes I saw Spicer with the bent
hay on bis back he came from the door with it I did not see him take it
from the door-I did not see him take it up-I had seen Spicer's man with a
truss before that.
MR. PAYNE. Q. How near was Spicer to his own door? 4. About five
or six yards off.

information-Whitehead came into the counting-house while I was there
said I was going to take him into custody-he asked what for-I said, "For
stealing a truss of clover, and a truss of bent hay"-he said, "Why you asto-
