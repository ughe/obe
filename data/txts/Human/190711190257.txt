The two men also got off the tram and Brown invited them in. We were in there about three-quarters of an hour. The two men had some drink which Brown paid for and he gave them another shilling each. After leaving the "Swan" we went to my house in Milford Road, about a quarter of an hour's walk. The two men were behind us in the Whitmore Road, about 100 yards. When we got to my house Brown asked my permission to invite them in and I gave it. They came in to supper. Before supper Brown asked prisoner to get some beer; he went out and got a gallon. Then prisoner went with Brown to the corner shop to get the supper. When they were on the point of leaving prisoner said to Brown, "Who is going to start the game?" The two men and Brown went into the passage and closed the door. I could see what happened. Brown went to shake hands, but prisoner struck him on the nose; then they wrestled with him but could not get him down. I could see from the light in the kitchen. I went to Brown's assistance, and got knocked about. I had a black eye. I could not say whose hand struck me. Besides the scuffle I could hear the jingling of money. The men then went off. My daughter ran to get assistance and I attended to Brown. My daughter is now very ill, and unable to attend here. I found Brown bleeding at the nose. He said he was injured in  ternally. He had to go to the hospital next day, and remained there as an in-patient for a considerable time. He afterwards had to attend the hospital for another seven or eight days.
 Cross-examined. I first saw prisoner in, the "Peacock" at about four o'clock. On that day I was not at work. I do not recollct what time we left home on that day. We came out with the prosecutor. We might have had one drink before, we met at the "Peacock." We went to cash the advance note at the "Peacock," and Smith was there. We were there about three-quarters of an hour and had two drinks, or it might have been three. We left there and went to the "Swan." I am quite positive prisoner came into the "Swan." Brown paid the fare of the two men and paid for the drink at the "Pea  cock." I cannot say how long we stopped in the "Swan." It was quite dark when we got home. They lift my house at about a quarter to 10; they had been there about three hours. There is no light in my passage, but when the kitchen door was open there was a full light from the kitchen, and I could see out there. The next time I saw prisoner he was coining out of the van at the Stratford Police Court. There were two other men coming out of the van. I did not see an old man get out. I was standing in the yard outside the Court. Two or three other people were standing there. I will swear that Mrs. Smith was not standing there. Mrs. Smith was nowhere near me until we were ranged to go up the passage way to identify. I can not say whether she was in the yard. She did not make any communi cation to me before we went up to identify. I went up and down the line twice before I identified prisoner.
