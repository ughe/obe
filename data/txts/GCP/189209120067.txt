JAMES SWINDLE. I live at 17, Lyndel Road, Stockport Road, Man.
chester-I am & warehouseman in employment - I got a taste for
literature, and have written a play and some poems and ballads-in
June, 1885, I received this comununication from the City of London
Publishing Company, successors to the Charing Cross Publishing Com-
pany-I answered it, and received this lette of 4th August, signed
u J. Bidney Tomkins, Secretary”-accordingly. sent some M8., including
the play, and I received this acknowledgment, dated 10th September,
signed "J. W. Tomkins.” (Stating that a favourable report had been received
from their roador, and requesting a payment of £40 as his sharo of the cost and
risk of publishing, etc.) - some friends in Manchester assisted me by sub-
scribing £40 to enable me to publish my works, and on 9th September
I remitted £20, for which I got & receipt signed by Tomkins
-on 10th September I sent 10s., on 30th September £10,
on the 12th November £5, and on 16th February £2 158.,
for all of which I had receipts signed by Tomkinson
3rd March, 1887, I sent an order for £1 58.; I got no acknowledgment
for that all this was money found for me; I am receiving weekly
wages, my book was never published—I repeatedly wrote about it,
and the matter was ultimately taken up for me by my Manchester friends
-it was put into the hands of a solicitor, an action was commenced, and
tried on the 17th February, 1892, at the Royal Courts, before Mr.
Jastice Grantham-it was an action against Morgan and Tomkins, the
City of London Publishing Company and the Authors' Alliance; I was
the plaintiff--none of the defendants appeared; they were not repre-
sented by counsel or solicitor-a verdict was returned in my favour for
£40, and £200 damages, or the return of the MS. and the costs of the
action-I never got any fruits of the judgment, no costs, no MS., nor
the £40; somebody paid my own costs for me-the facts of the case got
considerable publicity in the London and provincial press.
Cross-examined by Morgan. I paid £39 altogether; the acknowledge
ments were signed by Tomkins--as far as I know you had not a penny
piece of the money--it did not belong to me, but to my subscribers; it
was not sent for copies of the book - I cannot tell that the Authors'
Alliance put my work into the printer's hands-I received some proofs
from the City of London Publishing Company, I believe, to the extent
of eighty-two pages, or about a quarter of the entire work.
