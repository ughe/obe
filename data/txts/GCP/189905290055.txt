Re-examined. If we had known on April 24th that the prisoner had
received £2 78. 8d., and only paid in 138. 2d., we should not have
accepted it-if he gives credit to those not having ledger accounts, he
does it at his own risk--we do a very large business with small people, I
suppose, some 70,000 customers, independent of our large trade of clubs
and hotels.
ANY LOCKYER. I assist my mother at 26, Edward Street, City Rond,
in a general shop--we have mineral waters from White's, and have from
time to time bought them of the prisoner on April 22nd we paid him
58. 8d.--we did not owe him anything else--on the 29th we paid him
£1 2s. Ed. in discharge.
Cross-examined. That represents goods supplied during the week, to
we had received credit from the prisoner.

the prisoner has been in the habit of delivering goods—en April 24th he
left goods, value 168. 3d., which I paid, and he signed the book.
HENRY WOOD. I live at 32, Nile Street, Hoxton, and deal with

for which I paid him, and he signed.
GEORGE PACEY. Í keep the Eagle beer-house, Radnor Street, and deal

CHARLES HEATH. I am cashier at White and Sons--the prisoner has
to account to me after going his rounds—I was on duty on April 24th-
be had to account to me for goods value £4 68. 2d., being the difference
of what he took out and what he brought home-he paid me 13s, 2d.,
and the balance was booked against him as what is known as a short-on
the 29th he had an account for £4 178. 7d, for goods, and there was de
ducted from that an allowance for empty bottles, so that he had to account
for £3 198, 7d. --- he did not pay anything in.
Cross-examined. I know nothing of the settlements on a Monday-I
have no book called a van-book; I do not know that he had one I am
not the only cashier- there are travellers attached to each yard.

head foreman to go in the ordinary course to see that everything was all
