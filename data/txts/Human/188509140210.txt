BENEDICT ZIBACK. I live at 143, Barking Road, Canning Town, and am a soda water manufacturer; the prisoner has been an assistant of mine nearly six years, and on 23rd July we agreed to part—about 2 o'clock on the afternoon of the 27th I went into my shop and saw the prisoner—he asked me to give him a written character—I told him I had not re ceived one with him, but I would give him a verbal one—he walked deliberately out of the shop and said, "Governor, me and you have not done together"—about half-past 9 on the same evening I and my wife were sitting in the shop—it is wide open from the street—my face was towards the door—the prisoner came in and said "I have come for that character"—I said "George, I did not receive one with you, but I will give you a verbal one"—he then deliberately pulled a revolver out of his right-hand breast pocket—I heard the click and saw it pointed at me, and said "George, don't make a fool of yourself," and moved my head; as I said that the shot went by my ear—he said "Take that"—when he fired it I was sitting on one end of the seat, and he was standing at the other end—the seat is 4 feet long—I did not hear the breakage, but when I looked round I saw a looking-glass behind me broken, and the shot at the bottom of the corner, and it took a piece out of the wood—he turned round immediately and cocked the revolver again, I heard the click distinctly—my wife leaped at him and held him, and threw both her arms round him—directly I got up I said "Let him go, I will face him"—I was afraid he would fire at her—cries of "Police" and "Murder" were raised, and the police came—I did not see the bullet found. THOMAS READ. I live at Canning Town and am a bootmaker—on 27th I was passing Ziback's shop and heard the report of a pistol and saw the prisoner come out of the shop with a revolver in his hand—I took it from him—this is it ( produced )—he made no out-of-the-way resistance. THOMAS PLANT( Policeman K  609). I was called to take the prisoner into custody—he said, "All right; take me out of this"—he was sur rounded by a mob—he went quietly—I took him to the station and the prosecutor charged him with shooting him—I told him the charge—he said, "All right, I am glad I have not hurt him"—nothing had been said by or to him in my presence about Mr. Zibach not being hurt—this revolver was handed to me at the station—I examined it—it was loaded in four chambers out of five. HENRY DAVIS( Police Sergeant K ). I assisted in taking the prisoner in custody—on the way to the station he said, "I have worked for this man for five years and he has refused me a character; I did it in a passion"—shortly afterwards he asked, "Is he hurt?"—I replied, "I believe he is not" (I had not then seen Mr. Ziback), upon which he expressed himself glad—I afterwards went with Detective Dickers to the shop—I saw him find a bullet—I saw a mark on the wall about three or four inches in length and three-quarters in depth—the looking glass was penetrated through the front and a corner broken—the mark was exactly benind the looking glass and underneath it—the bullet was found six or seven feet from the mark on the wall at angles with the looking-glass.
   The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. "I was guilty of firing the shot, but I was so excited I hardly knew what I was doing."
