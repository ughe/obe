1361. THOMAS WATTS was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of
Match, 1 frock, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Robert Nutter: and MARY
WATTS, for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been
stolen; against the Statute, &c.
CATHERINE NUTTER. I am the wife of Robert Nutter; we live in Cot-
tage-row, Poplar. On the 23rd of March I missed a frock which hung
out to dry- found it again at Mrs. Smith's prisoner lived in the
neighbourhood.
Cross-ezamined by MR. LORD. Q. Do you know the frock A. Yes
-I missed it about ten o'clock at night.
SARAH ELIZABBTH SMITH. I live in Robin Hood-lane, This frock
was brought to my shop by Thomas Watts, between eight and nine o'clock
at 44 night-he said, 64 Mrs. Smith, will you buy this said,
Where did you get it ?"-he said, 44 My mother got it from where she
was at work, and sent me to sell it"-I said, 44 You rascal, you stole it; if
you don't fetch your mother I will give you in charge'ath went away,
and in a quarter of an hour his mother (the female prisoner) came, and
said it was all right-that she had had it given her where she was at work,
and if I would let her have a few halfpence on it she would thank me
I did so, and on the Monday a man and his wife came to buy some things,
and the woman said the frock belonged to a neighbour of hers.
Cross-examined. Q. He fetched his mother 2 A. Yes-I had known her
some time-] knew no harm of her.
GBORGE MURRAY (police-constable K 25.) The female prisoner was
brought to our station-house on the Monday, and some time after a boy
came and asked for her-I said, 64 Where is your other brother 2"-he
said, Outside"-. went out and took the prisoner Thomas Watts-h said
he was along with Bill Perry, and he gave him the frock to sell.
Cross-ezamined. Q. Have you seen Perry since A. Yes, but I do
not know where he is now.
THOMAS WATTS-GUILTY Aged 4.-Recommended to mercy.
Whipped, and Discharged,
MARY WATTS-NOT GUILTY.
