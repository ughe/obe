1506. SAMUEL EATON, breaking and entering the dwelling-house of
William Upton, and stealing 17 yards of cloth and other articles, value
201. 13.; his goods.

MR. W.J. PAYNs conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM UPTON. I am a tailor, of 48, Tabernacle-walk, in the parish of
St. Luke's. On 6th June I went to bed about 11 o'clock at night-the
window of my cutting-room was shut to with a catch-the shutters were sbut
to, but not fastened--about 8 next morning I was aroused by the ringing
of a bell— I came down, and found my cutting-room window open, and the
back-door also-the cutting-room is on the ground-floor-I went into the
front shop, and found the window cleared- I missed about seven yards of
cloth, six yards and a half of velvet, some kerseymere, some umbrellas, coats,
and other things—they bad been safe the night before I had seen
the prisoner.
Cross-eramined by MR. COCKLE. Q. Have you any partner? 4. No
I have lived in that house thirteen or fourteen years--I have four children,
my wife, and myself, sleeping there--I have been without a servant for a
short time-my daughter, my wife, or myself, close the house - I closed the
cutting-room window on the night of 6th June-I think I was the last person
up, I am not certain - I cannot say that I had closed all the doors and
windows that night I know they were closed-I know I closed the cutting-
room window-it had been open during the day-I put the shutters
to myself.
Joan MACKMIN. I am Mr. Upton's brother-in-law, and live at 51,
Tabernacle-walk. I am a linen-draper-about half-past two o'clock on the
morning of 7th June I heard a great noise in the street-I opened my bed.
room window, and saw some people in the street-I asked what was the
matter, and they could not tell me-I opened my back window, and saw
some goods at the back of my premises, which turned out to be my
brother-in-law's.
