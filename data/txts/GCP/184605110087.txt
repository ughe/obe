pieces good money.
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know the half-crown you just put down

George CHRISTOPHER CAINES re-examined. I marked it-this is positively
the half-crown.
CHARLES Terry. I swept out Mr. Bagley's shop on the 10th of April-
I removed the canister, and found a piece of newspaper with seven hall
crowns in it-they were outside the canister on the floor--the canister is
round, and the corner is square, and these laid in the corner behind the can-
ister--the moment I found it I just opened the paper, and gave it to Mr.
Bagley.
Cross-examined, Q. How near the door were they? A. About one yard
from it-there is a chair by the side of the canister-I am a porter in the
neighbourhood-I was not there every day from the 4th till the 10th.
JAMES BAGLEY re-examined. These are the seven half-crowns-I marked
them, and have kept them ever since.
Me. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These
eight half-crowns are all counterfeit, and have all been cast in the same
mould.
