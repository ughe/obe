I stopped just in Colebrook-row, as he turned the corner from a cry of Stop thief! the prosecutor and some others came up; I saw him drop the starch behind him; he tried to drop it into the river; English was then close behind me, endeavouring to interfere; I said, "You had better be off - I shall know your face again;" he went off.
   EDWARD SPENCER. On the night in question I saw Just running, with a blue paper parcel in his hand; I saw English near the shop - he put his foot out, and tried to trip up Mr. Jones.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BODKIN. Q. Where were you standing? A. At the corner of Camden-street, opposite the shop; they ran from me - it was between six and seven o'clock in the evening.
   THOMAS COPE. I am a constable. I took Just; I saw English in the crowd - he afterwards came to the office.
   JAMES ISAACS. I was in the waiting-room at the office - Just was brought in; English came in, and Just said to him, "You know, George, when I left you, I had been to take a watch home; there was a crowd, and a cry of Stop thief! they took me, and said I thieved this parcel;" English said, "You are lucky, by G-d;" Just said, "Go and tell my old woman where I am:" English then walked round, and looked at the bills; one Wilson came up to him, and said, "You had better bolt or run, George, or else you will be grabbed:" he was going out - I said, "Your friend wants you," and he was detained.
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. A harnessmaker; I assist my father, who is a watch-house-keeper.
   THOMAS COPE re-examined. Wilson was only charged with being in the crowd, and saying, "As a gentleman, I will see you do your duty."
 JUST'S Defence. English was not in my company. I had been to take a watch home.
 ENGLISH'S Defence. I was taking a walk, and happened to run against Mr. Jones - I begged his pardon: soon after a friend of mine was taken to Hatton-garden - I went there, and was committed.
 JUST -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 ENGLISH -    GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 1914.  WILLIAM LUSH was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 1 pewter pot, value 2s., the goods of William Dodds.
 WILLIAM DODDS. I  keep the  Dover Castle, public-house, Weymouth-mews, Portland-place. On the 9th of August I lost a pot, but did not miss it till I saw it at Bow-street - this is it.
   WILLIAM BERRYMAN LUSH. The prisoner is my son: I live in St. Giles', near the workhouse. I met him with this pot, and asked him how he came by it - he first said it was thrown at him by a boy, and then said it was given him to go and sell; I gave him in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Whipped and Discharged.
 1915.  PHILIP BENJAMIN MARSHEA& GEORGE McDONALD were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Alexander Cowan.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am a shoemaker, and live in Warner-street, Clerkenwell. On the afternoon of the 10th of July I saw the two prisoners together in Ray-street, Clerkenwell; Marshea had a pair of shoes, tied together with a piece of string; they stopped behind a hearse, and were breaking the string - this gave me suspicion: I told the constable, and he took them.
 ALEXANDER COWAN. These shoes are my property; they were safe at three o'clock on the 10th of July.
   WILLIAM ROBERTS. I was desired by Jones to stop the prisoners: I took the shoes from Marshea, who said his mother sent him to pawn them.
 MARSHEA'S Defence. My mother desired me to take the key to my brother, and as I was returning I picked up the shoes, and tied a handkerchief round them; the officer took me, and I said they belonged to my mother, for fear they should take them from me; they were not tied together.
 McDONALD'S Defence. I saw this lad, who told me he had found the shoes.
 MARSHEA -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.
 McDONALD -    NOT GUILTY.
 1916.  MARY ANN READING was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of April , 1 petticoat, value 1s.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 flat iron, value 1s. 6d.; and 1 pair of bellows, value 3s., the goods of Dennis Welch.
 DENNIS WELSH. I am a  labourer, and live in Adam and Eve-court, Whitecross-street. On the 22d of April, about seven o'clock in the evening, when I came home, I missed these articles from my lodgings; they were safe in the morning; the prisoner slept there the night before, but not before that: I met her in July, and she said she did not know me - I gave her in charge, and she said she would give me half a crown if I would go to her house.
   BRIDGET WELSH. I am the wife of Dennis Welsh. On the 21st of April the prisoner came to me and slept in my room; she was a stranger - she went away in the course of the morning; we had been drinking together, and when she was gone I missed these things.
   WILLIAM HOOKER. I am a labourer. On the 22d of April I was standing at the bottom of Adam and Eve-court; the prisoner came down with a bundle - I saw a pair of bellows and something round them, and a pair of bellows at the top; Mrs. Welsh came down in a few minutes, and asked if I had seen her - I directed her towards St. Luke's church.
   CHARLES RICHARDSON. I took the prisoner in charge; she wanted to settle the matter, but I would not suffer it.
 Prisoner's Defence. Dennis Welsh was gone to a fight - we had nothing in the house, and she gave me the bellows to make money of - she wanted me to stay, but I would not.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined One Month.
