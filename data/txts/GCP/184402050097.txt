Noah Davis. I am a navy-agent, and live in Upper-terrace, Islington.
On the 14th of Nov, the prisoner came to me with other seamen belonging
to the Phantom, and applied for an advance on his prize-money-he was en-
titled to several prizes taken by Her Majesty's ship Phantom-we cannot
tell the amount, we can only judge what it may amount to before it is pay-
able-I advanced him 441. 18.-- he went to Somerset-house, before the ex
aminers of prizes, and signed a prize-order in my favour to that amount,
including the order and stamp-seamen generally calculate that they are en
titled to a great deal more than they really are- he wanted about the amount
I gave him—he did not ask for more-he considered he would have about
201. or 307. more-I was to have the commission of 25 per cent.--about the
middle of Dee, he came and asked for a further advance of 201., which I de-
clined giving him on the 8th of Jan. a payment took place, and I received
281. 108. 10d., leaving my order not yet satisfied by 161. 10s. 2d.
JOHN TORBY. I am clerk to Halford and Co., navy-agents, in Norfolk-
street-they are agents to Commander Haines, late of Her Majesty's ship
Phantom-she was engaged in the suppression of the slave-trade on the Afri-
can coast, and made prizes--this paper (No. 8) is not genuine--I know
Captain Haines's writing perfectly well-it is not his signature, nor is any!
part of it his writing these other papers (1 and 2) are genuine--they merely
describe the time of bis service, and so on-I know nothing of the prisoner
-we have no prize-money due at present-on a rough calculation, I should
say about 31. or 41, would be due to the prisoner-not more than 51. in ad-
dition to what has been drawn, under Captain Haines's command--the Phan-
tom was under the command of Captain Butterfield, prior to Captain Haines
-it is not in my power to say what the gross amount due to the prisoner
would be-we are not agents to Captain Butterfield—I do not know how
much he may be entitled to under other commanders, but under Captain
Haines, the most would be 51. additional-certainly no such sum as 601,
it would depend on the number of prizes and slaves taken-I have not heard
of any prizes made by the Phantom, which could at all account for an expec-
tation of 2501.--that is a very large sum indeed for a common sailor to re-
ceive.
