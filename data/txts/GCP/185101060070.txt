JAMES LEE. I am the prisoner's father. At the time he was appre-
hended, I knew nothing of the name of the person of whom the rug was
bought I have since made inquiries, in consequence of which I served
this subpoena (produced) last night upon a person named Rochford, in the
tap-room of the British Queen-I saw him here when I first came this
morning, at half-past nine o'clock I cannot say whether he has heard
any of the observations you addressed to the Jury.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you speak to him this morning! A. Yes.
I asked him how long he had been here, and he said, “Twenty minutes ;"
that was all I said to him-I saw him last Tuesday night, I think, at my
solicitor's, Mr. Jones, at Hammersmith-I never knew him before that I
had been told his name, I cannot tell who by; and that he lived next door
to Mr. Brazier, the sweep-C went there from Mr. Jones's on Tuesday
night, and inquired for him I had never heard his name before ; but
Mr. Jones told me to go, and I went, and took him back with mehe
told me that he was a bird-catcher.
