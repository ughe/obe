REGINALD LARKIN, recalled. Deceased might have had a little drink
between 11 and 12, enough to make her excited. Such an amount
would leave no trace in the stomach.
Further cross-examined. On examining the brain I found nothing
to indicate chronic alcoholism; it would not show acute alcoholism.
Possibly she may have been under the influence of drink without my
finding any trace in the stomach.
JANE BIGNELL, recalled. When I saw prisoner that night at no time
was he holding a cloth up to his head, either going to or coming back
from the public-house.
Further cross-examined. He had a can in one hand, and if he had
the other one up to his eyes I never saw it.
Verdict, Guilty of manslaughter.
Prisoner had been convicted five or six times for dishonesty, but
there was no conviction of violence. It was stated that in all proba-
bility there had been previous drunken quarrels between prisoner and
deceased at various towns where they had been lodging.
Sentence: Seven years' penal servitude.
BEFORE THE RECORDER.
(Monday. January 15.)
BROWN, George, otherwise Carter (26, porter), breaking and enter-
ing the shop of John Piggott, Limited, and stealing therein an over-
coat and a waistcoat, their goods.
Mr. C. G. Moran prosecuted.
ROBERT LACEY, manager, hat department, Piggott, Limited, 1 and 2,
Milk Street, Cheapside. On December 6 I left our premises, all
properly secured, at 10.30 p.m. The coat and waistcoat produced
(Exhibit 1) were in the window.
Police-constable FREDERICK BENNETT, 133 A. On December 6, at
11.15 p.m., I was in Russia Row, about 15 yards from Milk Street,
when I heard the sound of breaking of glass. I went to Piggott's pre-
mises and found that a plate-glass window had been broken. I saw
prisoner running down Milk Street in the direction of Gresham
Street. I gave chase; half way down Aldermanbury prisoner threw off
the overcoat he was wearing; he was eventually stopped by Police-
constable Sansbury.
Police-constable GEORGE SANSBURY. On December 6 I was in
Gresham Street when I saw prisoner running, followed by Police
constable Bennett. I took up the chase. In Aldermanbury prisoner
threw away the overcoat he was wearing. I caught up prisoner in
Three Nun Court, Basinghall Street. I asked him why he was run-
ning away. He said, "It is not me, sir, but the man above," pointing
to a man who was coming towards us from London Wall.
