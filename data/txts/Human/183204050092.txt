 1011.  JOHN MOSS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , 1 saddle, value 2l., the goods of Richard Morland.
   CHARLES WALLER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in East-road, City-road, about ten minutes' walk from the prosecutor's, on the 18th of February, about half-past eight o'clock in the morning; he had a bag on his shoulder - I asked what was in it; he said a saddle, which a man gave him to carry to Friar's Mount, and he was to have 6d. for it - I took him, and found the prosecutor.
   CHARLES ARMSTRONG. I am in the service of Mr. Richard Morland. This is his saddle, but I had not missed it, till the officer came and asked if we had lost it; it had been in the stable, but the doors are never locked. it had been in the stable, but the doors are never locked.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined One Month.
 1012.  ELEAZER MYERS was indicted for stealing' on the 13th of February , 2 violins, value 3l. 10s., and 1 violin bow, value 3s., the goods of Thomas King.
   THOMAS KING. I keep a broker's shop in Upper Rathbone-place. On the 13th of February the prisoner came to my shop; he said he belonged to a Portuguese vessel - he took a violin out of my window, and asked the price; I told him 2l.; I then showed him another violin and a bow - he asked the price of that; I said 30s.; he then said he belonged to a Portuguese vessel, the Three Sisters, lying in St. Katharine Dock, and was employed by the captain to look out for violias for him; he asked if I would have any objection to let him take the violins to show the captain, if he left above the value of them - I said I had no objection to his taking them, if he left, the full value, if he would bring back the money or the violins, as I would take no goods or any thing of that sort in exchange; I particularly told him that; he then said he would wish to have the property he would leave back again, as it was of more value to him than the violins - he then took out these two shawls from a paper he had under his arm; he knelt down on his right knee, lifted up his two hands, and said, "Pounds more as more as, by Jesus Christ me will not deceive you;" I understood by that 10l.; I then let him have the violins, which were worth 3l. 10s., and he promised to bring back them or the money by three o'clock - he did not return; I did not see him again till the 24th of February, when I met him by accident in Tottenham-court-road; I stopped him, and asked where the violins were - he said he had got them, and I should be sure to have them again; that he had been looking for my shop two or three days, and could not find it - he told me he would give me the violins; he led me on to Cheapside, and opposite Bow church I gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did he speak tolerably good English? A. He spoke broken English - I did not bargain with him for a shawl for my wife in lien of one of the violins; I did not take the shawls for the violins - he left them as security; here are the shawls.
 COURT. Q. You had not sold the violins? A. No, my Lord, not without he brought back the money; I gave him to an officer - he ran from him he was pursued, and taken.
   JOHN GREEN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in Cox-square when he ran away from the other Policeman.
   THOMAS MORFORD. I am the Police-constable. The prisoner got from me, but was not out of my sight; I pursued him about a quarter of a mile - I was knocked down in Cox-square; I was kicked, beaten, and very ill-treated- I nearly lost one of my fingers, and could not go on duty for three days; we pushed the prisoner into a shop, and sent for assistance - several officers came to us; there were hundreds of people round us.
   SAMUEL STEVENS. I am foreman to a pawnbroker. I have the two violins, which were pawned on the 13th of February.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 MR. MANNING. These shawls are worth about 4s. each.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the prosecutor's shop, and asked if he wanted to buy shawls for his wife - he said Yes - he asked how much, and said he had no money but had good violins; I being a musician, can play a violin- he asked to change shawls for violins - I do not know whether I have a right to change.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1013.  JOHN NEWBERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of March , 1 mahogany book-rest, value 5s., the goods of William Artand and another.
 WILLIAM ARTAND JUN. I live with my father - his name is William; he has one partner - they are  in the book trade, and live in Queen Anne-street. On the 2nd of March I was at work at an opposite house, and saw the prisoner bring a book-rest out of my father's shop and give it to a butcher-boy, who was waiting outside - I pursued, and had him taken.
 STEPHEN ARTAND. There was a book-rest lost from our shop, but I did not see it; my nephew, the witness, told me of it - I ran to Wigmore-street; I saw the prisoner, and had him taken - I saw a butcher-boy, but he got away.
 GUILTY. Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
