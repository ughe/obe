On the 31st of August the prisoner was a watchman on board a lighter of ours in the river, with six casks on board, which were to be shipped on board the Argo; I sent him at five o'clock in the afternoon, and his especial charge was to take care of those casks; he was to remain there that night, and come to me in the morning.
   THOMAS THOMPSON. I am a Custom-house officer. I was stationed on board the brig Allies, lying in the river; between eleven and twelve o'clock, on the night of the 31st of August, the barge Jane, which had this coffee on board, struck the Allies, and remained athwart the bows till morning; I saw the prisoner and another person in that barge, while I was walking the deck; they had some conversation about some beer - the other one said he should like some, but he was close hawled and they had better finish - the other said, "I think it is as well;" they then walked the gunwale of the barge - the other person opened the cabin door - the prisoner then got to the bottom of the barge; I am not sure whether he went through the cabin - I went to the other side of the vessel to observe their motions more particularly; there was a hole of six or eight inches square through which I could look at them; the prisoner then produced some bags from under his jacket, which he adjusted over his knee - he then went under the tarpauling, and I heard the running of something which appeared to be coffee or pepper; it was then about a quarter to twelve o'clock - at twelve o'clock I called up Pierce Powen, my partner, to release me - and we continued watching together; I then saw a cask rolled from under the tarpauling without any person with it; and I then discovered the coffee running from a hole in that cask; in a short time I saw the prisoner before the cask - he took a plug from his mouth and put it into the hole of the cask, from which the coffee had been running, and drove it in with a piece of wood - during all this time, the other man was shuffling on the deck and whistling - a smack then passed by, and the coffee was stopped, while it passed, the others then said "It is all right;" and the coffee began to run again, while the man on the top kept moving the hatchway - the prisoner then drew the tarpauling, which had been spread for the coffee to run on, towards the cabin-door - I got into a boat, and went into the harge, and my partner came over the bow of the vessel; I told the prisoner I came for the coffee which he had drawn from the cask - "Lord bless you," says he, "I know nothing about it; I am the watchman;" I told him he might as well confess it, for I had been so near, I could have lent him a hand; I went into the cabin and found his companion; I moved a tar barrel, and found five bags of coffee, which weighed 22lbs. - I applied my light to the prisoner's face, and am quite positive of his person - I told them it was a fortunate job there was not a Police-boat coming by, for if it had, they would have been locked up; I left them on board the barge, and wrote to the Eonourable Board the next morning, stating what I had done; I am certain it was the prisoner, who put the plug into the cask - it was a star light night, and high water - I could see him very distinctly.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. This man tells me he was asleep when you went on board? A. No; he was standing on the vessel.
   HENRY FENNEY. I am foreman of the West India Docks; I delivered six casks of coffee, on board the barge Jane, No. 222; one of the casks was No. 65; I had weighed it, and it was 13cwt. 1qr. 9lbs.
   GEORGE RICH. I am a searcher. I saw the cask, No. 65, weighed on board the Jane on the 5th of September, it weighed 12cwt. 2qrs. 4lbs.
   JOSEPH FARNLEY. I believe this to be the same sort of coffee as was in the casks - it is worth 9d. per lb. without the duty; the prisoner had been eight years with us, and we had a good opinion of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been sent on board the barge the night before, and the next morning I left, and she was sent adrift in the course of the day; at night I was sent on board again, and I found these bags under the tarpauling in the cabin.
 JURY to  THOMAS THOMPSON. Q. Did you leave the deck to go to call your companion? A. No; I called him down the forecastle; I do not know whether the prisoner heard me - he slept about six feet from me. GUILTY. Aged 45.
 Recommended to Mercy by Jury, on account of his former character. Confined One Year.
 1715.  ANN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 gown, value 4s.; 4 caps, value 1s. 6d.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 pair of shoes, value 1s., and 1 printed book, value 1s., the goods of Elizabeth Lay, spinster. The prosecutrix did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1716.    SAMUEL STROUD and MARGARET, HIS WIFE, were indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 2 sovereigns, 1 half-crown, 4 shillings, and 4 sixpences, the goods of Joseph Batchelor , from his person.
 JOSEPH BATCHELOR. On the 14th of September, in the night, I went into a field with the female prisoner; I felt her hand in my pocket; I laid hold of her, and she cried out Murder! the man came down, and said, "What are you going to do with my wife;" I had not seen him before; I said, "She has robbed me" - he said, "Then take her to a constable, but you shan't ill-use her." I took her to a watchman, but he would not take charge of her; a man showed me where a constable lived, who at first refused, but afterwards took her into custody.
   JOSEPH ADDERLEY. I am a constable at Ealing. I was not at home when the watchman brought the female prisoner to my house - I received her in custody; I searched her the next morning, and found half-a-crown, and two-pence upon her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1717.  JAMES HUNT was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , 19 napkins, value 28s., and 3 tablecloths, value 11s., the goods of Hannah Rees, widow.
   CHARLOTTE REES. My mother, Hannah Rees, is in partnership with my brother. NOT GUILTY.
