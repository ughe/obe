In the Eclipse  —the oats were being lowered down to him—I said, "You did not tell me you were going to load the skiff"—he said, "Yes, with five quarters'—I was acting as meter, and Hopkins as lighter man, to Mr. Dudin—he had the management of it—I asked him whether Wheeler worked for Mr. Dudin—he said, "Yes"—I did not see any one but Wheeler in the skiff—it was about twelve or half-past twelve o'clock—there were several men in the Tennant. JAMES HASTIE. I am a fellowship-porter—I was employed in delivering the oats—I saw the skiff come with three bundles of empty sacks—Hopkins and the prisoner were in it—the prisoner assisted Hopkins in passing the sacks out of the skiff into the barge—I saw the prisoner take two sacks from the side of the Tennant , and lay them down in the skiff—I called to Adcock, and told him what I had noticed—the prisoner took the skiff away—there ap peared to be about five quarters in it, but we did not take notice—Hopkins said that Wheeler worked for Mr. Dudin, and it was all right—I should think the prisoner heard that—he stood on the barge—a boy came in the skiff, but he had been sent on shore—the prisoner rowed the skiff away.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know who was the owner of the skiff? A.  No—I saw the prisoner take two sacks—I do not know who took the other—the sacks were not weighed—the oats were measured by Adcock—I do not know whether the prisoner heard what Hopkins said—the skiff went in the direction of Prince's-stairs. JOHN DUDIN. I am a master-lighterman—Hopkins was in my employ; the prisoner was not—I did not authorize Hopkins to employ him—I found the skiff that has been mentioned at ten o'clock that night, with no oats in it—it had been in my charge that day—I had directed Hopkins to deliver the oats at Whiting's-wharf—I have been charged with the difference of the quantity that should have been delivered—I searched for the prisoner—I did not see him again till last Friday—such search has been made for him that I think the Thames police-officers would have found him had he been in the way—I have looked for him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner has worked for you? A.  I have no knowledge of his working for me—Hopkins had no authority from me to em ploy any one—he could have rowed the barge himself with 260 quarters in it—I have done it—all but the quantity stolen was put into the barge—the skiff did not belong to me, but it was in my charge that day—I gave Hopkins instructions to hire twenty sacks. GEORGE MADDOX( Thames-police inspector. ) I have been in search of the prisoner since the 4th Oct.—I could not find him—I have missed him from that time. JAMES LAMBERT. I saw the prisoner with his skiff of 4th Oct.—he asked me if I wanted a cast on shore—I said, "Yes"—I stepped in, and saw some sacks full—I do not know what they contained—the prisoner rowed to George's-stairs—I left him in the skiff—I do not know what was done with the sacks.
   MR. O'BRIEN to JOHN ADCOCK. Q.  Can you say that the prisoner must have heard you when you spoke to Hopkins? A.  I cannot—I called out loud, and was near enough for him to hear—he stood with his back to me—he did not turn round to me to look. GUILTY . Aged 38.— Confined One Year .
