MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 JOSIAH MARTIN ST. JOHN. I am a  coach-master, and live at Tottenham. White was my hostler- Brown was my under hostler - I keep my oats in the granary, which joins the hay-loft; I had fifty or sixty quarters of oats - I missed a great quantity from time to time; I discovered a sack of oats concealed one day, and turned Brown away - White went away a few days afterwards, without any notice; I knew of no robbery till Brown afterwards came voluntarily and told me of it; William Grant is a hayjobber; John is his son; I paid a 5l. note to John Grant about a month or six weeks before I turned Brown away.
   GEORGE BROWN. I was under White in Mr. St. John's service. About three months ago White proposed to me to rob master - I told him I would do nothing of the sort; he then went and got three bushels of oats from the granary, in a sack, and put into Grant's car, which stood in the yard; John Grant had brought it there, but was gone to town - William was not there - Grant is a hay-jobber; I had heard no conversation between White and him - John had brought a load of hay in the cart; he went to London to fetch another cart; he returned about half-past eleven o'clock, with a horse only, and took away the cart - I was afterwards coming back from Edmonton and met John Grant drawing the cart, with the corn in it, and a little loose hay; shortly after this White asked if I would have any thing to drink out of what Grant gave him; I said No; he said he had got some money from Mr. Grant, and if I liked to drink, or have part of it, I might; I had only seen John Grant there that morning; Mr. St. John turned me away on the 17th of May, about some corn found under the manger; I could not get a place, and came to him and gave information; nobody requested me to do so; White had left him that day; I believe it was after I gave the information; I have not spoken to White since.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. Your master accused you of stealing oats? A. Yes, those under the manger; I ought to have mentioned this, but did not wish to make a disturbance; I went to tell him of it this day week, because he was blaming me to every body, and I could not get a place; I should have told him if I had got one - the Grants live at Enfield-highway; I was driving a coach when I met him in the cart, about twenty minutes past twelve o'clock; I did not speak to him; he had returned about half-past eleven; the oats were in a sack; I was going out on a coach when he came to the yard; White handed the corn from the granary; I saw him do it; I did not mean to have a shilling of the money it fetched; White told me he was going to send some hay-seeds away - I cannot swear that the sack contained corn.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Was there any hay-seed at the granary? A. No: the sack I saw in the cart was the same as I saw put into it; I had passengers on my coach when I saw him.
 COURT. Q. Could you tell what was in the sack? A. No.
 MR. ST. JOHN. I have missed no hay-seeds; I had not got so much as a sack; there was nothing in the granary but oats; the oats were worth 12s.
   JOHN CAMP. I am constable of Edmonton. I apprehended the Grants, and told them the charge; the father said he knew nothing about it - the son said nothing; I neither threatened nor promised them any thing; as we went to the cage John said, his father, he was certain, knew nothing at all about it - but he had received them himself, and paid 5s. for them; he said so twice.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he say how he received it? A. He said it was put into his cart, as it stood in the street, and when he came back White told him he had put in some corn, and wanted 5s. for it; that he told him he had nothing but the 5l. note, which he received for the hay; that he got change and paid him 5s.
   JOHN PORTER. I am hostler at the Fleece, public-house, at Edmonton. I saw John Grant taken to the cage, and heard him say "Father, you are quite innocent of this here." I did not hear him say who did receive them.
 Cross-examined. Q. If he had said so, you must have heard it? A. I should think so.
 JOHN GRANT's Defence. If there was any corn in the cart, it was taken out before I returned.
   HENRY COTTERELL. I live in Newington-cause way, and collect the parish rents. I have known White eighteen years; I saw Brown on Sunday morning, and he denied every thing he has stated now; he said, "I will never go there at all."
   JOHN CAMP. Porter was at some distance when Grant said what I have stated, and might not hear him.
 WHITE -    GUILTY. Aged 50.
 JOHN GRANT -    GUILTY.
 Grant confined Six Months.
 WILLIAM GRANT -    NOT GUILTY.
 1272.  JOSHUA WHITE was again indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 3 bushels of oats, value 12s., the goods of Josiah Martin St. John; and WILLIAM SLEE was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to have been stolen.
 JOSIAH MARTIN ST. JOHN. I live at  Tottenham- the prisoner was my hostler; Slee lives nearly opposite to me - he keeps pigs and a horse and cart - he sells pork, and is a general dealer - he does not deal in corn or hay; I had missed corn for four months - Brown came and gave me information; White left the same day's without notice; Slee must have known White and Brown to be my servants; Brown occasionally drove my coach.
   GEORGE BROWN. I have known Slee three or four years; before Christmas last he asked me if I could get him any thing that would do for his horse; I asked if he meant hay-bands - he said No; I told him I could not - he did not say what he wanted. White said to me, one day after that, "George, if you can get any corn out, Mr. Slee will take it;" I saw White put a sack of corn on the footboard of the coach, and he said to me, "There is some corn for Slee, you can get Terry to carry it;" there was about three bushels - Terry is a labourer, but not in Mr. St. John's service; Terry came and took it away - this was about six weeks before I gave the information; I saw White, after that corn had been taken, at the Roebuck, public-house, where there is a shoe-club held; he said to me there, that he had got some money, if I liked to take part of it, I might; I owed him 1s. 3d. for my share at the club, and asked him to pay it for me, till master came home - he did so, and I paid him the next day.
