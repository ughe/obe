   By MR. RAVEN . In my opinion the string might have been tighter and become slackened—I believe that was accidental—the prisoner afterwards told me in the course of the day that she did not know what possessed her to try and kill the child—the child stayed in the infirmary, and the prisoner left well, on August 8th. ( The child was now in the prisoner's arms. )
   Cross-examined.  She was in bed when I first saw her—I had to use ordinary force to complete the labour; it was not the process of nature—I merely asked her how it was she got into this trouble, and she told me she did not know what possessed her to try and kill the child—I think she heard my question—when I said "this trouble," I meant being arrested—she was in a very weak state, but she was able to speak; she had rallied then; she was walking about the ward in nine days or a fortnight, and the child took about a fortnight to recover; it was with its mother all the time, and I treated it for a laceration of the neck—the wound was about 1/8 of an inch long all round the neck—it was an abrasion—in newly-born children very little is required to make a serious wound—there is not a tendency in the, folds of the neck to show very bad signs, but there is lower down—I do not think the same cause that would bring forward a tendency to produce those marks would cause laceration—there was a mark on the windpipe—no lasting mark is left on the child—I was in Court and heard the whole of the case before the committing Magistrate—the prisoner is very deaf and there was no" one defending her—I did not hear the answers she made to the Magistrate's questions. WILLIAM PARKER( re-examined ). I was present before the Magistrate and heard the statement the prisoner made—she said "If the—string got there it got there by accident," The deposition being refered to only stated that ike prisoner said "I have nothing to say."). NOT GUILTY. ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, DECEMBER 11 TH , 1876.
