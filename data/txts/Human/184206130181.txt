1908. HENRY BODMAN was indicted for embezzlement. MARY SPARROW. I am single. I keep a china and glass shop in Pad dington-street—the prisoner was my errand-boy—he came into my service on the 2nd of May—he received some monies for me which he brought to me—on the 3rd of May I sent him out with some china in a basket, to No, 6 York-buildings, New-road—he was to receive 22 s .—he did not re turn to bring the china or the money—I saw him again on the 2nd of June, in charge. EMMA WELLS. I live with Mrs. Easy, at No. 6, York-buildings. I bought a set of china at Mrs. Sparrow's, and the prisoner went with me to carry it in a basket—I took it out of the basket, and gave him the basket, with a sovereign, and 2 s . in silver.
   Prisoner.  This witness would not pay me, because I had not got a re ceipt. Witness.  I gave him the money in the passage. THOMAS HENRY THOMPSON( police-sergeant D  4.) On the 2nd of June the prisoner was given to me by his father, who told me what he was charged with—I found 1 s . in one of the legs of his trowsers, and 2 s . in the other—as we were going to the office I said, "What have you done with the basket?"—he said, "I threw it away in a court in Fleet-street."
   Prisoner's Defence.  She would not pay me because I had not a receipt. I was coming back, a young man met me, and took me to the Surrey Theatre. I threw away the basket in a court in Fleet-street. The young man kept me about a week, and then gave me 5 s . GUILTY. Aged 15.— Transported for Seven Years.—Convict Ship.
 1909. EMILY WYATT was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of April , 1 pair of spectacles, value 20 s  ., the goods of William Hexter . CHARLOTTE HEXTER. I am the wife of William Hexter, of Adam-street East, Manchester-square—the prisoner called on the 14th of April, under the pretence of telling me of a place for my son—(I had seen her once be fore)—I had a pair of silver spectacles on the table—they were safe about two hours before she came—I missed them next morning—these now produced are them. RICHARD KING TAYLOR. I live in St. Martin's-lane, and am a tailor. The prisoner lodged in my house from the 10th to the 16th of April—on the 14th of April I saw her wearing a pair of silver spectacles about the house—they were like these—I never saw her wear spectacles before or after. HENRY HUTCHINS. I am apprentice to Mr. Clark, a pawnbroker, in Long-acre. These spectacles were pawned by Miller. MARY ANN MILLER. I live in Parker-street, Drury-lane. T was ser vant to the prisoner—in April last I remember her bringing home a pair of silver spectacles, which she said she had found—she gave them to me to pawn, which I did at Mr. Clark's, for 3 s . 6 d .—I gave her the money.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I did take them and pawn them, but I meant to take them back. The prosecutor told me, if I would tell where they were she would not come against me: I did tell, and then she took me. I am a foreigner, and did not know the law was so strict. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined One Month.
