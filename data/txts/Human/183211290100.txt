217. THOMAS WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 2 wheels, value 6s., the goods of Samuel Drawater.
   SAMUEL DRAWATER. I hawk earthernware about in a waggon. The two wheels of my waggon hung in my back yard, in Rose-lane, Spitalfields; I saw them safe on Wednesday, and on Monday they were taken - I found the prisoner in custody with them; he was a stranger.
   SAMUEL GREEN. I am a Policeman I saw the prisoner on Monday morning, the 26th of November, about half-past nine o'clock, at the top of White's-row, Spitalfields, about a hundred yards from the prosecutor's; he had one wheel in each hand - he lifted up his head, saw me, and turned back; I went round Butler-street, and then found the wheels standing against the wall - I saw him a few yards off, and took him; he said he had bought them that morning, for 1s. 6d., at the back of the Cross Keys, of a man he did not know.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. About half-past seven o'clock I saw a man in Rose-lane with the wheels - he offered them for sale; I said I had but 18d. - he followed me, and said I should have them for that.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined One Month.
 218.  BENJAMIN KEMP was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of November , I live tame fowl, price 2s., the property of Harry Mayo.
   JOHN COOKSEY. I am in the employ of Harry Mayo, who keeps the  Three Pigeons, at Brentford. I was standing in the yard, and saw the prisoner in the yard - I stopped him, and found a fowl under his jacket; its neck was broken - it had been just killed; it was alive in the morning.
   HERBERT PRIDON. I am a Policeman. I received him in charge; the neck of the fowl was broken - it was warm; three more fowls were found, which had been knocked down.
 Prisoner. It was not alive when I found it.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined Three Months, and Publicly Whipped.
 219.  WILLIAM KERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of November , 1 trunk, value 2l., the goods of Joseph Upcott.
 JOSEPH UPCOTT. I live in  High-street, Hoxton. On the 21st of November this truck was kept by the side of the house - I had seen it there at one o'clock; I went out, and returned about half-past two - it was then gone; I saw it the next morning at No. 4, New-street, Shoe-lane Holborn; I knew it to be mine - I made it; I had seen the prisoner before - he came once to hire a chaise of me.
   ANN UPCOTT. I am the prosecutor's sister. The prisoner came to me on the 24th of November, at two o'clock, to hire the truck; I saw him standing at the door, looking at it - I went and asked him his business; he said he wanted the lightest truck we had got - I told him that was the lightest we had at present; he said if I was sure it was not the lightest he was not in a hurry, and would wait an hour, till I could get him a lighter one - I said the lightest we had was out; I went to the back of the house, found a lighter one, and said I was sure there was not a lighter one than that; he said, "I suppose you want my direction?" I said Yes - he said, "Give me a bit of paper;" he gave me his direction on my brother's card: we let trucks out at 3d. an hour - I saw the truck next morning at No. 4, New-street-hill, Shoe-lane, Holborn, at Mr. Burrows' - he gave me his direction, Robert Roberts, at No. 17, Wellington-street, Kingsland-road; it has got rubbed out of the card - he was taken up the day it was found.
   RICHARD PHILLIPS. I am an auctioneer. The prisoner brought a truck to me, on Wednesday, the 21st of November - he said he wished to have it sold; I asked if it belonged to him - he said it belonged to Mr. Upcott, who was just taken to Whitecross-street prison, and wished to have it sold directly, as he was in distressed circumstances - he asked for 10s. on it, which I refused, but at last let him have 5s.; I sold it for 28s., to Mr. Budden.
   JOHN BUDDEN. I live in Shoe-lane. I bought the truck on the 21st, at a sale in Chiswell-street (which is not in the City), for 28s.; Upcott claimed it - it has no name on it now.
 JOSEPH UPCOTT. I saw the truck at Budden's, and am certain it is mine; I never gave him authority to sell it - I was not in prison; it is all false.
   FRANCIS OAKES. I am sixteen years old. I went to No. 16, Wellington-street, and inquired for the name of Roberts - he was not there.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not hire it of Mrs. Upcott; I did not know her or Mr. Upcott - I was sent to Phillips with it, and gave him the message which was given me.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Transported for Seven Years.
 220.  JOHN LEARY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 1 watch, value 20s., the goods of William Pearce , from the person of Mary Pearce.
 MARY PEARCE. I am the mother of  William Pearce; this watch was his. On the 27th of November I had taken it out of pledge, at the corner of Fore-street, Limehouse, about one o'clock; it came on a very heavy rain, and I went into the Queen's Head public-house- I sat on a bench by the fire-side, and the prisoner was close to me; the watch was in my pocket - I had not been there ten minutes before he went away; I am quite sure the watch was in my pocket when he sat down by me, and in a minute after he left I put my hand into my pocket, and the watch was gone - he was taken the same evening; I saw him at Lambeth-street.
   JOSEPH HUGHES. I am a pawnbroker. On the 27th of November the prisoner pawned this watch, for 1s., which was all he asked for it - he pawned it in the name of John Leary; the prosecutrix saw it the next day, and said it was her son's.
   MARY PEARCE. The watch belonged to my son, who is in the East Indies; he had pawned it in December last year - I am certain it is the watch I redeemed.
 Prisoner's Defence. It is my own - I have had it in my possession nine months; I wanted a pot of beer, and pawned it for 1s.
   JAMES PEARCE. I am a son of the prosecutrix. I know my brother's watch - I know this to be the watch by the ribbon and key, and the watch; I have seen it in his possession scores of times.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
