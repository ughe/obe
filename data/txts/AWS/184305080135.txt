1595. JOHN CORDWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
April, 2 pairs of shoes, value 28. the goods of George Lowe; and that he
had been before convicted of felony.
CHARLOTTE Lowg. I am the wife of George Lowe-we keep a clothes-
shop in Union-street, Lisson-grove-on the 22nd of April, about nine o'clock
in the evening, the putty was cut away from a square in the window-j was
broken before-we heard the crack, and the glass was pushed in, but
nothing was taken away-it was put back again and the next day, at a
quarter before four o'clock, I caught the prisoner with his arm in at the same
place in the window-he took out these two pairs of shoes, and put them in
his other hand-he put his arm in again to take more, but I collared him
-he dropped these shoes on the area-he said he hoped I should forgive
him-these are my husband's shoes.
WILLIAM HOLDER (police-constable E 100.) The prisoner and the shoes
were given to me-he begged for merey.
Prisoner. I am guilty of being with the boy, but I am innocent of taking
them.
WILLIAM CROUCHER. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former con-
viction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office-(read)-] was at that time in the
police, and was present at his trial-the prisoner is the person.
GUILTY. Aged 4.-Transported for Seven Years-Parkhurst.
1596. JAMES ANDERSON was indieted for stealing, on the 18th of
April, 1 pair of boota, value 78. the goods of George Walker.
GEoRGE WALKER. I live in Greville-street, Leather-lane, and am a boot-
maker--this pair of boots are mine-I missed them on the 13th of April-
about four o'clock in the afternoon I went out, and they were then safe in my
window-] returned at five, and they were then gone--there was a piece of
paper stuck up at the window, in the place of a pane of glass, and when I
returned, I found that paper broken in, and these boots could have been taken
through the hole.
NATHANIEL SIMONDS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Baldwin's-gardens
-the prisoner pawned these boots with me on the 13th of April.
HENRY HAYWARD (police-constable g 97.) I took the prisoner-he
said, he had had the boots given to him by a boy named Jones, to pawn for
his brother.
Prisoner's Defence. I was on the water at the time these were taken a
boy named Jones brought me these boots in a handkerchief; he said they
were his brother's, and they were too small he asked me to pawn them.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUITY. Aged B.-Recommended to erey.-Confined One Month.
