   JURY . Q.  Had the prisoner a carpet bag with him when he left you at Lewes? A.  My impression is that he had something in his hand, but whether it was a carpet bag I cannot say—he did not come close to the carriage that I was in. GEORGE EDWARD THOMPSON. I am ten years old; I live with my father in Craven-street, Strand. I recollect the prisoner coming to our house in Jan.—it was on the day before we were to go to school—I do not remember the day—it was about the middle of Jan.—I have seen this paper before ( looking at it ), it was sent to my papa after I went to school—I came home from school before Christmas—we were to have six weeks' holidays—we were to go to school on a Wednesday—the day we were to go to school the prisoner came—he asked if I was going to school, and if not he asked if he should come and teach me private lessons in the house—he said if my mamma would give him a room he would teach me and my two sisters—that was agreed to by my mamma—he went down to Hoddesden on Tuesday—he said he was going to get his things, and he would come back on Wednes day—I had known him before—he was the French master at Hoddesden—he came on the Wednesday—I saw this bag on the Thursday, the day he began to teach us—I was in the room every day, he used to keep this bag on the sofa in the room—it was a bag exactly like this—I have handled it, I used to take the things out of this bag, and put them on the table for him—he taught me in his room—I saw the bag a good while; while he was there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you able to say positively whether this is the bag? A.  It is exactly like it—he had a portmanteau with him—I did not see that he had a small bag with swaps—he brought his things in a cab  —he came about 9 o'clock.
   COURT . Q.  How long did he continue to teach you after the Wednes day day? A.  About a fortnight. GEORGE BENCHARD.
