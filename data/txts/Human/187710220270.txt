He got on top of the policeman's back, I believe, as he slipped down—he also said "I cannot under  stand what Douglas is in custody for, he did not buy the iron, he only had part of the profit, which I gave him." His brother was charged with assaulting me, and referring to that he said "I am sorry my brother struck you, he did it out of love for me, I hope you will not be too hard upon him." He was then taken to the station—on the same day, about 10 o'clock, I took Douglas at Green Bank, Lewisham—he was in bed, but dressed—I told him who I was, and that I was going to arrest him on a warrant for stealing a quan tity of iron in April last belonging to Messrs. Penn—I took out the warrant to read to him—he said "Pray don't do that, I know all about it, and have been expecting these proceedings, and I am sur prised that Messrs. Penn should take the steps they have for a sum of about 30 l . I know the Penn family, and we are to a certain extent intimate." He offered every facility to my searching—his brother came in, and being surprised to see two detectives there, asked me what it meant—I said that I was there to arrest his brother, and he said "Now, Thomas, if there is anything you can tell the officers about this business do so." Thomas said "Well, all I know about it is this: I bid for Peiin's iron, and Porter heard of it. Porter saw me, and said 'What do you want to bid for Porter's iron for, I have had it so long; let me have it this time, I can get some overweight and you shall have part, or half of what I make,' and he afterwards gave me some money, 8 l . or 10 l ., I don't know which. I do not think there was anything wrong about taking that money, as I advanced him part of the money to pay for the iron, and, of course, I ran a certain risk." He said that he was not fit to be removed—I saw his medical man, and he was left there with an officer in charge of him.
   Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY . I understood him to say "I cannot account for the deficiency in the weight," or it may have been "forany deficiency"—he did not tell me that he had seen Whitling—I had been watching outside for a long time—he said that he had gone over the garden want to his mother's so that I should not see him, as he did not want to be locked up that night.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT . Ballantine. I believe Douglas is a wharfinger—I believe other officers have been to his wharf to make in quiries—I only went to his house—he might have learnt that proceedings were going on—there were four or five remands—I was present at all—ths Magistrate did not say on the last remand that there was no case against Douglas—he did not intimate his intention to discharge Douglas or any thing to that effect—I think it was on the last hearing that Whitling was produced—the Magistrate had given an intimation about Douglas case on the previous hearing, and then Wickling was produced for the first time, but not in consequence of what the Magistrate said—he was going to be a witness, that was understood, and he was there at each hearing, but his evidence was not given until the Magistrate had given an intimation—he had sworn information.
