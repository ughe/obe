Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When did it undergo this pro cess? A.  I do not know—I assisted in the operation—that was the only tub that was subjected to that process—it was the first time we had ever tried it in that way—I examined the cask on the day we were at the police-station—I think it was the 6th of Jan.—I can ascertain this oil had undergone the process by the peculiarity of its appearance—it is now different from other palm oil, by its having an acid smell—I believe that is only produced by the chemical process—I never met with it in any that had not undergone the process—it is a bleaching process—I believe it is commonly applied to palm oil—I do not know that it is common to palm oil to have this acid smell—the prisoner has been a frequent purchaser of staves of tallow-tubs at our warehouse—I suppose he has bought 5 l . or 6 l . worth of them—they are 10 s . a hundred—I do not think that one of them had had palm oil in it—a consi derable quantity of grease adheres to the tubs, but the staves are scraped before they are sold—there could not be much grease scraped off after they are sold—I never knew of its being done—I will swear that this grease, which is here produced, is palm oil—I have been in the business two years and a half—soap-makers have more to do with the business than I have. THOMAS ATKINS. I am pot-boy at the Grapes public-house, at Wands worth. On the 2nd of January a little before nine o'clock, I saw the pri soner coming from Mr. Knill's premises with a little boy—the prisoner had a pail on his shoulder, and his arm through the handle—it appeared to me as if it was very heavy, he stooped his back—he was going towards his own house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is your notion of a great weight, a hundred pounds? A.  It might be a hundred pounds—he was doing this quite openly. HENRY BAILEY. I am servant to Mr. Durant, a tallow-chandler, at Put ney. On the 4th of Jan. the prisoner came to his shop with the grease which has been produced and represented as palm oil, in a pail—there was twenty-one pounds of it—I paid him 2 d . a pound for it—it came to 3 s . 6 d .—it was given up to the officer—we had bought some of him previously.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you give him what you considered a fair value? A.  I did not know the value—I took it to the foreman, who did know—we were not exactly aware what it was—we put it with our kitchen-stuff. WILLIAM READ( police-sergeant V  18.)
