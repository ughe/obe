WILLIAM BAMFORD. I am a surgeon and apothecary, at Rugeley, in
Staffordshire. I first saw the deceased, John Parsons Oook, on Saturday,
17th Nov.-William Palmer called on me to attend on a friend of his, who
was unwell, at the Talbot Arms I walked down with him he said that
Cook had been dining with him the day before, and had taken too much
wine, champagne-I saw Cook, and asked him as to his having taken too
much wine the day before, and he assured me that he had taken but two
glasses—I found no appearance of bile which he threw off while I saw him,
but a constant vomiting-(The COURT considered that anything respecting
the symptoms had better be avoided now) - Mr. Palmer had been called up to
him that morning, at 6 o'clock-_I prescribed a saline effervescing draught
for him, a six ounce mixture on my second visit to him, on the Monday
evening, I prescribed some pills for him—the medicine and the pills I pro-
pared for him while Mr. Palmer stopped at my house-he stopped, I pre-
pared the pills, and he took them away. combero

-Palmer met me again between 12 and 1 o'clock-I was then going to see
