WILLIAM DEBONNAIRE HAGGARD. I am chief clerk in the bullion-office
in the Bank of England-in due course of post I received information from
Falmouth of the expected arrival of some boxes of gold dust-I have the
advices with me which I received--they are from the wagon-office-I
received four parcels of gold dust, not boxes- I never received two boxes
marked B C 18 and 19.
James Pear. I am accountant to the Brazilian Company. I came
to the office on Monday morning the 25th of March, at the usual time,
about ten o'clock-I know Messrs. Carne, merchants, of Falmouth-I
have not got by me any advices I received from them previous to that day
-no boxes of gold dust, marked "BC 18 and 19," arrived on the 25th of
March-we expected them-Mr. March, the secretary, arrived at the office
about a quarter before eleven o'clock, and in consequence of some conver-
sation between myself and Mr. March, I sent George Carthew to the Dub-
lin Steam-packet office to inquire if two boxes of gold, marked "B C 18
and 19." had arrived by the Dublin steam-boat-I remember Lewin Cas-
par coming to the bullion-office, at the Bank-I was there-- I went to as
certain if the boxes had been delivered at the Bank-I asked Lewin Cas-
par what had become of our two boxes of gold dust, marked B C 18 and
19"-he said, “They have been delivered to your house"-I said,
“How is that, when they are consigned to the Bank of England by the
bill of lading ?"--he replied that they had received a letter that morning
from Messrs Carne's, of Falmouth, saying, that in consequence of an error
in the manifest, they were not to be delivered at the Bank of England,
but to the agent of the Brazilian Mining Company in London-I asked
him if he knew the name of that agent-he said he did not remember-I
asked him if the name was March-he said, “That, I think, is the
name"-I afterwards went to the office, in Crutched-friars, and saw Lewin
Caspar there--(before that I had been to our office, and ascertained that
the boxes had not arrived)-I said, “These boxes have not been de
livered at our house, what has become of them?" he said, “If you will
walk into the office I will show you what has been done with them"-I went
in, and he produced two letters purporting to be from Messrs. Carne-these
are them (Nos. 3 and 4)-No. 4 had the address torn off, and the other was
addressed to Mr. Hartley-I called his attention to the one with the ad-
dress torn off, and said, "What has become of the back of this letter ?"-he
hesitated some little time, and then replied, " I don't know whether it was
torn off when I got the letter, or whether it has been torn off since"-he
did not say who it had been addressed to in any part of the conversation,
that I recollect-he called my attention to the signatures of the letters, and
asked me if I did not think they were the signatures of Messrs. Carne-
I said they appeared somewhat similar, and asked him if he knew the
handwriting in the body of the letter as coming from Messrs. Carne-he

whose writing it was I said I had never seen it myself - then looked
