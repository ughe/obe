557. MOSES LEE, Stealing two ponies, the property of John Parker.
MR. CHARLES MATHEWS Prosecuted; MR. GEOGHEGAN Defended.
JOHN PARKER. I am a cabinet-maker, of 2, Taunday Villas, Seven
Sisters' Road, Tottenham--on Sunday, 7th November, at 8.30 a.m., I
turned out on the Braemar Estate, & black gelding pony 12 hands high,
about six years old, broken knees, docked tail, and a piece out of the
off-side ear-I also turned out on the same day a dark brown gelding
pony 12 hands high, with a mealy nose, a scar inside the off-side hock,
& short tail, and six years old-I would not have taken 301. for the two,
and they were worth 101. each in the market-I gave nine guineas for
the little one--they were safe about 3 o'clock, and I missed them at 4.15
-about 10th January I was at Folkestone and saw Mr. Vincent driving
my black pony-I told him he had been stolen-I have not got him back
yet-about March 4th I saw the brown pony in Caledonian Road Cattle
Market in Mr. Harris's possession-I identified it, and got it back-
while I was looking for my ponies I came across George Howard, who
had lost a bay mare that day, from some ground directly opposite-after
that I went with Howard to Eltham, and saw the prisoner's father,
Moses Lee, and his uncle, Old Jack Lee, who gave me 121. and a little
chestnut pony-I spent a deal of money trying to find the prisoner-on
5th March Old Jack Lee came to my house and made & communication
to me—the prisoner was then in custody.
Cross-examined. The 121. I got from the uncle I took for my expenses
-I was 81. 168. out of pocket travelling about to look for my ponies--
after I had got the 121. and the pony I still signed the charge-sheet-I
did not take the 121. to square the matter-I paid 41. 10s. for the black
gelding-& fence and a footpath separate the estate from the main road
-the ponies were loose in the field-Mr. Harris only gave 21. for his
pony, because he was glandered.
