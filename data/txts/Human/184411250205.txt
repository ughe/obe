   COURT . Q.  Did you see whether the steamer changed her course or not? A.  I did not—I saw the skiff, as she came round the stern of the steamer, which was more than 300 yards from where I stood—I did not notice whether the steamer changed her course or not.
   JURY . Q.  Did you keep your eye on the steam-vessel all the time? A.  I saw the boat, as I was walking backwards and forwards—I did not lose sight of the steamer at all—if she had altered her course materially I should have seen it—a little deviation, perhaps, I might not have noticed—I kept my eye on her—I know she did not deviate twenty feet from her course, but if she did five or ten it might not have been noticed by me—she did not change her course anything to speak of—I observed that she did not change her course. SIR JOHN BAYLEY. Q.  Did you notice that her paddles were stopped? A.  Yes, just previous to the accident—half a second before—it was a momentary thing, just previous to her striking, just after she had cleared the stern of the schooner—I did not hear anybody hail on board, or any orders given—after the skiff was struck, ropes, and everything that was available, were thrown from the steamer, and every assistance rendered.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did the steamer stop and try to pick the men up? A.  The men were floated out into the stream—the steamer did stop for ten minutes—I cannot say it was stationary for ten minutes, but the engine was stopped—I did not see one of the persons that were upset en deavouring to reach the steam-boat—he was outside—they threw over part of the sails and ropes from the steamer—they had no boat—I know nothing more of the steamer than being a passenger in her frequently—not in that particular boat, but in any of the steam-boats—I saw the skiff turned round by the eddy, just when the steam-boat was turning round the stern of the schooner.
   COURT . Q.  How came the steamer to stop for ten minutes, what was she doing during that time? A.  She was coming to the pier for passengers, and she stopped on the accident taking place—I was waiting for her—she just glided to the pier—I got on board—she did not go on immediately with her passengers—I cannot say as to the time, as there was confusion—I should say it was about ten minutes—I observed that all the persons were thrown out of the skiff—I cannot say how many there were—they were picked up before the steamer went off—all that could be found at the time—I do not know how many were picked up—I was told they were all picked up—I was on board the steamer—we went on understanding all were picked up—that was my impression at the time.
