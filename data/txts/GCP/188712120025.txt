WILLIE LITTLEJOHN. I am clerk to Messrs. Low, Son, and Haydon,
perfumers, 330, Strand-Serné came to our shop about the end of June
last; he ordered some goods-he paid a deposit of 5l. on 5th July-we
supplied him altogether with goods amounting to 171. 188. 8d. from the
end of June to 4th August for articles of perfumery for a hairdresser's
business-the balance, 121. 188. 3d., has never been paid.
DAVI MORRIS. I am a house furnisher, of 260, Pentonville Road,
King's Cross-on 14th June this year Serné came to my shop and ordered
furniture to be supplied to him at 274, Strand, to the amount of 131.28. 6d.
- he was to pay 61. down and 11. a month; he did not pay the 61. down,
but I supplied the furniture-I applied several times for the money-he
paid 5l. on account; the balance, 8l. 28. 6d., is still outstanding I went
to 274-I was there on or about 17th August, and then went over the
premises -I made a rough estimate in my own mind of the value of the
furniture there ; as near as I could judge I think the outside value of it
was from 201. to 25l.-I afterwards heard of the fire-I next saw Serné
somewhere about 24th September; he came to my place, I think he came
with Goldfinch-Serné came into the shop, Goldfinch stood outside-
Serné asked me to give him some fresh goods, and said that I could also
put 21. or 31. extra on to make up for the trouble I had had in calling
for the account; he said he was insured, and he expected to get about
5001. or 6001. from the insurance office, and he would then pay me the
full amount--that was all, he then left-I believe he joined Goldfinch
outside and went away with him-a few days after Goldfinch came alone
-he said he came for some bills for Mr. Serné-I asked him wbat bills
he said he did not know-I said "Tell Mr. Serné I decline to have
anything to do with making out fresh invoices”-be then left.
