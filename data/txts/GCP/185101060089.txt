- I went to my aunt's on the Tuesday, and came home on the Wednes-

ing I did not get there till past ten-I had only just got in the fair when

not know that I was following him-I did not take up the horse's fetlock;
I could see the cut without taking it up- I did not hear that this horse
was stolen - I had left my own home on the Saturday morning I went to
Dartford and stopped all night I went to the Bull, at Lucomb, and then
walked the next day to Town-Malling-I know Horsham-I have not
been there the last four or five years- I do not know how far that is from
Town-Malling- I cannot tell whether it is thirty or forty miles-I did not
say anything to Steadman-I followed him into the public-bouse, and be
said, "Here is a glass of ale for you"--I saw forty horses sold; I did not
follow every one I follow persons that I know into public-houses when
they buy horses- I followed about five persons that day--I saw no one
with Steadman, only the man that took the money for the horse—that fair
is for the sale of farming colts in general.

ALFRED SYLVESTER. I live at Bromley-common. I was at Town-
Malling fair on 18th Nov. ; I saw a bay cob pony in the fair-it was for
sale, and the off-fetlock had a crack in it-I asked the man the price of it
- it was a man with a round frock on ---I offered to change with him with
one that I had.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'Brien. Q. Did you see it again ? 4. I
saw it as I came home from the fair by the Cock, at Kingsdown ; I believe
the prisoner had it I did not know him-I had never seen him till I saw
him at Kingston fair, which was a few days before Town-Malling fair-1 do
not know the day of the month-it might be pretty close on a fortnight
before Town-Malling fair ;-there I saw the prisoner-I do not know.John
Thompson; I should not like to swear that I had seen him before to-day
-I was not doing anytbing with Steadman at Kingston, only he came
to try to buy a horse of my master, Mr. Robert Adams-I went to the
fair with horses - have spoken to Thompson outside here this morning
- I did not understand you before-I saw him at Town-Malling fair-he
was running a horse up and down-that was about nine o'clock, or it
might be a little after-I saw the pony a little afterwards--I cannot say
how many horses were there ; they keep them in different places I did
not examine any other pony's fetlock that day-I examined this one
about a quarter past nine, when I went to take a pony to be shod, and I
saw this pony standing by the forge--a man with a round frock on had it
-I never saw the prisoner till I saw him at Kingsdown riding the pony-
that was close on two o'clock-he was coming by the same way that I was
-- he was riding it on its bare back I did not take anything-1 bad two
horses and a puny and cart.
MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. You saw Thompson about nine o'clock ? A.
Yes, it might have been later ; I do not carry a watch.
