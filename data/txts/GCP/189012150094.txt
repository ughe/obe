JOHN SWINDELL (E 59). I was called to this omnibus; the conductor
brought the prisoner down and said, "I charge this man with passing
this bad shilling to me; on Tuesday night last, he got on to my omnibus
and passed a bad sixpence to me, but left the omnibus before I discovered
it was bad"-the prisoner said nothing ; I said, “Where did you get this
money from?”-he said, "I was paid it by my master, Mr. Pettit, of Comp-
ton Street”-I said, “Is that all the money you have in your possession ?"
-he said, "I have not another farthing"-I searehed him on the spot, and
found 3s. in silver, and 1s. 8d. in bronze, all good- I took him to the
station; he never spoke a word-I searched him again at the station,
and found concealed in the lining at the back of his coat this leather
tobacco pouch, containing four more bad shillings—I did not rip his coat,
there was a slit in the lining, and I put my hand in-I asked him how
he accounted for having those four coins; he said, "That is the money
I was paid for my wages to-day by Mr. Pettit”-I said, “Well, I shail
go and see Mr. Pettit and inquire whether he paid you these five bad
shillings"_he said, “Well, I will tell you the truth; I have not worked
for Mr. Pettit for the last two years, what I have told you is all lies;
I will tell you the truth now; a man gave me the coins."
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. This single shilling is counterfeit; these
four shillings in the tobacco pouch are also counterfeit, but from different
moulds to the first-if a man's teeth almost met in a shilling, one part
going down while the other was straight, I should say that it was bad;
if he had bent it back again it would have broken-some of these
shillings came from the same mould as some of those in the last case.
The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate. "I deny that I gave
the conductor the bad sixpence on Tuesday night."
GUILTY-Fifteen Months' Hard Labour. The Court commended Swindell's
conduct.
