WILLIAM WESTCOTT, ticket collector, King's Cross Station, City and
South London Railway. I have lived at 26, St. Paul's Road for
five years. No. 29 is about 260 yards away on the opposite side of the
road. I generally leave for my work at five minutes to five in the
morning. I am supposed to be at King's Cross at five o'clock. My
first collection is at 5.38 or 5.40, and, as a matter of fact, I am never
there absolutely at five o'clock. A man named Varney, who is in the
employment of the London and North-Western Railway Company
lived with me at that time. He is now stationed at Wolferton, but
was then stationed at Euston. In the week from September 9 to Bep
tember 14 my time for leaving the house would be about five minutes
to five. There is an electric light standard opposite the house. I fan
cied when I came down the steps I saw a figure going up towards the
"Murray Arms,” Brewery Road. There is a passage that leads to
another house at the baok, and we have had people after the pigeons
and poultry before. I could not identify the man. I looked across
and thought to myself, "Somebody after the pigeons." I pulled the
door to, went down the steps, and looked round, and as I looked
round the man turned and walked off. I saw in the paper the descrip
tion of a man who had a peculiar swing in his walk. I am consciops
that I have had a swing in my walk, because they say it is good
exercise. Except for that statement in the paper, my attention would
not have been called to this matter at all. I have on now the coat
I was wearing that morning. (Witness turned up his collar, put on
his hat, and gave an illustration of his method of walking.) I have
come forward perfeotly voluntarily and made a communication to Mr.
Newton and had an interview with him and yourself last Sunday.
Cross-examined. It would take me from 20 to 25 minutes to walk
from my house to King's Cross. If we get there at 5.15 they book na
down as having been there at five o'clock. I should not pass No. 29.
As soon as I leave my house I turn down College Street.
FRANCIS GEORGE VARNEY, in the employ of the London and North
Western Railway, now stationed at Wolferton, gave evidence as to
calling the last witness in the week of September 9 and 14, and said
he had noticed Westeott's peculiar walk.
