HEAD, Charles (24, waiter); forging and uttering certain orders
for the payment of money, to wit, three bankers' cheques for the pay-
ment of £5, £5 10s., and £5 respectively, with intent to defraud.
Mr. H. Houston prosecuted.
As the end of the first witness's evidence prisoner pleaded guilty
to the first charge. He further confessed to having been convicted
st North London Sessions on July 4, 1905, of felony, receiving 12
months' hard labour; and another conviction was proved of forgery
and stealing watehes. The Recorder said he had grave doubts
whether prisoner should not be sent to penal servitude.
Sentence, Twenty months' hard labour.
BYRCH, Arthur Newman (19, messenger); stealing a post letter
containing a certain valuable security, to wit, & postal order for
88. 6d., the property of the Postmaster-General, he being employed
under the Post Office.
Mr. Forster Boulton, M.P., prosecuted.
WILLIAN GLEN, 298, Nuneaton Street, Glasgow, labourer. I pur-
chased postal order for 8s. 6d. produced and posted it at 8.50 p.m.
on May 31 ina letter addressed "M.A. Temple Association, 10,
Pugh Place, Golden Square, London." I kept the counterfoil pro-
duced. It was to purchase patent medicine for a friend.
LILIAN THOMAS, clerk in the office of the Century Thermal Bath
Company, Limited, proprietors of the M. A. Temple Association, 10,
Pugh Place, Golden Square, vendors of Flesh Food. The business!
it carried on at 205, Regent Street. Letters are fetched from the
post office at eight a.m. in a sealed and looked bag. I open letters.
We have not received the postal order produced.
CHARLES ABRAHAM Bart, overseer Western District Post Office,
Vere Street, W. Letter posted as stated to the M. A. Temple Com-
pany would reach my office at 7.29 a.m. on June 1. It would be
placed in a callers' box on the counter, where it would remain until
nine p.m, on that day; the letters are then placed in a sealed bag,
which is put in the sorters' office, with the exception of Saturday,
when the bag is sealed and placed in the inspector's room, which is
locked.
ALFRED SAMPSON SHIELDS, assistant inspector of telegraph mes.
sengers, Western District Post Office. Prisoner was employed under
me as boy telegraph messenger, and he occasionally took over the
pneumatic tube duty for meal reliefs for half an hour, when he would
be occupied within two feet of the callers' letter-box. On June 1
prisoner's duties were from nine a.m. till one p.m. From 12.30 to
one p.m. he relieved the tube attendant at the front counter.
