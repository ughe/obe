THOMAS BEIRNE (police-constable F 140.) I was on duty in Bedford.
bury, on the night of the 6th of February—I heard the cry, and went
up to the shop—I found the prisoner and the prosecutor struggling in the
kennel—the prisoner was over the prosecutor—I took him—we went
into the shop, and the place was all ransacked—the prosecutor found he
had lost four half-crowns and a little box of buttons—I found the box
and the half-crowns in the prisoner's pocket.

Prisoner's Defence. I was going along, three parts tipsy, saw the door
open, and went in to get a light for my pipe—the gas was burning—I put
these buttons and the money into my pocket—when the officer was asked
before the Magistrate if there were any marks of the door being forced
open, he said c' No."
THOMAS BEIRNE re-examined. I did not say any thing, for I had not
seen it then—since then I have seen two marks on the door-post, as if
with a bradawl, to push the lock back.
