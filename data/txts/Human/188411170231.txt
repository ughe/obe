THOMAS HANSON. I am a plasterer at 134, Weston Street, Ber mondsey—about a quarter past 12 on Sunday morning, 21st September, I was going along the Old Kent Road—I saw a crowd and saw Nash and Harris struggling—I saw Harris run away and heard Nash say "I am stabbed"—I saw Arnold standing about ten yards off, bleeding from the thigh—I fetched a constable and a cab and went with him to the hospital—I afterwards saw Harris in custody. THOMAS HAYNES( Policeman P  267). On Sunday morning, 21st Sept., about a quarter past 1, I was on duty in the Old Kent Road—Hanson came to me; in consequence of what he said I went across the road and saw Arnold insensible in the arms of two men, bleeding very profusely from the left thigh—I saw blood on the pavement—I at once got a cab, and with assistance lifted him in and took him to Guy's Hospital—Nash was also brought there in a cab; he had been stabbed—I saw a mark on his back—Nash handed me a hat—I afterwards saw the prisoner at the station without a hat—he was apparently shamming drunkenness—I tried the hat on him; he said it was his—on 3rd October I was outside his cell while Ferris was examining him to see if there were any marks on his arm, and I heard the prisoner say "I would like to serve you, you b—, the same as I served the other two, and would if I had the same tools"—when I first saw him at the station there were some marks of blood and mud on his face.
   Cross-examined.  We were not talking to him when he said this; I was standing outside talking to a witness—he appeared to be serious when he said it. JOHN FERRIS( Policeman M  136). About 1 in the morning of Sunday, 21st September, I was on duty in Tabard Street, Old Kent Road—in consequence of what some men said to me I went down Falstaff Yard—when I got halfway down I saw the prisoner there without a hat, bleed ing from the nose and mouth—somebody called out "That is the man that stabbed two men"—I told the prisoner I should take him into custody on that charge—he said "All right, master, but look at the way I have been knocked about"—as I was taking him to the station some man gave me this carving knife—there was no blood on the blade; there was just a slight stain on the handle—at the station I searched the prisoner, and found on him this clasp knife—there were fresh blood stains on the top of the blade, and about an inch and a half down—it was shut when I took it from him—on 8th October, in consequence of what occurred before the Magistrate, I examined the prisoner in the cell passage to see if I could find any marks upon him—there were none—while I was doing it he said "I would like to serve you the same, you b——, as I served the other two, and would, if I had the same tools."
