288. THOMAS LLOYD (20), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
MR. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted.
LILIAN RUTH COOK. I am the daughter of John Cook, who has two
shops in Peckham, a tobacconist's and a newspaper shop-on March
7th, I was in the tobacconist's department, and about 7.30 p.m. a person,
whom I believe to be the prisoner, because he used to come very often,
came in and asked for a pennyworth of shag—he tendered a florin, and I
gave him ls. 11d. change, and put the florin in the till, on top of the
silver - I cannot say whether there were other florins there--I took it out
three or five minutes afterwards, and am perfectly certain it was the same
coin--I sounded it and believed it was good and put it back again-I had
a good look at it—a lady looked into the shop and wanted change, and,
being doubtful of the coin, I sent it into the next shop by Bessie Cook-
my father came and made a communication to me--my cousin was in the
shop- the same person, I believe, came again on the Friday, for a penny-
worth of shag, and gave me a florin-I asked if he had no smaller coin-
he said, “ No "-I said, “I must get change "-I communicated with Miss
Dawes, and my father came and had a conversation with the prisoner.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I am not positive now that you are
the man, but I was at the time.
BESSIE LILY Cook. I am a cousin of the last witness-on March
17th I was with her at the tobacconist's shop when the prisoner came in
-I have no doubt of him-I took the coin in-my uncle--on March 26th
I was called into the shop, and saw the prisoner and a constable, and had
no doubt that he was the same man.
Cross-examined. I knew you because you put me in mind of some-
