 1361.  WILLIAM HURLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 1 watch, value 15s., the goods of William Gray.
   RUTH GRAY. I am the wife of William Gray- he is a baker. On the 28th of April the prisoner came for some flour for his master; I had no one at home, and I sent him to the bakehouse to get a pie out of the oven, which he did- I then sent him to fetch some coals - a watch, which had usually hung in the bakehouse, was missed the next morning.
   SARAH NASH. I am single - I have known the prisoner about five weeks. On the 28th of April I met him in the New-road, between nine and ten o'clock; he asked me to take a watch to pawn for him, which I took to the Edgware-road, and pawned it in the name of William Roberts, as he told me - I did not know his name.
   HENRY MATTHEW BAYFIELD. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Lisson-grove. I took in this watch on the 30th of April, but not from the witness.
   SARAH NASH. I pawned it at another place - it was taken out the second morning, and brought to me; I cannot swear whether this is the watch - it is something like it; I did not notice it - I cannot say whether I think it is - I do not exactly think it is the watch.
 Prisoner's Defence. Mrs. Gray sent me for the pie and some coals - there was a boy in the bakehouse.
 JURY to MRS. GRAY. Q. Was there any other person in the bakehouse? A. The man who works there came to me at six o'clock, and I paid him - he said all was right, but he is not here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1362.  ROBERT HERRING was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of Thomas Adams , from his person.
   THOMAS ADAMS. I am an umbrella-maker. I was in High-street, St. Giles', on the 13th of May - I felt a motion at my side; I turned, and saw the prisoner placing my handkerchief in his breast pocket - he ran; I pursued him, and he threw the handkerchief behind him; I still pursued- the officer saw it - he came behind me, and one of them tripped me up.
 PHILIP RILEY. I am an officer. I saw the prosecutor pursuing the prisoner: I saw him throw the handkerchief down and took him.
 Prisoner's Defence. Another boy took the handkerchief, and threw it on me - I threw it down, and the gentleman took me.
 The prisoner received a good character, and a witness promised to take him as an apprentice.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1363.  JOHN HICKMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of April , 4 sovereigns, 1 half-sovereign, 8 half-crowns, and 10 shillings, the monies of John Stock , his master.
 JOHN STOCK. I live in  Princes-street, and am a hair-dresser. The prisoner has been with me, as a journeyman, about three months; I have known him two years - on the 23rd of April I gave him 6l. (four sovereigns and a half in gold, and the rest in silver), to take to Mr. Myers, my landlord, next door, in part of rent - he returned, said he had given it, and it was all right; he then took a letter to the post, and never returned.
   JOSEPH MYERS. The prosecutor was indebted to me for rent, but the prisoner never brought me any money.
   CORNELIUS JOEL PRINCE HEYNDRICKS(Police-constable C 112). I took the prisoner on this charge; he said he was glad he was in custody, for then he should be punished and all would be over; that he had got intoxicated that evening, and the next morning he had but 24s. or 25s. left.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Confined One Year.
 1364.  WILLIAM HADDOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Fitzgerald , from his person.
   THOMAS HOBBS(Police-constable C 85). The prosecutor in this case is gone to France, but he gave his name as Thomas Fitzgerald when he was examined. On the 7th of April I was watching the prisoner in Hyde-park, during a review; I saw him go behind the prosecutor and take this handkerchief from his pocket - he tucked it under his coat, and buttoned it up; I laid hold of him, and took it from him - he was alone, and I believe did it from distress; I gave him something to eat.
 GEORGE STONE (Police-constable C 99). I was with Hobbs - what he says is true.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined Three Months.
 1365.  JOSEPH DIACRE and CHARLES MARKES were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of William George Brown , from his person.
   WILLIAM GEORGE BROWN. I am a clerk in a merchant's office. I was in Old-street between seven and eight o'clock on the 10th of May - I was told I had lost my handkerchief, and missed it; I did not see the prisoner.
   WILLIAM COLTON. I am an officer. I saw the two prisoners follow this gentleman and another down Old-street: and before they got to Golden-lane, I saw Diacre take this handkerchief and give it to Markes - I sent Cadby, who was with me, to tell the gentleman; and in going along Goswell-street we took the prisoners in company again - we put them into a shop, and this handkerchief was picked up there.
   JOSEPH CADBY. I saw the prisoners take the handkerchief, and I have had it ever since.
 The prisoners put in a written Defence, stating that they had found the handkerchief on the pavement.
 DIACRE -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 MARKES -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Three Months.
