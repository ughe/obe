WILLIAM CLARK. I am a cooper's labourer, and live in Easton-street.
On Friday evening, the 4th of March, about half-past eight o'clock, I wa
coming from home, and going to Laystall-street-coming up Mount Ple
sant, I saw two young men scrummaging or fighting together; I could not
well tell which I went on one side-I passed them-Parr ran after me
and caught me round the waist, and threw me into the kennel-I did not
know him before--be threw me down and doubled his knee into my belly-
I lost my senses then for a bit, and laid flat on my back, with my head be
ing bruised against the paved stones --when I recovered, I got on my
hands and knees- I found my watch lying on the pavement by the side of
me-the buttons were all torn off my waistcoat, which was torn open; and
I found my watch-fob pulled right out of my trowsers- I got up, and put
my watch into my side-pocket, and buttoned my coat-I followed the pri
soner Parr into Pool's-buildings—he returned from Pool's-buildings into
Laystall-street, when I met a policeman, and gave him in charge the
policeman took him into a wine-vault to search him, and asked me for the
watch-I delivered it to him-1 lost the seals and things, besides the watch
