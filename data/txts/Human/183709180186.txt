 2241. JEREMIAH FOSTER was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of August , 1lb. weight of mutton, value 9 d  .; and 1/2lb. weight of sum value 4 d  .; the goods of Abraham George Owen , the younger.
   MR. CURWOOD conducted the Prosecution . ABRAHAM GEORGE OWEN. I live at Lewisham, and am a butcher I have seen the prisoner come to my shop several times for the last six months—I lost this mutton about ten o'clock on Sunday morning, the 20 lb  August—the prisoner then came to my shop for some meat—there was another customer in the shop—I went to weigh some meat for that person—I had suspicion of the prisoner, and I looked at the meat I had in the shop before he came in—when I turned my head from the scale I mind a piece of mutton, and told him I thought he had got it—he said he had not got it—several persons were in the shop—he stopped a short time then said, "Good morning, "and went away—I sent for the constable, and told him I suspected the man over the road had got some mutton of mine—in went, and brought the prisoner in, with a bit of breast of mutton resembling that I had lost—he had bought some mutton, a piece of a breast. WILLIAM SUMPTER. I live in Reed-place, East Greenwich which is a mile and a quarter from Mr. Owen. I was at Lewisham that Sunday waiting for the Maidstone coach, and there I saw the prisoner not further from the prosecutor's than from here to Ludgate-hill—he had another man with him—I saw something drop, and called after them—I told them they were leaving their dinner behind—they turned off the footpath to come to me, and the prisoner said, "Will you swear that I dropped that meat?"—I said I could as safely swear I saw him drop that bit as I did the last (I saw two pieces drop from the prisoner)—the constable came and asked me if I would walk back to the. shop, and he took the two bits of meat—he had the roan in his charge at the time, and he took them back to the shop—I wont with them—Mr. Owen began to say something about some one—he said he thought this man had got it, and he would search him—the prisoner said, no, he should not—the constable searched him, and found about half a pound of suet in his pocket—Mr. Owen said that suet belonged to him—the prisoner said he had bought some suet of him—l believe there was an argument about some small quantity being bought. ABRAHAM GEORGE OWEN re-examined . I believe this is the piece I thought I lost—I cannot swear to it—1 sold him one piece of breast only missed one—the piece he bought was given up to his wife.
   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  Did you charge him with having stolen any suet? A.  No—I did not know that I had lost any—when the officer searched him and found the suet I claimed it—I will swear I did not admit that he had bought it—he did not buy a morsel of suet—if the officer has sworn it is wrong.
