BENJAMIN BOWEN. I am a clerk in the counting-house of Bradbury,
Greatorex & Co.-on 9th December the prisoner came there, and selected
goods to the amount of 5401.-he told me that he had 12001. capital, 800.
of which he derived under the will of his father, and 4001. was his own
savings-after questioning him, and getting his answers, I drew out this
paper, which he signed (This stated that his capital was 12001., the rent
his premises 1001. and taxes 201, that he had borrowed nothing, and that he
had lived, until the last few years, with Mr. Scarratt, of Kingston, who would
confirm these particulars.) He signed that in my presence-I made inqui-
ries—the result was not satisfactory, and the goods were not supplied
somebody from Messrs. Copestake's called and saw me; I believe it w
Mr. Barham-I showed him the document-this was a few days afterwards,
before I had the answers to my inquiries—Mr. Edkins also called on me
from Mr. Hitchcock's, about the same time, and I showed him the same
document.
