 Sentence, Four years' penal servitude. STOCKDALE  , George (44, tinsmith) and DUKELOW  , Peter (27, fireman) , both on August 19, 1909, stealing £1 10s., the moneys of Henry Newcombe , the elder.
 Mr. Eustace Fulton prosecuted. HENRY NEWCOMBE. I am manager for my father, the licensee of the Earl of Derby public-house, London Road, West Ham. On August 19, 1909, at 4.30 p.m., the prisoners were in my bar there were several others there who left, and the prisoners were alone. I had occasion to leave the bar, and as I got outside the door, behind which was a Cox's till, I heard a sound and turned sharply back, when I saw Stockdale with stick produced in his hands. It is a fishing-rod and has sticky substance on a roll at the end of it. There was in the till £5 10s. in silver and a sovereign and a half-sovereign. The two gold coins were missing. I called out to my father, "Dad, they are taking money from the till by means of a stick,' and came into the bar, when the prisoners ran away. I gave chase. I saw Stock-dale throw the end part of the stick away while he was running. I caught him. He said, "All right guvnor, I have not got it with me, I have just thrown it away. "I gave him into custody. Dukelow escaped. On the following Saturday night I went to Plaistow Police Station, where I readily picked out Dukelow from a number of other men.
 Cross-examined. I saw Stockdale with the stick in his hand and the money had disappeared. He dropped the coin in the bar outside the counter, picked it up and ran off. I did not recover the money. I did not see Dukelow pick any money up. He ran and threw the other part of the stick away. They had pulled the stick apart. Dukelow was not reading the paper when I left the bar.
 Police-constable  THOMAS GLADSTONE , 151 K, stationed at Plaistow. On August 15, at 5.15 p.m., I saw Stockdale detained in High Street, Plaistow, by the last witness, who said, "I want to give this man in charge for stealing 30s. from the till." I said to Stockdale, "Do you understand what he said?" Stockdale said, "Yes, sir; quite right, sir." I said, "I shall take you to the station on that charge." He said, "All right, sir." When charged at the station he said, "All right." I searched him, found 4d. in bronze and a seaman's dis charge book. When the charge was read over he said, "All right, sir."
 Detective-sergeant  CHARLES HUTTON , K Division, stationed at Plaistow.
