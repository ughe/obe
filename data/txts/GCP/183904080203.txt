COURT. Q. Can you form any judgment as to what that is really
worth ? A. I asked a person in the trade, and he said he could buy it at
13. 3d. a yard.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Would you have advanced the money you did, on
that piece of flannel, if you had thought it contained no more than it really
does ? A. I should not, a shilling a yard is the utmost I should have
lent upon it-in the evening of the same day, Baynton came to the shop,
and asked me to take in another piece of flannel-he did not bring it-I
asked him if it was the same quality he said, “ Yes, just the same"-I
asked what it contained he said the usual quantity, about 138 or 140
yards—he wanted 81. on that I offered him 77.-- he would not take
that-he took 77. 10s.-he went outside, and brought in the flannel
- I did not look at the mark on that piece, at the time, but I have
measured it since, and there are 80 yards-it appears to me to be the
same kind of flannel as the first piece-18. a yard is as much as I would
have advanced on it I did not see either of the prisoners again, till I was
at the Mansion House a woman called on me, and from what I heard I
measured the flannel.
