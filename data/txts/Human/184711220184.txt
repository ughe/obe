   MR. BODKIN. Q.  Where the books which have been produced, in the outer office in which Seager was? A.  No; they were generally on the table in the inner office—I have seen Seager in the inner office when I have called—I had no opportunity of looking into the books, or seeing what the transaction were.
   MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Did you ever see Seager in the inner office except in the presence of Gardiner and Holliday? A.  He was there when I drew this bill, and instructed me to draw it in the name of Gardiner and Holliday—bill-book and other books appeared to be then lying on the table, and letters and papers. JOHN PEARCE. I am carman to Mr. Gray. On 11th June I took six packages, marked "H C" in a diamond, to the Custom-house Quay wharf—I took with me a delivery order, signed "Gardiner and Co."—on the 15th and 16th I delivered ten more at the same place—I gave those on the 11th to Mr. Brown. JAMES BROWN. I am a clerk at the Custom-house Quay wharf—on the 11th of June I received from Pearce at the wharf six packets of cordage and a receiving order—( produced )—this is it—on 12th June I received this order for the delivery of those six packages, I can't say who brought it—they were delivered—the order was to deliver to Ward's lighterman. GEORGE WILLIAMS. I am a clerk at the Custom-house Quay wharf—on 12th June six packages were delivered to Ward's lighterman, by this delivery order. JOHN HOLDSWORTH. I am a waterman and lighterman in the employ of Mr. Ward—on 12th June I received these packages on board the lighter, took them to the Kent and Essex wharf, Bankside, and left them there—Seager ordered me to do it—he went with me in the lighter—I knew him before—he asked one of the clerks to allow him to leave them there till Mon day, as that was Saturday.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  How long have you been a lighterman? A.  About 40 years, and am well known in the trade—I knew Seager ten or fifteen years ago, when he was a respectable tavern-keeper at Finchley—I have not seen him for the last two or three years, because he has been out of the way, apparently, to me—he appeared to me to be thrown out of business by the railway—all the time I knew him he bore a perfectly re spectable character—I did not know where to find him—he did not desire me to conceal this transaction—he does not appear a person knowing much about shipping business.
   COURT. Q.  Was any reason assigned why the goods were removed there till the Monday? A.  They were going to Paul's wharf, but on account of the tide I persuaded him to land them there, or they would have had to lay in the boat all Saturday and Sunday.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  Do you know Mr. Booth's, in Watling-street? A.  No—Paul's-wharf might be the nearest landing-place to Watling-street—my orders were to take them to Paul's wharf—I have known nothing of Seager's character of late years—I know nothing about his being in custody in the course of the present year—he would know that taking goods to Paul's wharf, was not the way to the Cape of Good Hope.
