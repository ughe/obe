   Cross-examined. Q.  You say you heard the prisoner declare he thought the Sergeant-major had a spite against him? A.  Yes. THOMAS MOORE. I am a bombardier in the second battalion of the Royal Artillery. After this occurrence had taken place I went into the room No. 7, and the doctor came in soon after me—I examined the prisoner's poach about eleven o'clock, and found two bundles—one appeared smaller than the other—it appeared to me that one cartridge was gone out of one of the bundles, but I did not open them—I could tell by the feel and ap pearance—it was tied in a different way to what it usually is—I had not the slightest doubt that one had been taken out—one could not be taken out without my knowing it. WILLIAM RICHARDSON. I am a surgeon and medical officer. I examined the body of the deceased—I found a gun-shot wound passing through the body—that was undoubtedly the cause of death.
   COURT . Q.  Could you tell on which side it entered? A.  On the right of the spine, between that and the lower angle of the blade bone, and came out between the centre of the breast and the right nipple—it broke one rib in front, and two ribs behind, and passed through the heart. CHARLES JAMEISON. I am a gunner and driver in the second battalion. I know the prisoner—I saw him on Saturday afternoon, the day before this happened—he was speaking to me about his extra parade—he had to parade with the night guard, and he told me he would make it all right in the morning—he said nothing else to me that night—he said some time before that the Sergeant-major had a spite against him—I do not recollect bow lately before the Sunday that was. GUILTY — DEATH. Aged 18.
