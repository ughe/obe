   MR. HUDDLERTON called JAMES DORE. I live in Lant-street, in the Borough—I have known the prisoner about two years—he has been an honest man as far as I have known and heard—I called at his house one morning, in May, and heard Mr. Rice drive up with his horse and gig, with footman with him—he got out of the gig, came in front of the bar, and said, "Mr. Crussell, have you sold those things of mine yet?"—crussell's reply was, "No, I have not yet, Sir"—he said, "I wish you would"—Mr. Crussell said, "I had an offer for them yesterday, and I can get 12 l . for them, half in money, and half in goods"—Mr. Rice's reply was, "What goods?"—he said, "Coppers"—Mr. Rice made a reply, and said, "I wish you would sell them, and do the best you can with them;" and he said, "Good morn ing," and went away—Mr. Crussell sard, "That was ray landlord."
   MR. BALDWIN . Q.  When did this take place? A.  I do not know—it was some morning in May, from ten to eleven o'clock—I do not remem ber the day of the week, nor the day of the month—it was in the month of May—I would not say what day—I think about the 7th or 8th of May—I was in the habit of calling at Mr. Crussell's house, and other houses, for orders for cigars and tobacco on my own account—I saw Mr. Rice and his servant come there with a gig—I did not know Mr. Rice before, but Mr. Crussiell said that was his landlord—there was only me, and Mr. Rice, and the prisoner, and the footman, there, and the gig outside—this was in front of the bar in the Prince of Wales, in the Dover-road—there was no one else present—the prosecutor said, "Mr. Crussell, have you sold those things yet?"—these were the words—there was nothing said about fixtures—he said, "Those things in the back premises"—back premises were men tioned—I said the things in the back premises—Mr. Rice did not come in a carriage—it was a horse and two-wheeled gig, like what some of the doctors drive—I saw it.
