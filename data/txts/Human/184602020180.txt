647. CHARLOTTE RAND was indicted for stealing 73 postage stamps, value 6 s  . 1 d  .; 300 sheets of writing-paper, 10 s  .; 300 sheets of other paper 10 s  .; 3oz. weight of wafers, 6 d  .; and 129 letter envelopes, 2 s  .; the goods of Thomas Cockburn and others: 41bs. weight of sugar, 2 s  .; 1 1/2lbs. weight of black-lead, 4 s  .; 121bs. weight of butter, 12 s  .; 1 plate, 1 s  . 1 soup-ladle, 2 s  .; 2 dishes, 2 s  .; 1lb. weight of starch, 6 d  .; 2 ounces weight of spice, 6 d  ., 8 ounces weight vermicelli, 6 d  .; 6 kitchen cloths, 2 s  .; 3 boxes of lucifer matches, 6 d  .; 1 knife, 1 s  .; and 1 fork 1 s  .; of Edward Wilson her master; and ROBERT RAND for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. EDWARD WILSON. I am secretary to the British Plate-glass Company; Mr. Thomas Cockburn is one of the proprietors, and there are several others; I live at Albion-place, Blackfriars-road, where the business of the company is carried on; the prisoner Charlotte Rand was cook in the family, and had access to all parts of the house. In consequence of information, I directed the officer to watch her, and on Monday evening the 5th of Jan, she was brought back to the door in custody—I went with her and the officer to the station, and after having been there I went with the officer to No. 32, Wel lington-street, Waterloo-town, Bethnal-green—I there found Robert Rand—the officer stated to him that his wife was in custody for robbing me, and asked whether he had at any time received from her any articles that she had taken away, and whether he had any in the house—he stated certainly not, and he hoped I should be lenient with the woman; he had never received anything but a very few pieces of broken victuals—the officer said, "Well, I presume there will be no objection to searching the house?"—he assented, and we proceeded to search—in a desk in the lower room were these seventy-three postage-stamps—I cannot identify them—there were such articles amongst the property of the company—I cannot say that we have missed such—we have them in sheets—in the bed-room we found a quantity of paper and envelopes—the paper was of various qualities and descriptions, all of which are used by the company—( looking at the articles )—this kind of paper the com pany have from the manufacturer, at Maidstone, and this other from a dealer—we found 129 envelopes, some of which I had purposely made for the company—I think there were seven or eight screws of paper containing sugar, some loaf sugar, some in powder, and some in lump—we keep our white sugar in powder—there was some black-lead, some mould candles, six or seven different bundles of scouring-paper, containing eight or nine sheets each, about 100 sheets in all; two large pans of butter, one plate, two dishes, and a soup-ladle—this is the plate—I know it; it has been with us fifteen or sixteen years—we found some kitchen cloths, some boxes of lucifer matches, and a knife and fork—I believe them to be my property and the property of the company—some of them I know.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You have not missed any of these? A.
