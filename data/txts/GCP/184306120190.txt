1952. ELLEN PALMER was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June
1 sovereign, 2 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the monies of Stephen Goldine.
from his person.
STEPHEN GOLDING. I am a sailor, lodging in Bow-lane, Poplar. At four
o'clock on the 6th of June, I went into the Harrow, at Poplar-we had some
thing to drink- I met the prisoner there, and went home with her about five
to Red Lion-court-I had one sovereign, a half-crown, and some silver-I
threw myself on the bed, and in less than three minutes the prisoner came in
and deprived me of my money-I am certain it was her-I was not asleep-
I made no disturbance, but after she went out, I went to sleep, and in the
morning when I got up, I found my money was gone, and then I recollected
that the prisoner took it.
Prisoner. Q. Did you not give me the money? A. No.
ELIZA MASTERSON. The prosecutor and the prisoner came to my house,
and, about five minutes after the prisoner came down and gave me a half-
crown to pay for the bed, and she had some other money in her hand-she
went out-I said, “Don't go out and leave that man"-she said, "I shan't
be long"-I never saw her any more.
John ABBOTT. The prisoner came in front of my bar about five o'clock in
the morning, and asked me to take care of the sovereign-she walked into the
tap-room, and a short time after brought me 4s., and asked me to put them
with the sovereign-she called for a quartern of gin, and I did not see her
again.
THOMAS SMITHERS (police-sergeant K 3.) I found the prisoner concealed
under the bedstead at her mother's—she said she knew nothing of the prose-
cutor or his money.
Guilty. Aged 20.-Confined Three Months.
1953. ANN MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of May, 1
blanket, value 28., the goods of David Adams.
MARY ANN ADAMS. I am the wife of David Adams, and live in Match-
walk, Shadwell. The prisoner took a furnished lodging of me, and left on
the 27th of May, without notice-I missed this blanket.
WILLIAM CARPENTER. This blanket was pawned by the prisoner on the
27th of May, for 1s.
· Prisoner. I pawned it to get a little food.
Guilty. Aged 35.-Confined One Month.
1954. GEORGE CANNON was indicted for stealing on the 17th of May,
9 yards of silk, value 31, 58.; 1 couch-cover, 45.; 1 squab-cover, 18. 6.;
1 pillow-case, 18.; 1 stool-cover, 18.; 2 cushion-covers, 58.; and 6 yards
of merino, 28. 6d. ; the goods of Elizabeth Wells, his mistress; and that he
had been before convicted of felony.
ELIZABETH Wells. I am a widow, and am a calico-glazer- I live in Wile
liam-street, Curtain-road. On the 17th of May the prisoner came as my
errand-boy- I had no character with him-I sent him with nine yards of calico
and these other things to the Cross Keys, St. John-street-he was to book
them-he came back in a short time, and went away, and did not return
have not heard anything about the things—they were to have gone to mar
Stracey, a dyer and scourer, at St. Alban's—the prisoner did not give any
notice about his leaving.
WILLIAM HAWKINS. I am a book-keeper at the Cross Keys, St. John,
street. On the 17th of May the prisoner did not come there-he did not
bring any parcel for Mr. Stracey--I am quite sure of it.
