379. JAMES HATTERSLEY (20), JOHN POTTENDEN BIG
GENDEN, and JOHN GEORGE (a convict), Forging and uttering
a receipt for 871. 98. 8d., with intent to defraud.
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS Prosecuted Mr. FULTON appeared for Hattersley:
MR. PURCELL for Biggenden; and MR. STEWART WHITE for George.
FREDERIOK Day. I am publican out of business, and live at 125,
Globe Road, Mile End—I became acquainted with Hattersley nine or ten
years ago, when my father kept a beer-house in Old Ford Road-
Hattersley was in the habit of coming there he is a solicitor's clerk-I
first entrusted him with some County Court business about 1876–he
then lived in Sidmouth Street, Hackney-I did not know who his
principal was—some Chancery proceedings were taken in 1877, and I
employed him to represent me he told me he was in Mr. Biggenden's
service-I paid him money from time to time on account of costs-he
had not then delivered any bill of costs or asked me for payments on
account except the payments he disbursed-I learned from him that an
order had been made in August, 1879, for me to receive 877. 98. 8d., in
these Chancery proceedings, and I instructed him to obtain that order
George to personate me and receive this money, or anybody else, or to
to get anybody to endorse my name on the cheque-I did not receive
871. 98. 8d. from him or from Biggenden--not having received the money,
in November, 1879, I had a conversation with John Must, & clerk in
Hattersley's office, in consequence of which, two or three days afterwards,
I saw Hattereley and said "Have you received the two legacies due to
me?”_ he said "No"-on another occasion I said "I have heard that
you have received the money”_he said "I cannot receive it without
you," and asked me who told me so-I said "A clerk in your employ"
-he denied it, I saw him again a few days afterwards, and said "I
know you have received it and somebody must have personated me and
committed forgery”—I also said "I heard that you gave a man half a
sovereign to personate me and forge my name"-he said "Well, I have
received it, and it is all ate up in costs, and if you ask me any more I will
send you a very hot bill in " I put the matter into the hands of Mr.
Daniels, a solicitor, and some time afterwards it was taken up by the
Public Prosecutor-the receipt is not in my writing, nor did I authorise
any one to write it-I did not attend there with Biggenden-I never saw
this cheque till I was at the police-court--this is not my endorse-
ment on it.
