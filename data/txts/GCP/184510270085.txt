2024. THOMAS MILLER was indicted for stealing 1 needle-case,
value 4s., and 1 cotton-box, 28., the goods of Robert Saw; and that he
had been before convicted of felony.
ROBERT Saw. I live in King-street, Hammersmith. I had a cotton-
box and needle-case safe in my shop at six o'clock in the evening, on
the 23rd of Sept.-I missed them afterwards--these are them.
REBECCA SEAGRAVE. About eight o'clock that evening the prisoner
came to my shop and offered some lucifer matches--I suspected him,
and went to my door-I saw him steal a small tart-I informed the
officer, and he took him.
EDWARD URTHEY (police-sergeant T 19.) I went after the prisoner,
and found these articles on him-he said his father gave him them to
sell.
Prisoner. I went into the shop; I did not know whether they were
playthings or not.
JOSEPH YEARP (police-constable T 38.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's conviction from the Sessions House, Clerkenwell-(read-Con.
victed 12th Aug., 1845, confined one month, and whipped")—the prisoner
is the boy.
Guilty. Aged 10.-Judgment Respited.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)

2026. WILLIAM JONES was indicted for breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of Samuel Rutter, at Ealing, on the 21st of Oct., and
stealing therein 2 watches, value 5l. 58.; and 1 watch-case, 10s., his pro-
perty.
SAMUEL RUTTER. I am a watch-maker, and live at Old Brentford, in
the parish of Ealing; it is my dwelling-house. On the 21st of Oct., be-
tween seven and eight o'clock in the evening, I received information-I
looked at my window-I found it broken, and two watches had been
stolen therefrom-I proceeded after the prisoner, and overtook him at the
stone, which the watches were found within six inches of-I did not say
anything to him ; I waited for Mr. Ralph, to know if the prisoner was
the person he saw at my window-he said yes-the prisoner was then
watched, and an officer sent for-the watches were found within a quarter
of a mile of my house-these are those I lost that evening, and I have the
dial belonging to this case here the window bad been quite whole
before.
Eliza Rurter. I am the wife of Samuel Rutter. I saw the prisoner,
about three o'clock that afternoon, standing at the window-I am sure he
is the personat a little after six in the evening I heard a noise at the
window-I looked, and the watches were there safe.

o'clock that evening-I saw the prisoner standing opposite the prosecu-
tor's house-I took notice of him-I went out again in about a quarter
