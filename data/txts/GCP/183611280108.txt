Q. Before you went out of the house did you tell the landlord you had
lost your money? A. I got change for half a sovereign there, and I had
lost money while I was in the house-I told him so I had three half-
crowns, and paid him for what I had that was taken out of my pocket be-
fore I left the house I had four half-crowns besides, and three shillings,
and three sixpences.
COURT Q. How came you then to change the half-sovereign? 4. I
had paid a half-sovereign for a sixpence once by mistake, and I changed it
that I should not do the same again.

M. PAYNE. Q. You have said to-day that Spinks was the man
who followed you out, and said he could find you a bed 1 A. I believe he was
the man-I do not recolleet saying that Spinks was not the man, but that it
was one who had not yet been apprehended-1 do not recollect saying so
before the Magistrate.
COURT. Q. Did you not swear on the last trial that the person who
followed you out was a man in a flannel jacket, and you could speak to
nobody else who was in the public-house--you now say the prisoner was
in the public-house? 4. Yes, be was--I left the two men who joined us
in the public-house when I went out--this man took my money from my
pocket.
MR. PAYNE. Q. You said just now the prisoner was the person who
seized you by the throat, and threw you down, and was on you? 4.1
am confident the prisoner was upon me I always said so, as far as I can
recollect-I think I told the Magistrate it was the prisoner, as far as I can
recollect-I consider I was not tipsy-I was sober enough to distinguish
all that took place---it was a dark foggy night I do not recollect having
seen the prisoner since, except before the Magistrate.
