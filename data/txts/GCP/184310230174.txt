JOHN FIELD. I am a veterinary surgeon, employed on the premises of
William Reid and others, in Liquorpond-street- they had some nails of
particular sort-I lost some of them, to the value of about 40s.--I believe
these produced are them--they correspond in every respect.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. What nails are they? 4. For homme
shoes-I gave the description for the pattern of them I have never seen any
like them-I cannot say when I had last seen any of these--we have missed
nails for the last two years; but I cannot say that all these left at one time.
LEWIS DURANT. I am foreman to Mr. Branscomb, an ironmonger--We
supply the prosecutors with these nails--they are made for no one but them.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you not reason to believe that there are other
nails of this sort made ? A. I do not think there are they are drawn from
under the head very differently to what the generality of nails are.
HENRY REDMAN ( police-constable G 140.) I stopped the prisoners with
these nails in Goswell-street, on the evening of the 13th of October - Haley
had them on his head-1 asked Johnson if they belonged to him-he said they
did, and that he bought horse-parings-Haley said he worked for Johnson.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he not say he bought horse-parings all over
London? A. Yes, and that he picked the nails out of the horse-paring!
believe these nails have not been on a horse's foot.
John FIELD re-examined. I believe people in shoeing horses, drop some
nails on the ground-a poor man going about buying the horse-parings might
get a few nails, but very few, and they would not be in this condition--a fer
of these are rusty, but not many.
Haley's Defence. I am in the employ of Johnson; we travel all over
London; I have the sifting of the parings, and I put the new nails on one
side, the old ones on the other, and take them to a farrier's shop and sell
them.
JOSEPH GINGER (police-constable G 137.) I produce a certificate of Haley's
former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office-(read)-the prisoner
is the person.
JOHNSON-Guilty. Aged 36.-Confined Six Months.
HALEY-Guilty. Aged 19.-Confined One Year.

OWENpleaded Guilty. Aged 37.-Confined Four Months.
John O'REILLY. I live in Orchard-street, Lisson-grove. Owen lived in

SAMUEL GREEN. I slept in the same room. I was going home at eight
o'clock in the morning, on the 16th of Oct., and met the two prisoners going
to the pawnbroker's-Smith bad a bundle-he had lodged at the house, but
not then.
Joser DONALDSON. I am in the employ of Mr. Haywood, a pawnbroker.
I took in these articles of Smith, on the 16th.
Smith's Defence. When I met Owen I had not the least knowledge that
he stole these things ; I pawned them by his desire.
SMITH-Not Guilty.
