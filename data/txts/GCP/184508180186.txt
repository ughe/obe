-I have got foremen similar to the prisoner, and they are all authorized
to employ men-they all pay them, and the money they want I give
them.
Q. Is it not usual for a plasterer to enter into this kind of contract, to
do the work at a certain price, to obtain the goods on the credit of the
builder, and to deduct the amount from the contract ? A. I never heard
of it, and nothing of that kind took place with me and the prisoner-Mr.
Hook has contracted with a person to do the painting, and this work is a
contract on my part- I took the work for 4087.
Q. But I want to know whether any of the plasterers that you employed
were ever hired by you, or paid by you, except those two who called on
you? A. No, except they came with him, and I gave him the money-
I gave him the money for wages once, and I believe once or twice he had
money for some beer-I think I gave him 61. about the 6th of April.
Q. Have you not been served with a notice to bring your books here,
and your contracts ? A. That notice I took no notice of.
Court. Q. You paid the prisoner the sum-total, and he doled it out
to the different persons ? A. Yes.
BENJAMIN SMITHERS. I am clerk to Mr. Robson. I served a notice

MR. PARRY to DAVID MORGAN. Q. Do you undertake to swear that no
written contract was ever delivered to you by the prisoner? A. I have sworn
it-I do not know Mr. Franks--I do not know that I have ever seen him-
I do not know Mr. Franks (looking at him)-I only saw him at the office
-I never saw him at my counting-house in my life, or at any public-house
in company with the prisoner- I swear there are no entries in my books to
this effect, that I was to pay the prisoner so much for each house-I never
heard of such a thing as the hard core from the mortar being the perqui-
site of the builder-if he were to sell any of the remaining materials, I
should consider it incorrect-there is hard core from the mortar after build-
ing—it is very rarely sold-we are obliged sometimes to pay for taking it
away-the prisoner had no authority from me to contract with any other
parties to do this work there were first six houses, and then eighteen,
and then four small houses I did not go to his house and seize on every-
thing that I thought was mine-my things were in an open shed in the
yard-1 took my cart, and his wife pointed out the things that were mine
- he did not use his own scaffolding, it was mine-my scaffolding was
chiefly taken to my house, but some ropes and small things he took to his
place for safety-when I charged him with taking this hair, he did not say
It is my hair, you will be served with a writ of trespass"-- he never an-
swered me about the hair ; but when I spoke of hair being sold, he said, "I
will let you know all about that"-I did not give him into custody when
I spoke to him about the hair, because I was not positive that he had
stolen it-I was not as positive about it as I am now, I think I paid him
61. on the 12th of April -- I paid him from 61. to about 321. aweek, all for
wages-I cannot tell exactly how many men were employed on that work
- I used to go round each day and make a memorandum of how many
men were employed --I paid the prisoner 61., 121., 201., 281., 311. and
181., I think, was the last.
