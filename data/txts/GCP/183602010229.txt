Mr. Edwin. I am one of the clerks of Union Hall. I have got the
minutes of evidence which I took in the case of Thomas Stowell against
Robert Cullingford-(reads) "John Theak, of No. 8, Union-place, New-
ington, says; on Sunday, the 6th of December, at twenty minutes before
one o'clock in the morning, I was by the defendant's house saw two
respectable men go into the house, and at the same time two men come
out with pipes in their mouths; and soon after a female came out with a
mug of beer- I had a watch which I compared with Newington Church,
and found it correct."

Cross-examined. Q. What, do you call twenty minutes past one o'clock
in the morning: you call past twelve o'clock afternoon? 1. I should
call it so--the name of the defendant's house is not stated in the deposi-
tion-I do not remember hearing the oath administered-the prisoner
was ordered to pay the costs of the summons, which were 28. or 38., I
believe--the same defendant was brought up before, but I cannot tell how
long before (I believe within six months) by the same informer and the
same witness-I believe the fine was 40s.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. What were the names of the Magistrates present?
4. James Trail and Henry Jeremy.
THOMAS DAVID TAYLOR. I am a solicitor, and am concerned for Mr.
Cullingford-I attended on the 13th of December, when this case was heard
at Union Hall, before der. Trail and Mr. Jeremy--the prisoner appeared as
a witness-he was sworn to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing
but the truth-- he was then examined, and his depositions taken and read
by the clerk--the Magistrate dismissed the summons with costs-I asked
Mr. Jeremy what costs I was to receive-he said 88.--I cross-examined
the prisoner, and while the Magistrate was speaking to me, Mr. Cal-
lingford had paid 38. instead of my receiving it-I thought it was no
matter.
Cross-examined. Q. Your client pleaded not guilty? 4. Yes the
name of the house is written up. "County Terrace Tavern," but the right
name is County Coffee-house--they should alter it-it is situated in the
Connty Terrace, New Kent-road.
John HOWELL. I am a tailor, and live at No. 108, London-road. I
know Mr. Cullingford, who keeps the County Coffee-house-I work for him
on the Thursday before Sunday the 6th of December, I received an order
from him for a waistcoat-it was no done in time to be there before church
began on the Sunday, and I went to get there at the time church was over,
but I got there about ten minutes before one-I met Thomas Miles on the
way, and he walked with me when we got there the door was shut-Mrs.
Cullingford was in the bar-parlour-there is a window from there, which
looks into the street - I looked through, and saw her there I held the
handkerchief that contained the waistcoat up to the window-she knew me,
