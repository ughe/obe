MARY HOCQUEST. I am the sister-in-law of the last witness-these
canaries were mine--I saw the letter asking me to send them, and
believed the statement that the writer was a general dealer, carrying on
business at that address.
WILLIAM BENNETT. I live in Gower Street-on 14th December I put
this advertisement in the Bazaar newspaper: “Lady's nearly new 18-
carat gold watch, prive only 378. 6d. Oost more than double. "W. Ben-
Dett, Philpot Lane, E.C."-I received this answer: "From G. Slater,
manufacturer of solution for cleaning plate. If you have not sold the
lady's gold Geneva watch, price at 378. 6d., I should like to buy it.
Post-office order to my address. G. Slater"-I believed he was carrying
on business and sent the watch, but did not receive the money-I tele-
graphed, but received no reply.
Cross-examined by Mr. WARBURTON. I have not seen it since I have
read the rules of this paper, but the proprietor deducts a commission it
you do it through him there are several ways in which you can make
certain of not being swindled in the original advertisements you ask for
& 10-days post-office order, which would have made it safe, but that I
omitted to put in.
JAMES THORNTON. I live in High Street, Borough-on 21st November
I advertised & watch for sale for 21. in the Bazaar, and received an
answer-I tore it up, but afterwards collected the fragments—the
heading of it was similar to this other letter from George Šumby, con-
tractor and general merchant, Sunderland, residing at Monkwearmouth
-it asked me to send the watch, and said that a post-office order should
be sent by return-I sent it by registered letter, but never received the
money-I believed that he was a general merchant at Sunderland.
JOHN DAVIS (Ro-examined). On 3rd December I inserted this other
advertisement. Offering German canarios at 78. 6d. each; address, Miss Mary
Hocquest, 10, Grenvillo Street, Battersea Road, London)- I received this
answer, "If not sold kindly send by passenger train one as a sample.
G. Sumby, jun."-I sent one and received this card, “Dear Miss, -Bird
received yesterday, but has got his leg broken in transit. Kindly send
the remaining five; I will send the P.0.0."-I sent them off, but did
not receive the order-I wrote in the name of “Miss Hocquest"-I then
received this, “As I was leaving my house I was greatly surprised by
your telegram saying that you would communicate with the police; you
say money to be sent when the birds sing "-I did not say that-it goes
on, "You sent me common birds, just as Mr. Cross, of Liverpool, sells
two for 28.; I will take them into Court with me”-the five that I sent
were just the same as the sample.
Cross-examined by Mr. WARBURTON. I sent six at 78. 6d. each-Sumby
answered the advertisement, giving his address at Sunderland he wrote
again and said that he would not pay, he would rather be summoned-
there was nothing in my advertisement about the money being sent when
the birds sang—they were advertised two or three times-in one of the
advertisements I mentioned the birds' singing, but that was 14 days before
-there was nothing in his letter to show that that was not the advertise-
ment he saw, but it would not take the birds five minutes to sing-I am
an authority on canaries, I deal in them-he said that he would only pay
through the County Court, and he was put in the County Court.
