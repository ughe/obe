   Cross-examined.  My wife passed the half-crown to me and she gave the change—I tried it in my teeth and bent it—the prisoner was indoors when my wife gave it me. MARIAN ROSATI. I am the last witness's wife—about 11 o'clock on 1st September the female prisoner came in, asked for some ham, and ten dered a half-crown to me—I gave her change, putting the half-crown on the marbleslab at the back—I afterwards gave it to my husband—I gave the prisoner a two-shilling piece change and four coppers—she said the florin was a bad one—I took it back and gave her another—her half-crown was on the marble slab from the time I put it there to the time I gave it to my husband.
   Cross-examined.  I put the money on the slab—there were two florins and two sixpences there—I got my change from the back—she saw me put the half-crown at the back—there was no other half-crown there. GERALD GRIFFIN. I am a barman at the Hercules, in Kennington Road—about half-past 10 o'clock I was serving; the male prisoner came in and asked for a half-quartern of fivepenny rum in a bottle—it came to 2 1/2 d .—he tendered a half-crown—I noticed it was already dented and rather bad, and I broke it in the tester—he said he had got it from a respectable house of business—my governor sent for a constable; he was given in charge—about half an hour afterwards I noticed the female prisoner in the other bar—I did not serve her—I gave the half-crown to the constable.
   Cross-examined.  The male prisoner insisted the half-crown was a good one, he asked the constable to take it to a jeweller's shop and have it tested—I believe it was sent from the police-station to a jeweller's, and the first jeweller said it was a good one—it was afterwards sent to two others. HENRY BENNETT( Policeman L  218). On 1st September I was called to the Hercules, and the male prisoner was given into my custody—I received these pieces from the last witness—the prisoner denied it was bad, and wanted to claim it back again—he is brother-in-law to the female prisoner.
