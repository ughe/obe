744. JOHN LILLEY was indicted for stealing 2lbs. weight of sausages,
value 1s. 4d.; and 4 black puddings, 4d.; the goods of William Tebben
bam, his master.
SUSAN TEBBENHAM. I am the wife of William Tebbenham-we live at
Bow. The prisoner was in our service-on the 26th of Feb. I saw him
take some black puddings and sausages and put them into his pocket-1
took no notice, but told my husband when he came home.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Were these Epping sausages!
A. They were--the prisoner was to have 10s. a week and his board-he
never took supper-he might have had it-these sausages had been to
market the night before.
WILLIAM TEBBENHAM. When I came home my wife told me of this
I went out for a policeman, and then spoke to the prisoner-he went back-
wards, and took his hat off-- he took out what he had in his bat, and
threw it into the fat-tub-I said, “This is what I have long expected; 1
have been robbed, and I did not know how"-he took these things out of
the fat-tub, and said, “Here they are ; it is the first time; I hope you
will look over it."
Cross-examined. Q. Is this the first time you have mentioned about the
grease-tub? A. No, I said it before the Magistrate-I believe a part of
these things had been to Romford, where I have a shop- the prisoner was
in my employ for ten months-I owed him 10s.--I have his machine,
which is worth about 58.-it has been used since he was taken, by my
order.
William Morris (police-constable K 321.) I took the prisoner-I
found in his pocket four black puddings.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you known this man? A. Yes, twenty-
four or twenty-five years- I knew him when he was in business--I always
considered him a very respectable man.
Not Guilty.
