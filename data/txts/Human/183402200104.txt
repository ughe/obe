 497.  THOMAS JENKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 hat-case, value 30s., the goods of the Honourable George Anson ; and 1 hat, value 10s., and 1 glove, value 6d., the goods of John Wilson.
   JOHN WILSON. I am footman to the Honourable George Anson, of Curzon-street- I was with his carriage on the 13th of February - it stopped at his door - I got down, and a woman gave me some information - I pursued the prisoner in the way she directed me, and overtook him by Derby-street - I caught hold of him in the middle of the road, and he dropped the hat-case - I had not noticed it since we left the Custom-house - we arrived at Curzon-street about half-past five o'clock - it had been buckled on the carriage at my side - my hat and gloves were in it.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. What did you do at the Custom-house? A. We had just arrived from Calais - we were at the Custom-house about two hours - we were not detained about any gloves - there was some fog this evening - there were people about - I cannot tell where it was lost - the woman who stated she saw it taken is not here.
   JOHN JONES. I am helper in the prosecutor's stables - I lodge at the White Lion, in Derby-street - I was standing at the corner, and the prisoner passed me - Wilson was pursuing him - the prisoner dropped the hat-case - they had a scuffle - the prisoner got away, a young man who was with me picked the case up.
   SAMUEL WISE(police-constable A 86). I heard a cry of "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner run through Stanhope-gate - I took him back - this case and hat were brought to me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been at Paddington - I had an appointment to meet a young man, and was running to meet him, when I was taken.
   WILLIAM CLIFFEN. I am a plasterer, and live in Shouldham-street - I saw the man who dropped the hat-case- it was not the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. Where had you been? A. To May-fair; I was coming across Curzon-street, and saw a man running across the road, and another ran to meet him - they met in the road, the tall one struck the other, and the hat-case was dropped, and it rolled towards the gutter - I really think the prisoner is not the tall one, and I do not think the other one was Wilson - they both ran off - the tall one had a long great-coat, but whether it was black, blue, or brown, I cannot tell, as the night was so foggy - I could not see two yards from me - I ran towards Park-lane, to near where the prisoner was caught - I lost sight of both the men - when I first saw the two men in the scuffle, my two sons were with me, and could see the men, but they could not run fast enough to keep up with me - to the best of my belief, neither the prisoner nor Wilson are the men- I saw the prisoner in the custody of Wise, within two or three minutes after I saw him run away - he ran down Audley-street, and then Stanhope-street - a man, who appeared like a groom, picked up the hat-case, he was not like Wilson - I was about twenty yards behind the short man, when he ran after the tall man - when the prisoner was taken the tall man was gone.
   CHARLES CLIFFEN. I was with my father, and saw the scuffle about the hat-case, it was a foggy night - I do not think the prisoner is the man who dropped it.
 COURT. Q. How old are you? A. Fourteen - I have heard the prisoner's name since then - I never saw him at my father's house - I saw a man run away, he was not so tall as the prisoner - he had a long coat on - Wilson is the man who ran after him - when I first saw him there was a bit of a bustle in the middle of the road - I saw the man drop the hat-case, it did not roll that I saw - Wilson took it up and ran down a mews - my father went on, and we went on after him - I lost sight of the tall man when he turned the corner.
 COURT to  JOHN WILSON. Q. Are you the person who had scuffle with some person in Curzon-street? A. Yes; he dropped the hat-case, then ran, and I pursued him - I did not lose sight of him for a moment - as he was going to turn round Stanhope-street, there was a family coming out of a house, and he turned across the road, and ran towards Stanhope-gate, and the policeman caught him - the prisoner is the man - there was no other tall man present.
 Cross-examined. Q. Will you undertake to say there were no other men in the street? A. Not that I saw, except the servants - I will not say there were not.
 COURT to SAML. WISE. Q. When you took the prisoner, was he running? A. Yes, as fast as possible - I heard the cry of "Stop thief" two or three times - the moment I turned the prisoner round, Wilson came up - I saw no other men running, but some footmen who came up afterwards - Cliffen identified the prisoner as the man Wilson had scuffled with.
     NOT GUILTY.
 498.  JOHN KELLY and JOHN MILLS were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February ; 3 pewter pots, value 4s., the goods of John Warner , and 1 pewter pot, value 15d., the goods of James Alehouse.
   THOMAS GOODYEAR(police-constable, E 39). I saw the prisoners on the 1st of February, in Oxford-market - I followed them down Oxford-street, as far as Hanway-street - I saw Kelly turn round and say, "Piper," - meaning that I was watching them - when he said that, he went across the road - then Mills went down Charles-street, towards Soho-square - I found that they were parting - I ran and seized him - I found on him one pint pot - I then snatched hold of his collar, and run him down Oxford-street, where I overtook Kelly - I put him into a tobacconist's shop - I found three pots on him - Mills has a pocket made on purpose for pot stealing all round him.
 JAMES ALEHOUSE. I  keep the  Edinburgh Castle, in Welbeck-street- this pewter pot is mine; it is worth about 1s. 3d. - it was found on Mills - I have lost five dozen within the last five months.
