ERNEST BANGER, blacksmith, Southsea. In January I saw in the
"Portsmouth Evening News" an advertisement (Exhibit 13). "Man,
married, wanted as collector and caretaker for garage and contractor's
yard; permanent position for reliable man; cash security required.
A. B., 47, Clovelly Road, Ealing." I wrote for particulars and re-
ceived in reply a letter signed "A. B., for Geoffrey Jones," stating
that the duties would be to collect all moneys and take charge of
vans, harness, and horses, and that $75 was required for security.
After several other letters I met Jones at Guildhall by appointment
on January 24. He told me that the situation would be at Grove
Lodge, Tollington Park; he said he had been at the same place 30 years
ago and had 40 horses and vans there, but he had not got any there
now. He said he had a big building contractor's business at Waltham-
stow, with a number of horses and pantechnicons, and a big coal
trade business in Ealing. He said he had spent £100 in putting the
place at Grove Lodge in order. I told him I had not got £75 for
security but only £50; he eventually agreed to take £60, and I handed
over £57 10s., the deduction of £2 10s. being for my travelling ex
penses. Before parting with the money I had been over Grove Lodge
with Jones and Bee (the latter being introduced to me by Jones as
his confidential clerk), and was much impressed with the place; it
was a good-sized place, with accommodation for a lot of vans and
horses. Jones said he was spending a lot of money in putting the
place straight, and the workmen were there; he said he was going
to transfer there the business from Walthamstow in a week or two. I
spoke about security for my £60; I was told that that sum was nothing
to the stock there would be in the place. When I parted with my
money I believed I was going into a very good job. I moved up and
entered Grove Lodge on January 28 and stayed there just over two
months. The only business I did was one removal, for 28. 6d. There
were no horses when I went there ; after a week or so two horses
came. After the workmen had left a carman came; he was paid by
the week. When Jones called I used to tell him that I had no busi-
ness to do and I have said the same to Bee; they both put me off with
