 SAMUEL PUDDEFOOT, SEN. I am a  labourer and live at Finchley, in Middlesex. On the 11th of January, I lost some pieces of wood, which we make from felling timber trees, on the premises of Mr. Fenning, at Manor-house Farm; I had felled the timber, and the wood I made in felling belongs to me; it laid in small heaps on the field - I met the three prisoners on the road, about a quarter of a mile from the field, with each a full sack, but I do not know what was in them; I asked what they had got, and Gobbey said they had got some wood; I told him they had been into Mr. Fenning's field, they said they knew they had; I said it was my property; they offered to carry it to my house if I would settle it - I would not; they said they thought the wood belonged to the gentlemen of the ground; if they had known it was mine, they would not have taken it. I left them and went on to my son; who had been left to watch the wood.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When you left them, where did they go? A. They went home. I took out a summons for them on the Monday, and they were before the Magistrates on the Friday; I have not got the chips here; they took them home I suppose; I never saw them at all - they would have been worth 9s. if they were sacks of chips: the Magistrate did not demand of these men 14s. a sack; or say they should go to the tread-mill for a month; he said 3s. a sack, but the whole expense came to 14s. 3d.; he did not say he would send them to the tread-mill in my presence; their families have gone to the parish since.
   SAMUEL PUDDEFOOT. I am a labourer. I saw these men come to the field, and fill the sacks with my father's wood; I did not speak to them; I thought I had better not go near them.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far were you from them? A. About twenty-five yards; they could not see me, it was very dark; it was about five o'clock; I could see them, because I was watching - I dare say they could have seen me if they had looked: I followed them, when they were going off with the wood, but I was not near them when my father met them - I did not go off the premises; I was not near enough to distinguish their faces, nor the colour of their clothes, but I saw three men filling sacks.
 COURT. Q. How do you know that these were the men? A. By my following them, and my father meeting them directly - I did not see him meet them: if my father had not met them, I should not have known it was them.
     NOT GUILTY.
 673.  HENRY GARNSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of January , 11lbs. of bacon, value 3s., the goods of Richard Ward.
   SAMUEL HIND. I am superintendant of the business of Mr. Richard Ward, of Kingsland-road. On the 16th of January my boy called me - I ran out, pursued the prisoner, and took this bacon from him - it had been on some baskets, about two feet within the shop; he said he was very sorry, and hoped I would forgive him.
   JOHN WILLIAM WINTLE. I am headborough. I was sent for, and took the prisoner.
   JAMES CHANDLER. I was in the shop, and saw the prisoner come and take the bacon.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I am very sorry - I had not a halfpenny - it is the first time.
 GUILTY. Aged 68. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 674.  JAMES HENREHAN was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of December , 1 watch-chain, value 4s.; 2 seals, value 7s., and 1 eye-glass, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Thomas William Andrews.
 THOMAS WILLIAM ANDREWS. I am  shopman to Messrs. Neats, pawnbrokers. On the 22d of December their house was under repair; on which day I missed these articles, and other things, out of a box, in the sale-room, which was then under repair; I had seen them safe about ten days before.
   JOHN PERRY. I am shopman to a silversmith, of Oxford-street. On the 22d of December the prisoner brought a silver chain, a seal, and an eye-glass, to sell; I asked whose property they were - he said they were "bestowed" upon him - he produced them singly - I gave him 1s. for the eye-glass, and 3s. 4d. for the seal and chain.
   ANTHONY LAW. I am apprentice at the shop which Perry lives at. On the night of the 22d of December I bought a gold seal of the prisoner for 6s.
   THOMAS HOOKER. I am an officer, and received the prisoner in charge.
   GEORGE THOMAS KEYS. The prisoner was employed in repairing Mr. Neat's house, on the 22d of December - he always appeared a steady man.
 Prisoner's Defence. The rubbish was being put into the cart - I found these things among it.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Two Months.
 675.  THOMAS HOLLIDAY was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of January , 10 lbs. of bacon, value 7s., the goods of John Burden.
   JOHN BURDEN, JUN. My father, John Burden, keeps a chandler's-shop, in Little Guildford-street. On the 22d of January I was in the parlour, and heard footsteps - I ran out, and was directed up the street, but could not find any one; and while I was telling the watchman the prisoner came by the shop, with this bacon under his arm, and was taken - he did not say how he got it.
   THOMAS SAINT. I am a watchman. I stopped the prisoner with the bacon - he ran when he saw me - he afterwards said he did it through distress; I told him I knew better, as he had been in a good situation.
   JAMES CHANDLER. I was at the Red Lior public-house, Little Guildford-street. The prisoner came into the taproom, and offered this bacon for sale.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Two Months.
