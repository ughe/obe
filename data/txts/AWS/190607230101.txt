(Monday, July 30.)
JAMEs ROBERT HArNes, Divisional Surgeon. About three
a.m. on May 29 I went to 8, Phene Street. Deceased was
lying on his back. with his head towards the window between
the fireplace and the table. On examining the body I found
the wound under the left armpit. There was considerable
blood about; the body was blauehed, and there were indica-
tions that the man had died of hemorrhage. The following
day I was present at the post-mortem. I agree with Dr. Gros-
venor's description of the wound. There must have been con-
siderable foree used. This shorter knife would, in my opinion,
be the one that inflieted the wound. On the left arm I found
two slight marks or seratehes, one inch and inch
long; on the second finger there was an abrasion about
an inch long; there was also a small cut just over the radical
artery, above the wrist, on the side of the thumb. This last
may have been caused by a knife or by finger nails; the others
I should say were from finger nails. On the index finger of
the right hand there was a sman wound of a semilunar shape
just over the middle joint; this might have been caused by the
man striking his hand against a hard substance, a chair or
table. On the right side of the face there were four super
fieial marks running down parallel, and one at right angles;
I think the four were eaused by finger nails, probably shortly
before death.
A Juror. This knife is only 41 inches long; could that
cause a wound 5b inches deep, especially having regard to the
blade having to go through the clothing A. Yes, because
the tissues about the axillary are very elastie, and with the
foree of the blow they would probably yield, so that the wound
would be deeper really than the actual depth of the knife.
Examination continued. Three ribs were broken; that may
have been the result of the artificial respiration. From the
position of the wound I think it may have been caused by a
direet thrust or by a fall if the latter, the man must have fallen
