1719. WILLIAM BROMELL was again indicted for burglariously
breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Samuel Lankester, about the
hour of three in the night, on the 3Oth of Marcb, at Lambeth, with intent
to steal, and stealing therein, 1 medal, value 7s. 1 knife, value 1s.;
handkerchiefs, value 4s. Ilb. weight of cigars, value 58. ; 4lbs. weight of
ham, value 3s. ; Hb. weight of tobacco, value 1s. ; 13 pint brandy, value,
6s. 1 shilling 1 sixpence, 1 twopenny-piece, 100 pence, 100 halfpence,
and 10 farthings ; his property.
Mr. CHARNOCK conducted the prosecution.
SAMUEL LANKESTER. I keep the Red Lion, in Fore-street, in the parish
of Lambeth-I occupy the whole house. On the 3Oth of March I was
the last person up, on my premises-I retired at 12 o'clock, after fastening
every thing except one shutter at the back of the house, which was not
fast-the window was shut-the shutter was up, and a bolt went through
the window-cill to fasten it, but we could not get into the room, having
lost the key-the bar-door was locked- came down about seven in the
morning-my premises were undergoing repairs, which Brown was super:
intending-I found the parlour shutter taken down, the window open,
and the bar-door unlocked, which was locked when I went to bed-]
found several boxes in the bar had been opened-one was a club box-
they had locks to them-I missed the money and articles stated in the
indictment-this silver medal I can positively speak to from its general
appearance, and the letter 44 P" on it-it was taken from the club-box-
I have no marks on this twopenny-piece and the handkerchief-] lost
cotton handkerchief of this pattern.
Cross-examined by MR. RYLAND. Q. Had you any mark on the
medal? A. No-it had been in my possession about eighteen months-
I speak to it with certainty-I never saw one like it-it was given into
my possession when I took the house-it belongs to the club, which is
still in existence-] am answerable for it to the club-I have no doubt of
its being the same- saw it safe the Monday week before, under lock
and key.
JAMEs LOCKYER. I am in the service of Mr. Dempster, a pawnbroker
in the Blackfriars-road. On Thursday, the 31st of March, about six
o'clock in the evening, the prisoner sold me this medal-I asked him
several questions-he said he had had it six months, had pawned it
several times, and was tired of pawning it-I told him I thought it be-
longed to the members of the Vintners' Company, and they could give
him more than I could, I could give him no more than the price of old
silver-he said he had had it by him six months, and would sell it-I
gave him 3s. for it-he seemed surprised, and said, 46 Does it weigh more
than half a crown ?"-I said, Yes"- have no doubt of him, his face
was dirty, and he was dressed as a waterman- gave a description of him
to an officer, the man who was sent round with the Hue and Cry, describ-
ing the property stolen.
