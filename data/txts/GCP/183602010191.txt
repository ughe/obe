563. DANIEL FOOT was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of January,
2 rings, value 31s., the goods of John Foote, his master. MEN
More
John Foote, Jun. I am a surgeon, and live in Tavistock-street, Co-
vent-garden. The prisoner came into my service as errand boy, on Thurs-
day, the 28th of January, at 58. 2-week-I took two rings from my finger
that evening, and placed them in a drawer-I missed them on Sunday
morning the prisoner had absconded at that time on the Monday morn-
ing I took him, and then he asked if I would let him off if he returned them
both he is no relation to me.
William Bourn. I am shopman to Mr. Lamb, a pawnbroker, in Stan-
hope-street. I produce the ring which I took in from a young man, on
Saturday the 31st of January-it was not the prisoner.
John KIRKMAN. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into custody
he denied all knowledge of the rings--when going to the station-house he
said he might as well tell all about it-he took the rings, but did it through
distress-I asked what he did with them-be said he had given one to a
boy, who he knew very well, to pawn; and the other to a boy who ran
away with it.

564. BENJAMIN WALLER was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of
February, 180 stay-laces, value Sl. 10s., the goods of John Henry Machu,
his master.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
John Nelson. I have been six months in Mr. Machu's employ. The
prisoner was also in his employ, and slept in the warehouse. Last Wed-
nesday night, about half-past nine o'clock, I came into the warehouse
-I had been out for some beer--when I came in, I saw the prisoner
in the corner of the warehouse, by the silk laces he had got the silk laces
in his right hand, putting them under a wrapper-there were boxes at his
right hand which contained the laces--they were French white laces in the
top box, and underneath they were silk-there was a white paper hanging
out of the box-I did not say any thing to him we went and sat down
by the fire, and drank our beer-about twenty minutes after that, as I was
reading, he left me, and went to the staircase, took the wrapper and put it
into the gas-meter cupboard-he then came and sat by the fire again-
I said nothing to him till the clerk came, at about a quarter past ten o'clock
--I had previously made a communication to the clerk and when he came
in, I winked at him to send the prisoner away, which he did ; and while he
was gone, we went to the gas meter cupboard, and saw the wrapper, with
the laces in it-the clerk went for Mr. Machu-the prisoner canie in while
