ELIZABETH IRVING re-examined.  I had seen Richard Shaw the evening before, when Sparrow's mother met him in Blackfriars-road, and she called me out of a baker's shop—I asked him if be knew any thing about the necklace and ring.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you not promise not to prosecute him, and say you would not hurt a hair of his head? A.  No, I did not—I said I should like to have my necklace and ring—I did not care so much about the sovereign which I had lost, as I did about them.
   COURT . Q.  What did he say? A.  He said, "I have the duplicates of the necklace and ring, which Sparrow gave me; but I did not know they were stolen"—I said, "Will you get me the duplicates?"—he said, "Yes, I will"—I waited up till eleven o'clock, but he did not come—the next day I went to him, and he gave them to me.
   SPARROW— GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Four Months.
   WILLIAM SHAW
   RICHARD SHAW NOT GUILTY.

 196. SARAH COONEY was indicted for stealing, on the   18th of No vember , 1 pair of shoes, value 3 s  .; the goods of Joseph Wilkinson ; and that she had been before convicted of felony. JOSEPH WILKINSON. I live at Kent-street, in the Borough, and am a shoemaker. I lost these boots from my door on the 18th of November—I had seen them safe about one o'clock—one of them was brought to my shop on the Thursday following, to know if I had any larger. MARY BRADBURY. I was coming from Covent-garden with a load, and met the prisoner—I asked her to help me home, which she did—she then said she had a pair of shoes to sell, belonging to her husband, and she would let me have the duplicate cheap, as she wanted a cup of tea, for she had not broken her fast that day—I bought the duplicate, and got them out—when my son came home, he tried them, and they were too small—I sent one to the prosecutor's, to know if he had any larger—he stopped it. ELIZABETH MANNING. The prisoner gave me the boots to pawn lor for her, and I pawned them for 1 s . JAMES COPEMAN. I am an officer. I produce a certificate of the for mer conviction of the prisoner—I know she is the person.
   Prisoners Defence . I went to the Royal Oak—there were several per sons there, and one of them asked me to buy the duplicate of these boots for 4 d ., which I did—I got them, and as they would not fit my husband, I sent them to be pawned. NOT GUILTY.
   Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
