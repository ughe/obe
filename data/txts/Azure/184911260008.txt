Mark Frearson, head man to Williams and Sowerby, of Oxford-street—I
do not know what he had to do with it; I did notinow of the reward till I went
to Mr. Jones's—l had no talk with Mr. Jones about the reward, nor with any-
body ; that I swear—I have no document in my possession about the reward
—I have no guarantee for the payment of it—I have a guarantee for 51. per
cent. on what the property would realise if it was sold—I do not know whether
that is the reward offered in the bill—Mr. Parrington gave it me; I have not
got it here—I got it on 11th June—I never told Bishop after that that I bad
seen Mr. Jones, either by letter or word of mouth; (looking at a letter) I
adhere to that, all the one for this letter; it was an error; I was told one of
the gentlemen was Mr. Jones, but it was not—I have always been a jobbing-
gardener—I have not had a job since Aug., that was for about six weeks—I
had been unemployed some time before that—I was two months last winter
with Mr. Hodges, of Earl's-court, Brompton—l am paid 38. 6d. a day—I
once lodged with Bishop—I was not turned out ; I gave him notice—we had
no quarrel—we were on friendly terms up to the time I went to Mr. Jones
—I have never gone by the name of King or Watts—I uecer borrowed
money of Bishop—when I was out of work, I was living on money that was
left me by my mother on 23rd-October, 1844 ; it was 1251.—1 was examined
at the Bankruptcy Court, last June— I was not examined at the Surrey
Sessions, or at Kingston—I did not learn the statement made at the Bank-
ruptcy Court before I made it—I had not got it written down.
