 592.  JOHN HARDING was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 12 ozs. of copper, value 6d.; 3 weights, value 1s. 6d., and 17 copper coins, value 3d., the goods of John Warner and another, his masters.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY MILWARD. I am in the employ of John and Robert Warner, brass-founder s, Jewin-crescent- the prisoner was in their employ. On the 17th of January, in consequence of directions, I watched him, and about a quarter-past twelve o'clock I saw him take the copper out of the pan and put it into his pocket - it was old halfpence and pocket-pieces intended for melting; I told Mr. Handley.
   JAMES WILLIAM HANDLEY. I am foreman to Messrs. Warner. In consequence of what Milward said, I fetched the prisoner from the foundry into the counting-house, and told him he had something in his possession which did not belong to him - he said he had not; I said he had - he pulled out two halfpence; I said, "That will not do, I must have the rest" - he then pulled out 12 ozs. of small copper, said it was the first time, and he hoped I would let him go; I went with the officer to his lodging, and found a 1 lb., a 2 lbs., and a - lb. weight, and a piece of copper, which are masters'.
 Cross-examined by MR. STURGEON. Q. Is it unusual for men when melting to put money into their pockets? A. No, it is never allowed.
   JOHN ANDREWS. I produce the copper, which was delivered to me.
   JAMES WILLIAM HANDLEY. This is the copper I delivered to the constable - we have a hundred and forty men in our employ.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you hold out any threat or promise to him? A. None at all - he has a wife and six children; I believe master had a fifteen years' character with him.
 JURY. Q. What did he earn a week? A. For the last twelve weeks he has earned 30s. a week.
 GUILTY. Aged 35. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Two Months.
 593.  HENRY KILLMINSTER was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MR. ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
 JOSHUA GRIFFITH. I am servant to  John Drake, who keeps a coal-shed, No. 7, Lambeth-hill. On the 21st of January, about five o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came for 2d. worth of fire-wood - it was dark; he offered me a half-crown, which I took, and gave him 2s. 4d. change - he went away; I took the half-crown in to mistress, left it with her, and in about a quarter of an hour I saw him on Lambeth-hill, in Collier's custody - my master said to me in his hearing, "Is this the man who gave you the half-crown?" I said Yes, it was - he said he had never been near our shop; I am certain he is the man.
   MARY ANN DRAKE. I am the wife of John Drake. On the 21st of January, about five o'clock, I received a half-crown from Griffith - I had received no other that evening; I remarked in a minute or two that it was very had, and put it into a corner of the desk on a book - there was no other half-crown near it; I was particular in keepit by itself - in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour Masters came to change a half-crown; I thought it looked like the one I had from Griffith - I called my husband, who went away with her, and in five or ten minutes Collier brought the prisoner in; I gave him the same half-crown as I had from Griffith - I took it off the book where I had put it; I can swear it is the same.
   HANNAH MASTERS. My husband is in the Guards, and I keep a green-shop at No. 25, Lambeth-hill. On the 21st of January, at a quarter-past five o'clock, the prisoner was outside my window - I was in my room; he said, "Halloo, mistress;" I went out to him, but in the mean time two other men passed, and he said to them, "Two or three?" they said, "Get three;" they went on, and he stopped, put his hand on a savoy, and asked the price - I said 1 1/2d. - he only asked for one; I served him - he had 3 lbs. of potatoes for 2d., which made 3 1/2d.; he gave me half a crown, which I could not change, and went two doors below, to Mrs. Buckle's - she weighed the half-crown, and it was light - it never went out of my sight; I then went to Drake's, and in consequence of what passed there, she called her husband, and the prisoner was apprehended by Collier, to whom I gave the half-crown, but cut a piece out of it before I parted with it; Griffith said, in his hearing, that he had given him a half-crown - the prisoner said he had not been near his shop.
   JOSEPH COLLIER. I apprehended the prisoner about five o'clock that day, waiting at Masters' door for his change; I took him into Drake's parlour, and searched him, but found no money on him - he had three bundles of wood in his apron; I received this half-crown from Masters, and another from Drake.
