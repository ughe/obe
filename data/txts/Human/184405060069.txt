JOHN MAY( police-constable M  207.) On the 1st of March I took the prisoner—I searched him, and found this gold chain round his neck—I asked whose it was—he said his father's, he bad lent it him to ride out with.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How often was he examined? A.  Seven different times—he was locked up in a cell—two persons were brought to see him, not more, to my knowledge—he was not tried here, there was no bill found—I took him for firing a pistol—I did not then find out where he lived. WILLIAM PARKER. I am clerk to a working jeweller, Clerkenwell. I know this chain—it was made on our premises, and sold to  Mr. Watson on the 29th of Jan., according to the invoice.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is the invoice in your handwriting? A.  Yes—I weighed this chain when it was showed to me by the policeman, and it ex actly corresponded with the weight to a grain—we may make hundreds, and all different weights—I cannot swear that I have never made one of this weight—it would not lose by the wear.  1403. JOHN WEBB was indicted for stealing 1 coat, value 16 s  ., the goods of Joseph Lynes. JOHN SULLIVAN. I am twelve years old, and am apprentice to Joseph Lynes, a tailor, in Sun-street. On the 1st of May I was sitting in the shop, and saw a coat going off the block just inside the door—I jumped down, and saw the prisoner with it on his arm—he ran up Union-street, and I saw him stopped with it in his apron. JOSEPH LYNES. This is my coat—it hung at the door of my shop—I told the prisoner two hours before that he was after no good, and he made some very bad remarks. WILLIAM PRICE( police-constable H  25.) I took the prisoner in Crispin-street, with this coat in his hand—some person had stopped him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was going down Sun-street, and saw the coat; I picked it up, and thought of taking it to the station. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Two Months .
