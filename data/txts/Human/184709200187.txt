 2227. GEORGE WELL was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; I purse, 6 d  .; 1 bag, 3 d  . 1 half-sovereign; 3 half-crown; 14 shilling; 1 sixpence; and 4 pence; the property of George Hat Clarks ; having been before convicted of felony. ELIZABETH CLARKE. I am the wife of George Hat Clarke—we keep an eating-house and coffee-room. On Monday morning, the 6th of Sept., I was in my shop at Woolwich—I heard a noise in a small room near the street door, looked into it, and saw the prisoner—I asked what he wanted—he said a cup of tea—I told him it was not his place to go there, and accused him of talking something—there had been a bead purse on the table, with 1 l . 12 s . in it—I shut the door and called my husband—before he came the prisoner dropped a silk handkerchief, which I had left in the cradle behind the door—my husband came—the prisoner said he had not got anything, but afterwards pulled the bag and money out of his right-hand pocket, and put them on the counter. DANIAL NORTON. I am in the service of Mr. Clarke. On the 6th of Sept. my mistress called me—I saw the prisoner in the shop—he said he had got nothing belonging to my mistress—my master came and my mis tress asked him where the bag was—he put his hand into his pocket, and put it on the counter—he did not say anything—my master gave him in charge. SAMUEL WATTS. I am a parish constable. On the 6th of Sept. I took the prisoner—I examined the bag, it contained 14 s . 3 d .—there was a half-sovereign, one or two sixpences, and a few pence—I searched him, and found four shillings, ten halfpence, and fourpence on him. THOMAS DAWSON( policeman. ) I produced a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction—( read—convicted July ,1846, confined three weeks, and twice privately whipped: also again convicted at the same session, and con  fined six week, to commerce from the expiration of the first sentence)—I was present at the trials—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY . Aged 17.— Confined Nine Month .
