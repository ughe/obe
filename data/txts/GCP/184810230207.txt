Twelfth Session, 1847-18.

Henry SILVERLOCK. I am a printer in Wardrobe-terrace, Doctors'-com-
mons. The prisoner was in my employ as overseer or foreman-his wages
were 50s. a week-he had access to all parts of my office and stock-I employ
sixty or seventy men-I went with the policeman to the prisoner's house in
Blackfriars-road, on 26th Sept.-one officer went in the back premises with
my foundry-man, and there found some metal lead-I did not go with him-
I found this metal block, or moulding, in the front room, on the first floor-
I believe it to be mine--this cast is made use of in the working of the
wrapper of the Patent Journal that is a work that has been printed on my
premises--the prisoner would have no business to take any type or mould
from my premises to his own-this is stereotyped-I had printed the Journal
about a year and a hall-I was not present when the prisoner was given into
custody-I bad not charged him with it before I went to bis premises.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How long had he been in your
service? 4. About two years I printed a work called the Nautical
Standard-the prisoner was not originally employed in my jobbing business,
but on the Nautical Standard-he was put on it about April - that was what
he first came to work upon-be had no other employ to attend to then--he!
was I think on piece work-he earned about 368. &-week-Jones, my over-
seer left me, and the prisoner being a steady young man I put him in the
place, and increased his wages to 50s.-Jones' wages were 60s, a week-he
was only overseer, the prisoner was overseer, and had the printing of the
Nautical Standard-Jones also had the superintendence of that--Mr. Bar.
low was the proprietor of the journal-the prisoner was not in custody when
We searched his house-he was in my employ-I took my founder, Hicks,
with me to the prisoner's premises I did not give the prisoner any notice, or
make any charge against him at that time I ceased to be printer of the
Patent Journal the week before I knew when I went to search bis pre-
mises on the 26th, that I was no longer to be continued the printer of the
Patent Journal—I gave the prisoner notice to quit my employ on the 18th
Sept.--I did not at that time know that he was going to be taken into the
employ of Messrs. Barlow and Co., to be employed on the Patent Journal-
kuew it on the Friday following, before I searched his house all that I can
recognise of these articles amount to 6s, in value--the printing of the Patent
Journal did produce a profit, but a very small one-I believe this block to be
mine, as far as a man can speak to property on his premises-I mean to
swear to it-it had been made for myself -I know a person named Miller-
(looking at a sheet) - this is not the impression of my bloek-nor is this
other-(looking at another)-here is part of it here--this one has been cut
and pieced about--this centre appears to be it, but the top and bottom have
both been cut off--Miller bad nothing to do with this casting-he had to do
with some former blocks--these were at one time in separate casts--they
were sent to me to work, and I cast them for my own convenience the
small blocks I have no doubt went back to Mr. Barlow, or Mr. Miller as
their agent, but this block was never out of my premises -I know that Cap-
Sey and Cole, two men who were in my service, lodged in the same house
with the prisoner-I did not employ them, or either of them to make any
Search at the house-after I heard that he was going on the Patent Journal
they went there, being anxious to find out the guilt-I told all the men in
my employ that property was missing, and every man must look out-I deny
that I gave them authority, or direction to make any search there believe
Capsey and Cole, and the prisoner all left my service together-it was not at
my instance that Capsey and Cole left, nor by any interference of mine-
