 1677.  RICHARD FLEMING was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 watch, value 5l., the goods of Joseph Ellis , from the person of Sarah Ellis.
   SARAH ELLIS. I am the wife of Joseph Ellis. I went to the review in Hyde-park, on the 26th of June - I was standing against the rails, and felt something move my pocket; I turned, and caught the prisoner, who was close to me, by the coat; he ran - I followed him, and cried thief! I caught and held him till he put his hand into his side pocket, and gave my watch into my hand - this is it; it had been in my right-hand pocket; I gave him into custody.
   WILLIAM HOOKER(Police-constable D3). I took the prisoner - he said, "You are quite mistaken;" I took him to the lodge - he then said, "I am sorry for what I have done, I hope you will look over it - I have lately been married."
   JANE HORNBUCKLE. I heard the prisoner say, "Here is your watch, I took it."
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1678.  WILLIAM GODBELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of June , 1 shilling, 1 sixpence, and 3d., in copper, the monies of Walter Pitman , his master.
   WALTER PITMAN. I am a boot-maker. The prisoner was in my service for about a month - I missed some money, and on the 22nd of June I marked some silver and copper, put it into my till, and went out of the shop to get my breakfast, leaving the prisoner and the shopman there - I returned in about ten minutes, and found the till had been opened; I took the shopman and the prisoner, and accused them of it; they were desirous of being searched - when the officer came the prisoner took the money from his pocket, and said I have only 1s. 9d. about me, which my mother gave me last night; he was stripping to be searched- I said that is the money I lost, and the officer took him.
   RICHARD SHEPPERD STEVENSON(Police-constable S 165). I took the prisoner, and have the money, which is marked.
 Prisoner. The money was marked after he found it on me.
 MR. PITMAN. No; it was not.
 GUILTY. Aged 12. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1679.  ROBERT PURTON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July, 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of James Thompson , from his person.
   JAMES THOMPSON. I hold a situation in the Bank. On Sunday, the 1st of July, I was at Holborn-bars, at a quarter before twelve o'clock at night - I felt my handkerchief taken - I turned, and seized the prisoner with it in his hand; I gave him to the officer, but I saw him run from him, and I held him till the officer came up again and took him - I understood the officer had been knocked down and had lost the handkerchief; and while I stood there I was surrounded by thirty or forty bad characters.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was ten yards from the gentleman when he took me, and I was ill-used by the Policeman.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1680.  JAMES SUDDY was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of June , 1 necklace, value 10s., the goods of Samuel Tunnicliffe , from the person of Rachael Tunnicliffe.
   THOMAS LOVEDAY FAIRBRASS. I am in the service of Mr. Samuel Tunnicliffe; he lives in Sydney-street, Commercial-road. On the 23rd of June, his daughter, Rachael, was in the kitchen, and had her necklace on - she went up to the door, and I heard her cry out that she had lost her beads - I went up, and saw the prisoner about five yards off. I took the child in my hand, and took the prisoner - she said, in his presence, that he was the man who had taken the beads; he said he knew nothing of them; but when the officer took him he acknowledged taking them, and giving them to another man, and said if we would go with him he would let us have them.
   MORRIS CASEY(Police-constable K 216). I took the prisoner; he said if we would go with him, to where he lived, he would give up the beads.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was five or six hundred yards off when I was taken.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Six Months.
 1681.  MARY SMITH and ROSINA SAVELIN were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of May , 1 pair of boots, value 10s., the goods of William Moore.
   THOMAS LOUGHBOROUGH. I am in the service of Mr. William Moore; he lives in Upper King-street, Bloomsbury. On the 29th of May the prisoners came with another person, and asked to look at some shoes; it was some time before I could learn which of them wanted them; at last Smith took a chair, and I got two or three pairs for them to look at; they said they were not what they wanted; they went away, and I missed a pair of men's boots; I went in pursuit; they were all walking together, two doors from my shop; I told Mr. Moore of it, and left him to mind the shop while I went after the prisoners - I found them in a passage in Southampton-row, standing out of the rain - I requested them to come back, saying I had missed something - they said they had stolen nothing, and would not come back: the other got away; but I took the two prisoners back. Savelin dropped the boots from under her arm, in Green-street - I had just sold them, and laid them on a chair, to go home.
   PETER KENDALL(Police-constable E 89). I took the prisoners; I found only 1d. on them, and a duplicate.
 The prisoners put in a written Defence, stating that they had gone with  Mary Ann Brain, who wished to buy a pair of shoes, and she had taken the boots in question unknown to them, but they had never been in the possession of either of them.
 SMITH. -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 SAVELIN. -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
