FOURTH COURT; Friday, November 23.
(Before Judge Lumley Smith.) to the
MACK, John (25, labourer), and STEADMAN, George (36, tailor).
Robbery with violence on William Blandford, and stealing from him the
sum of 14s. 6d., a waistcoat, a belt, and other articles,
Mr. Metcalfe and Mr. Nicholson prosecuted.
WILLIAM BLANDPORD, 1, Sophia Road, Custom House, second steward
I was passing by the Liverpool Arms, Canning Town, between 11.30 and
19 p.m. on October 25 when a prostitute accosted meand asked me if I would
go home with her. After hesitation I went with her to 17, York Street.
At the front door I saw two men on the opposite side of the road sitting
on a window-sill. The woman tried to pick a quarrel with me, and asked
me if I was not a detective, pimp, or spy, and who were the men. She let me
into the door and showed me into the front room and told me to get into
bed. That was the last I saw of her. Steadman and another man not
in custody came in, and Steadman started hustling me and thumping my
bend, and said I looked like going through it and he intended I should
I had 148. 6d. in my pocket and a half sovereign and a 25. piece and a
small pocket-knife. I made for the front door, but it was barred and
bolted. Steadman said, "No, you don't get out of here, and he barred
me from getting out. I sang out “Murder" and "Police !" and Stead-
man said, "If you go on like that I am going to shove my fist down your
throat and gag you." My waistcoat has never been found. I got out
and informed the police and returned to the house with three officers, I
want in with them. Stendman was lying under the bed. An officer put
his foot under and said " Come out of it," and pulled him and a part of
my property out. Steadman said he had done nothing. I saw Mack the
sme evening. He came into the room and made out he was taking my
part, but I am afraid it was only swank and that he intended injuring
me. The man not arrested was also there.
By the Court. As soon as I found the woman did not return and
heard a voice, I put half-a-sovereign under the mattress in case I was
robbed, so as to be able to lay my hand on something that was not part
of the 14s. 6d. I had already given the woman 2s, 6d. and a 3d, piece,
and she was satisfied. I undressed-she did not. I left my waistcoat
with the 14s. 6d. under the pillow. The state of my eye is tho result of
Steadman's blows. Mack defended me at first, but he was afterwards as
bad as the other, for he would not let me out of the door. I can't say
that Mack struck me.
MACK (speaking from the dock). I identify as my property the
handkerchief, belt, braces, and tie produced. On the night of October 25
I went in to have a drink and met Steadman there, who was a stranger
to me; we got into conversation and I told him I bad had a bit of an
argument at home and did not care to go home. He said I could go to
his place and have a lay down. He opened the door with a key and went
right through into the kitchen. I know no more till the constable
ame into the room. The constable had this man and he turned round
and said, “There's another one there;" and the prosecutor said, “That is one
of them," pointing at me.
