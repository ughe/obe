 2091.  ELIZA WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 18 pincushions, value 7s.; 84 combs, value 14s., and 4 fans, value 10s., the goods of David Hicks.
   DAVID HICKS. I live in the City-road. I am a turner and toy-dealer. These articles were packed up, and directed to go to Portsmouth; they were taken from my shop, and some time afterwards a man in my employ was going up Hackney-road and saw these pincushions - I saw the prisoner eight or nine days afterwards in custody; I have lost property to a great amount, but I only swear to the pincushions, though I have no doubt they are all mine.
   MARY ANN MILLER. I live in Hackney-road. I bought these pincushions of the prisoner in exchange for a petticoat and a shilling, in the early part of July - she said they were her uncles; she had been at my shop for second-hand clothes, which I sell.
   ALLEN CAMERON(Police-constable N 91). I took the prisoner by Miller's direction on the 1st of August; she was then there with these other cushions and four fans, which she said were given to her by a girl.
   LOUISA BUTLER. I am the prisoner's sister. She asked me to wash her a cap, and I found these combs in it.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Three Months.
 2092.  ELIZABETH TUCKWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of July , 1 pair of sheets, value 1s. 6d.; 1 dish, value 1s.; 2 pillows, value 2s.; 1 blanket, value 6d.; 1 saucepan, value 2s.; 1 tea-kettle, value 2s.; 1 iron pot value 2s., and 1 candlestick, value 6d., the goods of Edward Maddle.
   ANN MADDLE. I am the wife of Edward Maddle- we live at No. 14, Upper Charlton-street, Fitzroy-square. On the 13th of July the prisoner took a lodging of us as a married woman, but no man came there - she staid in our back kitchen till the 22nd, and then left without notice, and we missed the articles stated; my husband found her in Oxford-street, quite accidentally.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. Was she there when you missed the articles? A. No; a young man had fetched her away.
   MARIA MADDEN. I bought of the prisoner this iron pot, these sheets, and some other articles - she said they were her own.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long was she in your shop? A. A quarter of an hour.
   SAMUEL APPLEBY(Police-constable E 28). I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Confined Three Months.
 2093.  THOMAS BROOKERY was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of June , 1 coat, value 4l.; 1 waistcoat, value 16s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 2l., the goods of Joshua Lloyd , from the person of Thomas Anderson.
   THOMAS ANDERSON. I am errand-boy to Mr. Joshua Lloyd - he is a tailor, and lives in Conduit-street. On the 16th of June I had a parcel, which contained a coat, waistcoat, and a pair of trousers, to take to Mr. Pontifex, at Holloway; there was Mr. Pontifex's direction on it - two different men came up to me; they spoke to me on the way, and looked at the direction on the parcel; when I got near to Holloway, the prisoner, whom I had not seen before, came up, and asked if I came from Mr. Lloyd, in Conduit-street - I said I did; he then asked me if these things were for Mr. Pontifex, of Holloway - I said they were; the other two men were not there then - the prisoner said Mr. Pontifex was waiting for the things to go out; I gave him the things, and he gave me 1d. - when he got a few yards from me, he said he would give me 6d. when he saw me again, and that Mr. Pontifex was to be measured for some more things on the Monday following; the other two men were afterwards tried here, and convicted.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. What day of the week was this? A. Saturday evening; I had had tea at five o'clock; I went out after tea - I heard the clock strike nine when I got to Camden-town, before the prisoner came up to me; I had not seen him before; he did not stay long talking with me, but I am sure he is the person - I knew him by his face; it was not quite dark - I did not see him with any other person.

 Cross-examined. Q. What night was it? A. On Saturday night, the 16th of June - I know nothing of the prisoner.
