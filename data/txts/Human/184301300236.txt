   Q.  How long had you the deeds in your possession? A.  Me and my solicitor together, I believe, for about a fortnight or three weeks—then I went on to my banker's, Sir John Lubbock, and told them that I wanted a pro missory note discounted—they immediately discounted me a promissory note for 700 l ., and one for 300 l ., and I left the deeds, as a collateral security, with Sir John Lubbock—in about a month I paid the note back, for which they allowed me the interest off—I got that money in my trade—I was then carrying business at No. 13, Wood-street, as a wholesale dealer, not with Mr. Hill—it was twelve months, or more, after the dissolution with him—I have been a great many times to the house at Camberwell to inquire for the prisoner, always wanting to get money—he owed me a very large account at that time—I have seen the prisoner and his wife there on several occasions.
   Q.  On the occasion as to which you have been giving evidence, did you, the prisoner, and Miss Constable, go up stairs together? A.  I went up stairs to Miss Constable and the prisoner—they were there—I never saw Miss Constable, to my knowledge, till I met her at the prisoner's—I am not cer tain whether that was the first time, or whether it was the night before—I saw her twice at the prisoner's.
   Q.  Did you on that occasion state to Miss Constable that you were glad you had found the means of trimming  Birch, who was no lawyer? A.  I do not know that I made use of such words as " trimming  Birch," or any thing of the kind—I think I could safely swear I did not—I said that I had not confidence in Mr. Birch—I did not make use of the word "trimming"  —I said it was my opinion that Mr. Birch was not a clever solicitor—I had no reason but my opinion for so stating—I have frequently heard gentlemen speak of one solicitor and another, and as my solicitor I certainly should not have confi dence in Mr. Birch; that was my only reason, and from his conduct towards the prisoner, in not giving him information about the deeds and things in his possession—I have no other reason that I know of—I did not say that Mr. Birch had made a mess of a deed which he had had prepared for the Thompson family—I will swear that—I never mentioned the Thompson family, neither did I know any thing of any transaction with the Thompson family—I believe at the second interview I had with Miss Constable, when I copied the receipts which were produced to me, I said that my solicitor would not take these deeds as security.
