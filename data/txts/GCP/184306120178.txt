1929. HANNAH MANSHIP and SARAH VICKERS were indicted for
stealing, on the 25th of April, 1 spoon, value 38. ; 2 plates, 5s.; 1 bottle
28.; 1 cup, 9d.; 1 saucer, 9d.; and 1 stopper, 6d.; the goods of Henry Armour.
Ann ARMOUR. I am the wife of Henry Armour, a cabinet-maker, we live
in Old Cavendish-street-on Friday, the 21st of April, Vickers called at our
house to look at some lodgings which she said she wanted for a family-se
said a lady would arrive that evening, and the rest of the family on the Tries.
day following, that she, herself, was the lady's maid-I showed her our
apartments-- it was then about three o'clock, and she went out in the afternoon
and ordered goods, which were brought to the house by the trades people,
she left at six o'clock, and said she was going to meet her mistress-she came
back afterwards with another woman, whom I did not see till the next even
ing, and it turned out to be Manship-Vickers was up and down, or in attend
ance on what she called her mistress-on the Saturday evening the drawing
room bell rang, and in the absence of Vickers, the maid, I answered it, and
saw Manship in the room alone-she inquired for her maid, I told her she
was absent, and I believed giving orders to trades people for the next day-1
offered to send her any thing she wanted-she said she was alarmed about
her majd being out so late in the evening, as she was perfectly unacquainted
with London-(I think it was then about ten o'clock)-I said, if it would be,
any satisfaction to her to know that her maid was not alone, I had permitted
my daughter to walk with her on the following Tuesday. Vickers left the
kitchen about balf-past three o'clock, saying she had to meet another part of the
family, and about five o'clock that evening I found the prisoners were both gone
-I missed a tea spoon, two India China plates, a water bottle, and a stopper
for the ink stand-they were all my husband's, and were in the apartments
let to the prisoners-they went up for their use when they required them-
these are them.

Cross-examined by Mr. DOANE. Q. When Vickers came, did she not tell
you that she took the lodgings for Mrs. Barker? A. Yes-Mrs. Barker and
family-she went out, and about six o'clock she brought Manship as Mrs.
Barker- no one else came while they were there. I do not believe any gentle
man slept in the house if there was a man in the house they must have let
him in themselves-Vickers never told me there was a gentleman visited the
lady--she did not say I need not let my husband know it-they took five rooms
furnished-they had two bed rooms, and another was to be fitted up for them
- we were to turn a drawing room into a temporary bed room when the
family arrived--there is a shop at our house which is kept by a gentleman
who is a house agent, but it is quite unconnected with us I think these
things before me were valued at 15s, at the station- I do not know whether
any one would advance that on them--the prisoners brought a small carpet
bag- I do not know what it contained-I saw no boxes of clothes--their
luggage was to have arrived by a porter, as they said, but it never came!
have no mark or initials on my spoons, but on this spoon there is a small
particular bend on the upper part near the handle-I can account for 118
being done, I have no mark on any of this property, but I have others to
compare with it I believe these plates are not common-I have others
exactly like them, and I have had them many years, I believe them to be
mine.
Court. Q. How many of these plates had you? A. Six-I had only
four left after I missed these two-I know them perfectly well--I have had
them fourteen or fifteen years, they were kept in a china closet, and I saw
them from time to time—these were sent up for the prisoners' use on
day they quitted.
