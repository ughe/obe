 1468.  THOMAS EDMONDS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of June , 1 silver spoon, value 5s., the goods of Elizabeth Sewell.
 ELIZABETH SEWELL. I  keep a pawnbroker's-shop, in Gray's Inn-lane- I knew the prisoner as a customer. On the morning of the 22nd of June he came and bought plates and other things, which came to 10d.; about ten minutes after he was gone I missed a spoon - he drives a cab about.
   RICHARD BAYLIS. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner at his lodgings, in Canterbury-square, on the 22nd of June, and found the spoon there.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1469.  THOMAS SIMPTON was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 2 handkerchiefs, value 6s.; 1 shirt, value 3s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 18d., the goods of Thomas Marsh.
   THOMAS MARSH. I live in Edward-street, Hampstead road. The prisoner is a labourer, and lodged in our house for about a month - I missed these things from the bedroom, and spoke to him; he denied taking them - I insisted on it that he had them - I found a pair of my stockings on his feet, and the duplicates of my sheet and handkerchiefs were found on him - I never allowed him to pawn them.
   JAMES HOPPER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Grafton-street. I have a handkerchief, pawned on the 2nd of May.
   EDWARD HALL. I am a pawnbroker. I have a handkerchief, pawned by the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in Seymour-street, and a young man asked me to pawn them for him - when I came out of the shop he was gone.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Whipped and Discharged.
 1470.  ANN SILVER was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of May , 3 half-crowns, 2 shillings, 3 pennies, and 1 farthing, the monies of John Casement , from his person.
   JOHN CASEMENT. I work at a nursery at Kensington. On Saturday night, the 29th of May, I went out to see a friend, and met the prisoner in York-street, Westminster; she put her arms round me, and said, "Will you take me with you?" I said No - she put her arm round me, and in an instant I felt her hand in my pocket, where I had three half-crowns, two shillings, and 3d., and took it out; I had been drinking, but had my senses - I accused her of taking my money: she denied it - I gave her in charge directly, as a Policeman came by; he asked her what money she had about her, and I am sure she said she had none - he found my money on her.
   RICHARD NEWLAND. I am a Policeman. The prosecutor charged the prisoner with robbing him - she said, so help her God, she had not a farthing about her, and I might search her; she pulled her pocket inside out - I then observed her hands clenched; I opened her left hand, and found three half-crowns, and in her right hand 2s. 3 1/2 d.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1471.  SUSAN FENTON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of May , 3 pairs of stockings, value 2s.; 2 books, value 3s.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 pocket, value 4d., and 1 handkerchief, value 2d., the goods of Maria Waxham.
   MARIA WAXHAM. I am a servant out of place, and was lodging in White Horse-alley.; I left there the beginning of March, and left my box there, locked - I went to Islington, for the benefit of the air; the prisoner did not lodge there then - I came home the beginning of May, and on unlocking my box I found the bottom of it broken out- I suspected the prisoner, who was then lodging there, and spoke to her about it; she denied it - I found a pocket handkerchief and an apron under her bed; she had been lodging about three weeks in the room my box was in - I showed her the things; she said she knew nothing of them but that one day, when she wanted a clean pocket, she took mine - she was out of a situation.
   JOHN BALL. I am a pawnbroker, of Smithfield-bars. I have three pairs of stockings, pawned by the prisoner, in the name of Mary Ransom.
   JAMES TERRY. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner, and have a petticoat, apron, and handkerchief, given to me by the prosecutrix; the prisoner said she took the pocket when she wanted a clean one, but how the other things got there she did not know - at the next examination she said the books were at a pawnbroker's on Snowhill.
   WILLIAM TRANAH. I live with Mr. Reeves, of Snowhill. I have two books, pawned in the name of Ann Harrison, Cow-lane - I do not know who by.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. The officer said if I would confess, I should not be prosecuted.
   JAMES TERRY. It is false.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1472.  SARAH ROSE was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 3 yards of flannel, value 2s.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 5 yards of lace. value 10s.; 1 pair of gloves, value 1s.; 6 pieces of net, value 3s., and 2 towels, value 1s., the goods of James Wilson, her master.
   ANN HOPPER. I live with my brother-in-law, James Wilson; the prisoner was about a month in his service; we had no other servant. I missed some articles of my own- my brother called in an officer, who searched her box, and found these articles in it; it was not locked - we did not tell her we were going to search before the officer came.
   DANIEL McGILLIN. I am an officer. I was called in; Mr. Wilson told me to search the prisoner's box, which was up stairs, unlocked - I found all this property in it; Miss Hopper claimed them - the prisoner said they were her own, and not her master's.
   JOSEPH BARDEN. On Saturday, the 11th, while the prisoner was in the watch-house, she told me she bought part of the property at a linen-drapers at Shadwell, and took me to the shop - the person denied having had such things.
