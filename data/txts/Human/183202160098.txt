GEORGE CORFE. I am a farmer. This wheat was taken from my barn - I compared it with what was left, and have no doubt it was mine; the prisoner had worked for me, and knew my premises.
 Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing of the wheat - I had been watching Mr. Corfe's premises when he had expected to be set on fire; I heard a piece of work that night - I went back, and saw Young and Barrett struggling; Barrett said, "Young, you may go, and if I had known you had been this road I would have gone some other;" I gave Barrett a lift up with the wheat.

 787.  THOMAS WEBSTER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 copper builer, value 10s., the goods of Henry Wood , and fixed to a building of his.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am an officer. On the 13th of February, about a quarter before seven o'clock in the evening, the prisoner past my house in Clerkenwell, about ten minutes' walk from the building where this copper was taken from - he went into a rag-shop, just below where I lived, to sell it; he came out in two or three minutes, and told another boy it was all right - he went back, and went into the shop again; I took the other boy, and sent a Policeman, who took the prisoner; the other boy was discharged.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am a Policeman. I went and took the prisoner coming out the second time, with the copper in his hand.
   HENRY WOOD. I live at Shepherd's-bush - this copper was in a building belonging to me; I have seen it fitted, and it fits exactly - I have not seen the premises this eighteen months; it is my house, and the tenants have left it.
     GUILTY. Aged 11. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 788.  RICHARD JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of February , 2 shirts, value 6s., the goods of William Moore.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating them to belong to  William Atkinson and Henry Holtzenyer.
   SARAH MOORE. I am the wife of William Moore, and am a washer-woman - we live at Tottenham. I had these shirts on the common to dry; one of them belongs to Henry Holtzenyer, and the other to William Atkinson-I was not at home when they were taken.
   JAMES LANBOURN. I am a gardener, at Tottenham. On the 15th of February, between one and two o'clock, I saw the prisoner running away with the property concealed in his apron; he might be three hundred yards from the prosecutor's house - I went and took him with the shirts; there were two other persons; we took them all, but the other two were discharged; one of them had been watching near the dung-hill, and he struck me on my arm.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Did you tell the same story before the Grand Jury as you have here? A. Yes; they threw out the bill against Dison and Humphries - I am gardener to Mr. James Roe.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The things were laying in the road, and I took them up and put them in my apron.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 789.  GEORGE WELLBANK was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of February , 10 yards of printed cotton, value 5s., the goods of Edward Belton.
 EDWARD BELTON. I  keep a draper's shop in Lisson-grove. On the 8th of February, about half-past four o'clock, I was at home, but did not see this cotton taken.
   MICHAEL JOHNSON. I live next door to the prosecutor; I saw the prisoner and another boy for some time opposite my window; I watched them, and saw them run; I then went out; the prisoner had got the printed cotton, and dropped it on my rails; I suspected he had stolen it - I took it up, and he was pursued and taken; the other. boy got away; I am quite sure the prisoner is the person who was running with the cotton and dropped it; he only ran into the next street.
   THOMAS FURSEY. I am an officer. I have the property. (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence (written.) I passed the shop of the prosecutor; whilst doing so, a boy, who had taken a piece of print, threw it on the ground and ran away; a person from the shop ran after him, and I picked up the print, intending to keep it until the person who had gone in pursuit of the thief returned.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 790.  PETER WALES was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of January , 2lbs. of mutton, value 1s. 5d., and 3 lbs. of beef, value 1s. 6d., the property of Henry Rogers.
   WILLIAM ROGERS. I am brother to Henry Rogers, a butcher. On the 12th of January, in the evening, I was serving in the shop, and missed some meat off the board, but did not see it taken; the prisoner was taken by the Policeman, who brought the meat back to the shop; he had got half a mile; he had not bought any thing.
   THOMAS KELLY. I am a Policeman. On the 12th of January I was in Brick-lane, Old-street; I saw the prisoner and another boy; the prisoner had a basket - I followed them to Golden-lane, and took the basket from the prisoner; I saw two joints of meat in it - I said,"Where did you get this?" he said a boy gave it him; the other boy ran away - I inquired, and found the prosecutor.
   WILLIAM ROGERS. I can swear to the meat, by the score of the mutton, and the beef I had cut myself - it was between seven and eight o'clock in the evening.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. -    Judgment Respited.
 791.  JAMES WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of February , 1 carpet, value 10s., the goods of Mary Price.
   MARY PRICE. I live in Union-walk, Union-street, Kingsland-road; the prisoner lodged eight months with me, and was a shoe-maker; I keep a small clothes-shop. On the 10th of February I missed a piece of carpet - he was not at home then, but came home at night; I sent for an officer, and charged him with taking it - he denied all knowledge of it, but in going to the station he told me it was at the pawnbroker's.
   WILLIAM EVETT. I am a pawnbroker I took in this carpet from the prisoner.
 -. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner; he told us where the carpet was, and said he had taken it. (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. It was my intention to have redeemed the carpet, or I should have absconded.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Months.
