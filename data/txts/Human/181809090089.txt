 1332.  REBECCA MALLING was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , one silver watch, value 30s.; two watch keys, value 2s.; one piece of foreign coin, value 3d.; one pair of trowsers, value 10s. 6d.; and one pair of shoes, value 10s., the goods of John Passmore ; one pair of trowsers, value 10s. 6d.; one pair of stockings, value 2s. 6d., and one pair of shoes, value 10s., the goods of William Hall.
 JOHN PASSMORE. I am  in the LifeGuards. On the 31st of July, Hall and I fell in with the prisoner at seven o'clock in the evening, at Paddington, and went with her and another girl to Ivy-court, St. Giles's. Between four and five o'clock next morning we missed the prisoner and our property. We were intoxicated.
   WILLIAM HALL. I was with Passmore. I also missed my things.
 Prisoner's Defence. They were both intoxicated, and gave me the watch; they behaved improperly to me, and left the room.    NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1333.  JOHN BELL BRODERICK was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , one watch, value 30s.; one seal, value 1s., and one key, value 1d., the property of Thomas Brooksbank.
 THOMAS BROOKSBANK. I am a  sailor. On the 31st of August I lost my watch out of the Reward brig, which laid off Hermitage tier. The prisoner was an acquaintance; he saw me put it in the trunk, which was in the forecastle. I left him there, returned in about an hour, and missed it.
   JOHN SPENCER. I am a pawnbroker. On the 31st of August the prisoner pledged the watch with me.
   WILLIAM HUTCHINS. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner, and found the ribbon and seal upon him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Fined One Shilling, and Discharged.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1334.  SAMUEL BAMFIELD and JOHN SHEEN were indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , two pair of sheets, value 1l. 10s. the goods of James Henry Leigh.
   ANN HIGHAM. I am housemaid to Mr. James Henry Leigh, who lives in Portman-square. On the 25th of July, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prisoners in the kitchen-passage, which communicates with the area. They were dressed like sweeps, and had a black sack with them. They ran away when they saw me, leaving the sack on the area steps. I pursued, but lost sight of them. A soldier stopped them. I found two pair of my master's sheets in the sack, which I had put in the housekeeper's room just before.
   WILLIAM POTTER. I am a corporal in the Guards. I went in pursuit of the prisoners, and saw them at the corner of Portman-street, just by the prosecutor's house. They ran up Bryanston-street. I stopped Bamfield in the Park, he got through some broken rails there; I told him he was my prisoner, and must go with me; he said he would not; I found a pair of silk stockings on him, and took him back. Higham said they were the two. I never lost sight of them.
   WILLIAM MASON. I am a servant in Chapel-court, King-street, I saw Potter pursuing the prisoners. I took Sheen in Seymour-place. He threw a latch key away. Higham said he was one of them.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 BAMFIELD'S Defence. I found the stockings in the Park.
 SHEEN'S Defence. I saw him find them.
 BAMFIELD -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 SHEEN -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1335.  LYDIA HILL was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , 20s. in copper monies numbered, the property of William Luff, the elder.
 WILLIAM LUFF, SEN. I  keep  the Fountain public-house, in King-street, Seven-Dials. The prisoner lodged with me, on the same floor as my bed-room. On the 2d of September, about half past nine at o'clock night, my children went up to bed. Suspecting the prisoner, I watched her, and saw her leave her room and go into my bed-room, where my money was. She had no business there. My little girl came down and went up again, I followed her half way up the stairs, and my little boy gave me some information. I had told him to watch; I then went into my room, and missed two papers of halfpence which had been safe half an hour before. I immediately went into the prisoner's room, and told her I was sorry to accuse her, but I missed two 5s. papers of copper, and nobody but her and my children had been in the room. She strongly denied it. I said I would send for an officer. She then gave me 10s. worth, and said my son gave them to her. She said she had two more papers, which her husband had given her. He was sitting in the room. He told her she must have spent all the money he gave her. I asked her to show them to me, which she did. I told her I could swear to them, and that she had taken all four that evening. I had marked them with a cross. She then said my niece gave them to her.
