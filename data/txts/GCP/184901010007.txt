363. WILLIAM CHAPMAN, stealing 1 gelding, value 31; the property
of John Appleton.
John APPLETON. I am a rope-maker at Stepney. On 15th Aug., the
prisoner and another person came and asked me if I would purchase a horse-
Í said, I would see it in the morning-next morning the prisoner came alone
-I walked with him to the Fountain public-house, Mile-end-road, to look at the
horse--there were three men there; one of them, I believe, had brought the
horse up from the country- I told the prisoner I thought the horse was not
worth more than 558, he said it was worth all the money which was asked,
which was 31.- I said to bim, " Is it all right about this horse, you are not
going to take me in ?"-be said, No, so help him, Jesus Christ, he would
sooner suffer his arms to be severed from his body than take me in- I bought
it for 31., and told him to bring it home, which he did-he then asked me to let
him put it in harness, that he might show it to a person, and he and my man
put it in-he drove off with it-he said he should be back in an hour-he
never returned the cart and harness came back about eleven o'clock at
night.
Prisoner. I asked you to lend me 31. to buy a horse; you told me to
come in the morning; you gave me four five-shilling-papers of halfpence and
two sovereigns, and I went and got the horse-you lent me the money. Wit-
11888. I did not lend it; I gave the three sovereigns for the horse-I have
drank with the prisoner since.
RICHARD HEATH. I am a horse-dealer, of Green-street, Twigg-folly. I
met with the prisoner and another on a Wednesday, about four months ago,
at the Rabbits, in the Romford-road; he had a brown gelding to sell -I
bought it of him.
Joux APPLETON, re-examined. I saw the gelding in Heath's possession-
it was the one I bought.
GEORGE TEAKLE, (police-sergeant S 8). I took the prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. I spoke to the prosecutor about it, and said I would
pay him for it; he said, " Very well."

364. WILLIAM CHAPMAN, was again indicted for stealing 1 mare,
value 31.; the property of George William Henry Young,
GEORGE WILLIAM HENRY YOUNG. I am a horse-dealer, of Pavilion-yard,
Whitechapel. On 21st Nov., the prisoner came to my yard with Henry
Cook, and asked what I would take for the big brown mare in the stable-
I said, “Three sovereigns"-he said, that was too much-I refused to take
any less-they told me they could go and sell it for the money-I told them
to be sure and be back with the mare or the money in an hour or an hour
and a half, for I had got another customer that I could sell it to, that had
looked at it on Saturday night I allowed them to take it-they did not
come back- saw them the next day in Whitechapel-road, and hallooed out
"You are pretty fellows; where is my mare!"- they said it was a!l right-
I could not see an officer-I went on to Romford-I saw the prisoner about
a fortnight afterwards in a public-house, and gave him in charge-I tried to
get Mr. Abbott here, who bought the mare, but he would not come.
