544. FREDERICK BROOKS (37) and LUCY WRETTON (30), US
lawfully conspiring to procure themselves to be managers of a busines!
belonging to William Charles Parsons; also for personating Henry White
MR. BESLEY Prosecuted; MESSRS. WARNER SLEIGH and RAVEN Defended
HENRY WHITE. I was living on the 20th of April when I was examined
at the police-court, at 75, Kynaston Road, Stoke Newington-I am a licensi
victualler-my first house was the Lord Hill at Paddington between fine
and six years ago—I then went to the George at Holloway-I left it them
years ago I went to the Wellington in April, 1878, and left in April, 1873
- I had occasionally employed Brooks when I was at the Lord Hill, the
George, and the Wellington-he showed me my trade-I could not a
what I paid him-neither of the prisoners were ever employed by me to
manage the Wellington—I never employed Wretton at all-she used to
come with Brooks as visitors, and if I asked her to do anything she would do
it-I took her to be Mrs. Brooks Brooks was never a regular servant de
mine—it is not true that owing to Brooks I sold the Wellington ato
profit, nor that he left his position of manager on account of my leaving
The house-I never lived at 53, Almorah Road, Islington-I was never
that address to my knowledge-I never gave a character to Parsons a
behalf of Brooks and his wife--I do not know Mrs. Skinsley-I neve
lodged in her house.
Cross-examined. I have known Brooks eight or nine years-he thoroughly
understood his businesg—he taught me he did not lodge at the Lord in
-I went under his instructions, and what was done was by his suggestica
-I was at the Lord Hill eighteen months-I sought his advice about goin!
to the George-when I went there he helped me to set it going afresh-
went from the George to a private house in Wellington Street, Barnsbury
Road, and then I went to the Wellington-I consulted him about it-he said
he thought it would do-he has assisted me there for a week sometimes, and
when I was out he was my representative-he halped me in preparing spirit
for sale over the counter-I have left him in entire charge over and over
again-I found him upright and honest--if asked I would have given hims
reference according to his ability and what he had done for me.
Re-examined. He was not in my house since April, 1879-I should
have told Mr. Parsons so, and that he was my servant, not manager
have not been in business since April, 1879-I sold the Wellington sixe
seven years ago I cannot swear to dates without my book, which is not
here I first knew him when I was a carman at Somers Town- a friend of
mine who is in the public-house line introduced him I did not visit hin
at his home, nor Wretton-I never asked him if he was in a situation-1
think he slept one night at the Wellington-I simply knew him in business
-I employed five persons at the Wellington, a barman, potman, two bar
maids, and a servant.
WILLIAM CHARLES PARSONS. I am landlord of the Eagle Tavern, Best
India Dock Road, Poplar-I have been a licensed victualler seven years
come June-I once lived and held a licence at the Tenterden Arms, Devons
Road, Bromley-by-Bow-I kept a barmaid, potman, and a servant, and com-
ducted the business with that staff-the adopted daughter of Mrs. Flowers
is my wife-Mrs. Flowers is a widow-she is interested in the Tenterden
Arms—the licences are in her name, the mortgages are in mine-I left the
Tenterden Arms to go to the Eagle on the 25th March, 1879-the Ten
terden Arm
