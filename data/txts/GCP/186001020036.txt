In opening the case Mr. Bovill directed the attention of the Court to the
wording of the 251st section of the Bankruptcy Act (12 and 13 Vic. c. 106), and
invited an expression of opinion as to whether the words, "with intent to defraud,"
applied to the concealing, embezzling, and other offences alluded to in the section,
or whether they were to be taken as applying only to the non-surrender : this
point had been referred to by Mr. Baron Parke in the Court of Criminal
Appeal, in the case of Reg. v. Davidson and Gordon (Dearsley's Crown camer
586), but had not been argued or decided. MR. BARON MARTIN considered that
the intent to defraud, mentioned in the 251st section, applied to the intent in
the act of non-surrendering ; but he would rather hear such evidncee as mu
considered necessary, and deal with the points as they arose. Mr. Boviu
further referred to the cases of Reg. v. Hilton, 2, Cox's Criminal cases, Reg.
v. Arnold, Sessions Paper, Vol. XLIV. p. 649, and Reg. v. Lewis, Sessions
Paper, Vol. XLIX. p. 63, as shewing that the mere production of the adjudiar
tion was sufficient to establish the fact of bankruptcy, without putting the
prosecution to the formal proof of the various stages of the proceedings. Me
BARON MARTIN, although inclined to think such proof unnecessary, considered
that the evidence had better be adduced, that, if necessary, the matter might be
reserved and decided.
