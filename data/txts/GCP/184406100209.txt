1791. ANN HEAD was indicted for stealing 1 ring, value 6s., the goods
of Moses Marks : 1 watch, value 1l. ; 1 watch-guard, 128. ; 1 key, 4s. ; and
8 sovereigns; the property of Martha Levy, her mistress, in her dwelling-
house.
Mary Levy. I keep a broker's-shop in Prospect-place, St. George's. This
ring was in my room ; it belongs to my daughter's husband, Moses Marks,
and this watch, guard, and eight sovereigns were safe in a cupboard in my
bed-room on the evening of Sunday week--I missed them on Monday morn-
ing, and the prisoner too-she was my servant-I did not see her again till
Tuesday morning, when her mother fetched me-I said, "You hussy, what
have you done with my things ?”-she began crying-there was my watch,
guard, ring, and four sovereigns, and 10d. on the table-she said she had
bought a gown, a bonnet, and shawl with the remainder of the money.
ALFRED WINGATE (police-constable L 117.) I produce these things.
Prisoner. There was not so much money as she says. lt
Guilty. Aged 16.—Recommended to mercy.-Confined Fourteen Days.
1792. WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing Ilb, weight of jam,
value 18. 3d. ; 1 jar, 3d. ; 110z, of mixed spice, 28.; 1 bottle, 2d.; 30lbs.
weight of sugar, 19s. ; 3 lbs. weight of currants, 18. 6d.; loz. weight of
mace, 6d.; and 1 oz. weight of cassia, 2d.; the goods of Frederick Hills, his
master.
FREDERICK Hills, confectioner, Kennington. The prisoner was my porter
-I missed these things- I went with the officer to the prisoner's house, and
found them—the prisoner had no business with them.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST, Q. Did you ask him to show you
his lodging? A. We did—we spoke to him first in the bakehouse, and he
said he would show us his lodging—I can swear to the jam; I made it-
the prisoner said it was mine on the evening we took him, and he said the
other articles were not mine-he did not say why he had taken the jam-
I found it on his shelf in the room his mother occupied I found all these
articles in that room—the jam is not very stale-he did not deal with me be-
fore he came to live with me--I said before the Magistrate that he said the
jam was mine-what I said was taken down in writing–I do not find here
that he said the jain was mine-(looking at his depositions)-it is an oversight
-he did not say the pot was mine- I took up the jam-pot and said, “This
is 'mine"-at that time he said it was mine-he did not say so as we were
going to the station-- he did not say that the pot was mine and the jam
was his, of his own making–1 am not aware that he had been in the habit
of selling buns-he had been in my employ before,
Henry LUPTON (police-constable L 7.) I went with the prosecutor and
found these things on a shelf-the sugar was under a pot, and these bottles
and other things were under the bed—the prosecutor said, “These are mine,
and this bottle of spice I believe to be mine; I lost it about Christmas”-this
sugar, he said, was his; he had lost about half a hundred weight of moist
sugar at one time, and there were 22lbs. found there.
