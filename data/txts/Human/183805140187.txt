I remember the last election for that borough—the nomination-day was on a Saturday—on that day I attended at the Castle hotel to assist at the dinner—my master, Mr. Mason, dined there with Mr. Miller, and some other gentlemen—they dined, it might be, about five o'clock—I assisted in waiting at dinner—the party broke up about six o'clock, I think—I saw my master go out of the hotel, and Mr. Miller also—that was about six o'clock—I remained about an hour after they left, and then I went—I did not go straight home—I remained about the town about three quarters of an hour—I arrived at my master's house about eight o'clock, or a few minutes to eight—Mr. Miller was not in the house when I got there—he came in in about a quarter of an hour, or twenty minutes after—I did not let him in—Mrs. Mason was at home when Mr. Miller came in—he went into the drawing-room—Mrs. Mason was there—he took tea—I waited at tea—Mr. Miller and Mrs. Mason took tea together—Mr. Miller did not leave the house of Mr. Mason again that night—I was at home all the evening after I got home—Mr. Miller went bed about ten o'clock that night, or a few minutes after—I took tome not water to him in his bed-room, it might be ten minutes after ten o'clock.
   Cross-examined by MR. JOKES . Q.  Are you a domestic servant; a footman to Mr. Mason? A.  No—I live in the house—I am a miller—Mr. Mason is a miller as well as a hat-maker—I eat in the house—I had been assisting in waiting at that time—that is not what I do generally—I assist when there are parties—there are two female servants—they were at home.
   Q.  What had you to go by as to the time that night? A.  There was a clock in the kitchen—I was brought up from Newcastle to give evidence before the Committee of the House of Commons, but was not examined. EMMA HARVEY. I was formerly in Mr. Mason's service in Newcastle, but have left now. I was there at the time of the election—I remember Mrs. Mason coming home from the Isle of Man—it was on the Thursday of the election—Mr. Miller was living at our house at the time, and he dined there on the Friday, the day after—I remember the Saturday, the day after that—he did not dine there that day—I was in the house during that Saturday evening—I remember Mr. Miller coming home about half-past eight o'clock—it was quite light then—he took tea with Mrs. Mason when he came into the drawing-room—he did not go out again that night—he went to bed about eleven o'clock.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you brought to town to give evidence before the Committee of the House of Commons? A.  Yes—I was not examined—I cannot exactly say the day of the month I left Mr. Mason's service—it was about six weeks since—I left because I did not much like Mr. Mason's—there was no other reason—I had no quarrel or misunderstanding—I thought to better myself.
