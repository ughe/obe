 343. ELLEN CONWAY and MARY BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the   14th of December , 1 cloak, value 1 l  . the goods of An thony Price . CATHERINE PRICE. I am the wife of Anthony Price, and live at Gray's-buildings, Marylebone. On the 14th of December, the prisoners came to my room and sat a little while, and when they were gone, I missed this cloak from the door—I got a policeman and found the prisoners—I told Conway if she would give me my cloak or the ticket, I would not harm her—she denied it.
   Conway. Q.  Did not you and I go and have something to drink?—I was very tipsy. A.  We did not. JOHN ROSE. I am in the service of Mr. Toulman, a pawnbroker. I took in this cloak of both the prisoners about five o'clock the same day—Conway took the money.
   Conway. Q.  Did I appear sober? A.  You were not drunk. ELIZABETH SPREADBOROUGH. I searched Brown at the station, and the duplicate of the cloak fell from her person—she said some person had put it into her bosom.
   Brown's Defence . Price lent Conway the cloak to pawn—I did not take notice of it—Conway pawned it—I had the ticket, but I forgot it.
   Conway's Defence . I do not recollect any thing of it.
   CONWAY  — GUILTY. Aged 42.
   BROWN  — GUILTY. Aged 29. Confined Four Months.

I am a builder, living in High-street, Knightsbridge. I am doing some work for Mr. Hyam—I bad employed Flynn to work there—I have lost some lead from there—I have compared the lead pro duced with what remains, it fits exactly—it was fixed, but it was the pri soner's duty to remove it. JAMES FLYNN. I employed the prisoner to work under me at Mr. Hyam's house, to repair the roof—I gave him orders to remove this piece of lead, but he ought to have left it there—I had no instructions respecting the lead, it was Mr. Gooch's.
