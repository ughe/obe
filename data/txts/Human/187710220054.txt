M. and a diamond; I do not know whether they went to the scales"—the prisoners had no right to make up those bales, and no right to be in the warehouse at all. RICHARD HENRY SLOCOMB. I am clerk to Mr. Mellor, who is clerk to the Great Eastern Railway Company—I wrote the order produced marked A—I received a copy from Mr. Hammond in. the presence of Mr. Mellor in the Wool Exchange, addressed to the superintendent of the London Docks—I copied it, and handed it back to Mr. Mellor—I went down to the docks daily during the sales—I saw Ryan and Hammond there—on the 14th September Hammond told me to ask Ryan to let him have the weights of the four bales—he did not say what four bales—I did not know what he meant—I told Ryan, and he said "I will see Mr. Ham mond in Coleman Street." JAMES MELLOR. I am an officer of the Great Eastern Railway Com pany, and acquainted with Mr. Hammond—previous to 12th September he came to me about writing some orders for him, giving me drafts of what he wanted written—I directed Mr. Slocomb to write that of the 15th—I think it is A. M. I to 4—the other I copied myself, and gave the copy to Hammond—I know Ryan as the foreman of the dock warehouse—I sent the documents on to Messrs. Jacomb and Co. at Hammond's direction, and afterwards received this cheque ( produced ) for 56 l . 9 s . 1 d . from Messrs. Jacomb after the sale on loth September, and paid it to Mr. Hammond—I endorsed it—it is made payable to me. FREDERICK CHARLES JACOMB. I am a wool-broker of the firm of Jacomb, Son, and Co., of 61, Moorgate Street—I received the order A. M. I to 4, and in consequence placed in my sale of loth September the four bales of wool—they were  lotted 17, and the sale effected on that day for 56 l . 9 s . 1 d .—I signed a delivery order for a number of bales, in which those four are included—the goods were delivered to my order from the Dock Com pany—the cheque produced was given to Mr. Mellor by our firm—there are a great number of purchasers go to the docks when the bales are on show—every bale is shown—they pull out pieces of wool to examine it, and sometimes cut the bale right open and take out half the bale.
