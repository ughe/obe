I was arrested on June 27, but my letters and cheque-books were stolen on June 11, 16 days before any charge was made against me. As regards my habit of changing cheques into cash I produce a large number from various customers. I have evidence as to over £11,000, but that does not represent one half, because people do not always keep their cheques, and I cannot trace them. When I was at Tokenhouse Buildings I made some alterations to circulars once or twice.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Muir. I began to attend at 43, Moorgate Street in 1900 for about three times a week for an hour in the morn  ing. I was then looking after some companies. One of them was the Rand Roodeport Gold Mining Co., which is still alive. The officials of that company were Mr. Tyler, Mr. Atkins, Mr. Fardell, and Colonel Thompson. I was only interested as a shareholder. My name did not appear as a public officer of the company. Another was the Teign valley Mining Company, which I think has been re constructed. It has never paid any dividend. I was a director of that, and I think Mr. Ashworth was. There was a third, whose name I have forgotten. Another company was the Kalgoorlie Prince, which is still in existence. I had some shares. I believe it is earning money. I have ceased to be a shareholder for three or four years. None of the companies paid a dividend in my time. I believe there were two secretaries. One was named Nicholas; I forget the name of the other. I think there was one clerk. Harris in 1901 or 1904 became secretary of the Rand Roodeport. It was a few months after that that Smith appeared upon the scene. He came there with the idea of starting this business. He consulted me about it on and off for about two years. We talked the matter over, but I do not sup pose I advised him to do it. Smith had been in the tea trade, and had become bankrupt. He had not much capital to start a business with. I should say the business of an outside broker was not a risky but a safe business. I do not endorse the suggestion that it is con ducted oy the outside broker against clients. It does not follow that if the clients win the outside broker must lose; they may both quite easily win at the same time, and very often do. I do not agree to the proposition that the outside broker's profits are limited to the amount of the cover. If you say the client's liability is so limited that is perfectly true, but the broker cannot always win what the client loses; sometimes the cover lost may go into three or four hands. I consider the position of the outside broker a safe one, not necessarily at the expense of the public. It may be at the expense of the Stock Exchange. As far as I knew Herbert Smith's business was a model of what an outside broker's ought to be, I did not encourage Smith to start the outside broking business, I said I would back him.
