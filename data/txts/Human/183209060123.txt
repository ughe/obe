 2012.  FRANCIS MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , one half-crown, the money of William Robert Syer , from the person of Harriet Jones.
   HARRIET JONES. I lived at Mr. Syer's, in Tavistock-street. On the 20th of July I was sent out to buy some brandy at Mr. Watson's; I went up to the prisoner, who keeps a fruit-stall in Drury-lane, to buy a half-penny worth of cherries; I had a half-crown in my hand - he snatched it out of my hand, put it into his trousers pocket, and ran over the way to Mr. Watson's to get change, and left a strange boy to mind his stall - I went to the public-house, and said to the publican; "If you please, that is my money" - the prisoner then drank a glass of gin, and the publican gave him 2s. 4 1/2d.; I asked the prisoner again for my money, and he said he had not got it - I then lost sight of him for six or seven minutes, and then he came back to his stall; I asked him for it again - he said he had not got it, and if I did not go away, he would knock my head off; I then told the Policeman - I have never had the half-crown since; I had the halfpenny in my hand, which I gave him.
   MARY CHURCH. I live with Mr. Wilson. I was passing at the time; I saw the prosecutrix go to the prisoner's basket about half-past nine o'clock: she asked how he sold his cherries - he said a half-penny a bunch; she took one bunch, and when she opened her hand, the prisoner snatched the half-crown out of her hand, put it into his trousers pocket and went to the public-house; she followed him, and said, "If you please, that is my money" - the prisoner said if she did not go away he would knock her down.
 Prisoner. Q. Did she not go to a man who sold baked potatoes, and say she must have given it to one or other of us? A. No.
   GEORGE CAREY(Police-constable F 45). I saw a mob round the prisoner's stall; I went up and asked the prisoner how he could account for his conduct - he said he thought it was a bad one, as he had been taken in the night before - I found 2s. 4 1/2d. on him.
     GUILTY. Aged 51. - Confined Six Months.
 2013.  CHARLES BOLTON& JAMES THOMAS BENN were indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 4 bottles of ginger-beer, value 8d.; 1 pottle of nuts, value 8d.; 2 ozs. weight of sweet-meats, value 4d.; 1 shutter, value 5s.; 1 plate, value 1d., and 1 tray, value 4d., the goods of William Cole.
   WILLIAM COLE. I live at Hammersmith, and am a boot and shoemaker; my wife keeps a little shop. On the 9th of July one of my shutters was taken down and the window broken; I came down, found the glass out and the property stated gone; there was a blue-edged plate and a small tray: I went out with the officer, who called me up, and we met another officer, who advised me to go home again - I went home and found my shutter in a neighbour's garden, under some pales; I then went to bed, and was afterwards called up by Salmon, who had these two bottles, which are mine.
   NICHOLAS SALMON(Police-constable T 96). I was on duty at half-past twelve o'clock that night, and saw the two prisoners drinking ginger-beer within a few yards of the prosecutor's house - Benn then went and hid one bottle behind a post; I then went on, and met Wood, who told me of the robbery - I went back and found the bottle which I saw Benn put by the post, and one more; the other articles have not been found - the prisoners had been cracking nuts.
   AGNES COLE. I am the prosecutor's wife. We lost four bottles of ginger-beer - I know this one by a particular mark.
 BOLTON -    GUILTY. Aged 24.
 BENN -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
