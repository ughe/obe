130. JAMES RUSSELL (19), Feloniously possessing, with intent to
utter the same, counterfeit coins.
MR. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted.
WILLIAM WILLIAMSON. I am a sergeant of the Criminal Investigation
Department-on January 6th, about noon, I was with Mott at the corner
of Cockspur Street, and saw the prisoner examining something which he
put into his pocket and threw this tissue paper (produced) into the road;
there was an impression of coin on it-we took him, and I said, "I
suspect you of having counterfeit noney”-he said, “I have nothing."-
I put my hand in his pocket and found 198.--I said, “This is all you
have ?” he said, “ Yes, there ought to be 198.”—I said, "Where did you
get it?”_he said, “I cannot tell you; if I did they would kill me.”
EDMUND MOTT (Police Sergeant). I was with Williamson and cor-
roborate his evidence.
.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am Inspector of Ooin to Her Majesty's
Mint-these nineteen shillings are all counterfeit, and from two or three
different moulds - they are generally wrapped in tissue paper and taken
out one by one as required-they are very well made, and are mostly cast
from a good coin in a worn condition.
Prisoner's defence. I had no thought of uttering the coin; they were
given to me by a man, and I have no knowledge of him.
Guilty.--He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction at this Court on July
27th, 1892, of feloniously having counterfeit coin in his possession, and
three other similar convictions were proved against him.-Twelve Months'
Hard Labour.
