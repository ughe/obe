On Monday, 8th Nov., between twelve and one o'clock, the prisoner came for half a pint of beer—it came to a penny—she gave me a sixpence—I told her it was a bad one—she said she was not aware it was bad—I insisted on her giving me good money, and she gave me a penny—I kept the sixpence in my hand—West, the officer, was in the house, I gave it to him.
   Prisoner.  She never told me it was bad till after it was given up. WILLIAM CHAPMAN. I am a baker, in Bermondsey New-road—the pri soner came to my house on 8th Nov. for 1lb. of bread—she put down a sixpence and a farthing, so as to have 4 1/2 d . change—the sixpence was bad—I look it and bent it—an officer came in—I gave it to him and he took the prisoner. THOMAS WEST( policeman, M  249.) I was in company with Whitlamb on 8th Nov.—I saw the prisoner come out of Mr. Read's shop—I went in and got this sixpence ( produced )—I followed her to the Red Lion—I heard Adams say, "This is a bad sixpence"—I beckoned to her, and she gave it to me—( produced )—I saw the prisoner again about nine o'clock in the even ing in Bermondsey-road, and followed her to Mrs. Chapman's—Whitlamb went in there. JOHN WHITLAMB( policeman, M  89.) I went into Mr. Chapman's shop that evening and got this sixpence—( produced )—I took the prisoner, and found 1 s . 8 d . on her silver, and some odd pence. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—these three sixpences are all bad—two of them are of the reign of George the Third, and they are from the same mould. GUILTY .    MESSRS. SHEA and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. CAROLINE ASHTON. I am the wife of Thomas Ashton, a licensed victualler, of Bride-lane. On 2nd Nov. a tall young man came to the bar and called for a pint of beer—he gave me half a sovereign—I tested it on my finger; it was good—the prisoners came in at the other door—Howard asked for a glass of port wine and a glass of porter—she threw me down a sove reign—I gave her the half-sovereign I took from the man, and said, "I will give you 9 s . 6 d . from the till"—she said, "Oh dear me, how light this is!"—I said, "It is a good one"—she said, "I would rather have silver a great deal"—I said, "Nonsense, it is good"—Mr. Mitchell said, "Here is silver," and gave her four half-crowns—I rung the half-sovereign on the counter, and am prepared to say it was a good one—Mr. Mitchell had not examined the half-sovereign before he gave her the silver—the moment Howard had re ceived the four half-crowns the prisoners left—as soon as Mr. Mitchell took the half-sovereign he pronounced it to be bad—he showed it to me—I wrapped it in paper, put it on a shelf, and afterwards gave it to the officer.
