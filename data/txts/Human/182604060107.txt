 Middlesex Cases, Fourth Jury, Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 856.  TRIPHEMA BARTHOLOMEW was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , at Paddington, 1 sheet, value 4s.; 1 table-cloth, value 2s., and 1 blanket, value 5s., the goods of William Daniels , in a lodging-room, in his dwelling-house, against the statute.
 WILLIAM DANIELS. I live in  Bray-street, Paddington. The prisoner came to lodge at my house on the 16th of February - the articles stated were let with the room; she remained with me till the 2d of March - I then gave her notice to quit; when she went she took the key of the room-door, and a bonnet belonging to my wife; I got an officer to open the door, and missed the articles.
   THOMAS PROBERT. I am a pawnbroker, and live in the Edgware-road. I took a blanket in pledge of the prisoner - I have a sheet and table-cloth - the ticket for the sheet is my writing; I do not know who pawned them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   MICHAEL MORRIS. I am an officer. I produce the duplicates of the blanket which I found in the prisoner's room.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 857.  JAMES GAYTON was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of March , 19 yards of printed cotton, value 10s., the goods of William Spooner.
 WILLIAM SPOONER. I am a  linen-draper, and live in Chiswell-street. On the 21st of March I saw this print outside the rail, about one o'clock, and about a quarter before two I was informed it was gone.
   THOMAS MENDHAM. I am an assistant to Mr. Lazarus. On Thursday the 21st of March, I was in his shop, which is opposite Mr. Spooner's, and saw the prisoner trying to lift the print from the iron where it hung - he had a companion; he then passed up the street, and in a short time returned and handled it again - I turned round to answer a question, and on turning my head again I saw it was gone - I ran out and pursued the prisoner, who took the right hand turning of Finsbury-square - I called, Stop thief! and he dropped the print from his apron - I took it up, and followed; I lost sight of him, but he was stopped, and I brought him back.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me drop the print? A. I did not actually see you, as I stumbled at the time - I was about fifty yards from you.
   JAMES BEDLARE. I am a watchmaker. On Thursday, the 21st of March, between ten and eleven o'clock, I saw the prisoner and another at Spooner's door, and two or three hours afterwards I saw him in custody.
   SAMUEL BRIDGES. I am an officer. I took the prisoner a few minutes after two o'clock.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have a witness here who saw me at home at the time; one witness said it was Wednesday, and two that it was Thursday.
     NOT GUILTY.
 858.  JANE HANNAH LAYFIED was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of March , 3 yards of toilenette, value 5s., the goods of James Christmas.
 JAMES CHRISTMAS. I am a  tailor, and live Whitechapel-road. I missed some toilenette on Monday, which I had seen safe on Saturday the 10th of March; the prisoner was afterwards apprehended; I told her she was the last person who should have robbed me; she said she did take it - McGrath shewed me a pawnbroker's shop kept by Howse, where I found it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN VANN. I am an officer. The prosecutor applied to me to take the prisoner - on the 28th of March I went to her house, and took her - as we were going to the watch-house she said she hoped Mr. Christmas would forgive her.
   MARY McGRATH. I live in Size-yard, Whitechapel. The prisoner called for me to go to the other end of the town; I was not ready - she told me to come on to Aldgate-church - she came there to me and then left me, and went down the Minories, and came with a bundle, which she said was a black silk gown - she went on to a pawnbroker's and told me to wait; I looked in at the window and saw the toilenet there - she then came out and said she could not get enough for it - she pawned it at the corner of a coach yard in St. Giles' - she came out and said, thank God she was rid of her black silk gown.
   JOHN HARRISON. I am an officer. I went to Mr. Christmas and got a piece of toilenette; I then went to a pawnbroker in St. Giles's, and compared it with this - it corresponded exactly - it was pawned at Mr. Howse's, but he could not swear to the person.
