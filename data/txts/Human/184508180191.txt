   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You did not mark the money yourself? A.  My wife marked it in my presence—I suspect the 10 l . in gold, which was in the prisoner's box, was mine—I mean to lay hold of it, if the prisoner is convicted, if they will give it me—I opened my shop on the 21st of June—I have been a cheesemonger all my life—I have been unfortunate twice—I have been a bankrupt once—that is about five years ago—I cannot tell exactly what I paid—there were two dividends, and after that I paid a composition of 6 s . in the pound—I opened my shop six months afterwards—I suspect the money in the prisoner's box was mine, and that it has been taken since I have been there—I have not jumped into a particularly good business—there was no agreement made with the prisoner for wages; I was to pay him what he deserved—I was to be the judge of that—if we had had any settlement I should perhaps have given him 1 s . 6 d . a week—I had not paid him at all, but that is not uncommon—I found him a living—I did not suppose I was taking a man who had not a penny in the world—if he had asked me for money he would have had it at once—I imagined he had money, certainly, or he would have asked me for it—I used to have money in the till, as soon as it was taken, not before, except coppers—if a person came in for change, the prisoner was to do what is generally done, send to a neighbour's shop for change—I should not have been very angry with him to have given change out of his own pocket, or if he had taken it again out of the till when silver came in—there was 2 l . 19 s . in all when I took it out at night—there was no gold nor 5 s . pieces—I put in twelve shillings and six half-crowns—I can-not tell what there was in besides—I had not been to the prisoner's box before I took him into custody—I might have seen it—I certainly had not looked into it, nor bad any one by my direction—one thing which caused my suspicion was hearing him rattle his money of a night after he went to bed. JOHN TREFFORD( police-constable P  176.) On the 81st of July Mr. Higgs gave the prisoner into my charge, and accused him of robbing him—he said he had not—I found on his person a half-crown and 1 1/2 d .—Mr. Higgs asked him if he had any money in his box—he said, "Yes, ten sovereigns and some silver"—I took the key of his box from him in search-ing him—I found in his box ten sovereigns, and 18 s . 6 d . in silver, and amongst it this half-crown, which Mr. Higgs said he could swear to, and to his wife's marking it in his presence—the prisoner said, if he had any marked money, he must have got it in giving change.
   MR. BALLANTINE called JAMES CADBURY. I am a cheesemonger.
