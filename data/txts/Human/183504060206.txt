   COURT . Q.  What does your family consist of? A.  Only one more, that is a girl—she sleeps in another room by herself. CHARLES PERRENE. I am pot-boy at the Bull public-house, at Peckham. I know the prisoner Constable—he lives up the Bull-yard—hit father's house faces the Bull gates—I remember the night of the 25th of March—I saw Constable in the tap-room—he left between half-past ten and eleven o'clock—I may say a quarter to eleven o'clock—he unlaced his boots before he left, which was his usual practice—I went out with him and saw him go into his father's house—after he had got in, I distinctly heard him fasten three doors—I saw no more of him that night.
   COURT . Q.  Does he stay at your house every night till eleven o'clock? A.  He generally stops there till nine or ten o'clock, not generally till eleven o'clock—I had occasion to go into the yard that night—he usually unlaces his boots at our house—the Bull is about half a mile from Mr. Gray's, or it may be three quarters of a mile. JOHN SHERWIN. I am a labourer, and live at home with my father. On the night of the 25th of March I slept in my brother's room—he came home at eleven o'clock—I heard him go to bed directly after he came home—we both sleep in the same room—I did not go to sleep before three o'clock next morning—my brother was in bed from eleven till three o'clock—Hurley slept in the same room, and was there that night.
   COURT . Q.  What caused you to lay awake from eleven till three o'clock? A.  I am troubled with boils—I was at work for Mr. Booker, a green-grocer, on the 25th of March—I leave there about three or four o'clock in the afternoon, and do nothing after that—my brother was taken next morning. WILLIAM SHERWIN. I am the prisoner's father, I am a labouring man, and live at Peckham-Rye. I am afflicted with rheumatism, and cannot work now—the prisoner lives with me—on the 25th of March he came home about eleven o'clock at night—I let him in—I saw him go up stairs to go to bed—my wife gave him a light, and went up afterwards and fetched it down—I sleep in the room below his—I did not see him again till the policeman called at seven o'clock the next morning—I did not sleep from eleven o'clock that night till four o'clock the next morning—I am very much afflicted with rheumatism—my son never quitted my premises between his going to bed and the policeman coming in the morn ing—he was in bed and asleep when the policeman came—I awoke him myself—Hurley lives in my house—he slept in the same room as my son. EDWARD HURLEY. I am a day-labourer, and lodge in Sherwin's house.
