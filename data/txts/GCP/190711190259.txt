Mrs. SMITH, wife of Charles Smith, dock labourer. At half-past
three on the afternoon of November 15 Henry Edward Brown came
to my house and my husband cashed an advance note and gave him
£3 10s. I afterwards went with Brown and Mr. and Mrs. Read to
the "Peacock." We remained there about half an hour. I had one
drink. I saw two men come in. One of them I knew before.
have no doubt about prisoner being the man. I had never seen the
other one. He was the man I gave evidence against at the Old
Bailey-Maxwell. I was present when prisoner was put up for iden.
tification at Stratford Police Court. I did not speak to Read with
& view to pointing out the man. I did not get to the Court till 20
minutes to 11. I know nothing of what happened after they left the
Peacock," I only know Chasey was with thet: Mr. Smith gave
Brown three gold sovereigns and a half-sovereign for his advance
note.
Cross-examined. I did not see the prison van drive up. I am
quite positive prisoner did not see me and Mr. Rond standing in the
yard when the van drove up. I have known prisoner for five or six
years. I heard that there had been a scuffle at this house the next
night after it occurred. I knew that Chasey was with them up to
the time they left the "Peacock," and I was told he was one of the
men who was in the row. Having known that for two years I was
taken to Stratford Police Station and identified Chasey, having
known him for five or six years and been with him up to the time he
left the "Peacock."
: Inspector BLANTREE, K Division stationed at West Ham. I was in
charge at the West Ham Police Court on the identification of prisoner
He was brought in from one of the cells and placed with nine other
men in the passage of the police court. The witnesses, Mr. Read, his
daughter and Mrs. Smith were called in one by one, and ench iden-
tihed him. I made no suggestion to any of the witnesses. It was
my duty to see that the identification was properly carried out.
Cross-examined. I do not think Mrs. Read was asked to identify
prisoner. I was not in charge of the case. I was not present at the
police court when Mr. Read identified him. I was standing in the
pessage when the little girl identified him. She was not taken up
and down the line of men: she walked up the line once and back
again, then went and touched him. I do not think the detective said,
"Do not be frightened," just when she got near prisoner. He might
have used those words because the little girl seemed alarmed. I
did not hear them. Some of the men in the line might have had hats
on. It was a wet morning, and we had difficulty in getting men.
Several of them had cloth caps on, and I gave prisoner the option of
having hats on or off.
