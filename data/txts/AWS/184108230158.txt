GEoRGe ADAMS. I am a surgeon. I attend at the London hospital-
the deceased was brought there on Saturday, the 5th of June-he was in
a state of insensibility- examined his head, and found a quantity of
blood flowing from his ear-] found no mark of any blow outside-he was
in the hospital till the 13th of June, and then died-he continued insensi
ble the whole time-I afterwards examined his body-his skull was frac-
tured, and the brain torn considerably-there was a quantity of blood in
the base of the brain-before I opened the head, I examined to see if there
were any external marks-I attribute the immediate cause of his death to
pressure on the brain from the blood-the appearances I saw might un-
doubtedly have been occasioned by a blow or fall-it was the lower part of
the skull that was fractured, behind the ear-it would very likely be
occasioned by the head coming in contact with a stone-I should think
it almost impossible it could have been caused by a blow from a man's
fist-it was no doubt a recent injury-I do not remember seeing the wit.
ness Hagerty at the hospital-] saw him before the Coroner when Con-
nor's body was there-it was the same body I had examined.
