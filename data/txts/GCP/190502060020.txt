188. FREDERICK PAXTON (33), Feloniously sending to Lisette
Ducros a letter demanding £5 from her with menaces, well knowing the
contents thereof.
MR. LEYCESTER Prosecuted.
LISETTE Ducros. I am single and live at 17, Clarendon Street, Pim-
lico-I occupy the lower part of the house and my aunt the upper part-
I have known the prisoner for about a year-he is the husband of the
charwoman whom I employed two or three times--he lives near me
January 18th I received this letter; I do not know the writing (Read): "To:
tenham Court Road, January 17th. Madam, -It having come to my know
ledge that your house, No. 17, has been under observation and likely to
be, I wish to let you know that if you care to send me £5, that will engage
my services and I can promise you without fail to put matters right with
the man that is engaged watching. Of course I cannot tell you how much
he will want, but I know that he can be squared with money as we done
so before in a similar case. I see him every day,and, by what I hear, you
will be perhaps brought up for keeping a disorderly house. He told me
he had the case we!! in hand, and that one of your friends who visited
you was a Mr. in the War Office, and you had a child by him. Of
course that would be very bad for a gentleman to be brought into a thing
like this. Perhaps he will help you out of it if he values his good name;
of course I am only mentioning this to let you know that I have heard
all about it, and that you should take my advice; employ me and Ice
assure you everything will turn out all right. You won't have to delay
if you send me the amount, that is at once. I will get to business and
also I can give you valuable advice and tell you what to do. You will
also, whatever you do, let no one know about this matter, as it means
this man's position lost if it came to his superiors' ears. Of course I am
taking this entirely of my own accord and have not made any agreement
with him yet. I do not think he will require much, and I am a private
inquiry agent, and, therefore, come in to hear a lot of cases of this kind,
and have done a lot of good. I shall call on Thursday morning at address
given, and if you do not send money by then I know you do not require
me; it might then be too late, but I will let you know to-day, Wednesday,
if anything is likely to occur. I am informed you have neighbours; they
are the ones to be careful of, as it is true that this man was put to watch.
I do not think he will be there till Saturday again, but could not say for
sure. They may raid the honse when you have some gentlemen there, or
perhaps will only bring you up for keeping a disorderly house, but what-
ever you do, don't delay one hour if possible. I must not come near of
would have seen you personally, and I am engaged in similar business
80 can only correspond through the post. This is a private address I give
you, where, at any time, if you require the servioes of a private detective,
hoping to serve you, Yours truly, R. Atkinson, care of Newsagent, 131,
Charing Cross Road, London, W.C. P.S. Be sure to register letter.
