4th of
857. JOHN CLARK was indicted for stealing 1 half-crown, 14 shillings,
and 2 sixpences, the monies of Catherine Malone, from her person.
CATHERINE MALONE. I am single, and live at No. 1, Three Kings-court,
Whitecross-court-- I have lived there since I left my situation, six or seven
weeks ago. I have been acquainted with the prisoner better than twelve
months-he proposed to marry me about a month ago-for about two or three
months he had never stopped from my place-in consequence of his request, I
left my situation, and went to live with my sister-after that he wanted money
to furnish him with some clothes, and I let him have it-be imposed on me the
belief that he was going to marry me, and I furnished him with money to rig
himself out-he then took me to buy a wedding-ring at my own expense, and
then persuaded me to give him money to buy furniture, a table and chairs, and
to take a room-he represented that he had collected articles in the room--he
asked me for the key of the room to put an article in- I went very early the
next morning for it, and could not get it-I found the banns were not put up
where he said they were-Tuesday, the 16th of Feb., was fixed on for our
marriage-he appointed Mr. Swaine's lodging, near Cripplegate church, to
meet me to go to get married, I found him there-in going, as I supposed, to
church, when we got to Aldersgate-street we went to a public-house we had
some refreshment, and I changed a sovereign-I bad 178. or 18s, in my
pocket-1 sat down near the prisoner-he asked me to show him what money
I had in my pocket-I was not in the act of counting it bad changed a
sovereign, and I had my hand in my pocket, putting the money in-he
Teached over and snatched my money from my hand-he turned the pocket
up, and took every farthing of money-1 resisted-I put my hand on his, and
said, “You bave got every farthing of money I have, and I could take better
care of it than you"-he said, "It would look very stupid of me, when we
get to church, not to have the money to pay the fees, but to have to ask you
for it"-he assigned that as a reason for taking it from me- we got into a cab,
and when got out, he pointed to a church just by, and said, “That is the
church"-he took me to another public-house, and then he cut away through
the courts, leaving me to pay the cab-I had not a farthing, but my sister,
who was with me, had a few shillings, or else we should all bave been given in
charge - afterwards found the prisoner in Red Lion-court, wearing the coat
I had bought for him-I asked him for the ring and the money he thieved
out of my pocket-he said he had lost the ring.
Cross-examined by RR. ROBINSON. Q. How many friends were with you?
A. Two, my sister Margaret, and a young English lady, Elizabeth Sperrin
they were in the public-house-Mrs. Swaine was there-the prisoner was on
my left-hand side-I had no one on the other side-the prisoner was not at a
distance-my pocket was on my right side, and he put his hand down, and
turned out what was in my pocket-Mrs. Swaine and Elizabeth Sperring were
at the other end, and the table between us--there was no one near me and
the prisoner-my sister saw what was going on-she was not with the others
-Mrs. Swaine's back was turned to me the other young woman and herself
saw nothing, but they heard the making mention of a few words--I thought
that money would be expended in the marriage fees--the prisoner said he was
to give it to the clergyman.
Q. Did he not say he intended to carry the money because he intended to
Wear the breeches ? A. I wanted to have the money back, and he said,
"What I have I will keep, because I will wear the breeches"--that was after
he had got the money-he did not count the money, for he put it into his
pocket as soon as he took it.
