722. GEORGE NAHRGANG (37), Stealing eight ounces of extract of
mutton, two ounces of cotton, and other articles, of Francis Renshaw and
another, his masters.
MR. HARRINGTON conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM WILLIAMS. I am day watchman to Messrs. Cousin & Renshaw,
of Upper Brook's Wharf, wharfingers—it is my duty to search the workmen
when they leave-on 2nd October I searched the prisoner, and found about
two ounces of cotton in his pocket, value about 2d.-he said he had taken
it to put in his ear, as he suffered from pain in his head.
EDE
ALFRED MCKENZIE. I am one of the firm of Cousin & Renshaw, of
Upper Brock's Wharf-in consequence of what we heard we instructed the
last witness to search the workmen as they left—this cotton is ours; this
cochineal is clean, and is similar to what we have in our establishment-if
scattered on the floor it would not be allowed to be taken away by the
workmen, but it has the appearance of being taken from a bag-all the
articles are worth about 11.--this is a kind of paper which we use ; some
paper has our name printed on it.
WILLIAM BRAY (Câty Policeman 571). I took the prisoner on 3rd

lodging, and found a similar quantity of cotton, 8 oz. of extract of mutton,
about 1 oz. of cochineal, about 11 lb. of sugar, in a paper bearing the name
of the firm, 1 lb. of nutmegs, 14 oz. of cinnamon, and in the prisoner's box
