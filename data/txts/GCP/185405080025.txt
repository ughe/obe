601. JEREMIAH KAYLER was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. BODKIN and POLAND conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH BIRCH. I am shopwoman to Mrs. Hamilton, who keeps a
tobacconist's shop in Holborn. On Wednesday, 19th April, I was in the
shop-the prisoner came in at near 1 o'clock, I served him with 1d. worth
of tobacco he gave me a sixpence, I gave him bd. change - I put the six-
pence in a cigar box, by itself—the sixpence looked a very old one-I
thought it was good, but I put it alone—the prisoner left, and in about ten
minutes he came again for another ld. worth of tobacco I served him and
he gave me a sixpenny piece, I gave him bd. change a man was coming in
at the time, and in the hurry I put the sixpence in the till—there was very
little other money there—but that sixpence that I put in the till was never
found—the prisoner left—he came again the next day and had another 1d.
worth of tobacco, and he changed another sixpence, I gave him bd. change
-and knowing him I looked in the box, and found the other sixpence had
not been taken out, and I put this one with it-before I put it in I tried
it-the prisoner went out of the shop, I bent it and put it in- I gave those
two sixpences to Mrs. Hamilton on Thursday evening, the 20th-no one
serves in the shop, but Mrs. Hamilton and I-I am quite sure the prisoner
is the man.
Prisoner. I was never in her house but once, and I was taken the same
evening. Witness. He brought another counterfeit sixpence on the Friday
- my mistress was there and took it
MABY ANN HAMILTON. I keep the shop in Holborn. On Thursday
evening, 20th April, the last witness gave me the box with two sixpences in
it, which I gave to the officer-on Friday 21st, the prisoner came to the
shop, I was in the way he asked for a Id. worth of tobacco, he gave me a
sixpence--I saw directly it was bad before I gave him the tobacco, I told
him he had given me a bad sixpence-he said & gentleman gave it him, for
minding a horse-I told him that was what he told me three weeks before,
when he brought a bad sixpence, and I cut it and gave it him back again-
he said he had never been in the shop before-just at that moment Birch
came in the shop and said, “That is the man that gave me all the other six-
pences"-he said he had never been in the shop before I sent for a con-
stable, and gave him in charge--I gave the constable the sixpences.
John HAYNES (policeman, F 154). I received these two sixpences from
the last witness on 20th April.
JAMES CLAMP (policeman, F 110). I received the prisoner in charge,
and received this sixpence from Mrs. Hamilton—I asked the prisoner his
address, he said he had no place of residence.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint- these
are all counterfeit.
Prisoner's Defence. I never was in the house, but once before.
GUILTY. Aged 20.-Confined Twelve Months.
