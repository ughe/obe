Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1388. JOSEPH TAYLOR and THOMAS HARVEY, stealing 1 watch
apd 1 jacket, value 31.; the goods of Robert Dunbar, in a vessel on the Thames.
"Robert DUNBAR being called on his recognizances did not appear.
GEORGE LITTLE (Thames-policeman, 42). On 30th May, about half-past
two o'clock in the morning, I saw two persons run away from a yessel lying
off Bermondsey-I got out of my boat, ran after them, lost sight of them for
about two minutes, and then caught sight of Taylor, I did not know him
before--I ran after him 300 or 490 yards, caught him, and as I brought him
back we met Clifton, who gave me this jacket (produced), and said, "I saw
that person drop this jacket" - Taylor said, "No, you did not"-Taylor
aid he had been to the fair-I went on board the schooner and saw Robert
Dunbar there.
"Henry CLIFTON (policeman, M 229). About half-past two o'clocky od
30th May, I was on duty in East-lane, Bermondsey, and saw the prisoners
together, coming towards me from the steps leading to the Thames--they
made a bit of a stop-Taylor dropped the jacket and ran back, Harvey passed
me-I picked it up, and met Little and Taylor.
PETER MORGAN RANDELL. I am a sailor. I was in Horsemonger-lane
Gaol, charged with taking something from a ship-Taylor was there, he asked
me what I was there for, and I asked him what he was there for-he told me
it was for taking a jacket and watch from the Scotch Lassie-he said he took
them out of the mate's berth-I asked what he had done with them-he would
not tell me.
Taylor. R. You said to me, “Did you steal it?" A. Yes, and you said,
"Yes."
Nor GUILTY.
