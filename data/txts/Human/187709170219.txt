   Cross-examined.  The prisoner did not ask me to go to the house and see his wife—I never saw her until 1 or 2 o'clock in the morning at the station, when she had been to hospital and had her wounds dressed, the prisoner's wife was cut—I saw only the bandages—I think the prisoner has been in a reformatory, but am not certain—I do not know that he has certificates of character from where he has been working—the passage is about 12 feet long, and 3 1/2 feet wide—I think the prisoner had been drinking. SAMUEL SAUNDERS. I live at Tredegar Road, Bow—on 7th August, I saw the prisoner and prosecutor at the door having very high words, and the prisoner dared the prosecutor to enter the house—he said "If you come in here I will cut you"—I saw the prisoner's hand up and when he went in the prosecutor followed, but he had not been in two minutes before he came out and rolled and fell against a lamp-post—the women were inside fighting and hallooing "Murder"—I saw the prosecutor's wound and much blood.
   Cross-examined.  The passage was not altogether dark, a lamp throws a light about a yard across it—one of the women was cut—I am sure the word "cut" was used by the prisoner.
   The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate : "I was not in my right mind, and don't know what I did." GUILTY of unlawfully wounding— Four Months' Imprisonment.
