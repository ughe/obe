   Cross-examined by Arthur Workman.  Only Mrs. Workman was present when she showed me the impressions of the cash box keys you left to be given to me to make, and she only was present when you told me you had been to the place and could find it blindfolded—the police came to see me at Pentonville, and said they had heard I knew something about the intended burglary, and asked me if I would give evidence in this case; I asked if I should get any benefit, and they said they could not promise anything or hold out any inducement. WILLIAM BROWN. I am a labourer, at 1, Garden Row, London Road—in May last I was sent to Wandsworth Gaol for a month—Workman was in the next cell, and spoke to me on many occasions while going to chapel or out in the exercise ground—he asked me to take a message to a Mrs. Clark, 104, Warner Road, when I got out, and to tell her the "Coppers," Hunt and Chamberlain, had been, and he was sorry "Pat" had "fell;" they wanted him (Workman) to come up and give evidence against Pat, but he told them he knew nothing about it, and they went away very cross—Workman asked me to go his wife, 14, Bath Place, Addington Square, Camherwell, and tell her the Coppers had been about Maudsley's job, and she must keep her "guts" what she knew about it, and that he had told the Coppers he had been with her to Maudsley's 18 months ago to look at the models while the clerks were in the country.
   Cross-examined by Arthur Workman.  I have been convicted of felony once before—your wife was not at Bath Place, and I went to her at Cam berwell Workhouse as you told me, but did not give her the message; she said, "Come down to-night"—I gave Mrs. Clark the message—I went to Carter Street and gave information that you had given me a message to your wife that she was not to give information to the police if they came that you had been to Maudsley's to see the keys—a man stopped me and said I should be well paid if I did not give information against you—the police summoned me to come here—I do not know if I shall be paid as a witness—I was fetched to the police-court.
   Cross-examined by Emma Workman.  I told you at Camberwell I had a message from your husband, and he was very sorry Pat had fell—I did not go down at night.
