I keep the Boyton Arms beer-shop, near the canal in New Church-road, Camberwell. The church bell-ringers practise the art of bell-ringing—we have a peal of seventeen bells for their practice—it is a society held in our club-room—I saw the prisoner at my house—I cannot say what time he came there—I saw him at half-past eleven o'clock, when he came down stairs to go away, and I saw him some short time before, between ten and o'clock I should think, sitting down with two others—he made a remark to me, which is the reason why I know him—he was in (be club-room where the bells were—it is open on Sunday evenings—I wu before the Magistrate—inspector Giffbrd sent the officers to know whether what the prisoner had stated as to being in my house was correct, and at I identified him they did not swear me.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Was the prisoner in the habit of frequenting your beer-shop? A.  No—I never saw him there but once before—there were very likely twenty or thirty persons in the room that night—I am not aware that there is anybody else here from my house—I did not see the prisoner after half-past eleven o'clock—I should say it was rather more than that—my house is about half a mile from the George, I suppose, but I never saw the house to my knowledge.

JAMES HISTEY.
