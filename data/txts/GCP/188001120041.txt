THOMAS BANFIELD (Policeman B R 22). About 7.45 p.m., on 15th
December, Brooks spoke to me, and the prisoner passed by, and the former
gave the prisoner in custody for uttering a forged cheque at his shop-the
prisoner said " You must have made a mistake, I know nothing about it
-Brooks said "I have made no mistake; I am sure you are the man; I
can swear to you; I shall give you in charge "--the prisoner said "All
right; I will go with you anywhere; I am a gentleman " at the police-
station, when I was told to futeh Mr. Whiting, the prisoner said "I know
Mr. Whiting very well "--when he came the prisoner hung his head and
said nothing I searched him, and found 41. 108. in gold, 21. 8s. in silver,
and 7d. in bronze.
Cross-examined. I am well known in the neighbourhood -the defendant
gave me his name and address, which were correct there were seven or
eight present when Hayter identified the prisoner there were more than
three-they were not all prisoners, some were fetched in from the
street the prisoner had his hat on; another man had a peak-cap-he
was dressed about the same as now, except his bat-I went to his
lodgings in Mina Road, Old Kent Road, but found nothing.
Re-examined. Emma Morris pointed out the room--she said he brought
no luggage there,
EMMA MORRIS. I live at 3, Mina Road, Old Kent Road, with my
husband--the prisoner came to lodge there in October last - he took
one furnished room at 48. 6d. a week-he did not tell me where he came
from or what he was—he was in no employment, and had registered
letters come every fortnight I did not see him open them he had no
luggage, just an extra shirt or two-he said he had a private income-he
lived there till taken into custody.
Cross-examined. He used to pay me he told me the registered letters
contained letters from his relations-he always behaved honestly to me, and
was steady and respectable in his habits.
Re-examined. He did not say who his relations were.
