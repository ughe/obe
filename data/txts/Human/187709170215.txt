   Cross-examined.  I never heard of the deceased child being taken to a medical man—I have two children—I never saw my own child fed on bread and treacle and never heard of bread and treacle being given—when I saw the deceased child getting ill I got very  anxious about my own and got somebody else to mind it—the deceased got gradually thinner and would not take any notice; it got very dull indeed. HANNAH SMITH. I am the wife of John Smith—we have no children; Eliza Onslow is my sister—on 16th July, she came to me with Mrs.—Barales and the child—I declined to take it, but sent for the pri soner who came and engaged to take it, and I supplied it with clothes—I saw it a fortnight afterwards, it was very ill with diarrhoea and got very thin—it got better, but still continued thin—I suggested taking it to Dr. Coffin, and the prisoner went there with me once—he ordered it cow's milk instead of condensed milk—I do not whether it had had condensed milk before; the prisoner took it to Dr. Coffin by herself the second time, but she brought it to me both going and coming back—when. it was ill I took it some beef tea; the prisoner said its mouth was so bad that she did not know what she could do for it—it got very ill, and she did not expect it would live the night through, and she promised to send for me, but it got better—it was a fine child when it was taken to her—I afterwards saw Mrs. Barales at the house—the child was then wonderfully altered; I had not seen him for a fortnight—he had got very thin and ill—he was not smartly dressed, but was in my own opinion dirty—I saw a bottle with milk in it lying by its side when I went i, Mrs. Barales was there then.
   Cross-examined. MR. COFFIN  gave the prisoner some medicine for the child's mouth—it was treated for its mouth as well as for diarrhoea—I had no reason to believe it was neglected—the prisoner was always at home when I went—I attributed its getting thin to the diarrhoea—there was nothing to lead me to suspect that it was neglected—I called sometimes once and sometimes twice, and when it was ill I was there three times—the prisoner said that she gave it baked flour and condensed milk and sweet biscuits, and said that it always ate very well.
