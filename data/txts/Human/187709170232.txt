   Cross-examined.  I had a shop of all sorts in Poland—I have never had a business transaction with the prisoner; he went with me about business but I did not do business with him before—I went with Mrs. Pfeiffer to Gatti's restaurant, and from there to the Canterbury Music Hall after dinner—she left me there and went home alone—the word "Chamois" is not in this receipt, it is "Skins"—it was written at Charing Cross Station, not at Peckham—Ffeiffer said he was rather unwell and wanted to take fresh air, and asked me to go for a stroll, and he gave me this receipt after a couple of hours' walking about; but I was at his house from 12 o'clock till 4.30—the would-be merchant stayed with us about an hour and a half, and when he left I was left alone with Pfeiffer for a couple of hours longer—I went out with Pfeiffer about 5 o'clock—he went home from Charing Cross about 7 o'clock and I met Mrs. Pfeiffer about 7.30—I said before the Magistrate that he gave me the receipt marked A at the time—that is true; he gave it me the same day, at Charing Cross—I pressed him to give it to me, and he said "Well, let us go to the station and I will give it you"—I did not know that Gatti's is close by—this is very likely the first time I have mentioned its being written at Charing Cross—they did not ask me the question before the Magistrate—it was not written on the 16th—after the conversation between Mr. Woolf and Mr. Kipps, Mr. Woolf told me that Kipps was willing to give 200 l . for Mr. Pfeiffer—we remained there half an hour.
   Re-examined.  Pfeiffer gave me the card with Kipps' address, and said he would see me—Mr. Woolf spoke to Kipps in English, and he told me that Mr. Kipps was willing to give me 150 l . back of the money, and 50 l . for expenses incurred, to get Pfeiffer out of prison—Woolf told me I must go to a solicitor—I did not propose any compromise through Woolf—he told me the matter was already in the Justices' hands, and the best thing would be for him to go to the solicitor of this man. DAVID VON HAND.
