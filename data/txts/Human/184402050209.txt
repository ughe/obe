MARY MORAN. I am the wife of Timothy Moran, of Star-court, St. George the Martyr, Southwark. The prisoner was tipsy—I took her into my house as she said her husband was angry with her—I left her in the house on the 20th of Jan. when I went out—I returned between seven and eight o'clock in the evening—she was gone, I found my husband's box broken open, and missed the articles now produced. JOHN RUSSELL( policeman. ) I apprehended the prisoner in George-alley on Sunday morning, the 21st of Jan., and found a shawl, shirt, and handker chief, in the room she was in. WILLIAM MARTIN( policeman. ) Ifound two shawls, two petticoats, a gown, sheet, and other articles—some in the room the prisoner was in, and some in the next room, in George-alley—the prisoner was intoxicated, and said nothing. MARY ANN REDMAN. I am the wife of a policeman, and employed at the Bagnigge-wells station, to search the prisoners. On the 21st of Jan. I found on the prisoner two handkerchiefs, 5 s . 1 d ., and a pair of stockings, which the prosecutrix claimed—I told the prisoner I had found the money—she said, "What money? I had no money; I suppose it is the money I took out of the box?"—the stockings were on her legs—she said they were her mistress's. MARY MORGAN re-examined.  These are all mine—we lost about 5 l .; worth of property—she was six or seven nights in my house—I have got all my things back but a few shillings. GUILTY  . PATRICK MURPHY was indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Walter Cutchy , in the night of the   8th of Jan ., at   St. George the Martyr, South wark , with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 3 coats, value 1 l  .; and 1 waistcoat, value 5 s  .; his property. WALTER CUTCHY. I am a hosier and clothier, and live at 67, Blackman-street, Borough. On the night of the 8th of Jan. my house was closed, and all safe—I went to bed at eleven o'clock—I was awoke about two by the policeman—I went with him into the shop, and found one of the fan-lights over the street-door broken—it was safe when I went to bed—I missed three coats, and one waistcoat, which I have not recovered—I found a stick and two waistcoats behind the door—they bad been removed from the place they occupied over-night. RICHARD JAMES( policeman. ) On Tuesday morning, the 9th, I was on duty, and saw the prisoner standing on another one's shoulders, with his arm through the prosecutor's fan-light—I ran across the road, and as soon as he dropped off the other one's shoulders, I took him into custody, and conveyed him to the station—I then returned to the shop, called Mr. Cutchy up, and on going into the shop he missed three coats and a waistcoat—I saw some broken glass on the table, and a stick and two waistcoats, which had been knocked down—the goods were hanging upon brass rods, inside the fan-light—I dare say this stick would bring the goods off the rod to the window-there is no hook to it, or anything peculiar about it.
