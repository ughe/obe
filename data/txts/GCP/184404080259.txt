SABAH SIMMERS. I lived at No. 1, Birdcage-alley, next door to Carroll's.
I heard cries of murder this morning, about balf-past ten or near eleven
o'clock-I looked through a hole in the wainscoting of the landing-place, and
saw Mr. and Mrs. Carroll and the deceased in the front room, second floor,
quarrelling- I heard the deceased call Mrs. Carroll very ugly names, and on
her doing that, Mrs. Carroll struck her with her hand in the face---she did
not fall down from the blow-Mrs. Hobbs called the name again, and then
Mr. Carroll struck her with his fist several times--they all left the room, and
went out of my sight-I heard the deceased talking to Mr. Carroll-she had
a German-silver fork in her hand, and was beating it up against the wall, and
asking him if he wanted to make property of it-I heard her say to him, that
she was no man, she was a woman, and not to hit her with a poker I did
not hear her say "You don't hit me with the poker" ---the next thing
I heard was, that the deceased was dead—when Carroll went down, and I
saw him, he had nothing in his hand.
Cross-examined. Q. How large is the crevice, have you to stoop down to

Court. Q. When the prisoner struck her, was it with the fist or open

WILLIAM RENDLB. I am the parish surgeon. About ten o'clock on
Saturday morning, the 2nd of March, I was called in to see the deceased-
both Mr. and Mrs. Carroll came to fetch me-they waited for me, and accom-
panied me back-I examined the deceased, and observed she was bruised
about the face- I did not observe that she had drank very much I should
think not-she was dead when I got there I could not account for her death
then-there were external bruises, but not sufficient-I opened her head on
the Monday-externally there were marks of bruises over the left eye, and
also a contused wound behind the right ear-on removing the scalp, there
were large clots of blood effused, corresponding with the external marks-
there was also evidence of blows being struck immediately behind the right
ear-I should say that was not done with great violence-I saw the piece of
tongs at the inquest-it may have been produced by that a fist would not
produce the wound behind the ear, it must have been something sharp there
was no wound on the temple, but three or four external bruises--in my
judgment death was caused by concussion of the brain, produced by repeated
blows-I should not think the blow behind the ear sufficient of itself, but, in
combination with other circumstances, it would aid it was the only wound-
