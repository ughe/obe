359. EDWARD COGHLIN, stealing 1 wateh, value 8L. 8s, ; the goods
of Samuel Henry Freeman, in his dwelling-house.
Mr. PARRY conducted the Prosecution.
SAMUBL HENRY FREEMAN. E am a jeweller and wateh-maker, at 147,
Fleet-street. On Wednesday, 2nd Jan., the prisoner came to my shop, and
asked if I had got a good watched said I had, and showed him two or three
silver ones, which I considered suitable for a man in his station of life; he
looked like a sailor-he said he did not mind about 21. or sl. more, but he
must have a good wateh, it was for a present to his uncle in Americarl
then produced a gold one, the price of which was 8l. 18a.- he looked at it,
and seemed to make up his mind, and purchase it-I laid it on the counter,
and he said, 44 Will you say 8l. 8s."-I said no, 81. 188. was the pricehe
then asked for a good heavy chain, I showed him one-he said it was not
heavy enough, had I got a heavier one-l went to the other side of the
counter to get a heavier one, and a handful of pepper immediately came into
my eyes, which blinded me, and when I recovered my sight the watch and
the prisoner were gone--the transaction altogether did not take ten minutes-
I had the opportunity of seeing his face-] have no doubt the prisoner is the
man-I had never seen him before, to my knowledge-I have since seen the
wateh in the prisoner's possession,
Cross-ezamined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. When you saw him again you
understood that the duplicate had been found in his possession ? A. Yes-I
have only been in business one month--before that I was assistant to Mr,
Curtis, a pawabroker, silversmith, and jeweller, in the Strand-I am in the
habit of seeing a great many people--the prisoner was not at the shop more
than ten minutes, during part of which time E reached down a chain, and got
out three watehes, but I was more particularly looking at him-I have never
been mistaken in speaking to anybody, to my knowledge1 think I have
been a witness about six times-I do not think I had had a customer all
day, before the prisonerI had a little girl directly after- should know her
again-she is a customer-I do not think any one else came-I next saw
the prisoner the next evening, in custody--I am sure I am not mistaken
about his being the person did not, to my knowledge, see him in Fleet.
street the next day--I was out nearly the whole of the day, trying to find
him-I did not see him go past my shop.
Mr. PARRY. Q. I suppose it made a strong impression on you A. Yes
-[ conversed with him, and I heard his voice after he was apprehended-
am quite satisfied he is the man.
