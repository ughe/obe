DONALD MUNRO. I am a clerk in the employ of the Brighton Railway
Company. This account (looking at it) is my writing it is for 21. 5x. 6d,
it is the account of Friday, 22nd Aug.-I wrote it out by the wish of Mr.
Gower-he came into the office one morning with his right hand tied up
he said he had got his thumb smashed, and would we be kind enough to
write a few lines for him-another clerk and I were in the office-I said,
"Certainly," and I wrote this from a slip of paper which he produced be
took the slip of paper away with him-I copied this from the paper and
from his dictation, both-he held the piece of paper in his hand and read it
out to ine-the paper was about the same size as one of these duplicates-1
did not observe whether there was any printing on the paper he read it
from the account I wrote was written on a regular form, an excess form
the prisoner gave me that form to write it on in the account I made out
I do not see anything like 48. for tickets for Reigate what I made out ww

CHRISTOPHER EDWARD COCKERTON. I am station clerk at the London
Bridge terminus. I should say the prisoner has been in the employ of the
company from ten to twelve years he has been head ticket collector for
four or five years past—of late the up trains stop at Forest Hill, and the
tickets are taken there—the prisoner had the superintendence of that station
-it was his duty, at the close of the day, to fill up a form, and half was for-
warded to the London station that ought to contain all the money received
—this counterfoil of 18th Aug. did not come to me this duplicate came to
me as the duplicate of the excess book of 18th Aug.-the duplicates have
come to me from Forest Hill by casual persons, in an envelope, generally-
the prisoner did not bring them at all himself, they came by the train-by
the paper that came on 18th Aug., the amount represented as received is

was sent up to me was 21. 58. 6d.-it reached me in the same way in course
