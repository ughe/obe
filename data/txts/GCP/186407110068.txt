WILLIAM ATTRILL. I saw an advertisement in the Daily Teegraph in
February last; in consequence of which, I went to Magnrigle's office in
Farringdon-road-he said he had a vacanoy for a clerk, with a premium, to
attend to persons who came into the office, and answer letters-I was to pay
251. premium, and to have a salary of 401. for the first year, and 51. increase
the second year the agreement was for two years-it is signed by
Magarigle--the 251. was paid over to bim, and I went into the service at
Farringdon-road-Broughton and Keighley used to come to the office while
T was there sometimes frequently, sometimes not at all-Broughton came
two or three times a week occasionally sometimes Keighley came several
times & week, sometimes not at all—there was a register kept, for which
people paid five shillings for employment-I have heard of one or two
getting employment through Magarigle-one was named Howell-be got
five or six shillings a week, I would not swear to any other case-I know
of no partnership being carried out through Magarigle, except with the
persons in these cases-after Magarigle was in custody, Keighley came to
the office-he had the keys-be had the key of Magarigle's private room-
I have seen this advertisement-book at the office—ihis is the receipt I had
(Read: “17th February, 1864. Received from Thomas Attrill the sum of
Dl., in part payment of 251.”)—the other 201. was paid.
