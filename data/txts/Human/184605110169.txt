1202. HENRY HILL was indicted for stealing 1 half-crown and lgix. pence, the monies of Edward Kent , his master. MARY KENT. I am the wife of Edward Kent, wine-merchant, Bedford. place, Old Kent-road: the prisoner was in his service. I sent him, on the 2nd of April, for a shin of beef—he went, and brought the young man with him, with the shin of beef, and a note saying it was 3 s . 6 d .—I said I understood it was only 3 s .—the prisoner went down, and returned and said that would do—I gave him a half-crown and a sixpence, to give to Mr. Neal's servant, whose name was Paynter.
   Prisoner.  When I left she stopped the money for it. Witness.  That was a different concern—he absconded on the 15th of April, before I had found this out—his mother came two days afterwards for his wages—Mr. Kent said he would not pay anybody but Henry—the prisoner came on the Saturday, and we deducted a half-crown that he had absconded with before. GEORGE JOHN PAYNTER. I live with Mr. Neal. I brought the shin of beef on the 2nd of April—it came to 8 s . 6 d .—the prisoner came down and asked me if 3 s . would not do—I said I could not take any less than 35.6i., at I gave 3 s . 6 d . for it—he went up, and came down again and said, "Very well, Mrs. Kent will call and pay"—he did not give me any money. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Six Months .
   Before Mr. Recorder.
 1203. GEORGE COOPER was indicted for stealing 80 cheroots, value 6 s  .; the goods of Frances Strode . FRANCES STRODE. I am single—I live in the Triangle at Kennington-cross—a lad about the prisoner's size came to my shop last Saturday—he took a bundle of about half a pound of cheroots out with him—I have no recollection of his features. CHARLES ROWE. I am apprentice to a shoemaker in Walworth-road—about ten o'clock, last Saturday, I was going by the Triangle at Kennington—I saw a woman on the pathway calling out "Stop thief!" and a boy run ning from where the cry was—he ran across the road and down Queen's-row—I ran after him till he was nearly at the top—he was stopped by the police man, and said that he was running after another person—I am of opinion he ran from the place where the woman stood—I only saw one person running—I never lost sight of him—there was a woman running, but nobody else—I did not see anything in his hand.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You did not see anything throwi away? A.  No—I saw him before Mr. Smith commenced running. CHARLES JAMES SMITH. I live in Queen's-row, Kennington, about fourteen yards from Kennington-road—I was in my shop—I heard a cry of "Stop thief—I ran out and saw two persons running—I tried to stop the first—he made a leap past me—I cannot swear that it was the prisoner—he dropped 1 bundle of cigars on the ground—I distinctly saw it fall—Rowe was running after him and crying "Stop thief!"—I kept my eye on the person till he got into the arms of the policeman—though I do not recollect his person, he wai the person that Rowe was following.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You stooped down to pick up the bundle and then ran again? A.  Yes—I did not see the prisoner's face till I got to the station—the inspector asked if I knew the lad—I said, "No"—I asked the conitt-ble if the prisoner was the person he stopped, and then I said it was him that dropped the bundle—there was nobody else in the street.
