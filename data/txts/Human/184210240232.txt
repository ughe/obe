Some months—I have known of it since May last, I cannot Bay how long before—I was present on the occasion of Mrs. Clark coming from the inquest, and saying that it was again postponed—that was after the first adjournment—Mrs. Clark stated some observation made by out of the Jury—the prisoner smiled, and my mother chided her for laughing at it—she said she did not consider it was a time for levity—the prisoner directly said if she laughed it was not right, if she cried it was not right, what was she to do?—she had cried at various times, but not that evening, and my mother had cheered her when she was low spirited, comforted her, and said, "Don't cry"—the prisoner said she considered people were very much prejudiced against her, and she did not know for what or for why—she said a great many would glory to see her hanged, but there was one above who knew all, and that was the one she would trust to—she is a person of a good and humane disposition—we have been brought up together as sisters—she was very open until her marriage, and then her circumstances had been so different, that her spirits have been curbed.
