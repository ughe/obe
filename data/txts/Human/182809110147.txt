 1825.  JAMES TOONE was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 pair of boots, value 2s., the goods of Peter Thwaites.
 PETER THWAITES. I am a  bootmaker, and live in Aylesbury-street, Clerkenwell; the prisoner was my apprentice. On the 17th of August, about two o'clock, he was leaving my shop with some dirty linen; I searched his bundle, and found a pair of child's double soled boots; I asked him how he came to put them there - he said he had an order for them on the Sunday before, from a neighbour, I think he said, next door to him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had he not before that sold two pairs of boots and shoes to a gentleman? A. He never took but one pair from me, by coming and saying he wanted a pair for a gentleman. I did not want to get his indentures cancelled - it was not because he was an apprentice from a school, and the consent of the Trustees was wanting, that I did not have it done; I do not know Mr. Hudson; the prisoner said he had a pair of boots ordered from him, and he was taking them to him.
   HARRIET THWAITES. I am the prosecutor's wife. I missed the boots from the stock.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am a constable, and took up the prisoner. I asked him how he came to take them, and what he meant to do with the money - he said to spend it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was any one present at this conversation? A. Yes; it was in Mr. Thwaites' shop - he did not say he had got an order for them while I was there - I swore this before the Magistrate.
   PETER THWAITES. When I opened the bundle he gave me the account I stated, and then I went for an officer - the officer came in the mean time.
   HARRIET THWAITES. He said he had got an order for them, and was going to take the money and spend it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Why did you not state that in your first examination? A. I was not called in at Hatton-garden; the officer has not been speaking to me since I went down - he was speaking, but not to me; I heard some one tell him to hold his tongue.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1826.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of August , 1 skittle-ball, value 4s., the goods of David Skivens.
 THOMAS OSBORNE. I am a shoemaker, and lodge at the sign of the Victory, near Hackney-road. On the evening of the 13th of August I saw the prisoner go out of the skittle-ground into the field; I spoke to Massingham, and he went after him; I saw him take a ball from him, and he said he was very sorry.
   JAMES MASSINGHAM. I am servant to Mr. Skiven, who keeps the  Victory public-house, in Cambridge-street, Hackney-road. Osborne gave me information - I came out, and saw the prisoner with this ball under his arm; I took it from him; he said, "Pray let me go - pray forgive me - this is the first thing I have done."
   DAVID SKIVENS. I am owner of the skittle-ground - this is my ball.
   JOHN COOK. I am an officer. I was sent for: the prisoner said he was very sorry.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had a paralytic stoke, and which had partly deprived him of the use of his intellects.
 JURY to MR. BARRETT (clerk of the papers). Q. Is there any truth in this statement? A. He has been much afflicted, and has been in the workhouse; I have not seen any change in him with respect to his faculties; but he has stated that he was better.
 GUILTY. Aged 45. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
