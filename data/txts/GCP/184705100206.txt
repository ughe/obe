Webster's Defence. My Lord and gentlemen of the jury: the offenced
which I stand charged is one of a very heavy character, and which, la
very sorry to say, has brought me before your notice this day. My Land
and gentlemen, perhaps you will not credit what I am going to say to you
but I will fearlessly speak to you, convinced that I am innocent, and the
what I am going to say is true. It is true that I was in Richmond durine
the day on which the fire occurred, and I also was in the company of the
other persons charged with me, and that I was also present at the fire; but!
can assure your Lordship that the stack was not fired by me. But beine
reduced to the deepest distress, through the want of work, and not barine
tasted food for some time past, I was induced to accompany the other per
sons, and accuse myself of committing the crime, which has caused my friend
a great deal of anguish and regret, that I had not applied to them for assis
tance. My Lord, I was never in the company of those persons accused with
me, but on theday the fire occurred; nor can I, to my recollection, ever
member seeing them before. The only thing that criminates me, is the
marks which the officer states was made by the shoes which I wore; but!
can assure you that they were not the print of my footsteps; for I never
entered the field where the ground was capable of receiving any impression.
My Lord, it is not at all improbable that the marks were caused by some perso
who assisted at the fire, and who, in the excitement of the moment, has nk
since remembered crossing the field. But, my Lord, I hope you will pause
before you proceed to sum up the evidence adduced against me; for it would
be doing me a great injustice without being convinced of my guilt; for the
confession which I signed in an unthinking moment, and which has been
used as evidence against me, which the keen pangs of hunger has induced me
to put my signature to, is a falsehood, of which I have had reason to repent.
My lord, taking all these considerations before your notice, and weighing the
evidence brought against me, I may be led to hope for a favourable issued
my trial, and which I may be led to expect from an impartial and uncorrupted
jury.
WILLIAM Pocock (police-constable F 14.) I produce a certificate of
Webster's former conviction—(read-Convicted June, 1844, and confined two
years)— I was present at the trial —he is the person-he has been convicted
at this Court since that, and had three months- I proved the former convio
tion then.

HOBBS-Guilty. Aged 23.-Transported for Fifteen Years.
LEWER-GUILTY. Aged 16.-Transported for Ten Years.
