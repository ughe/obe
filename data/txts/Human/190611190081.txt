Webb said in Jackson's presence that £300 had been placed in the business. Jackson did not make any comment. They told me they were opening a shop at 38, St. Peters Street. On July 16 I got an order from Webb, Taylor, and Co. for £60 worth of goods, and they were delivered the following day at 38, St Peter's Street. They have not been paid for. Afterwards I saw Jackson and Taylor at our office, and they asked us to have the lease of the shop, 38, St Peter's Street, assigned to us as security against a further credit of £50 or £60. I saw Webb on July 19 at their office in Cloth Fair, and he said that they had had some trouble with their banking account, because they had arranged to pay in money to toe Ludgate Hill branch of the London and County Bank instead of the Tower Bridge branch, and the money had not been transferred from Aldersgate Street quick enough to Tower Bridge, and that was the reason way some of their cheques were returned unpaid. On July 16 we had a cheque for pre  vious goods, but no cheque for the £60 worth. On July 24 he made an empty promise from a friend of his who would pay all the creditors in full if we would make the firm bankrupt in order to get Jackson and Taylor out of it. Fisher was there when he said it.
