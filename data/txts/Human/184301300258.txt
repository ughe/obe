MATTHEW GODBARD( police-constable M  167.) I took the prisoner on the 8th of Dec.—Mr. Judd told him what it was for—he said he was very orry, and wished Mr. Judd to look over it, and come to some sort of terms—he said he hoped I would persuade Mr. Judd to be merciful to him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you take a book from him? A.  Yes—he said he was very sorry he had spent Mr. Judd's money in a drunken frolic—he did not say how much it was—he had this book in his hand—he and Mr. Judd were together some time, and had some conversation about accounts—they were talking about some former balances, and the prisoner said, "I thought you would have taken this as you have the other"—Mr. Judd said he would do no such thing—they went to their books, and were some few minutes going through them—I did not see that Mr. Judd had his book in his hand—he had some papers. CHARLES WRIGHT. I am attorney for this prosecution. I attended at the prisoner's first examination—he was remanded from Thursday till the following Saturday week to give me time to inquire—he attended then, and was remanded till the 7th of Jan. to give me further time—before the 7th of Jan. a Session was holden here—I came and preferred this bill by desire of Mr. Cottingham—I applied that the depositions should be taken, and the Magistrate said he saw no difficulty in the matter.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you tell him for what you wanted time? A.  I supposed there were further cases—I do not think I had one of the witnesses there at first—I did not enter into the several charges—I did not know of the washhand-stand then—it was on the Thursday evening—I rather think the looking-glass was the only charge that was then made—I think the others came out afterwards—I went again on the Saturday week following, and then the Magistrate did not take any depositions—the prisoner complained that I had not gone before the Grand Jury, and the Magistrate said, "I gave time to find cases"—the prisoner said the Magistrate had remanded him to give me an opportunity to go before the Grand Jury—on that occasion no depositions were taken—the Magistrate did not tell me that the Grand Jury would sit on the 5th of Jan.—he said he would remand the prisoner till the 7th of Jan. to give me an opportunity to go before the Grand Jury.
   COURT . Q.  When the prisoner complained that he had been remanded for you to make application to the Grand Jury, what did the Magistrate say? A.  He said, "Certainly not"—he gave me time to gain evidence. NOT GUILTY.
