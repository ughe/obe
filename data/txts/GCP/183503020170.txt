JAMES HUMPHREYS Wood. I am a Captain in the Royal Artillery,
quartered at Woolwich. On Saturday night, the 21st of February, between
half-past eleven and twelve o'clock at night, I was in a street in Woolwich,
which I do not know the name of, returning to my quarters- I was accosted
by a man in the undress of the Marine Corps, who instantly knocked my
hat off my head-I only observed one man at that moment-I was rolled
up in my cloak, and in raising my arms to protect myself, or to save my
hat, I fell, and at that time I saw two men, the one who knocked my ha
off, and another man close behind-they both ran off-in about a minute,
I saw the watchman, who asked if I had been ill-treated—I said I had
he said, if I would remain where I was, he would go in pursuit, and in 1
short time he returned with the hat, and said, “Do you intend to proceed
against the parties ?"-I said, “Certainly," and I gave him my address
I could not tell who the men were, but I noticed one was in the dress of
marine, and the other had a red jacket and a foraging cap on-I saw the
prisoners, on the Friday following, in the Court at Woolwich, but I could
not say whether they were the men I had seen that night, or not-at the
time my hat was taken off, the man made some casual remark about the
night, but I paid very little attention to it.
JAMES MORRIS. I am a parish watchman of Woolwich. I was on duty
