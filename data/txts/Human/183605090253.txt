JOHN CHAPMAN. I was with the prosecutor at the King's Arms—we had been there three hours and a half or four hours—we had ale to drink—I do not think we had any thing else—I paid half-a-crown for the drink—as we proceeded along a street leading into the Borough-road, we were not by two persons—they were together—I could see there were some persons in the street, but not so as distinguish them—they came up towards us, and both attacked my friend—I ran away, and cannot tell what happened afterwards—I did not see my friend on the ground—they were both strug gling together when I quitted the spot—they had not got him on the ground when I left—I passed on when they attacked me—it was in the horse way I rather think; it was by the side of the pin manufactory—it was not on the flag-stones—I cannot say what they were scuffling about when I ran off—I cannot say whether anything was said when they came up—I did not call "Murder"—it was put down as such before the Magistrate, but I did not assert it—this is my signature—I do not think it was read over to me—I was sworn.
   Q.  You were sworn to tell the truth, and said, "I saw the prisoners walk-up to us, and immediately the prosecutor was on the ground—I ran off directly, and called out "Murder, and went home:" now, did you me him on the ground? A.  No, I cannot say I did.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Perhaps you forgot what you swore before she Magistrate? A.  There might be a little error regarding what I might say before the Magistrate—my evidence was taken down—I cannot say it was send over—it was not, to the best of my belief—I was asked to sign it, and did—I had been taking more ale that night than I usually did—it might have got into my head—I cannot remember a word passing between the parties when they began the scuffle—I was frightened, I cannot say that I called out "Murder"—I do not think Harding paid any thing that night for drink—another person was with us—he paid about 1 s . perhaps—I cannot say whether it was 1 s .—6 d ., or 18 d .—I think the ale was 9 d .—I cannot say whether it skittle-ground—I do not think we drank more than four quarts—there was smoking—I did not stop long enough to ascertain whether one of the prisoners was tipsy. JOHN RICHARD COWLING. I am a policeman. I was in the Borough-road on the night in question, and heard a cry of "Murder" for Harding—I heard the cry of "Murder" from more than one person, I think—I went up, and found the prosecutor and David Nunn strnggling on the ground—I did not see the other—I passed a third person just before I got there, but whether it was the other prisoner or the last witness I cannot say—I parted them—the prosecutor said he had lost his hat—John Butt, a policeman, came up with it immediately—the prosecutor claimed it—there was no handkerchief in it—neither the prisoner David nor the prosecutor were drunk—the other prisoner was brought back in custody, with the hat—It think he had been drinking a little, but he was not drunk.
