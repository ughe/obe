Had you observed anything at all peculiar in the prisoner's conduct ?
No more than that at the time he was addressing me there was a shake
of the head-I nerer had an interview with him in course of my business ex-
cept once-I observed nothing particular in his manner then, except that he
stood at the door and did not turn his face towards me. ro
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Your observation of him was merely,
that there appeared to be something eccentric or odd about him! 4. He did
not appear to me to be in anger at all, the only thing I noticed was the shaking
of the head as he was uttering those words, when I received the ball-I had
had a short interview with him some months before; I observed nothing remark.
able about him then, except that be stood at the door, uttered a few words,
and did not turn bis face towards me, and left the counting-house-when he
came in, he stood sideways, and did not turn his face to me--he mentioned
that he could not pay the account, and immediately left-I cannot say there
ve anything remarkable or out of the way in his doing that; he was en-
gaged in picking his fingers at the time, not muttering to himself, merely
mentioning that he could not pay the account--that was on the first occasion
-there was never at any time any quarrel between us, nor any appearance
of misunderstanding this was a mere matter of business.
Court. Q. There was nothing particular in his manner at all to attract
your attention? 4. Nothing whatever.

MR. CLARKSON. Q. I do not know whether you observed either after
the time, or almost at the same time when he discharged the pistol at you,
whether he discharged another at himself? A. I was looking, and I found
he was getting something out of his pocket; but I cannot say that I noticed
what was done with it.
Court. Q. I understand that after you felt the ball and heard the pistol,
you looked and saw him apparently going to take something from his pocket,
but you cannot tell whether he did or not? 4. Exactly.
