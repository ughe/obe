 MR. FIELD. The whole of the money found on Boyle is counterfeit; the shilling and half-crown produced, are also counterfeits - they are all of the same metal; the halfcrown appears to be from the same mould as the others - the shillings are three different impressions.
 Cross-examined. Q. I believe these two metals are different? A. They are precisely alike, but one has undergone a preparation to fit it better for circulation, to imitate money which is worn and greasy.

 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.

 1447.  MORRIS MAGNUS was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of May , 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; 2 gowns, value 2l.; 1 waistcoat, value 1l.; 1 pair of trousers, value 30s.; 2 shawls, value 30s.; 20 sovereigns, 7 shillings, three 10l., and three 5l. Bank notes, the property of Isaac Bradnack; against the Statute.
   JOHN THOMAS KINGSTON. On the 3rd of May, at half-past three o'clock, I was in Wentworth-street; I saw the prisoner come out of George-yard, carrying a blue bundle - he looked aslant two or three times, then turned to the left and walked gradually on; three or four of his companions came to him and asked how he got on - he went to No. 55, Wentworth-street, where he was taken into custody - the end of Wentworth-street goes into Osborne-street; I saw a horse and chaise - there was a lady in it; in about ten minutes I saw Mr. Isaac Bradnack talking to the lady sitting in the chaise - he then went to Lambeth-street; he did not go to the house I saw the prisoner go into, but to the office; he complained of the loss of a blue bundle, and I went with Healy to the house in Wentworth-street, that I had seen the prisoner go into - Healy entered the house, and came out with the prisoner; that was about three quarters of an hour after I saw the prisoner go in with the bundle.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you search the house in Wentworth-street? A. No; I did not go in- I had not known the prisoner before; I am sure I am not mistaken in his person.
 ISAAC BRADNACK. On Tuesday, the 3rd of May, I was sitting in a chaise at the corner of  Wentworth-street, close by Osborne-street, about half-past three o'clock - a man came up to me, and asked me some indistinct question; I had not any suspicion of him - I stooped down and said,"What do you say?" he then left the chaise - I turned to the left, and missed a bundle in a blue handkerchief: I saw a man running away with it - I did not see any other man running; I have no doubt it was the prisoner - I called,"Stop thief! stop that villian!" he had run behind the gig, and up Wentworth-street - the bundle contained a new waistcoat, a pair of pantaloons, two silk dresses, two shawls, three 10l. notes, three 5l. notes, and fifteen sovereigns wrapped up in a piece of brown paper; and in another paper there were five sovereigns - I have not seen any of it since.
 Cross-examined. Q. This money would have been more safe in your pocket? A. Yes; the man ran with his back towards me, but he turned his face two or three times - I did not say I could not swear to the prisoner; I said it was a very serious thing to swear to a man, but I had no doubt upon my mind - I only meant that there was a possibility of a doubt.
   DAVID HEALY. On the 3rd of May I went with Kingston to No. 55, Wentworth-street - I went into the house, and inquired below who was up stairs; they said a woman who lay in, and that was all - this was about four o'clock in the afternoon; I looked through the crack of a door, and saw two persons lying in bed - I went in, turned down the bed, and found the prisoner in bed with a female - he had all his clothes and his shoes on, and so had the female; they had both been under the clothes - I brought the prisoner down stairs, and asked Kingston to look at him - the prisoner said he was in bed in consequence of his shirt being washed; he had no shirt on - I had got from Kingston a description of the man I was in search of, and the prisoner's appearance corresponded with that; I took the prisoner up to Whitechapel - I had another with me, and as we were going up the passage of the office Mr. Braduack pointed to the prisoner, and said, "That is the man;" I said, "You must be very particular, it is a very serious thing;" he walked up and down the passage two or three times, looked at the prisoner again, and said, "I am confident that is the man;" he persisted in asserting that he was the man.
 Cross-examined. Q. I dare say the gentleman described him as a man without a shirt? A. No, Sir; the prosecutor said that it was a serious thing to swear to a man, and so did I - the prisoner went with us to Whitechapel, in custody; I cannot say whether it had struck four o'clock - it was a short time after the prosecutor alleged he had lost his bundle - I searched the house, but found nothing belonging to the prosecutor; the woman who was in bed was Elizabeth Hall, I believe - she was taken and discharged.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   ELIZABETH HALL. I keep the house in which the prisoner was taken - I was not in bed with him; that was Elizabeth Christmas; the prisoner had not been out - he had been at home all day; Christmas had washed his shirt and it was drying in the yard - I had been up to him three times in the course of the day, to make him get up; I did not know he had not a change of linen, and he did not like to tell me.
