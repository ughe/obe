EDWARD TATE (17 ER). On July 20th I was on duty in Drury Lane about
2.40 p.m., at the corner of High Holborn, and saw an old gentleman run-
ning along Drury Lane in a most excited state-he made a complaint to
me, in consequence of which I went to Bottenden Street, but did not see
either of the prisoners-later in the afternoon I went with Oxley and
Callaghan to the Sun public-house - I took hold of Crowther-he struck
me a violent blow on my hand with a very large walking-stick-he hurt
me, and I was compelled to go on the sick list for three weeks; I had no
use of my hand-he also kicked me several times on my legs-I over-
powered him, got him outside the house, and took him to the station-he
resisted on the way, and I had to strike him several times with my
truncheon in self defence-I have not seen the old gentleman since.
Cross-examined by Crowther. He told me he had been knocked down by
a gang of roughs, pointing down Drury Lane, who attempted to rob him
-I asked him if he could give any description--he said, "No"; he had
