WILLIAM GIGGS. I bought this cart of the prisoner on the 25th of May for 7 l .
   Prisoner.  My name had been on the cart above twelve months before I had it—it was on it when I had it. GEORGE DEADMAN re-examined.  Another man hired it before the prisoner, and let him put his name on it—when it came back to our place we asked how it came to have that name on it, and they said they could sell a load of hay better if the cart had a name on it, and they thought it came out of the country. HENRY BAKER. I sold the cart for Mr. Giggs. GEORGE VEALE. I bought the cart of Mr. Baker. DANIEL DEACON. The prisoner left the cart in my place in May last—he came there and sold it. THOMAS LANGLEY( policeman. ) I took the prisoner into custody in Drury-lane—he said the cart was at Paddington—he did not say any thing about selling it. JAMES DEADMAN re-examined.  He was to pay every week, but he did not pay at all—he lived in James-street when he first hired it, and then moved—we did not know where to. GUILTY. Aged 33.— Confined Six Months .
 333. WILLIAM EDMONDS and CHARLES SIMPSON were indicted for stealing 2 cocoa nuts, value 8 d  ., the goods of Joseph Huxley . MARIA HUXLEY. I am the wife of Joseph Huxley, of Regent-street, Vauxhall-road. On the 7th of Dec. I missed two cocoa-nuts from inside my shop—I ran out, and caught Simpson, but the nuts have not been found. THOMAS BOTTOMLEY. I was going on an errand—I saw Edmonds go into the shop, and take two cocoa nuts—Simpson was outside to see if any body came—they walked off together.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  You know them both, do you not? A.  Yes—I am not in the habit of playing with them—I have seen them about with had boys—it was between five and six o'clock when I saw them—I was sent out to get a candle. JAMES HENDERSON( policeman. ) I took the prisoners—Simpson said Edmonds took the nuts, and threw them into the waste ground in the Vaux hall-road. NOT GUILTY.
 334. DAVID CLEARY and PATRICK CALMAN were indicted for stealing 92 yards of flannel, value 4 l  . 10 s  .; the goods of John Naylor . JOHN NAYLOR. I am a linen-draper, and live in Tottenham-court-road. On the 2nd of December, between eleven and twelve o'clock in the morning, I was dressing my shop-window, and the prisoners came in, to look at some white window-blinds, which hung over some flannel—Calman said they would wear black (I saw he had a black patch on his head) they did not buy that, but bought a yard of green leno—about two or three o'clock I saw them again, and about a quarter past four a lady came in and bought something, and then I saw Calman again—a man called out that a piece of flannel was gone—I saw cleary with it—I called, "Stop thief," and he dropped it. CHARLES PURFORD( policeman. ) At a quarter before four o'clock, on the 2nd of December, I was on duty, and saw the two prisoners together, looking from shop to shop—I watched them for three quarters of an hour—I then got into Goodge-street, and heard a cry of "Stop thief"—I ran down Char lotte-street, and found Cleary running—I took him, and on the road to the station, this ticket of the flannel dropped from him—the prosecutor identified him as the person he took the flannel from.
