The prisoner had been my barman for about five months—in con  sequence of information, I called in sergeant Parker, and marked 3 l . worth of silver in his presence—it is my custom to clear the till at night, and to put into it 1 l . worth of silver tied up in paper, 5 s . loose, and 5 s . in copper—our business begins at five o'clock in the morning, and the prisoner was there alone till breakfast time—we have a great morning business from potato-men and others—our average taking is from 9 s . to 10 s . in a morning—I had given the prisoner notice, and he was to quit my service on Tuesday, the 7th of Feb.—on the night before that, I gave 1 l . worth of marked money to my housekeeper, Mrs. Birt, to place into the till—I do not sleep in the house, bat on the 7th I went to the house, and took a policeman with me—I told the prisoner I wished to search his box—he made no objection—the officer asked him if he had got any money about him—he said a few shillings, and that was all—the officer asked him to show him the silver—he pulled his purse out—it contained 22 s . or 23 s ., and amongst them were two half-crowns and five shillings that were marked with the marks I had put on the money I gave my housekeeper—he was then asked if he had any more money about him—he said, "No, I have no more"—the officer then searched him, and found two shillings and a sixpence folded in paper—one of the shillings was marked—he was asked bow they came in his pocket, and made no answer—with respect to the other money, he said that he had got change for a sove reign that morning.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did he apply to you for his wages? A.  He came down for his wages—a little more than 4 l . is due to him for two months and a fortnight—I was to pay him quarterly—it is not unsual to pay every week—I paid him 5 l . in Dec.—I have not paid him the 4 l .—I have his clothes—I am ready to give them up, but I have not been applied to—I think the exact expression he made use of when he was asked what he had got in his pockets, was, "A few shillings in silver"—he did not say "somesilver"—he said "a few shillings"—I swear he used the word "few"—he pulled a bad half-crown from his pocket, and put it on the mantel-piece—that had no business in his pocket.
