FANNY CARR. I am the wife of William Carr, a cab proprietor, of
Claremont-mews, Clerkenwell. On 23rd Dec. I was standing at the loft
door of No. 18, about half-past twelve--I saw George coming down the mews
with his horse and cart-he asked the prisoner to move his father's cabo-
the prisoner made use of bad language, and told him to move them himsell.
and struck him, knocked him down with this trussel, and while down
chucked it on his head-he was standing as close to him as he possibly
could-after George had gone to the doctor's, I saw the prisoner and Mrs.
George-she said to the prisoner, “You will nab it; he has gone to the
hospital"-he said, " I wish I had killed the b-r, I want a rest;" and said
he could do three months off his head at any time---George and the prisoner
were perfectly sober.

John RICHARD WAGSTAFF. I am in the employ of Mr. Dew, a hosier, of
10, Claremont-terrace. On 23rd Dec. I was outside the stable-door in the
mews, and saw George come in with his horse and cart-be pulled up-two
cabs were before his door-he asked the prisoner to move them-be told hin
to move them himself and be b-d-he moved the cabs—the prisoner came
and struck bim right and left in his face, and knocked him down, and as he
was getting up he pushed him down with this piece of wood, and chucked
it at his head-he had not done anything to provoke him--they were both
sober. .
