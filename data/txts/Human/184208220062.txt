 2254. ROBERT BAGGOTT and JAMES PULLEN were indicted for stealing, on the   11th of July , 10 1/2 lbs. weight of ham, value 10 s  ., the goods of John Smith . JOHN SMITH. I keep the Victoria tea-gardens at Bayswater. About twenty minutes past eleven o'clock at night, on the llth of July, I missed a ham—I saw it safe just before—this is the knuckle of it.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Is not your name Jones? A.  No, I went by that name for thirty years—it was the name of my nurse who brought me up—I did not know my parents. JOHN KIMBREY( police-constable D  102.) On the night of the 11th of July I stopped Baggott with a ham under his jacket—Pullen was walking with him towards the Edgware-road, down Cambridge-street—I asked Baggott what he had got—they walked on, and I spoke to them again—Pullen fell back, and said it was a ham they had picked up down by the Crown, at Bayswater—I saw there was no dirt upon it—I said, "You never picked this ham up there"—they said they were coming from Ham mersmith, and picked it up between the Crown and the Victoria gate—the ham was warm.
   BAGGOTT  — GUILTY. Aged 23.
   PULLEN  — GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined Three Months.

ROBERT ROBSON. I live in Long Acre. About nine o'clock, on the 8th of August, I was going down Snow-hill—I missed my handkerchief—I turned, and saw the prisoner running—this is my handkerchief—no one was near but the prisoner. CHARLES GRANT. I live in Alpha-grove, Holloway. I was in Far ringdon-market, and heard a cry of "Stop thief"—the prisoner passed me—I followed him to Shoe-lane—he went into a house—he threw this hand kerchief behind him—I took it up, and gave it to Mr. Robson.
