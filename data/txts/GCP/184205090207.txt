GEORGE JAMES FLETCHER.On Good Friday night, the 25th of March,
I went to bed at half-past eleven o'clock-the house was perfectly fastened
before I went to bed-I was very particular in seeing every thing fast,
because it was a very stormy night-I got up about a quarter after six in
the morning, and found the window of the back-room on the basement
story broken open-we use that room as a counting-house--the window
does not open-it is a fixture-part of the sash was cut out, and two squares
of glass taken out--I found about 8l, worth of copper money missing, con-
sisting of 58. packages, and about 48. worth of halfpence in a bag--the

RICHARD OGLE. I am a green-grocer, and live in King-street, Dept
ford, about 200 yards from Fletcher's. On Saturday morning, the 26th of
March, I saw the prisoner at a quarter after six o'clock with another young
man named Barry, in New King-street-I heard the other say, "Go back,
you b—-, for the policeman is coming"--they were coming in a direction
from Fletcher's at the time--they then turned towards London-the young
man with the prisoner went a little way up to see if a constable was coming,
and then they both joined and went towards London I saw no more of
them--the young man had beckoned to the prisoner, and then he joined him.
