CHARLES TALMEDGE. I am principal gauger to the East and West India
Dock Company-on the 31st of March I ganged thirteen puncheons of rum
on the quay- I found an average deficiency of four gallons in each puncheon
from evaporation and condensation of the liquor during the voyage- I gauged
the puncheon, No. 815, and found a deficiency in that of five gallons, and in
819 a deficiency of six gallons.

into the West India Dock? 4. Yes-Gingell was not on board or I should

saw no more of it.

1064. JOHN ROBERT NOYES was indicted for stealing, on the 18th
of April, 48 brushes, value 6l., the goods of Edwin Bliss, his master; and
MARY ANN NOYES for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing
the same to have been stolen; to which
JOHN ROBERT NOYES pleaded Guilty.
MR. WILDE conducted the Prosecution,
CHARLES JEFFREYS. I am manager to Mr. Edwin Bliss, a brush many-
facturer in Barbican. The male prisoner was our errand-boy--we missed a
great number of brushes from time to time-I have seen some brushes since
which resemble those we missed-I know Alice Noyes from seeing her at our
house-I gave the prisoners into custody.
EDWARD KING. I am assistant to Mr. Cocksedge, pawnbroker, Church-
street, Shoreditch. I produce eleven brushes-part of them were pawned by
Mary Ann Noyes--1 bave one pawned by her on the 4th of Aug., 1845, in
the name of Ann Smith-I knew her by that name generally.
WILLIAM UNDERWOOD. I am assistant to Mr. Attenborough, a pawn.
broker. I produce eight brushes, pawned by Mary Ann Noyes, in the name
of Ann Robinson, on the 3rd of Dec., and on the 19th of March.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. Is yours a considerable place of busi-
ness? A. Yes I have seen her as a customer perhaps four or five times in
the last six months.
MR. WILDE. A. Have you any doubt of her? A. No.
WILLIAM BOLTWOOD. I am assistant to Mr. Cotton, a pawnbroker. I
produce four bryshes, pawned by Mary Ann Noyes--I had seen her before,
and knew her in the name of Ann Evans-she offered some more brushes,
which I did not take in.
ALFRED PRITCHETT. I am assistant to Mr. Sowerby, a pawnbroker. I
produce fourteen brushes-four were pawned on the 27th, and four on the
29th of Aug.--the others were in the window for sale--four of them were
pawned by Mary Ann Noyes for 68.--I cannot say who pawned the
others.
Cross-examined. Q. Her name is not on the duplicate ? A. No, they
were pawned in the name of Ann Smith-I suppose it was six months since
I had seen her before.
MARY ANN NOYES-Nor Guilty.
