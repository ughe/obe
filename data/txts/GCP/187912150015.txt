The Prisoners' Statements before the Magistrate. Manning says: "I
know nothing about it. I did not know it was a bad one. Upton says:
"I know nothing at all about it."
Manning's Defence. I met Upton, who asked me to have a drop of rum.
We went into the public house, and he said “Call for half a quartern of
rum," and he gave me a florin. I put it on the bar, and as soon as the
mistress gave me the change I gave it to him. The policeman found 28. 14d.
on me, which could not be the change of the florin. I never attempted to
move from the spot, but tried to prove my innocence.
Upton's Defence. I earned 21. by gambling, and I had 21. 38. in my
pocket. I was drunk, met this man, whom I knew, and asked him to have
a drop of something, but I did not give him 28. I paid myself. That florin
was not bad. I tried to bend it, but could not. I did not throw it away,
I spent it. The one she took was bad, no doubt, but whether it was the
same or not I don't know. My benefit was to have come off last Tuesday, but
I was locked up.
TREBUIE
MRS. WILMER (Re-exumined). Upton appeared perfectly sober.
MANNING-Not Guilty. UPTON-GUILTY. -Eighteen Months Im-
prisonment. He had been seventeen times convicted of assaults on the police,
and drunkenness.
