 1469.  EDWARD EVANS and WILLIAM WOOD were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 1 key, value 3d.; 1 trowel, value 1s. 6d.; 1 pair of slippers, value 1s.; the goods of Richard Chalener ; and 3 locks, value 3s.; 4 finger-plates, value 1s. 6d.; and 2 brass knobs, value 6d.; the goods of the said Richard Chalener, then fixed to a building, against the Statute.
 RICHARD CHALENER. I live at No. 23,  Grafton-street- the house is under repair - these are my articles, and were fixed to the building - I missed them at five o'clock in the morning, on Monday week.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you mean they were all fixed to the building? A. These locks and plates and knobs were - I have fitted them to their places, and they correspond exactly.
   HENRY ALDERSON(police-constable C 164). On the 2nd of September I was on duty in Grafton-street - I heard a noise at No. 23 - I went over, and the gentleman at No. 22 looked out, as he had heard a noise - I waited till Bishop came up - we went into No. 22, and got into No. 23 - we heard some persons run up stairs, and we went up- I saw a hand taking a ladder through a trap door at the top - I jumped up, caught the ladder, and went up - I found the trap door of No. 24 was open - I went in, and found Evans in bed, with his stockings and drawers on, and Wood was on a bed on the floor - there was some lime on his shirt, and on the bed likewise - I found a bunch of keys on the table, and a knife - Bishop and I desired them to get up, and they were taken to the station-house - I went back to No. 23, and found a sack, with these things in it, on the roof - there was a puddle of water on the roof, and I found wet marks in the room where the prisoners were - Wood's shoes were wet.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you find Evans's shoes? A. No; Wood was in his shirt; but it was some minutes after I tapped at the door before it was opened - I did not examine Evans's feet, but his hands were dirty - there were some women on the stairs of No. 24.
   HUGH BISHOP. I am an officer. I went in with Alderson - I saw some persons go into No. 24 - we got down, and tracked them by some lime - we knocked at a room door, but it was five minutes before we got admission - I heard some bustling inside - Wood was on an old piece of bed on a blanket, and Evans was in bed - I asked him how long he had been in bed - he said about two o'clock - I took him to the station-house, and in going along he threw this knob down an area - I found some of these things on Evans, under his shirt - this key fits one of the locks, which was found on the premises.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you tell the magistrate you heard a hustling in the room? A. Yes; I cannot tell whether that is in the deposition - I only saw one woman in the house, who appeared to live there - I went into the next room - I did not look at Evans's shoes - he took them from under the bed, and put them on - this lantern was burning on the roof.
 Evans's Defence. I have got locks to fit these keys.
 Wood's Defence. I was sitting smoking with Evans.
 (  Alfred Dixon, a saddle-maker, of Short's gardens; and Alfred Watson, a publican, gave Wood a good character; Thomas Hacket, a broker, of Gould-square; Thomas Coxworthy, Westminster Bridge-road; William Ashman, and Bernice Grant, gave Evans a good character.)
 EVANS - GUILTY. Aged 51.
 WOOD - GUILTY. Aged 56.
 Recommended to mercy - Transported for Seven Years.
 1470.  THOMAS SUTHERLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of August , 1 saddle, value 3l.; 1 bridle, value 4s.; and 1 horse-cloth, value 20s.; the goods of John Tilbury.
   NEWCOMB MASON. I am an assistant to Mr. Tilbury, of Mount-street, Grosvenor-square- I missed a great many saddles, bridles, and horse cloths - I was passing Mr. Langdon's shop last Wednesday, and saw this horse cloth, which I knew was my master's - I then traced it to the prisoner - the saddle was found at Lambeth - the prisoner had been a helper in the stable, but left nearly three months ago.
   WILLIAM LANGDON. I am a saddler. I bought the saddle and horse-cloth for 2l. 10s., of Marrs, whom I have known eight years.
   EDWARD MARRS. I sold the saddle, horse-cloth, and this snap-bridle to Langdon - Riggs brought them into Mr. Jackson's yard, where I was at work - I did not know Riggs, but I came into the yard, and one of our men said,"Here is something to be sold, do you know a man that will buy them?" - I said, "Yes, if it is all right" - I took and sold them, and paid Riggs 30s., which was what he asked.
   THOMAS RIGGS. One night, a fortnight before this, I met the prisoner at the corner of Duke-street - he asked if I was doing any thing - I said, "No;" he told me to go to him the next morning - I did so, and we went to a house, and had a pint of beer - he then said he had these things to sell for his brother, who was going out of town with a gentleman - I asked him if it was all right; he said it was - I took them to Mr. Jackson, and gave them to the ostler, who gave them to Marrs - I went back and asked the prisoner if it was all right; he said he would not have any thing to do with it without it was.
 Prisoner's Defence. I found them in the Swan passage, near Mr. Tilbury's - I gave them to Riggs to sell - he came back, and said he had sold them for 26s. - he gave me 25s. and we spent 1s. - I gave him 4s. for his trouble.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Confined Nine Months.
