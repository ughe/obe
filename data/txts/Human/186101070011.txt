   Cross-examined by MR. PALMER . Q.  At the time you met the prisoner, how far were you from the prosecutor's house? A.  About three hundred yards—I was not watching—I had not been set to watch by the prosecutor—the prisoner was going towards Whitechapel—he told me that he had seen the man who employed him before—he did not say he was to be paid for carrying the parcel—he carried the hamper to the station, and it was locked up in a cell there by me that Saturday night—it was re-searched on the Tuesday—I did not search it on the Saturday, we merely took the bottles out and put them back again—it was full of straw—I saw that there were no more bottles there and that was all. JAMES MCLEOD. I was with Watkins when the prisoner was appre hended—we examined the hamper, took the bottles out, and replaced them—we did not examine further—the hamper was put in the cell and locked up—I examined it again on the Tuesday—I then carefully examined the straw, and found amongst it these two pieces of paper; one with the name of "Warner and Barclay" written on it, and the other, "55, Fore-street, Finsbury"—that is where Messrs. Warner live—I communicated with the prosecutors immediately afterwards and then went to the prisoner's address.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many people had access to the cell where the hamper was? A.  Two of the reserve men, two acting-sergeants, and two inspectors—the key was left in the office—I tied the hamper up with a string when I left it there.
