WILLIAM HALL. I am divisional surgeon at Tottenham-On 10th
March I was called to the station to see the prosecutor-he was uncon-
scious on my arrival, but after half an hour he beoame partially so, and
before I left he was pretty clear-I examined the state of his mouth and
throat-I found a very strong odour of snuff, and particles of snuff
clinging all around his jaw-the remains of what he had been drinking
was shown to me and it was very much impregnated with strong snuff-
I tasted it; it was of a most acrid character- I think drinking a quantity
of stuff like that would put a man into that state--from the potency of it
it would be very nasty-I think he must have been very drunk before he
could be got to take a large quantity of it.
Moekom's Defence. I am quite innocent of giving the gentleman any
snuff, if it was given to him. I was in such a state I did not know if there
was any. I had been a customer and neighbour of Mr. Cox for some
years. He trusted me for a little beer score; we fell out about it, and
he told me if I came in his house again he would have me in.
Heath's Defonce. I was not in this man's company at all, and he can
say the same.
