879. PATRIOK OONNOR (46), Feloniously wounding William
Oleaver, with intent to do grievous bodily harm
Mr. ST. AUBYN Proseouted, and Mr. GEOGHEGAN Defended.
WIIaM CLEAVER. I am a labourer in the Arsenal at Woolwich-I
married a daughter of the prisoner, and we lived in the same house as
the prisoner and his wife-on the night of 24th August, about a quarter
past eleven, I and my wife came home together from the theatre she
knocked at the door, the prisoner opened it-I stopped at the gate talking
to a friend-a soon as the prisoner opened the door. he rushed at me
past my wife with an axe in both his hands, and aimed a blow at my
head with it; I put up my hand, and received the blow in the palm of my
hand, which was cut open about two inches long-I immediately closed
with him, threw him to the ground, and held him till a constable came,
and he was given into custody-my hand was dressed by the doctor at the
station.
Cross-acamined. My wife took away the axe from his hand-I saw that
he had a wound on his nose next day I suppose that was caused in the
struggle-I did not bite him, he said that I did, but it is not true-] saw
him that evening about eight, before I went out, and he bade me good-
night; and I said, 44 Good-night, father," he spoke in a perfeetly friendly
manner-I have seen his discharge from the Army, where he had been
twenty years, it stated that his conduct was good; he has an African
medal, and a good conduct pension of 4s. a day.
ALFRBD AYRES (251 R). On 24th August, shortly after eleven, I was
called outside the prisoner'e house, ad found the prosecutor had him on
the ground, holding him; the prosecutor was bleeding from the hand--I
asked the prisoner why he did it-he made no reply-I believe he was
quite sober-on the way to the station he said, I will do for him," and
at the station he said the same-I produce the hatehet; the prosecutor's
wife gave it me.
Cross-ecamined. At the station the prisoner said he believed he had
been bitten through the nose by the proseoutor-the doctor examined
his nose-I saw marks of blood about it-he was rather exeited-when
I went there he was lying flat on the ground on his back, in the front
garden; he was not struggling, he was lying quite still, and the prose-
cutor holding him-he looked very wild-the hatchet had been taken
from him by that time-I believe the prisoner had been thirteen years
with his regiment in India.
SIDNEY WORTHINGTON. I am a divisional surgeon-I saw the prose-
eutor at the station about midnight on the 24th-he had an incised
wound an inch and a half long, running down the centre of the right
hand to the bone-this hatehet could have done it it is not very sharp
-there must have been considerable violence used-the prisoner had a
wound at the side of his nose, which I dressed; it was a lacerated wound,
like a tear.
GUITy of unlawfully wounding.-Nin Months' Hard Labour, and find
one surety in E100 to keep the peace for twelve montha.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Esg.
