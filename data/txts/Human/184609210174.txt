   Lewis's Defence . I was standing at the corner of King—street; Mr. Clark crossed the road, and asked me to walk down with him; when I got down he gave me 1 1/2 d ., and because I would not submit to him, he tore my clothes, and then said, "You stole my handkerchief;" I said, "If I have, call a policeman;" he would not, but wanted to pull me down Surrey—square; he would not, submit to him, he tore my clothes and then said, "You stole my handkerchief;" I said, "if I have, calls policeman;" he would not, but wanted to pull me down surrey—square; he said if I did not go down a turning with him, he would cut me with a razor; several men came up; he dropped his umbrella; he took it up and struck me and I struck him again; Freebody did not strike him; the officer came up, and I told him what was the matter; the prosecutor had torn my frock; I wanded to pin it up; I went to the Railway and met Wales; she was giving me a pin, and the officer came and took us.
   Wales's Defence . I had not seen Lewis for three weeks; I met her by accident, and she asked me for a pin.
   JURY to JOHN CLARK . Q.  Were you sober? A.  Yes—I only had 4 d . worth of gin and water at the Green Man—I have not the least doubt of the prisoners—I have seen Lewis and Freebody about the road—I did not see Wales when Lewis came up—I was going home.
   COURT . Q.  What are you? A.  I was in the police—I left it about twelve years ago—I have a stipend from Government.
   LEWIS  — GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Nine Months .
   WALES  — GUILTY. Aged 23.— Confined Three Months .

 1964. JOHN COFFIN was indicted for stealing 1 10 l  . Bank note, and 6 5 l  . notes, the property of Alfred Davis . ALFRED DAVIS. I am a law stationer, and live in Castle—street, Holborn. On the 21st of Aug. I played "Richard the Third", in a private theatrical performance, at the Bower Saloon in Stangate—street—I believe it is a licensed theatre—when I went to play I took one 10 l . note, and six 5 l . notes, which were in my pocket, and transferred them from my own clothes to my theatrical pantaloons, which I had on—the prisoner acted as prompter—I lost my property that night—I took off the pantaloons about ten o'clock that night, and threw them from the dressing—room, in which I had been dressed, into another dressing—room, and my impression is that I omitted taking the money out of the pantaloons—one of the notes has been traced—the prisoner was with me part of the time when I undressed—I do not know what became of him when I threw the pantaloons over—if I see the notes I can identify them—here is one with the name of "Butcher and Son" on it, in my writing—here is another with the same writing on it—these are two of the 5 l . notes, and this is the 10 l . note, which is also mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  What is the writing? A.
