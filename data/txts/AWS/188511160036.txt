ARTHUR Cox. I live at 262, City Road, and am a hardware turner
-on Monday, 26th Oetober, about 3 o'elock, I met Darey at a publie-
house-I had never seen him before-we walked about together getting
some bills till about 5 o'clock-I saw him again about a quarter-
past 6 o'cloek in the George public-house, and remained in his company
till about 8 o'eloek-I then left him, and returned to the George about
9 o'eloek--Darey was there-I did not see White at that time-I was
drinking in the same compartment as Darey, but not with him-there
were other persons there-at a quarter-past 9 o'cloek I was drinking
some soda-water, Darey was near me, and I discovered his hand in my
right-hand trousers-poeket, and at the same moment White put his hand
into my left-hand trousers-poeket and took a packet containing six
sovereigns-almost at the same moment I was struck very violently in the
face by Darey, who knoeked me down-I bled very much, and was
partly insensible for some time afterwards-th prisoners made the best
of their way out of the house, having got my money-when I got up
some friends bathed my face, and I was taken home in a cab-I was very
unwell from the effects of the blow, and did not go to the police that
night-next night I went to the George, Darey came in and asked me to
have a drink with him, and put out his hand to shake hands with me--I
made no reply, but kept very close to him for fear he should escape-
sent some one for a policeman-he must have heard me do so; he re-
mained, a policeman came, and I gave him in chargeon Friday afternoon,
the 30th, I saw White at the police-station among a number of others-I
did not feel quite certain of him at first, he seemed to be dressed smarter
and differently-] saw him again on the 31st at the police-court and then
recognised him-I have no doubt about him now.
