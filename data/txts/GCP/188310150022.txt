ROBERT BOLTON (Detective Constable). On the night of 3rd October I
received instructions from Abberline to go to the post-office, 104, Briok
Lane-I there saw Mr. Robinson, the postmaster-I secreted myself in
& back part of the shop underneath the staircase-about 10.30 I heard
the door unlock over the stair, and shortly after saw the prisoner come
down, strike a match, then go to the candle box, light a candle, go to a
pickle jar and eat some pickles; it is an oilshop-he then went to the
till at the back of the counter and took out something and put it into
his pocket-from there he went to a desk, which he broke open, and
took out something which he put in his pocket-he stayed there some
time, and then returned from the counter-I said " What are you doing
here ?” he said "Dou't lock me up"-I said I should charge him with
breaking and entering the place, and on suspicion of breaking and
entering the place on previous occasions-he said "I have not been here
three times before, governor "-I took him to the station, searched him
there, and found in his trousers pocket 48. 5}d. in bronze, and half a
sovereign in his left-hand vest pocket and 28. 6d. in his right and tickets
relating to a benefit on the Toth inst.--I found the key in the door
through which the prisoner came down.
Cross-examined. The key was the right one I believe-I am not aware
that he has a defect in his speech—he was not the worse for drink on
this night that I know of-I have learnt from his father and mothěr that
he is 16.

ROBERT WILLIAM ROBINSON. I am an oil and colourman and post-

88. worth of tickets produced relating to a benefit concert in & desk,
and the desk was locked-on Monday morning I saw a mark of candle

the candle had been placed there-I had previously communicated with
the police-this is the right key to the lock of the door; I had last seen

for him to take some work through the door-the prisoner lived on the
first floor in the same house-there is a separate entrance to that-Brodie
lives on the same floor as the prisoner.
