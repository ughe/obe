The prisoner came and had a glass of porter in a tumbler: I missed a glass the moment I took the porter to him: he went out - I followed, and charged him with stealing it; he gave it me from his pocket - he was not in the least drunk; several other glasses were found on him, or master would not have given him in charge - he had got about twelve yards from the house.
 RICHARD LANGDON. I saw the glass produced from the prisoner's pocket, and found three or four more in his pockets and his hat.

 1278.  JOHN DAKER was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of May , 100 iron nails, value 10d., and 36 screws, value 2s., the goods of William Bush, his master.
   MARY BUSH. I am the wife of William Bush: the prisoner was an odd man in our employ for five or six months. On the 26th of May, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, I saw him take some nails and screws from the nail-house which adjoins the shop - he put them in brown paper, tied them up, and carried them down to the cellar, and in about two hours and a half, I went into the cellar, and found them concealed in some shavings in a basket; I brought them up, and kept them till the foreman came - we carried them back to where we had taken them from.
   WILLIAM WARREN. I am foreman to William Bush. Mistress gave me information; I went and put the property back to where it had been found - there was above three dozen screws and one hundred of tenpenny nails; the prisoner took them away afterwards.
     GUILTY. Aged 53. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1279.  JANE SWAN was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May , 1 gown, value 1s. 6d.; 1 apron, value 1s.; and 1 cap, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Catherine Swan , the elder; and 1 gown, value 5s.; 1 cloak, value 5s.; 1 shawl, value 2s., and 1 bonnet, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Catherine Swan the younger.
   CATHERINE SWAN. I am a laundress, and live at No. 2, Aldersgate-street. The prisoner is my daughter - she was living at home on the 5th of May, and for about a month, she worked at my business while she was at home- she left without informing me; this property was below stairs where she slept; it was wearing-apparel - mine and my daughters; I saw it there at night when she went to bed - she left between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; I did not see her leave myself - she came home on the Saturday following; she is seventeen years old -I do not allow my things taken without leave; she brought some of them with her again - she had no permission to pawn any thing; she had no clothes of her own at home - she came home distressed for clothes, and her sister lent her these to appear respectable in; she had been living with me a month - I lent her my clothes, but not having sufficient, her sister lent her her's as well; it is very painful to bring a child here - I merely do it for a warning to her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1280  ROBERT GREENAWAY was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of April , 1 bed, value 1l. 18s., and 1 blanket, value 2s., the goods of Robert Lucas Chance.
   JAMES THOMPSON. Mr. Robert Lucas Chance has a warehouse in St. Paul's wharf, and is a glass-merchant. This bed and blanket were at the wharf in a warehouse; I saw it safe on the 22d of April - the prisoner had been employed as watchman to our lighters, and had access to the premises - I missed the property on the 23d of April, and charged him with it; he denied it - I found a duplicate in a pocket-book which was found by one of the clerks lying on a counter, and I found the same pocketbook on the prisoner's person, when he was searched - I had taken the duplicate out before that.
   GEORGE CANNON. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and found the pocket-book on him, containing a duplicate, but not of this property.
   FRANCIS WHITLEY. I am a pawnbroker. On the 29th of April, I took in a bed and blanket - to the best of my belief the prisoner is the man, but I cannot swear to him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1281.  ANN WILSON was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling house of John Frodsham , on the 21st of April , at St. Bennet, Gracechurch-street, and stealing therein 2 watches, value 10l. 10s., his property.
   JOHN FRODSHAM. I am a chronometer, watch, and clock-maker. My house is in the parish of St. Bennet, Gracechurch-street. On the 21st of April, about eleven o'clock in the day, I was standing in the shop and heard a window break - I looked round, and it might be half a minute before I put my eyes on the pane which was broken; the prisoner then had her hand through the pane, helping herself to watches; I hallooed to her - she immediately left the quantity she had collected, and walked away with two in her hand; I opened the door, went out after her, and took her with the two watches in her hand - I am certain the window was safe before she broke it; it is my dwelling-house - the watches are worth ten guineas; here they are - I know them.
 THOMAS KELLY. I only apprehended her.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 18.
 1282.  GEORGE MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 224 lbs., of sugar, value 5l. 18s., the goods of John Edwards.
   JOHN EDWARDS. I am a grocer, and live in Fleet-market. On the 1st of May the prisoner came to my shop with two samples of sugar, and asked what price I should charge him; I showed him four samples - he fixed on two; 2 cwt. was packed up, with 6lbs. of tea, and another parcel of sugar - he asked if I would send somebody with it to Mr. Green's, Saffron-hill; I sent my apprentice with him with the property in a truck - he distinctly stated that the goods were to be paid for on delivery, and I told my apprentice he would have the money on delivery; I never knew the prisoner before.
   EDWARD HOPKINS. I am an apprentice to Mr. Edwards.
