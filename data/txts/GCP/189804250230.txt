at the South-Western laundry soon after 8 a.m.-you know you were
very cruel to that child-your daughter Eliza, aged eighteen, stayed at
home when you were at work, and when you were ill she took your place
for a fortnight, and afterwards I took it-you knocked the poor little

Re-examined. She commenced going to this place to work before
Christmas-she was not at work when the child came, she went three
or four weeks afterwards—she did not return to dinner--I heard the
smacking and the crying in the morning and at night-I know nothing
about the coal-hole except what I was told—there had been no quarrel
between us about getting my daughter's work.
FANNY DARVILLE. I am fifteen years old—the last witness is my mother
-I live with her-I remember the prisoner's child coming to the house in
November-he was a nice little child-he could walk and eat, and get up
and down stairs till he gradually got worse-he got thinner-I noticed
sores on him, but he had none when he first came-I only saw him fed
once by the prisoner-I think that was before Christmas-he had become
thinner-I had seen sores and bruises on him, and a cut on his hip, and
sores on his chest his mother said he had fallen off & chair-my
mother asked him in the prisoner's presence who had hit him, and she
said, “Don't call me mother," and slapped him I have heard the child
being smacked.
Cross-examined. I was before the Coroner three times, and gave
evidence twice-I did not say anything the first time about seeing you
ill-treat the child, because I was not called in-nobody told me what to
say between the two times—I can only tell the same as my mother because
I was there at the same time.
FREDERICK DARVILLE. I am thirteen years old-Caroline Darville is
my mother-I am at Highbury Green Industrial School I went there on
March 7th–I was at home with my mother last Christmas and all the
time till March 7th-I remember the little boy Charlie when he first
came to the prisoner-he could walk about and go up-stairs-I noticed an
alteration in him afterwards-I used to hear him crying when he was
getting up and going to bed I went to the coal cellar once before
Christmas at 10 o'clock, and saw him there naked, and a dog tied up in
the cellar-I let the boy out and he crawled into he kitchen-I went
up-stairs and told my mother-he was there crying when I went down at
8 a.m., but I did not open the door then the prisoner said she did it
because he wetted the bed.
Cross-examined. You came up-stairs to mother and told her you had
locked the boy in the cellar--the dog was barking; he had been kept in
the coal-cellar for a little time.
ANNIE BARNES. I live now at Alfred Cottages, Tooting-up to
February 20th I lived with the prisoner--I noticed the child when it first
came--it appeared to be healthy-it had no sores on it, but it afterwards

returned sometimes at seven and sometimes at 8.30—the dog was kept in
the coal-cellar-I saw the child's mouth black, and he had a black eye-I
