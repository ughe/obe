We missed some—we had perhaps two or three cwt., but you can tell if there is a handfull or two gone—the prisoner had been in the service for two or three years—Mr. Crame stopped the prisoner, and sent for me—he said to him, "William, have you any objection to be searched?"—he said, "No"—he did not say that he took it with intention of giving it in at the counting-house to my knowledge—he did not pull it out of his hat till he was in the counting-house—I believe he mentioned about taking a little for a bonnet—I should think all that he had about him he took out of his own accord—I believe there was nothing taken from him by anybody but himself—I was there at the time. PETER M'QUILLAN( police-constable K  375.) About six o'clock that evening the prisoner was given to me by Mr. Crane, with some oxalic acid. HENRY CRANE. I am partner with my father, Henry Samuel Crane. The prisoner was going out—I was in the yard, and called him back—I believe all had gone out but the prisoner—he said he should like to have a proper person to search him—I said I was there, and the foreman, I thought that was sufficient, and then he submitted tolerably well—he took off his hat, and part of the oxalic acid was in it tied in his handkerchief—I said we would get a proper person to search him and take him, in the mean time the foreman got some more out of his pocket—I was at that time just outside the door—I have not a doubt that it was our property—we discovered that some had been taken by certain marks on a small parcel—I compared what was found on him with the bulk.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It is all alike, is it not-? A.  Not always—I could swear to oxalic acid under some circumstances—this was rather a bad colour—it was not quite finished—when he took it from his hat he said he only took it for an old woman's bonnet—he said I should find nothing about him—he has worked with us several years—he has a wife. NOT GUILTY.

 2553. REBECCA AGGAR was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of Aug ., 1 peck of wheat in the ear, value 9 d  .; the property of George Biggs . GEORGE BIGGS. I live at Barnard Castle farm, Barking—on the 21st of August, I saw the prisoner near some shocks of corn—I rode round to her, and found her with a large quantity of ears of wheat in her apron, and a bag near her with ears of corn in that—they were the same as the ears that were in the field—I asked what she was doing—she said she thought she was doing no harm—the shocks of corn were in the field, and she had no business there.
   Prisoner.  I broke off the ears—I did not know the rules of the field—I never had been gleaning before in my life, and I am afraid they told me wrong—I neither took an ear of corn from the shocks, nor yet touched the shocks, only what was between the shocks. GUILTY. Aged 43.— Confined Two Months .
