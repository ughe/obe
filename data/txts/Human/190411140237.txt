I am a solicitor, practising at 14, Great Marl borough Street—I have known Hooley eight or ten years; in September, 1900, I advanced him £2,000 by way of loan—this is the cheque for 1950; I do not quite recollect how the odd £50 was made up—it is dated September 13th, 1900, pay to J.A. Bradshaw or order £1, 950, and it is endorsed by J.A. Bradshaw and paid into the National Provincial Bank of England, Limited, Piccadilly Branch; that is Mr. Ormerod's account—in September, 1901, that sum of £2,000 was still owing to me—the prospectus and the report of the speeches produced were shown to me by Hooley: I cannot say when—I was pressing him to repay the £2,000 which he owed, and he brought to my notice this Construction Company, which he thought was a very good tiling, and said if I would take 2,000, he thought he could get them for me; he then produced some plans and a list of the concessions of the company—I read in Sir Kenneth Mackenzie's speech the statements about the dividend and bonus, and the balance of £100,000 at the bank—I believed those state ments—the first lot of shares I bought was a lot of £2,000—this is the transfer of 2,000 shares, September 24, 1901—I accepted these shares in satisfaction of the debt—this transfer dated September 11, 1901, from H.J. Lawson to Mr. Bradshaw is a transfer of all those shares identified by the same numbers—on the transfer to Bradshaw the consideration is stated to be 5s.—on my transfer the consideration is £2,000.
