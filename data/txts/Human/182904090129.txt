 Cross-examined. Q. How far is the gutter from the house? A. A very little distance - the court is very narrow; I found the shawl very near the gutter, the bonnet lying in the gutter, and a patten was found in the gutter, but I did not see it - I went to the watch-house; Coombs said nothing in my presence of knowing any body's voice.
   WILLIAM EDWARD COOPER. I am an inspector of the watch. On the morning of the 30th of March, about two o'clock, I was in Playhouse-yard, and met Daley in the custody of Finch and Woods: I took the girl to the watch-house - I afterwards went to No. 8, Lion and Lamb-court, and saw O'Donnell in bed, with all his clothes on except his flannel jacket, and covered over with the bed clothes; I shook him, and told him it was of no use to play tricks, and if he did not come out of bed I would pull him out; he got out of bed, and put his flannel jacket on - he then put one of his boots on up stairs, and the other down; I took him to the watch-house - the minute he entered the watch-house he began to speak; the prosecutrix said,"That is one of them, I can swear to him by his voice" - she had not seen his face then; one of the arms of his jacket was wet, and the other was wet round the cuff; the prosecutrix's things were delivered to me at the watch-house- the bonnet and shawl were delivered to me by Brown, the watch-house keeper; the handkerchief was given to me by the prosecutrix - she had it round her neck when I took her to the watch-house; Brown is not here; I received them in the watch-house - the patrol gave me one patten in Playhouse-yard, and Brown the other; I have had them ever since - I found no money in the handkerchief; there was no knot in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was there the slightest appearance of there having been a knot in it? A. It was as she gave it to me in the morning when she was going away to the office, just before eleven o'clock; Brown was not bound over to come here.
 MR. BARRY to  JAMES McDONALD. Q. Why did you not keep the handkerchief? A. Her neck was bare, and she had nothing on her head; nothing had been said to me about money being in it then; when I picked it up and gave it to her she did not ask me where the 1s. 6d. was -I picked that and a patten up close to the water-run; the things were nearly close together, but not quite.
 COURT. Q. Did you see the other things lying about? A. They were all picked up before I got to them; I saw the shawl lying down, but, it being dark, I saw nothing else.
   MARY CANNON. I live at No. 20, Playhouse-yard, which is five houses from Lion and Lamb-court - I know No. 8 there. On the morning of the 30th of March, after two o'clock, I saw the prosecutrix going into No. 8 with the watchman, and as soon as she entered the lower room I saw the one pair of stairs window open, and saw a bundle chucked out of the window, but what was in it I cannot tell - Daley chucked it out; I knew him before - I have often seen him in the court, and at the door; there was a candle on the window-ledge, so that I could see him; I am certain it was him - he had no coat on; it was a small sized bundle; I cannot say how large, because I only saw it by the light of the candle - I could not see where it fell, because there is a privy between my window and the house, so that I could not see the bottom of the court - I had to look sideways; my house is on the opposite side, four or five yards from No. 8; No. 7 is directly opposite to me - only one side of the court is called Playhouse-yard, and the other Lion and Lamb-court; I have not to turn a corner to go to the house.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did you first give an account of this? A. On Tuesday I went to Cooper's house; I did not call out that a bundle was thrown out, because I did not know the girl had been robbed - I heard a scream; there was a stir in the court - I am a shoe-binder, and single; I have lived there nearly four years - I was in bed, but heard screams, and got up; I am an English woman - a great many Irish live in the court; I saw the prosecutrix in the watehman's hands, and could have called over to them if I had liked; they were not above five yards from me.
 JURY. Q. When you saw the bundle thrown out, was the patrol outside or inside? A. Inside; I remained at the window till he and the prosecutrix came out - I spoke of this on Monday; but did not tell Cooper till Tuesday.
   WILLIAM FINCH. I picked up a shawl, and gave it to McDonald, I think or Woods.
   JAMES McDONALD. Finch did not give me the shawl; it was never in my possession.
   ROBERT WOODS. The shawl, bonnet, and one patten were given to me, but I do not know who by, I am sure, in the bustle.
   ELIZABETH COOMBS. This is my handkerchief; I know it by the colour - I have had it a good bit: I believe my sister hemmed it; when it was given back to me my money was not in it; I never thought about the money then - it had been tied up in one single knot; it could not have fallen out unless it was untied - the bonnet is mine; I trimmed and lined it myself; the shawl I know by a piece out of the corner of the border - I know the pattens; I had had them about a week or a fortnight: all the things are mine, and what I had on when I went into the court.
 O'DONNELL'S Defence. I had nothing to do with her; I never raised my hand to her, nor hurt her; I was not near Blackfriars-bridge that night; I never left Golden-lane - I never went further than the lane: I was never in Fleet-street. The watchman says he took me out of bed in my clothes; I had my boots, jacket, and waistcoat off; I had nothing to do with the woman.
