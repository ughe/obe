   MR. LYON . Q.  Did you make him any promise in words? A.  No—I told him any information he gave me, would not be forgotten—I dis tinctly suggested to him that he should be a witness—( MR. LYON contended that Witham's statement under these circumstances was inadmissible, as a hope of reward was held out id him. THE COURT considered that the evidence could not be excluded, as Without was not then under charge, and no promise or threat was made to him. )
   MR. STRAIGHT . Q.  What did Witham say? A.  He said "I come to you consequent on feeling regret that so many innocent men should be thrown out of employ"—I had discharged some of my men—I had closed one department of the premises at Upper Marsh—he said • I have offered wax by Castle's instructions to three persons, one is Mr. Hale, of Lambeth Road, and Mr. Steane, of Lambeth Walk, and to an oilman in Union Street, whose name I have forgotten, but I did not succeed in selling any"—he also said "It is within my knowledge that this robbery has gone on for two years and a half, and to the extent of about 3 cwt. a week on an average"—I said "Will you give me any further information?—he said "I must have time to consider of it"—I think he said he wanted twenty-four hours, on some hours—he left, promising to see me at the railway station next morning—he did not see me, because I gave instructions for his apprehen sion—I have a traveller in my employ named Soames, who in April showed me some wax he had purchased for me—I examined it and identified it as my manufacture—it was white wax finished, and was worth 2 s . 8 d .
