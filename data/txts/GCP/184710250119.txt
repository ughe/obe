JANNE ANN Bass. I am kitchen-maid to Mr. Bura. Auger came there
several times to see Pavior a month or six weeks ago I saw Elizabeth Auger
in the servants' hall, talking to Pavior-her son was not there then.
Cross-examined. Q. Was it not two months ago? A. No- I was examined
before the Magistrate-I cannot say whether it was two months before I was
before the Magistrate.
FREDERICK POOLB. I am shopman to Mr. Roberts, pawnbroker, Morti-
mer-street, Cavendish-square. I produce a silver tea-pot, a knife and fork,
six salt-stands, thirty-eight knives, and a candelabra-stand--they were pledged
by a man at different times; I have no recollection of him, it is three months
180-I find in a packet which the officer has produced duplicates corresponde
ing with them, with the exception of one, which was left with us--the silver
ted-pot was pawned on the 9th of Oct., for 31.- there is no mark or crest on
il-I have known Frank Auger and his mother five or six years—I had taken
in pledges of him in the name of Clifford - these tickets are all in the name
of Clifford, except the tea-pot, which is in the name of White-I bave six
salt-stands, pledged by Elizabeth on the 6th of Aug. I do not find a corre-
sponding duplicate to them, as both tickets are in my possession when the
last pledge but one was brought, this ticket of the salt-stand was brought with
it, by the same woman to have it redeemed-she gave the name of Ann Clif-
ford, No. 10, Wells-street--that was the name I knew her by.
Court. Q. Did you know anything of her? A. The first pledge I took in of
her concerning plate was some pepper-castors, -for two guineas--they were in
the house a week or ten days, and she brought money and redeemed them
she was asked whose they were--she said they belonged to a gentleman of
extravagant habits who did not wish his name to be known ; that she was in
the babit of pawning at Mr. Baker's, a pawnbroker in the neighbourhood;
and if we would lend her money on them she would bring them to us.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Who did she say employed her to pawn them? 4. A
gentleman of extravagant habits, who was in the habit of pledging a great deal
of plate.
Cross-examined. Q. She brought the things to a place where she was
known? A. Yes-she did not say anything about receiving the plate from
the hands of the gentleman--she did not say, on the first occasion, that he
employed her to pawn them, only that they belonged to him-she did on the
occasion of pledging the candelabra-stand-she told me she should not be
able to go to him that day as he lived some little distance off, and she should
have to take an omnibus.
