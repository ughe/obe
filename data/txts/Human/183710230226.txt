WILLIAM REDSHAW was again indicted, with JAMES PAGE , for feloniously assaulting Frederick Hazell , on the   14th of Octo ber , at St. Mary, Lambeth , and feloniously threatening to accuse him of an infamous crime, with a view and intent thereby to extort and gain from him divers of his monies.—2nd COUNT  , for assaulting and demanding, with menaces, monies from him, with intent to steal the same
   MR. JONES conducted the Prosecution, FREDERICK HAZELL. On the 14th of October, in consequence of a ring at my mistress's bell, about three o'clock in the afternoon, I went to the street door, and found the prisoner Redshaw there—he said, "How do you do? I want two sovereigns, and I must have them, for I am going into the country this evening"—I said I had not got them—he said he must have them, he was going into the country in the evening—I said, "I have not got them"—he said, "You have got a watch, let me have that, and I will pledge it, and send you back the duplicate "—I said, "I have not got one, where am I to get one?"—he said, "Get it as we do, how you can "—about this period of the conversation another person came up, who I had seen with the prisoner on the 12th, in Blackfriars-road—that person said to me, "How pale you look, how you tremble, give him the money, he will not say any thing "—I was trembling at the time—Redshaw then said, "You must have more clothes than you have got on, let me have them, and I will pledge them and send you back the dupli cate "—I told him I could not get any money then, but I would meet him at eight o'clock in the evening, and if I could not bring all, I would bring as much as I could—my mistress was not at home at that time—she is a widow lady—there was no man in the house at the time—Redshaw said, "Go and ask your mistress for the money "—I told him she was not at home—it was then I said I would meet him in the evening—I could not say what time my mistress would be at home that evening—she might be home in about an hour or later, I could not tell—Redshaw asked what time she would be at home, and I said, "In the evening"—it was after that I agreed to meet him in Prince's-square, in the evening, and said I would bring him as much money as I could—I asked him to meet me at the corner of White Hart-row, which is the second turning from where I live, at eight o'clock in the evening—he said he could not wait so long as that, he would meet me at half-past six o'clock, at the corner of Prince's-square—they then both went away—while I was in conversation with them, the prisoner Page walked past, and looked at me, and went and stood two doors off—that was after I had been talking to them about three minutes—I do not think he was near enough to hear our conversation—I had never seen him before—I did not see what became of Page when the other two went away—I left home
