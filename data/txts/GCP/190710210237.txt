Cross-examined by Mr. F. E. Smith. I had had no other expe!
rience on the Stock Exchange. I read the whole of the circular of
October 13 through, and I thought I was actually buying the shares.
I thought I was getting the value of them for my £5, and thought
I was buying five shares at a value of about 98; but I suppose I was
making a mistake. I did not ask at any time what the notual value
was. I thought I understood the circular, but evidently I did not.
It said something about the shares rising and falling. I knew when I
sent the money that my liability was to be limited to the £1 per share
deposit. I think I pencilled notes of my replies to letters, without
actually keeping copies. (Mr. F. E. Smith put several letters to wit-
ness with a view to showing that she was wrong in her dates, and
varied her notes in the actual letters sent.) I was willing that the
loss on the Brighton "A" should be covered by the rise in the Tintos.
When Tyler and Co. asked me to leave the matter to their discretion
I suppose I understood that I was taking the chance of whether the
shares rose or fell. I was willing at that time to leave it to their dis-
cretion, and to go beyond the original risk. I believe so, I know
that if I had not varied the original arrangement there would have
been no more money due.
Mrs. JULIA HENRIETTA PITTET, Frederics Road, Chingford, widow,
said she had received a circular on October 24 from William Tyler
and Co. She had never bought any stocks or shares before, but on
reading the circular, which she thought she understood, she deter
mined to send some money for some Rio Tinto shares, and sent
cheque for £15. Later on witness received a letter (October 29) ad-
vising her to increase her interest as the shares were almost sure to
improve, eto, but she did not send any more money. A further cir
cular was sent her, dated November 7, in which the United States
Steel Corporation was mentioned, and setting forth the great advan
tages of securing an interest therein. Witness read this through
but told them they must settle the account for the Rio Tintos before
she sent more money. She wrote on January 7 this year requesting a
settlement, but got nothing. In consequence of what witness saw
in the newspapers she wrote closing the stook, but got no answer.
About a fortnight later she wrote again, having obtained advice, and
told them they (Tyler and Co.) were only a bucket shop and did no
business. No reply was obtained. I had no other communication
with Tyler and Co. I have had back no part of my £15. I con-
sulted a friend, a Mr. Thomas Clay, with reference to this matter.
