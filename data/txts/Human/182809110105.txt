   BOYD SILVESTER. I am an officer of the Thames Police. I apprehended the prisoner at her mother's house in St. Mary, Rotherhithe, on the 28th of July, I think it was; I told her she was charged with marrying two husbands; she said she had only one; I said nothing to induce her to confess; she said her first husband was Miller, but he was dead, she said that about four years ago he went on board the Sally, Captain Sinclair, and that all hands were lost; she said she could prove that she had got some money for her child; when she got to the office, I asked if she knew anything about Jones - she said she did, but no one could say she had married him, but one woman, and that woman I might find if I could; when she saw the witness before the Magistrate, she said she was married, but Jones went one way and she went the other - this was at the examination; I showed her the copy of the register at the office; I do not know whether what she said was taken down.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did Hogg apply to you about this? A. I think on the 27th; I got a warrant at his desire on the Saturday night, and executed it on the Monday.
   ELIZABETH BLISS. I am the wife of the prisoner's brother - I was present at her marriage to John Hogg, on the 14th of October, 1826, at St. Ann, Limehouse.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know Jones? A. No, I have been married nine months; I did not know the prisoner in 1825 - I have seen Hogg write; I believe this to be his hand-writing, it is similar to his hand-writing which I have seen before - the prisoner had a little furniture; I know that her mother has been obliged to pay the rent of the house in which Hogg lived with her as his wife; she went by the name of Miller, and as a widow up to the time of her marriage with Hogg; she has one child by her first husband: I have known her altogether about three years, but have no recollection of her being said to be married to Jones in 1825 - I never knew a person of the name of Jones pretending to be her husband; I lived in the same house, but never saw a person of the name of Jones there, or claiming her as his wife.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   ANN BLISS. I am the prisoner's mother; she is twentytwo years of age - I remember Jones, and the circumstance of its being stated, that the ceremony of marriage had been performed; she came home that day and told me it was no marriage at all - Jones did not come home; she continued to live with me, in the name of Miller, in my house; she never went by the name of Jones, and was never from under my roof - Jones never lived with her as his wife; Hogg has made me pay the rent of the house he lived in since he took up the prisoner - he put a broker in and seized the goods which I had put in the place when they lived together; I paid six sovereigns for the rent.
 COURT. Q. Where did Jones live before he married your daughter? A. In the neighbourhood; he did not come with her from church, but he came there in the afternoon; I do not know what became of him that night - I left him in the house when I went to bed, about eleven o'clock, and saw no more of him till next day - my daughter was in the room with him when I went to bed; I do not know where he slept - she slept in the house with me, but not in the same bed; Jones was only about my house two nights, but there is no one can prove that they saw her and him go to bed, or in bed - he afterwards went to his work, and has been at his work; he lived in the neighbourhood, but never troubled her nor me either.
   ISABELLA YOUNG. I live in Rotherhithe, and have known the prisoner from a child; I never knew her but in the name of Miller - after she married Miller till she married Hogg, she went by the name of Miller; I called her so myself, and she answered to it; she is a very respectable woman.
   MARY ANN KENT. I have known the prisoner many years by the name of Miller till she married Hogg.
 - WARDLE. I have known her as a neighbour sixteen years; she bore a very respectable character, and went by the name of Miller till she married Hogg - I understood that she had been to church, but it was an illegal marriage, and she lived with her mother.
 COURT to ANN BLISS. Q. Was anything said about the marriage when your daughter and Jones were together? A. Yes, he told me the same as she did; and said she had no more demand on him than I had.
     GUILTY. Aged 22.
