242. WILLIAM CLAYTON, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 28.; 1
ap, 6d. ; 1 pair of trowsers, 28. 6d. : 1 frock, 1 handkerchief 2 stockings,
value 28. ; and 3 sovereigns, the property of Edward How, in a vessel on
the Thames.
EDWARD How. I am master of the Arab—it is lying at Mill-hole, on the
Thames- I left the articles stated and my money safe in my bed-case in my
state-room, on 12th Nov.-I missed them on Sunday morning the 14th, be-
tween five and six o'clock-the prisoner was my apprentice-he deserted the
ship, and could not be found I went after him, and found him at Islington
- be was brought out of the theatre to me by his uncle-I asked him how he
could do what he had done-he said he knew nothing about the money-I
gave him in charge to the officer--he was dressed differently to what he was
before-he had my boots on his feet-my cap was found in a bag which was
In his aunt's possession-he was a good boy:
WILLIAM SIMMONDS (Thames police-constable, 62.) I went to the pri-
soner's lodging with the captain- I found the prisoner at the City of London
EDWARD How re-examined. Q. How did you know it was the prisoner's
lodging? 4. From the Marine Society's office-I afterwards spoke to the
prisoner about the things found in the bundle-he said nothing about them,
but on the Sunday morning he told me he took three sovereigns out of my box.
CAROLINE ELTHAM. I am the prisoner's aunt. He brought the bundle to
my house at eight o'clock on the Sunday morning-I got an order, and took
him to the theatre-he told me the captain gave him the boots - there was a
crack at the side-I got them mended.
GUILTY of stealing the boots. Aged 17.-Judgment Respited.
