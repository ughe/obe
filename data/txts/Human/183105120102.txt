   BENJAMIN KELSEY. I am bar-man at the Marquis of Anglesea public-house. I supplied the prosecutor with a glass of ale - when he got to the door I heard a scuffle; I went to the door, and saw him engaged in a scuffle with the prisoner - they both fell on the pavement; the prisoner extricated himself, and ran up a court that leads into Russel-street - I saw a man pursuing him; the witness appeared perfectly sober.
 Cross-examined. Q.Are you quite sure he was not intoxicated? A.If he had been I must have observed it - he said he had been robbed directly the prisoner went away; I did not follow the prisoner, as I had my business to attend to, and others were following - I did not observe any person about at the beginning of the scuffle, except some hackney-coachmen; I did not observe whethere he had a watch - I did not see any one in his company; the court he ran up was a thoroughfare - I saw him brought back; I am certain he is the man who scuffled with the prosecutor.
 COURT. Q. Did you see him come back in company with the Policeman? A. I did.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been in the cook's shop - I was just going by that house, when the prosecutor caught hold of me and said I had robbed him; he pulled me about, and I pulled him about - we both fell down together; I certainly did run away, but I had no knowledge of the watch - I am a hackney-coachman.
     GUILTY. Aged 28 - Transported for Life.
 1181.  JOHN BAKER was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , 2 planes, value 2s.; 1 saw, value 1s.; 1 pair of compasses, value 3d., and 1 chisel, value 3d., the goods of Joseph Baxter.
 JOSEPH WILKINS. I live in Bond-court. On the morning of the 9th of April I saw the prisoner in Pye-street, at a quarter before one o'clock, with this plane under his coat - I then searched his pocket, and found this other plane, this saw, compasses, chisel, and line bob; I said I thought it was not right - he said the tools were his own, and I might take them, but he would not go; I said he must, and secured him.
 JOSEPH BAXTER. I am a  carpenter, and live in Queen-street, Edgware-road. I was at work in Albion-street on the 8th of April, and left my tools in the building, at seven o'clock - I went the next morning, and missed these tools; they are mine, and here is the stamp I stamp them with.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Three Months.
 1182.  ELIZABETH BOLDWITH was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of May , 1 tin pail, value 2s., the goods of Richard Henry King.
   ROBERT HUGHES. I am footman to Captain Richard Henry King, who lives in Gloucester-place. I was in his pantry on the 10th of May, at a quarter before two o'clock- I saw the prisoner in the area; I looked out at the window, and saw her take the pail, and proceed with it up the steps - I gave an alarm to the cook, who followed her, and brought her back with it.
   BENJAMIN ETCHELLS. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner and the pail.
 Prisoner. I did it through distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Confined One Month.
 1183.  CHARLES CHRISTMAS was indicted for that he, on the 31st of March , feloniously did utter a forged order, for the delivery of goods, well knowing the same to be forged, which said order is as follows:-Mr. Twilly, - Give Christmas a pair of shoes.
 By order of T. TUCKER.
 with intent to defraud the  overseers of the poor, for the time being, of the parishes of St. Giles in the Fields, and St. George, Bloomsbury; against the Statute, &c.
 THREE OTHER COUNTS, varying the manner of stating the charge.
 THOMAS TUCKER. I am assistant-overseer of the poor of St. Giles in the Fields, and St. George, Bloomsbury - they are united, for the purpose of maintaining the poor. I have a paper, which I got from Mr. Twilly, the governor of the workhouse; here is the signature of T. Tucker to it, but it is not my writing. nor written with my authority or knowledge; I am in the habit of giving written orders to paupers, to get clothing, daily and hourly, I may say- there is no other person named Tucker who gives such orders; the orders I give are on pieces of paper similar to this, and the wording of it is similar - they are as nearly alike as possible; I am not very particular in wording them - if it had been my signature Mr. Twilly would have been authorized to deliver the articles stated; he would have been bound to comply with it - it is the usual way in which we have been in the habit of giving articles to the out-door poor: Mr. Twilly has apparel by him to comply with such orders; it is entrusted to him by order of the directors of the poor, who are appointed by the joint vestry, under the local Act, which I now hold in my hand- there are twelve from each parish; the Act is the 11th of George the 4th.
 MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. By what authority do you act? A. I am one of the assistant-overseers - I have my appointment with me; we act as overseer in every point of view, as regards the relief of the poor - I do not in general write "By order of T. Tucker;" I generally write, - Give such an article to such a person, T. Tucker.
 COURT. Q. Is your election in vestry? A. Yes. I might have used the words "By order" in one of my papers, but not in general - I should consider that this means that T. Tucker wrote it.
 MICHAEL DRISCOLL. I am errand-boy to the master of  St. Giles' workhouse. I saw the prisoner a day or two before Good-Friday - he came to me in the lodge, and gave me a paper, which I looked at, and put on the file; I did not mark it, but I can tell this paper by the name at the bottom - I took no copy of it.
     NOT GUILTY.
