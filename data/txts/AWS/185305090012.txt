Q. In reference to Sherman and Chapman, was not your name mixed up
with them, and was it not alleged that you had been a party to bribing them
to the amount of 211. A. No; Sherman came to me after the election, and
said something about some money that somebody had promised him, and I
told him to leave the house-I know Pratt-I was not charged with inducing
him to vote by getting him a license from the Vice-Chancellor-he got a
license a good while before-my belief is, it was a considerable time before-
I told him I would apply to the Vice-Chancellor for a license-] believe he
did vote-he was a publican- know Pratt, and he is in Court here-I did
not promise him a license, certainly not; he had two landlords- did not
canvass them.
Q. Did you not tell Pratt that if he got a license the house would be worth
1501. more to him A. I think very likely the value of the house would be
enhanced, but I do not know whether I did say so-I said that I would apply
to the Vice-Chancellor, but on consideration I would not-he has got the
license, but not by my interference-I am not aware that Pratt's was one of
the houses that were opened for the election- ordered bim to open his house
for three or four persons whom I employ, not about political purposes, but
perfectly legal parposes--the amount of it was 108. or 128.- received a
notice to produce a letter written by Mr. Metealfe; but I looked
through several hundred letters, and could not find it-I had some conversa-
tion with Mr. Metealfe-I do not recollect that I told him I should hold him
responsible- wrote to him-I thought he had done wrong in employing
Scott at all in the chambers without consulting me-if I had introduced a
clerk that had robbed Mr. Metcalfe I should have held myself responsible for
it-I did not see Scott in the Committee of the House of Commons, or up in
the gallery- swear I did not-I know Thomas Butcher and William Baker
-they have never been my guests, and they have not applied to me for
money, certainly not I had but a few hours to seek for the letter that Mr.
Metealfe sent me, and I could not find it.
Mr. SLEIGH. Q. Was that letter in answer to a letter that you wrote to
him A. It was-the prisoner has never dined or breakfasted with me, or
been received as a guest-there has never existed any intimacy but that
between a master and a servant, certainly not.
Mr. PARRY. Q. Did you send any message to Seott by his son A.
No; I think I may have told his son and his wife, probably, that I should
send for a policeman if he did not come.
