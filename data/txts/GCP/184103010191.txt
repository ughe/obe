Cross-examined. Q. When did this last conversation take place ? A.
Partly on the first day and partly on the second day, when I met her
the first day I apprehended her, and took her before the Magistrate, and
she was let go, on making an appointment to meet me the next day-
the Magistrate did not enter into it the first time, neither of the husbands
came forward-he did not say, on that occasion, that the first marriage
was not sufficiently proved-he said so on one occasion-- there were
four or five examinations.
COURT. Q. Was it after you had told the Magistrate what you have
told, us that he said it was not proved ? A. No, at the first examination
he did not enter into it-I did not tell the Magistrate on that occasion
MR, PRENDERGAST. Q. Was there any evidence at that time of the first
marriage? A. Nothing more than what she stated to me the first day-
she gave me the certificate, and the next day I asked her who this Ri-
chard and Frances Bell were-I cannot say whether I asked her that day
or the next-I think it was the next day-the certificate was produced
when I first apprehended her, but it was not then that I asked her the
question, to the best of my belief.
Q. Was the certificate in your hand on the second occasion ? A. Yes,
I opened it, and produced it to her, and I said, “Who is Richard Bell and
Frances Bell," and she said, “The man and woman belonging to the
church"- this passed in the King's Head public-house, where she pro-
mised to meet me-Mr. Clothier, the father, gave me instructions to appre
hend her-he did not tell me they could not prove the marriage-the
prisoner told me that Clothier knew she was a married woman-I said,
"Did Mr. Clothier know you was married ?”-she said, “Yes; because I
represented myself as a widow."
Q. How came you to mention what you did to the Magistrate ? A.
After he entered into the case, he said there was not evidence, and then
the solicitor for the prosecution asked me whether I had heard her say any
thing--that was at one of the examinations-I think it might be the
second, he did not enter into it the previous night there was no solicitor
the first evening the first examination was the second time of going
before the Magistrate--we went three times after that examination-I
gave this evidence about the conversation more than once-after the first
examination the Magistrate said there was not evidence enough I had
then given this evidence to the Magistrate.
Q. Did you not tell me it was after the first examination that you said
this? 4. Yes I had been examined on the first examination the soli-
citor had spoken to me both before and after the examination- I had not
given my evidence to the Magistrate at that time, I had not said a word-
I went forward, and gave my evidence about apprehending her-I cannot
take my oath to all the words that passed.
Q. Did you say a word about the conversation till after you were spoken

and she said that was what she got from Mr. Clothier- I did not state that
till after the solicitor had the conversation with me.
