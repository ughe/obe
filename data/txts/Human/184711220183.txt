C." and the diamond—that is the mode by which goods intended to be shipped are marked—when I took the invoice on the 11th, Seager said, "Very well," and told me to get the others as quickly as possible—I might have seen him again before the 28th, but only in the outer office, to ask him if Gradiner and Holliday were within, and to deliver the account of the second parcel—I delivered the invoice and general account—the invoice of the second parcel was made out in the same way as for shipping—I do not remember what Seager said about the second invoice—until the 28th, I thought the goods had been shipped for the Cape of Good Hope—I went, on the 28th, for a general settle ment of the account, and saw Seager—he said Gardiner and Holiday had gone to the docks respecting some goods that there was a dispute about, and had left word that I was to draw a bill on them at three months, as arranged—I asked whether the goods there was a dispute about were mine, or whether my cordage was shipped—he said my cordage was shipped.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  I suppose each time you went you found Seager occupying the outer office? A.
