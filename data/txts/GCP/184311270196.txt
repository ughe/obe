CHARLES CROCKER. I am Sir Richard Jodrell's footman. On the 28th of
Oct. I corded some of the luggage and carried it down stairs-in particular I
corded a large box with brass nails and oval top-I locked it I did not see
what it contained it was corded across, and a card was on it-I gave the key
to Hearndon-- I came up by the same train as the luggage, and some of the
servants, Hearndon, Jeffrey, and Applegate- I saw the box after the arrival of
the train at London-bridge--the number of boxes were all right then-this
- box was a heavy one-I saw it placed on a van, belonging to Butler, to be
conveyed to Portland-place-I lifted it out of the luggage-van of the railway
and saw it placed on the right-hand corner of the van behind, close to the tail
board-there was a large hamper and wine, and other things, on the corre-
sponding side-Butler's little child was sitting on the hamper-we arrived at
London-bridge about five o'clock, and got every thing loaded on the van about
half-past five-on leaving London-bridge the van turned up Union-street
-Applegate went with it-I arrived at Portland-place about half past six-
before the luggage-I went in a cab with the two female servants--the lug.
gage arrived some time after me, and was emptied out of the van into the hall
-1 assisted in unloading it-when I took it up stairs I found the box in
question was not there. I have not seen it since.

Cross-examined by Mx. PRENDERGAST. Q. You did not pay any more at-
tention to this trunk than the others? A. I had more to do with it than the
others-I am certain there were brass nails in it-there were five trunks of
this description, but only half the size of this-it went by a van belonging to
Stevens, from the house at Brighton to the railway-Applegate went with the
van to the station-I did not see it moved from the van on to the railway-
there is a place to put the luggage in, and that is locked-it was full a quarter
after two before the train left, and it ought to have left a quarter before two-
I looked at the clock at the station at Brighton when we got there it was
half-past one-1 did not notice when we left.
Cross-eramined by MR. CHARNOCK. Q. Why do you recollect that you
lifted this trunk? A. One of the policemen came and told me to leave it
for one of the porters--the other trunks were black leather and nails-I be
lieve there has been a reward of 101. offered for the discovery of this box-
I do not know whether it is to be paid on the conviction-we applied at the
railway terminus the same night-the other footman and the butler went to
know if a box was left there-) said I was certain I placed it on the van,
but Lady Jodrell wished them to go.
