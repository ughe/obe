1549. WILLIAM WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 6 pieces of printed cotton, containing 77 yards, value 40s., the goods of Henry Hammer.
   ROBERT FISHER. I am apprentice to Henry Hammer, who lives in Ratcliff-highway, and is a linen-draper. On the 14th of August, about half-past seven o'clock, I was putting some things outside the door; the prisoner and two others came by - one of them put his hand to a pile of prints, and suddenly withdrew it; they went on about seven doors, and kept coming backwards and forwards; I went and stood behind a pile of goods; they came again, and one man took some prints; I went out, and the first man had part of them on his shoulder; some were on the pavement, and the prisoner was picking them up; the first man dropped the prints; the third man ran off - I secured the prisoner, who was stooping.
 Prisoner. The other two men were tipsy, and I pushed the other against the cottons; some of them fell - I stooped to pick them up, and was there near a quarter of an hour, picking them up. Witness. He was not there a minute.
   JOHN SHIELDS. I took the prisoner into custody - he said he was merely picking them up, to give to the young man.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.
 1550.  THOMAS WORCESTER was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 20 yards of printed cotton, value 14s., the goods of John Scott.
 JOHN SCOTT. I am a  linen-draper, and live in High-street, Shadwell. I received information on the 26th of June, between six and eight o'clock in the evening; I sent my young man out, and he brought back the prisoner, with this print in his apron.
   WILLIAM PITT. I followed the prisoner, and found a piece of print on him, which had hung on a rail outside the shop. I told him he had taken a piece of print - he said he had not; I asked what was in his apron - he said Nothing - I found the print in it.
   EDWARD STEARN. On the 26th of June, between seven and eight o'clock, I saw three men skulking about my window, at No. 226, High-street; I went out and saw the prisoner go to Mr. Scott's, and take the print; I could not leave my door, but I went to my next neighbours, and told them of it; the prisoner was brought back in about five minutes.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw this piece of print on the pavement beyond Mr. Scott's door.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1551.  THOMAS WILMORE was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of June , 1 coat, value 2l., and 1 pair of trousers, value 1l., the goods of John Gwynn.
 JOHN GWYNN. I live in Vernon-place, Old-street. On the 21st of June I had been knocked down, and was much injured - the prisoner came to my house, on the 22d, and said he heard I had had an accident; he said "I have a leech at home, and if you will come I will give it you;" I said "What can I do with my clothes;" he said "Take them with you," and he put them into his handkerchief; he employed his son to put on the leech, while he dried my clothes and brush-them, and, while my head was down, he took my clothes out and pawned them - I did not miss them for two or three days.
 Prisoner. Q. What was the cause of my coming to your house on that morning. A. I do not know; on the day before I took a watch away, belonging to Mr. Edwards, which you told me to pawn, I did not know but what it was yours, but it proved to be another person's.
 Prisoner. On the 20th of June, at eleven o'clock, this man was sitting in company with me, at my door, taking some porter - two young men came by, and one asked me to let him drink - he would leave his watch with me, and I said he should have it when he called for it - the prosecutor called for it the next morning, with a young man, named Berridge, and said "Let me look at that watch," which I said they might - he then ran away with it.
 Witness. No - it was not the next morning - but he gave it me to pawn; I was going to Bow, and in going along I said "This cannot be the watch the young man left," but I took it to pawn - I saw Berridge that day.
 COURT. Q. Did he complain of your taking the watch? A. No; I did not desire him to pawn my things.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. Have not you got the duplicate of these clothes? A. No, I gave it to the officer - they were pawned in the name of Wilmore - I never saw anything of the money - he did not give me the duplicate - I found it in his house - it was given me by his landlord, who said "If you will keep a secret I will give you the ticket."
   JOHN BOARDS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a coat and trousers, pawned at my house on the 22d of June, by the prisoner; I knew him very well - he said he pawned them for a person of the name of Gwinn.
   JOHN COOPER. I heard the prosecutor tell the prisoner to pawn these things for 2l.
     NOT GUILTY.
