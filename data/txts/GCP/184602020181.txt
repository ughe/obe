STEPHEN THORNTON (police-sergeant / 26.) I was employed to watch
the movements of the female prisoner-on the evening of the 5th of Jan.,
between six and seven o'clock, I saw her leave the premises of the British
Plate-glass Company-she appeared to have something under her cloak-she
went to Seacoal-lane, and was met by Braham-they went together into the
Jobby of a pawnbroker's shop-I took them both-I then proceeded with
Mr. Wilson to the house in Bethnal-green-I there saw Robert Rand I
asked if he was the husband of Charlotte Rand, and told him his wife was in
custody for robbing Mr. Wilson, and I wished to search his house-he said he
had no objection, and in a box below I found seventy-three Queen's beads
asked how they came by them, and the mother replied her son had found
them-I found these other articles up stairs--the man did not give any account
of them-I asked, when I found the butter and other things, where he got
them from, and he said he should reserve his defence.
