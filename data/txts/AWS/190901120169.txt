When I received the statements to take with me they were already
receipted, without the signature. The stamps were obliterated and
cancelled. I nearly always signed in pencil. Until November when
I got some information I did not know the stamps had been through
the post before. I have seen Mr. Southee write. I have had letterb
from him. The signatures on the stamps in the bundle ealled
Kelson's I take to be Mr. Southee's. Until November 2.I never
ruled stamps at all. On September 20 I fell ill and went to hospital.
I was then living at Trevor Square, Brompton Mr. Southee came
to see me once. He expressed his sympathy. After leaving the
hospital I went to his house at Westeliff. For the first week I paid
nothing. After that I was to pay E1 a week. The last time I stayed
there was the day before the last hearing but one at the Mansion
House. On November 2 Southee, two friends of his, and myself
went out to tea. After that Mr. Southee and myself went upsteirs
to the office. As we were entering the office I told him I would be
back again in few minutes. I was away ten minutes or a quarter
of an hour. When I came back Southee was not there. I noticed
the papers on the table were upside down and that kind of thing,
not as I left them. When he came in again he told me he had
received a message by telephone from Crow, of Kilburn, that the
Inland Revenue had called there and collected his receipts, taken
them away. Southee was upset, worried. I believe I said to him
I did not see what it mattered because I knew we had always put
stamps on receipts over E2. He said that was not the point, the
stamps were bad stamps. I am positive he said that. I told him
I did not see the point. I believe he told me then he would tell
me going down in the train. He said we had no receipts for
to-morrow's journey, and gave me some stamps and asked me to
prepare shem in the ordinary way. They were good stamp.
believe he took them from the drawer in the safe. He said, Do
you know how to rule them off1" I said Ye9. I fixed the stamps on
the blank statements and ruled them off. There were about forty.
On the way to the station I asked him what the joke was; I thought
he was playing some joke. He said it was no joke, beeause one of
the boys had been sticking on stamps. I believe he told me they
had been through the post or they were otherwise used stamps, 1
knew there was a Mr. Walker coming into the business. Southee
said he supposed Walker would erv off if he heard of the trouble.
After dinner that night we talked it over. We told Mrs. Southee.
I do not think the question whether it would be fine or imprisonment
was gone into that day. The agreement with Mr. Walker Was to.
be signed the following morning He told Mrs. Southee that Crow
had rung up on the phone, or one of his customers, and that the
Inland Revenue had taken the stamps. He said it was impossible
to know when the Inland Revenue were going to all, but that he
would rather have Arthur and Miss Shrimpton out of the way for
a few days, and he gave me instructions when I got to the office
to send Arthur to Whitechapel and to tell Miss Shrimpton 88 she
had got such a bad cold to go home for a few days. I did so.
