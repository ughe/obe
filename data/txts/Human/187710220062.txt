   Cross-examined.  I should think I had not been on the race-course more than a quarter of an hour when I met Grant—I could not say whether he was alone or not at first—there were a number of people standing about looking on—he was not in company with any one; he was alone—I told him I did not know anything about betting—I was fond of seeing races—I think I have been to the races twice during the last twelve years—I did not hear Cannon come up and advise Grant to back a horse called "Wild Suffolk Lad"—I was cross-examined at the Mansion House—I swear I never heard the name of "Suffolk Lad"—Grant gave Cannon some money in my presence, shortly after Cannon came up to him—he went away with that before anything was said about my betting—Grant called out to Cannon and said "You had better do something for my friend here"—I did not give him 2 l . on that race to back some horse—I swear I did not bet on any races at all—I gave money twice—there were only three races run all the time I was there—we went before the last race—I cannot tell you whether Cat's-eye ran in that race—I gave 2 l . or 3 l . on the first race—Cannon was introduced to me as "Mr. Cannon" when he came up first of all to speak to Grant—the first introdution about the betting was Grant saying to Cannon "You had better put something on for my friend"—I did not give him money to back any horses whatever—I gave him money because he came to me representing that the money had been paid away by him on my account, through a misunderstanding between him and Grant—I thought, although I had not commissioned him to do anything, it would be very dishonourable for me to let the man lose the money on my account—I said "I have not come  down to the course for the purpose of betting"—Cannon handed a ticket to me—he took it back—he did not go and make another bet on the same race, or on a place  bet he did not give me a second ticket on the first race and say he had laid 3
