Detective Sergeant JOHN GILLAN, V Division. Acting under in-
structions received from Detective-Inspector Scott, for a month prior
to the arrest of the prisoner I, in conjunction with other officers,
bave been keeping observation in the vicinity of the "White Lion
and the "Prince of Wales" public houses, Garratt Lane, Wande
worth, and 33, Foss Road, Tooting. We were disguised a street
loafers. During that time I saw prisoner on five or six occasions in
company with three other men, John Smith, Ernest Lee, and George
Lee. On December 14, in company with Detective Lowroy, I saw
Smith and the two Lees enter 33, Foss Rond, and leave about a
quarter of an hour later, at 4.30 p.m. All three passed into Garratt
Lade till they came to the "Fountain" public house. There they
stopped, and I saw John Smith tako something from his jaoket pooket.
It was a small packet in brown paper. He did it, and handed
something to each of the Lees. Then they separated, and Ernest Lee,
entered 866, Garratt Lane, which is a confectioner's shop. I saw
him go to the counter and purchase some sweets, receive change, and
come out and join the other two, who were outside. I entered the
shop, and in consequence of what I said to the manageress she handed
me florin. I examined it and found it was counterfeit. I marked
it with my initials, J.G., and subsequently took possession of it. At
the time I handed it back and left the shop and followed the three
men down the road. I saw George Lee enter 898, Garratt Lane, a
small draper's shop kept by Mrs. Cottam. He remained there a fow
minutes and then came out and joined the other two men at the
corner of Devour Road, about 50 yards off. All the three men passed
down to Tooting Broadway, where they stopped. I saw each of the
Lees hand & gmall parcel to Smith. Then they all three walked down

took a packet from his pocket, undid it, and handed something to
one of the Lees. They separated, and a few minutes later George
Lee entered 62, Mitcham Road, a baker's and confectioner's, kept by
Mr. Martin. I saw him purchase two buns and a doughnut. He
came out and joined his companions. They all three went into the
"Free" beerhouse and ate the buns and doughnut and had some
refreshment. Then they went down Mitcham Road for about 100
yards, when they again stopped. Smith again took a brown paper
packet from his pocket and banded something to each of the Lees.
