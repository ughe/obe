70. JAMES RAMSEY and WILLIAM WOOD, stealing 1 peck of
apples and 1 peck of potatoes, value 18. 6d., the goods of William Mason,
their master
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution,
JOSEPH Cox. I am groom to Mr. William Mason, of Chingford. He was
absent on 21st Oct. -Ramsey was his gardener, and Wood was a labouring
man under him-on that day I saw Ramsey leave Mr. Mason's premises with
a basket full of something Wood left the premises with him, and he had a
bag with something in it-I gave information to a policeman, and he
brought them both back in custody-Ramsey had the basket, and Wood
the bag - there were cabbages and apples in the basket, and potatoes in the
bag--there were apples and potatoes on my master's premises
Cross-examined by Mr. HORRY.Q. How long have you been with Mr.
Mason 4. Two years I do not know whether Ramsey came to my
master from Mr. Dimsdale, the banker--the garden that Ramsey has to
attend to is about an acre-there are all sorts of things growing therehe
has to do what he thinks fit-Mr. Mason is not here-- whether he gave
him authority to take these I do not know-it was my doing to give them
into custody.
At
THOMAS HOLDEN (policeman, N 346). I received information from the
last witness, and apprehended the prisoners at 7 o'clock in the morning -
saw them come off Mr. Mason's premises-Ratnsey was carrying a basket
nearly full of apples, and Wood had a bag with about a peek of potatoes
in it-I stopped them, and asked what they had-Ramsey said, I have
got some vegetables that my mistress gave me "-Wood said Ramsey gave
him the potatoes in the bag—I took them to Mr. Mason's house, and found
they had no right to take them there were two cabbages at the top of the
basket.
WILLIAN MASON, Jun. I am twelve years old. My father was not at
home on 21st Oct.--the two prisoners were in his employ as gardener and
under gardener.
