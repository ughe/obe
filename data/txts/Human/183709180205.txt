Lambeth-walk. On Wednesday, the 6th of last month, the prisoner came to the bar for a pint of porter, about half-past seven o'clock at night—he gave me a shilling—I gave him 10 1/2  d . change—I put the shilling under the foot of a glass, but I put it into the till when I took it of the prisoner, and then took it out—there was no other shilling there, only one sixpence—I looked at it directly the prisoner left, and saw it was a bad one—I marked it—the next morning, (Thursday,) the prisoner came again, between eight and nine o'clock, for a pint of porter—he gave me a shilling—I said it was bad—he said it was not—I told him he had been there the night before—he said he had not—I went round to him—Mr. Carins, my master, was there—I am sure the prisoner is the person who came the night before—I gave the second shilling to my master, then went and fetched the other than I had marked and put under the glass—I gave that to Henry Brackstone. GEORGE CARINS. I keep the Feathers. I heard the dispute between the barmaid and the prisoner—I came out from where I was at breakfast, and saw the prisoner going out of the door—the bad shilling laid on the counter—the barmaid took it up and gave it to me—I went for a police-officer—I did nor receive more than one shilling from her, and I gave it to the policeman, with another which I got from Brackstone—the prisoner was searched, and nothing found on him. HENRY BRACKSTONE. I am barman to Mr. Carins. I got the shilling from the barmaid, and gave it to Mr. Carins. WILLIAM BOLD( police-constable L  24.) I received these two shillings from Mr. Carins—I took the prisoner, and asked if he had any home—he said, "None whatever"—I asked him where he worked—he said he was out of work; that he had been working at the other side of Vauxhall-bridge, at an iron foundry—I found nothing on him. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. These are both counterfeit, and from the same mould.
   Prisoner's Defence . I went there in the evening, and had a pint of beer, but I am certain it was not a bad shilling—the next morning I went and offered a shilling, she said that I had been the night before, and given a bad shilling—she ran round and stopped me—on the Wednesday night I was coming over Westminster-bridge, and a woman coming over asked me to carry a bundle, and she gave me the shilling, and the next one was one that was given to me. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined One Year; Three Months Solitary.

   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution . ELIZABETH ELLIOTT. I am housekeeper to Mr. Cubitt, at Clapham, he is a customer to Mr. Terry, the butcher. I know the prisoner—Mr. Cubitt had his bills weekly—the prisoner used to bring them, and I paid him a week after—when the bills were paid, they were delivered to him to get receipted by his master, and bring back again—the prisoner put "paid" himself to this bill of 1 l . 10 s . 5 d .—the date of the last item is the 19th of August—I paid it the prisoner on the 22nd—on the 12th of September I paid the prisoner two bills of 1 l . 16 s . 5 d . and 9 s . 3 d ., making in the whole 2 l . 5 s . 8 d .—I gave him the bills to get receipted—I asked him for them—he said it was all right, and he would bring the 4 d . change—the next morning I had some suspicion, and inquired of Mr. Terry the next day.
   Prisoner . I paid the 2 l . 6 s . to my master.
