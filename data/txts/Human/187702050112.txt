I live at the Artichoke stables, and am foreman to Mr. Tilling—I heard the collision, and when I got there the defendant had just got up and was going behind his own van towards the milk van, and I heard them ask for each other's addresses—I should say that he was sober.
   Cross-examined.  I did not speak to him—I was within a yard of him—he seemed sober enough—I had never seen him before.
   Re-examined.  I saw him walk, he did not walk like a drunken man. HENRY BARNES. I am a livery stable-keeper—I went up to the defendant after the accident and asked him if I could take care of his horse and van—I did not see him more than five minutes—I saw him get up on the wheel of his cart to get his hat out, and a gentleman asked him to come and look after his wife—he spoke well to me and asked me to take care of his horse and van and to tie the springs up, which were broken.
   Cross-examined.  I have said that he might have had a glass—he spoke to me very well.
   Re-examined.  He spoke very thick and he said that he was very much shook.
