977. RICHARD WALSH (21), Burglariously breaking and entering
the dwelling-house of John James King, and stealing therein 1 watch, value
7., bis property. Second count, for receiving the same.
MR. CUOPER conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES King. I am assistant to my father, John James King a jeweller,
of 21, Kingsland-place, Kingsland-road-I reside there on 1st January I
fastened up the place about 12 o'clock - at that time all was safe; the doors
were seoured in the usual way, and the windows the same about 4 o'clock in
the morning I was called up by the police, and on coming down found the place
in grant confusion the thieves had entered by the back window; that
window had been closed and fastened the night before we lost property on
that consion to the amount of 3,4001.- amongst the property lost was this
watch (produced) I know it-we gave 77 for it to Mr. McKewan.
THOMAS MOKswan. I am a watch and clock manufacturer at 140 and
141, Cheapside-I sold this watch to Mr. King in 1854, for 71., amongst
others on the night of 18th January last, in conseqnence of a communica-
tion from Mr. King, two policemen in private clothes orme to me, and by
their wish I went to the bar of a public-house in Red Lion-street, Holborn
- party whom the police introduced to me brought the prisoner to me
therehe produced to me a silver gilt watch he said it was gold, and
wanted 71. 108. for it-he then produced another, which I was not quite sure
was Mr. King's property, but it.subsequently turned out to be so I took
the number of it - a third one was then produced, which I also took the
number of; but not being sure of either of those I asked to see another,
and this watch was then produced-he asked 41 or 52 for it at first, but ulti-
mately took 50s.--I paid him for it, and then left the public-bouse, and met
the police-sergeant by appointment--I am quite sure the prisoner is the
man I saw-I could swear to him among a thousand.
