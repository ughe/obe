 London Cases, First Jury, Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1602.  CORNELIUS M'COY and JAMES CONDON were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of September , a handkerchief, value 2 s., the goods of William Whiffin , from his person. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1603.  SARAH SANDERS was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , a sheet, value 18 d.; two pillows, value 5 s.; two pillow-cases, value 6 d.; a candlestick, value 2 s., and a flat iron, value 1 s., the goods of Lewis Lloyd , in a lodging room.
   ELIZABETH LLOYD. I am the wife of Lewis Lloyd; he lives in Christopher's-court, St. Martin's-le-grand. The prisoner took a room on the second floor in my house, a month ago last Friday; the articles stated were let to her; she left without notice, but was not indebted to me - her husband paid the rent. I let her the room for her husband, but he did not come till the evening - he came home every night. I received information, went into the room, and missed the articles.
   WILLIAM STAPLES. I am a pawnbroker. I have two pillows pawned, I believe by the prisoner, on the 9th of September, in the name of Saunders. I cannot swear to her person.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1604.  ELIZABETH GAINSFORD, MARY THOMPSON, HANNAH BROOKS, and MARY ANN PORTER, were indicted for that they, on the 15th of September , in and upon Thomas Henry Ingham , feloniously did make an assault, and put him in bodily fear and danger of his life, and from his person and against his will, a watch, value 2 l.; a seal, value 1 d.; a key, value 1 d.; a ribbon, value 1 d., and two shillings, and a penny, from his person, feloniously did steal.
 THOMAS HENRY INGHAM. I am a  scale-board cutter, and live in Ewer-street, Union-street, Borough. On the 15th of September, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, I was in Butler's-alley, Little Moorfields. I made a bit of a stop to ease myself, and three women went before me - they were Gainsford, Porter, and one who is not taken. I went on, and the one who is not here said "Come old fellow, are you not going to have sixpennyworth to night." I said "It is a pity but you had sixpennyworth at the tread-mill." I turned my head, and saw Thompson and Brooks coming after me; they immediately struck me, knocked my hat over my eyes, and knocked me down, and began to ill-use me in a most cruel manner; one of them said "Cut the b - 's ear off, and frisk him;" they took my watch from my pocket, and 2 s. 1 d. from my right-hand waistcoat pocket. I knocked down Porter, and the one who is not here, and got the watch from them; they ran away into Little Moorfields - I followed, and said, I should detain them till I got assistance to take them. The whole five then immediately beset me, and ill-used me again. After I had been knocked about in a most horrid manner, and a great many persons collected, an officer came named Herbert. I asked him to take them into custody; he said he had nothing to do with it, I must go about my business, or he would take me - they all burst out laughing. In about half an hour, I got the assistance of some friends, and found an officer named Payne; he took the girl who is not here, searched her, and let her go. I then went to Cripplegate watch-house, and the constable sent an officer with me. I found Gainsford and Thompson in Little Moorfields; we took them to the watch-house, went out again, and found Porter, who ran away, and got in between the two horses of a hackney coach; we took her, and the next evening, I took Brooks in Little Moorfields. I asked her where she was going - she said, "To take a walk." I said, if she would go with me to the corner of Moor-lane, I would talk about that. I then gave charge of her.
 JURY. Q. Were you sober? A. Perfectly so.
   JOSEPH HORTON. I am a constable. On the 15th of September I was at the watch-house. The prosecutor came and stated he had been robbed of his watch, and he had spoken to some officer, who would not assist him. I went with him to Little Moorfields, and we saw Gainsford. I told her I had a charge of felony against her; I told her to walk before me. I went down Little Moorfields, and there I took Thompson; I took them to the watch-house. When we were walking to Guildhall from the Compter, Gainsford and Brooks said they knew something about it. They said the prosecutor had asked a woman named Place to go down the alley for sixpence, which he gave her, but before she would go down she would spend it in gin, that as soon as she had drank the gin she would not comply with his wishes, and he struck her a violent blow, which caused her to cry Murder! and the others went up and assisted her; they admitted having struck him, but as to robbing him they denied it.
   ANN WOOLMAN. I live in the Horse Ride, No. 2. I was coming down Moorfields, about half-past eight o'clock that evening, and saw Gainsford and the prosecutor quarrelling; he accused her of robbing him; she struck him; and a young woman not here pulled off her cap, and some of them held him against the wall, while the others beat him.
   JOHN TURNER. I am an officer. The prosecutor came to the watch-house, and stated that he had been ill used; an officer was sent out with him, and the prisoners were taken and sent to the Compter.
 GAINSFORD's Defence. I was going down Little Moorfields, and I met a young woman; she said she had been down a court with a young man, and would I have any thing to drink, and we went into a house; as we came out the prosecutor met us, and he struck her; they fought for some time, till fifty or sixty people were collected; she cried out Murder! and I went up to take her part.
 THOMPSON's Defence. I was going along Little Moorfields and heard a noise, and saw the prosecutor running about like a madman; I think he was intoxicated; I went away; and as I was going along White-street, about an hour and a quarter afterwards, the officer tapped me on the shoulder.
