only in the habit of advancing money on goods as brokers, if bills of lading
are sent to us-among persons with whom I had business were Leman Hart
and Co.--the defendant came to me in reference to a renewal of some bills;
I don't think it was with respect to a bill of 1501. of Leman Hart's which
had been dishonoured about the end of January ; I never heard of it-I
never heard that an action had been brought against him in respect of certain
bills he had given to a Mr. Storey ; I never heard of the naine of Storey; I
never heard of the bill before now-I never heard that he had parted with
one of these bills, and that Dickenson was obliged to defend an action-I
was not aware that his account was attached till quite recently; I believe at
the Mansion House-I did not know that he had two accounts, one at the
Bank of England and one at Prescott's; I have learnt it since; I heard it
given in evidence by Prescott's clerk that the account there was kept low,
and almost practically closed, because of the attachment-I should think a
man in Mr. Dickenson's position would depend very much upon getting
remittances from those to whom he consigned goods--I knew nothing of his
business except in these transactions with Morgan and Co.- I have not
discounted for him-I don't think I ever had a direct transaction with him
-I knew that Morgan and Co. had renewed some bills for him; that was
all I knew-Leman Hart's were not quite in the same condition-the
defendant asked me to renew a bill of Leman Hart's for him after Leman
Hart had failed—I knew that the goods that were got from Mesars, Morgan's
in January went to Australia; he did not make away with them-I attempted
to attach them; we found afterwards that they were properly in the hands
of persons out there, and I could not do 80-I do not know that the con-
signments have not all been realised; I have not inquired into that I know
that there has been a great depression in trade for the last eighteen months
- I don't find that there has been a difficulty in getting returns from ship-
ments to Australia - I think there is danger in any bill that is drawn, to a
certain extent--before I saw the defendant I would not have trusted him
with 502; afterwards I would have trusted him with 5001.- I wrote to
Morgan and Co. a few days before I saw him, that they were not to trust
him, and afterwards that there was little danger in doing it. (A letter of
18th January from Chaplin to Norris, and a reply of 20th, were read; also
a letter of 23rd January from Chaplin to Norris.) I was to form a decision
and communicate to Mr. Chaplin-I did not learn from the defendant at
the interview on 29th that he had not received a remittance from Australia
as be had expected-what took place at the meeting I could not say--there
was a great deal said – I don't think he said anything at the meeting; I don't
think he spoke I was not at the bankruptcy meeting, I am referring to
the meeting of creditors.
