John EATON (police-constable S 193.) On Thursday evening, the 4th of
May, I was sent for, and found Manship in a pawnbroker's shop, in the
Hampstead-road—I told her she was charged with a robbery in Old Caven-
dish-street-she said, “I never was in Old Cavendish-street in my life"-I
took her to the station, and the inspector asked where she lived—she said, "I
will not tell anybody"-on the Saturday following, I went to a lodging in
George-street, Hampstead-road-I found this water-bottle there, two china
plates, this cup and saucer and stopper, and a duplicate for a silver spoon.
* ELIZABETH West. I live in George-street, Hampstead-road. On Sa-
turday, the 29th of April, the two prisoners came to my house for apartments
-I showed them some, and let them to Manship-she brought some things
in between eight and nine o'clock the same evening on the following Wed-
nesday, I said to Vickers, who went by the name of Smith at my house, “I
think I know your face somewhere"-she said, “Oh, dear, no" I said,
"I am sure I do ; is not your name Vickers ?" she said, “No”-I
said, "Do you know Mrs. Stevens, of Sussex-street !”-she said, “No”—I
afterwards went with Vickers to the station, to inquire after Manship-I re-
member the constable coming, and finding these articles in the room, which I
let to the prisoners—I was present when he found them-Manship told me
Vickers was her sister.
THOMAS BARRETT. I am shopman to Mr. Crouch, a pawnbroker, in
Grafton-street East. This silver tea-spoon was pawned on the 29th of
April, by Vickers—I am positive it was her-I knew her very well—this is
the duplicate of it--I knew Manship.
