WILLIAM ROWE. I am clerk to Mr. Robinson, a merchant, in Cheapside. Captain Mitchell is captain of the Graham  —he is entitled to draw a monthly note in favour of Mr. Weldon—this note was left with Fairbairn—Mr. Finlay leaves the money for Mr. Weldon, and Fairbairn had to receive it for Weldon, the chief officer of the ship—I paid it to the prisoner in March, for Fairbairn—I should have paid it to her if she had come—she was not well when she came the last time, and I said, "Leave the note with me"—she said, "I will send some one for it"—I said, "Leave me the name," and she left, "Napier"—I put the name in the corner—when the prisoner came he answered the descrip tion, and I paid it him—when I paid the money I parted with the reeponsibi lity—Mr. Finlay had no other responsibility about it, nor any share in it—if anything, it was the property of Fairbairn. NOT GUILTY.

 1360. SAMUEL WANTS, was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of April , 2 pairs of boots, value 3 s  ., the goods of Joseph Hopkins ; to which he pleaded GUILTY.— Confined Three Months .
 1361. HENRY GRANT, and JOHN WALKER, were indicted for steal ing, on the   29th of March , 2 coats, value 3 l  .; 1 gown, 10 s  .; 1 shawl, 3 s  . 6 d  .; 1 handkerchief, 2 s  .; 1 ornament, 6 d  .; 1 blow-pipe, 3 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 pair of gloves, 6 d  .; the goods of Henry Robinson . HENRY ROBINSON. I keep the Coach and Horses in Cross-street, Hat ton-garden. On the evening of the 29th of March, my wife went up stairs to the bed-room to get some change—she called my father out of the club-room, then came down, and called me—I went up, and found Grant under the bed—I fetched the policeman, and when I got him, Grant had been pulled from under the bed—Walker then came in—he called him some name, and said, "You shall not go to the station-house without me; if you take one you shall take both"—these articles were under the bed, and were pulled out with Grant—I had seen them safe under a window in the club-room, which is in the first floor, two hours before—the bed-room is on the second floor—I had seen the prisoners in the club-room before, and I did not observe Walker again till he came into the bed-room, and said, "You shall not go to the station without me."
   Walker. Q.  Was not I in the club-room when you were called to go up stairs? A.  I could not see you then—I saw you afterwards. MARY ROBINSON. I passed the club-room that evening, but the door was shut—I went to the bed-room, and found Walker under the bed and Grant against the fire-place—I went down stairs, and gave information, and my husband's father went up stairs—I did not go up again—I had left these wings on the table in the bed-room about three hours before I saw the pri soners—this china figure was on the mantel-piece.
   Walker. Q.  How can you tell it was me under the bed, you said I had a blue coat on? A.  No, you had a jacket—it was you—I did not see your face, I saw your dress—you was lying on your face.
