RICHARD MONKSTELL I live in Baker's-rent, Hackney-road. I have
known the prisoner since last summer-I had a difference with him once,
but it was tnade up the night following-on Tuesday night, the 21st of
June, about half-past nine o'clock, I went to the Victoria public-house, in
Hackney-road, in company with Richard Wheatley-the prisoner was
there, in company with a young woman, named Rachael Adams, and two
mote, sitting on ench side of him I have known Rachael Adams ever
since I have known the prisoner-I never lived with her-I had no
conversation with the prisoner- I did not drink with him he left the
room, and came down stairs before I did, and stood in the passage--
I followed him in a few minutes-- when I got down stairs I saw him
pull out a large knife from his right-hand pocket-I dare say it was
six inches long, handle and all, when it was open-he said he would stab
the first one to the heart that came bear him, and made use of a very bad
Word-I immediately went up to him, and told him to shut up the knife,
and not stab tay cousin with it-James Spencer is my cousin - there had
been a few words before I came into the parlour, but I had not heard
them--he had said something which I had heard mentioned, and in
consequence of that I said, “Don't stab my cousin" he told me to get
away, or he would serve me the same, and he immediately up with the
knife to make an attempt to strike me I parried the blow off with
my left hand, or it must have struck me I had made no attempt to
strike him--he followed me up and struck me twice on my head, and made
it bleed - was staggering towards the pavement, he followed me up, and
stabbed me in the chest, making use of a very bad word-I was as sober as
I am how he said he would stab me to the heart, and he jobbod
the knife into my chest I was immediately taken off to a doctor at
the corner of Crabtree-row, and my wounds were dressed--the prisoner
went into a tobacco-shop---I saw bim go in-I did not give him any provo-
cation before he struck me. es
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. How many persons were there at
the beer-shop, besides yourself and the prisoner ? A. A great number
there might have been twenty-it was half past ten o'clock when I got there
I went with Wheatley-I had been to bed, but got up to go to the neees-
sary, and then went with him-I can't say whether Adams had been
living with Spencer-I have seen her at his room-as the prisoner at-
tempted to strike me, I parried the blow off with my left hand, and I must
have struck him then at the side of his face-he then followed me up with
the knife, and struck me twice-only two persons at the beer-shop were
friends of me and my cousin dare say I knew half a dozen in the
room by speaking to them I was not fetched to the house for any parti-
cular reason.
Q. How did you know there was any intention of stabbing your cousin ?
4. Because there had been a few words beforehand, which I had been told
of by my cousin, when I came to the public house I was not fetobed to
make a party against the prisoner-I had no reason to know that I was to
do 80-the prisoner stood in the passage, opened his knife, came to the
threshold of the door, and said he would stab the first person that came
near him-he did not say he would stab my cousin-my cousin went near
him, and wanted to get the knife out of his hand I wanted to do so too-
I did not lay hold of him and shake him.
