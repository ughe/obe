   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What was the nature of this flourishing busi ness you were carrying on? A.  A branch of the iron business—I had been in it four years, about two years and nine months before I became acquainted with the prisoner—I think I had been living in Tredegar-square about six months before I married—the prisoner was living at Tredegaer square the whole time—I removed and lived with them in Tredegaf-square—at least they lived with me there—I engaged the apartments—I believe I and the prisoner occupied the house on the same day together—the prisoner might be in the occupation of the house a few hours before I went there, but not a day with his family—the prisoner, his wife, the daughter I afterwards married, and three children, lived there—before I went to Tredegar-square I had a furnished bed-room near Southwark-bridge, at Bank-side—I did not live there with the prisoner—I removed my clothes and trunks to Tredegar-square from Bank-side—I had only one room there—my habits were economical—before that I lived at Mrs. Wallace's, a friend of mine, at No. 6, Stamford-grove East, Upper Clapton—I cannot toll you how long I lived there—these are foolish questions, they have nothing to do with the case—the prisoner did not live with me then, nor I with him—I believe he lived in Garnault-place then—I went to Garnault-place after the first time he came to my warehouse, previous to my going to Trede gar-square—I visited them there—I never went there to live—after leaving Tredegar-square I went to Herman-hill, on-Epping-forest, and had the greater part of a furnished house—I did not agree to board with the prisoner and his family at 100 l . a year, nothing of the kind—they engaged the house at Herman-hill, but I found the money to pay the lodgings and support them.
   Q.  Now, on your oath, was it not during the time you were living there that some of these sums were paid? A.  What I speak of the pri soner having had, was expressly for Wall's business—I found money for the housekeeping—I lived at Herman-hill from April to June, I think—I went to live with him originally in May, 1839, and we continued to live together, from one place to another, till January, 1841—the prisoner's wife had the management of the house—I cannot say exactly how much I paid them for housekeeping during that time—I kept no account of it—on my oath the prisoner had 1000 l . from me for the purpose of carrying on Wall's business—the prisoner did not pay the whole of my wedding expenses—I paid for the whole of them—Mrs. Hitchcock received the money in my counting-house last March—I cannot say the precise amount—I kept no account of it, but she, the prisoner, and all the family, had new suits of clothes—I have received money from him to the amount of a few shillings, not to the amount of several pounds—I think Wall came to my warehouse twice, and met the prisoner there, with some other members of his family—they met for the purpose of talking of this law-suit—I should certainly not have trusted to the prisoner unless I had seen Wall—what I heard from Wall and other members of his fauie mily, and from his friends, certainly impressed the belief on my mind that he was entitled to the property—unless that belief had been impressed on my mind by Wall and his friends, I should certainly not have trusted to the prisoner's representations.
