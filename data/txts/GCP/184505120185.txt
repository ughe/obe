1192. WILLIAM GRACE was indicted for stealing 1 gelding, price
61., the property of William Newett, his master.
WILLIAM NEWETT. I am a carman, and live in Shooter's Hin-road-
the prisoner was in my service. On Friday morning, the 4th of April, 1
gave him a gelding, and told him to go to the Catherine Wheel inn
with it, and to see the ostler, who would direct him to where I had the
gelding from I had bought it, but did not like it, and wanted to return
it-he was to take it to the man, and if he did nat take it in, he was to
take it back to the ostler at the Catherine Wheel-I saw the prisoner
again on the Sunday morning-be said he had left the horse all right at
Mr. Adams's, the man I had it of-I said, “Very well "-I bad changed
another horse away, and was to give 8l. for this one, but it was not sound,
and I returned it.
GEORGE Hook. I am ostler at the Catherine Wheel in the Borough.
On Friday morning, 4th of April, about ten o'clock, the prisoner came with
a borse - he asked if I knew it-I said "No," but I looked again, and
said I did-he asked if I knew where Mr. Adams lived-I said yes,
about 300 yards from the Catherine Wheel, the second public-house on the
right hand-he said he should go with the horse there, and return and
give me an answer about it, but he did not return
Prisoner. There was no such words; I said I was to leave the horse
with a man named Hook ; I said to him, “You take it in and keep it a
week till my master comes ;" he said he could not take it in; he directed
me out of the yard; I had no name or number to go to. Witness. No,
he told me he would go with the horse, and I said if he could not find the
man to come back to me again.

Thomas Moor. I am foreman to Mr. Giddings, a horse-slanghterer.
The prisoner came to me that morning to sell the horse, as he said, for
the best price he could get for it-it was a glandered horse, and unfit to
be about-I paid bim 21. for it, and he gave me a receipt.
Heild
Prisoner's Defence. My master came, and gave me 88.. which was all I
received of him for three weeks; he sent me to the place with the horse,
and if I could not get rid of it there I was to sell it, and to bring the
money home; these were the last words he said to me; the ostler refused
taking it in, as it was glandered; he said it was more than his place was
worth; my master told me I was to take it to this man, and he had better
have the money he could get for it, than nothing.
WILLIAM NEWETT re-examined. "Q. Did the prisoner tell you he had
sold it, and give you the 21.? A. No-he said he left it at Mr. Adama's,
and tied it up in the stable-I did not know the horse was glandered, but
it was not sound, and would not do for me.
Guilty. Aged 26.-- Confined One Year.
