ORLANDO, Valentino (24, correspondent), who pleaded guilty
last Sessions before Mr. Justice Pickford (see page 620) of wounding
his wife Marie Orlando with intent to do her grievous bodily harm,
was brought up for judgment
Mr. Justice Bigham said he understood that prisoner had cast of
the foolish suspicions he had entertained against his wife, that his
wife wanted to rejoin him, and that they had decided to return together
to their native country, Switzerland. In these circumstances he (Mr.
Justice Bigham), with the full concurrence of Mr. Justice Piokford,
had decided to release prisoner on his own recognisances in the sum
of £100 to come up for judgment if called upon.

GYDE, Henry Warwick, and MARCUS, Edward Septimus Ber-
nard, otherwise called Septimus Marous; both conspiring with one
Walter Darby, by false pretences to defraud such of His Majesty's
liege subjects as should be induced to purchase debentures and be
come debenture holders in certain publio companies called the Welsh
Blato Quarries, Limited, and the North Wales Slate Quarries,
Limited, of their moneys and valuable securities; both unlawfully
obtaining by false pretences from John Dawson Belf banker's cheques
for £180 and £210 respectively, and £390 in money: from Joshua
Heywood banker's cheques for £18, £29 5s., and £45 respectively, and
£92 5s. in money; from Fielder Orme banker's cheques for £90,
£280, and £90 respectively, and £460 in money; from Albert Ed
ward Farrow's banker's cheques for £99 and £90 respectively, and
£189 in money; from Alfred Ernest Barlow a banker's cheque for £18
and £18 in money; from Lucretis Goodwin banker's cheques for
£98 15s., £90, and £40 respectively, and £228 15s, in money; from
George Fentress banker's cheques for £80 and £50 respectively, and
£130 in money; and from Jane Price a banker's cheque for £42 10s.
and £42 10s, in money, in each case with intent to defraud.
Mr. Muir, Mr. Travers Humphreys, Mr. Graham-Campbell, and
Mr. Bigham prosecuted; Mr. F. E. Smith, K.C., M.P., Mr. George
Elliott, and Mr. S. H. Lamb defended.
