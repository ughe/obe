JOHN HENRY BREEZE, I am goods clerk at the Eastern Counties Rail-
way station at St. Ives, which is about three miles and a half from Hough-
ton. I only know the prisoner from seeing him at the station-he came
there to receive some goods, which had come down for him by railway-I
cannot say how they were addressed, but he signed his name William
Leader in this book in my presence--the book is in my writing, it only
gives the name-I took it from the invoice--- We held the goods for Leaders
and delivered them to him as Leader-I did not see them; somebody else
delivered them.
WILLIAM ELY. I am a publican, of Whitton, in Huntingdonshire, adjoin-
ing Houghton. The prisoner asked my permission for his letters to be
addressed to my house, and I gave him leave four or five letters came, the
first one was delivered to him by my wife in my presence--I saw two or

COURT to ALFRED FULLER. Q. Where is the bark put after it is groundt
A. It is shot on the floor, and afterwards put into casks—we saw places
where bark had been taken off the hoop of the stone which keeps the dust
in, that was what he had got to put into his pocket-I missed four or five
good handfuls-after it is ground it runs down between the two stones, and
the dust flies up and settles, that is the finest—in another room a great deal
had been shot down, and we could see where it had been shot down and
picked up, none was left but the dust on the floor; that was not a proper
place for sifting it-we do not sift it at all.
COURT to MR. HOWARD. Q. What quantity do you have in a week
A. One or two tons—I cannot tell to what extent my stock has been
decreased, from the immense quantity which is ground, and the quantity of
dust which hangs in hundredweights about the walls of the room, and
which is just as valuable as the rest.
Guilty. Aged 30.- Confined Two Years.
