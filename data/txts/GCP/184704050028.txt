Prisoner Reeve. Q. Did anybody else sleep there that night? A. No-
several came there to breakfast--a pane of glass was put in the window of
another room several days before, not of this room.
JOSEPH OCKLEY (police-constable A 153.) On the 3rd of March, between
two and three o'clock in the afternoon, I was on duty at the House of Com-
mons-Mr. Baynham pointed out Smith to me in Palace-yard - I pursued
and found him in a coal-wbarf in Abingdon-street-I said, "I want you"
he said, “What for?"-I said, “A person charges you with robbing him
he said, “Who is it; how can he prove I have robbed him ?"--I said, "He
is up in the street; let us go and hear what he has to say"-when we came
into the street he saw Mr. Baynham, and said, “How can you prove I have
robbed you ?"-Mr. Baynham made some answer-I took Smith to the station,
searched him, and found 8d. on him-I afterwards took him to Fleet-street
station--coming towards the City, he said, "I have seen something about this
in the newspapers; we owed bim some money while we were with him, and
that is the reason he has charged us with stealing the money, I suppose-
said I should have to mention what he said to me--he said nothing more till
he got to the station-he there said to Mr. Baynham, “Would not you like
to get your money again, old fellow ?"
Prisoner Smith. I was not sober ; I was very sick at the first station.
Witness. He was sober, and was not at all sick-he might have been
sick after I left him he was about an hour and a balf at the first
station.
