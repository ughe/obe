Will you swear you were not charged with having abused and ill-
med Mr. Brown? A. He made a complaint, 1 tell you, but a charge is a

Will you tell us whether there was not a complaint or a charge made

Have you done nothing to meet with the disapprobation of the Ma-
gistrates in this case ? A. Not that I know of-I did not receive the
disapprobation of the Magistrates, that I am aware of-the possession of
this house belonging to Phillips was improperly got away from me it was
communicated by a solicitor to my superintendent, that it was the order of
Mr. Broughton to give up the house, and I told him so, and he said, "Let
it be generally understood in this Court that I never gave such an order"
I was ordered to give it up by my superintendent, and Mr. Broughton
said publicly in Court that he never gave any such order; and Mr. William
Heritage said he was bound to give some explanation for going to the
superintendent; and Mr. Broughton said, “I suppose it must be a mis-
take," but for that I would have had other charges against Phillips here
to-day--I took possession of the keys of the shop-there is another way
going into the house, through the passage - I had not the key of the
front door-Phillips and his family had free access to the whole of the
house, without the shop-I took possession of the keys of the two shops
without the knowledge of the Magistrate or my superintendent-I was not
Teinonstrated with by the Magistrate for so doing--Mr. Broughton said,
* The police know what they are doing."
