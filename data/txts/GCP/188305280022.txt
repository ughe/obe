THOMAS RANDALL (Re-examined). I gave the description which I got
from Mrs. Townley, of the man who took the rooms--the description was
not from the cabman-I do not know a book called 98G, but it is the
G Division,
RICHARD CRAWLEY (Examined by MR. FULTON). I am chief inspector of
the Great-Eastern Railway-on 6th March I received a description from
Evans of the man he says he saw in Satton's Yard, and took it down in
writing at the time in this book. (MR. FRITH declined to put the book in
widence.)
MARY ANN TOWNLEY. I keep a haberdasher's shop at 199, Lever
Street I had a card in my window, and about nine days before January
25th I let a room to a young man at half-a-crown & week-he gave
the name of Forty, and said that he was going to get married-he
brought a bed, and said that he would bring chairs and tables next week
-on 25th January he came in a cab, came into the shop, borrowed
some green string, and went into his room- the prisoner is nothing like
him-there is a vast difference between them he was & very dark man
with dark bushy whiskers that was the man who came and went away
in the cab the cabman came back three hours after he took my lodger
away and asked me if the young man had been back-I said what
young man ?” he said "That dark young man I took away with the
two canvas bags who came out of your shop the police asked me to
describe him, and I said that he was a dark man, taller and stouter
than the prisoner, and with dark bushy whiskers, and the cabmari said
that he was a dark man with dark bushy whiskers- I went with the
police to the prisoner's house-they said that they were going to identify
my lodger, and that they knew where the man was, because the cabman
had identified him they gave me a half-crown—when I came out Tsaid
that there was such a great difference between the prisoner and my
lodger that if the cabman said he was the man he either had no eyes or
he saw double-two days afterwards the police took me to the City to see
another man-he was a great big dark man with bushy whiskershe
was not the man either the prisoner told me that he knew I was speak -
ing the truth, and that the right man was in the country that is the
man (Pointing to a constablo) I never saw the prisoner in my life till
the police entered his house--I have lived in first-class families, and my
character will bear the strictest investigation, five years with a physician
and three years with a lady, and I married from there.
Cross-examined. The man took my room on a Tuesday and paid half-
&-crown rent in advance-he brought a bed and stick-Inspector
Crawley came between January and March–I did not tell him that abont
7 o'clock the same evening the dark man drove up in a pony cart and
stopped at my shop, having another man with him if any statement of
that kind is made it is false—they asked me if I could identify the
cabman, and I said “No”-I did not tell Inspector Crawley a word
about the second man; this is the first time I have heard that there was
a second man-I did not say that it was not the lodger, but the second
man who came to take away the bed-it is all untrue- the man with the
bag came out at the side door, not through the shop-I was in the shop,
I was not looking out at the window-there were not two men this is
the first time I have been asked about a second man.
