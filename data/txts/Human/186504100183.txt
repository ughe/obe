 476. JOSEPH SMITH  (18) , Stealing 4 shirts, the property of Thomas Wilson .
   MR. MOORE conducted the Prosecution, and MR. TAYLOR the Defence. THOMAS WILSON. I am a draper, living at 65, Wellington-street, Wool ner standing at the corner of the window peeping through a guard—I saw him make a move in the direction of the counter—I went up to him, and asked him what he wanted—he appeared confused—he said, "I want a penny worth of thread"—I served him with it—directly he had gone, I found these shirts wrapped up in this handerchief—the handkerchief smelt of polish or vaanish—the shirts were my property—I went after the prisoner—he had got about fifty or sixty yards when I caught him—I brought him back, and said, "I want you respecting those shirts you have taken"—he said, "I beg your pardon; I hope you are not going to account me of taking them?"—I gave him in charge.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not the prisoner outside the shop when you first saw him? A.  No; in the lobby—the shirts were about seven or eight feet from the lobby—I did not say a word to him about the shirts when he was in the shop. SAMUEL LING( Policeman, R  109) I was called by the last witness to take the prisoner into custody—I found the shirts on the floor, wrapped up in this handkerchief—I told the prisoner that I should take him in charge for stealing four shirts—I asked him what he was, and he said a French-polisher—I noticed his hands were covered with polish—I examined the handkerchief, and found stains upon it—it smelt of polish—the handkerchief has now lost its smell, but the stains are visible. GUILTY.**— Confined Eighteen Months .
 477. JOHN GROOM  , Stealing a mare, the property of George Heath .
   MR. WELLS conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE HEATH. I live at Rushy-green, Lewisham, and am a cattle dealer—on the 7th February I turned four horses out to graze in a field in Stansted-lane, Lewisham—one was a grey mare—at 4 o'clock in the after noon on the 8th February I saw them safe in the field, and on the 10th the grey mare was gone—she is worth about 10 l .—I next saw the mare in Mr. Peplow's possession on 22d February. JESSE SAMUEL SIMPSON I live at Bell-green, Lewisham—I have known this field in Stansted lane for twelve years—I had charge of the horses for Mr. Heath—the grey mare was in the field on the night of the 9th of February. JOHN PEPLOW. I am a cab proprietor, of Cumberland-place, Old Kent-road—on the 10th February, about 11 in the morning, the prisoner came to me—he had a grey mare with him, and he asked me if I would buy it—he said he wanted 4 l . 10 s . for her—I said I could not give him so much, as it was very lame, but I would give him 2 l . 10 s  —he said he came by her by "chopping" with a baker at Plumstead for a pony—he said the mare had been turned out on Plumstead marshes.
