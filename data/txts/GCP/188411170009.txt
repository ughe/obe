First Session, 1884–85.
-I left Edinburgh about 15th September to go to the Manchester Races
with Ward and Richards--they said they could do better at the post
than stopping at Edinburgh, and they asked me to go with them--we
went to Leeds first, and stopped there one night, and then went on to
the Manchester Races when we were on the course at Manchester Ward
left me, he said he was going into the betting-ring-he returned shortly,
and said he must back a horse for 501., and wanted the 501. from me he
told Richards as well, who was along with me in the Grand Stand-he.
said if it was for 2007 or 2507, he would bet for him as well I did not
give him the money then; I had only a patent purse, and I could not
get the money out quick enough Richards said it was all right, I could
give him the money, passing a 501. note to Ward - I did not examine it,
I believed it to be one Richards had a lot of money on him at the time
they were on his own bets; they were passed before mine were
Ward then went away towards the ring- I gave Richards 471., and told
him I would give him the other 3-he said, "All right” after the
race was run Ward said we had lost when I gave the 471. to Richards
I did not know what horses I was supposed to back, Ward did not tell
me I made a 501. bet besides that, I did not advance the money, I was
to pay him in London ; I had money in London-Ward made that bet;
that was in the presence of Richards; he said we had lost that he told
me the names of the horses were strictly private I did not kyow the
names Ward said he had lost a lot of money on the Manchestern.des,
he did not state what amount. I came to London on 20th September
I stopped at the same hotel with Ward, Williams's Hotel, Bow Lane,
Cheapside-on 22nd September I went to the Holborn Restaurant with
Ward-I saw Richards and Don there. I was introduced to Don by
Ward-he told me that he was a thorough gentleman, he had known
him 20 yeans, and had had many transactions with him, and always
found him a straightforward man; he also said he was a great sporting
man in England and France, and know all the racing men and trainers,
but he did not associate with the jockeys, they were a low class of people
I saw the prisoners nearly every day between the 22nd and 29th Sep-
tember-on Sunday, 28th September, Ward said there was very important
business to attend to on the following day at 10 o'clock, and according to
that arrangement I went to the Inns of Court Hotel at 10 o'clock with
Ward-Don and Richards were there when we got there we went into
A room there Ward said he was going to back Sir Reuben; that was
the name of a horse that was going to run in the Cesarewitch-he said
he was going to put on 2501, or 5001., he was not certain which I was to
put on 1001., and I consented to do 80-I had not the money with me at
the time; I told Ward so—he said it did not matter because I could
give him the money another time-the other two prisoners were both
present then he said he was going to provide the money in the mean-
time-before Ward started he got the money from Don and Richards
I saw what I thought notes pass between them from Richards and Don,
and Ward pulled out some notes and said, “Here is two 501. notes," and
he said, "That will do for you”-I did not see whether they were 501.
notes-he then put all the notes in his purse, and said he was going
away to put them on Sir Reuben-I and Don walked out towards the
Holborn Restaurant-before Ward left he said I owed him altogether
