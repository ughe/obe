CHARLES BUTTON. I am a compositor, of 140, Alderminster Road I
passed the spot, but did not notice the boys—I noticed a pony about 60
yards from me, coming towards me, and galloping, I should think twelve or
fourteen miles an hour the prisoner was riding on it, and ran right
among the boys-he did not make the slightest effort to pull up-he had
no whip or stick- I think there was a rope, instead of a bridle-I saw the
boy knocked down-he had his back towards the pony-I had my eye on
the boy about a minute before he was knooked down.
Cross-examined. I am not accustomed to horses, or to ride or drive-I
was about thirty yards from the boy when the accident happened.
JURY. Q. Did you see the boy run from the pavement into the road
and back again ? A. No, and I am positive he did not
ROBERT PARSLY (Policeman M 138). I took the prisoner.
Cross-examined. I went to him the same evening-he first asked me
how the child was I told him the result, and he said " It is a bad job"-
I ascertained that his cart had met with an accident on the tramway that
evening, and the tire of the wheel was taken off, and that he was riding
home to his master on the pony,

Witnesses for the Defence.
EDWARD ATLERLEY JACKSON. I am a telegraph boy, living in the Old
Kent Road-I was in Alderminster Road, and saw the prisoner riding a
middle-sized pony-he was about twenty yards from some boys who were
playing in the middle of the road--the pony was prancing I did not hear
the prisoner call out, or speak at all. I was going in the same direotion
as the pony-the boys were playing with marbles or stones, and I heard
somebody hallon to them to get out of the way, and then the little fellow
ran back again the pony was then near the middle of the road-I saw
the accident happen.
JOHN SCHMIDT. I live at 90, Alderminster Road--about 6 o'clook on
this evening I was looking from my window, and saw the boys playing in
the road, and the pony about fifteen yards from them-I balloaed out
* Get out of the road " three boys ran away, and then the little boy oame
back again to pick up the stones, and he was knocked down-I opened my
window and listened, but I did not go into the road.
GEORGE BUTCHER. I am a hawker, and let flys at the Mews near Alders-
gate Street—the prisoner has been six or seven months in my employ to
put my horses in, as I have only one hand he has driven for me at races, and
has experience in riding and driving-he is very careful indeed-be never
had an acoident with me he was on a pony 11 hands high, & poor little
thing worth 4l. or 51.--the utmost of its pace is eight miles an hour-I
have driven it-it is broken-winded, and cannot run further out of harness
than in.
