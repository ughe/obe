2060. GEORGE LUSH was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of October , 1 book, value 30s., the goods of Charles Smith , his master; and JAMES SMITH was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   SAMUEL CHARLES SMITH. I am a book-binder, and live in Cecil-street, Strand. Lush has been in my service at various times, and was so on the 6th of October, when this book was taken from my premises; it is the Haldane edition of Aristophanes - I had seen it five or six weeks before - I had had it bound for a gentleman, and have had it by me five or six months; my name is stamped in it - I was sent for on the 6th of October, to Mr. Bohu's, and they desired me to go to a pawnbroker's, where I found the book; I have seen Smith before, but did not know him personally.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I suppose an ordinary person would not know the value of it? A. No, only scholars; there was a crest in it, which is torn out.
   GEORGE LAW. I am servant to a pawnbroker in St. Martin's-lane. Smith brought this book to our house on the 6th of October, about a quarter to one o'clock, to pawn - I asked if it was his own property; he said it was - I did not understand the language, and sent it to Mr. Bohn, to ask what it was; he sent word back that it was stolen - we detained him; he was stupified, and hardly knew what he was about.
   WILLIAM CECIL. On the 6th of October I went to the Cock and Bottle public-house, to take some refreshment; I saw this soldier, Cock croft, sitting on something, which was wrapped up in a horse-cloth; in a few minutes the prisoner Smith came in, not very sober, and said to the soldier, "Give me what you are sitting on;" the soldier said,"No, I shall deliver it into the person's hands who gave it me to take care of:" I did not take any particular notice till about ten minutes, when I saw both the prisoners looking at a book; I knew Lush, and said, "Will you let me look at it?" he held it to me, and I looked at it - he asked what it was worth; I said I did not know whether it was Latin or Hebrew - he said, "It is Greek;" Smith then said to Lush, "I will give you 1l. for it;" Lush said, "I shall not sell it, it is not my property - I got it so" (making a cross on the table) - I left them there with the book.
 Cross-examined. Q. And this was openly in the public-house? A. Yes - there were a dozen more persons, who might all see it; Smith seemed tipsy.
   TABES COCKCROFT. Lush gave me the book in the public-house, and I sat upon it; he said, "Take care of it for a few minutes for me;" in about a quarter of an hour Smith came and owned it - I said I would not give it up to him; in a little time Lush came and had it - they went and sat down at the end of the room; I took no more notice of it - I saw them talking, but did not hear what they said; it was about twelve o'clock, just as I came off guard.
 Cross-examined. Q. There was nothing to prevent any person seeing what they did? A. No - Smith was very much in liquor.
 LUSH - GUILTY. Aged 26. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor, believing him to be in liquor. Confined Three Months.
 SMITH -    NOT GUILTY.
 2061.  RICHARD CARTWRIGHT and VALENTINE GANLY were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of October , 1 table, value 4l., the goods of William Edward Dolton.
   WILLIAM EDWARD DOLTON. I am a cabinet-maker, and live on Saffron-hill. On the 18th of October I received information that I had lost a table - I did not see it taken; I first thought the prisoner had taken it to the person I had it to repair for, and did not at that time take any steps to recover it - I found the prisoner Cartwright very drunk in a public-house in Cow-cross; he said he knew nothing of it - Ganly then came in, and said."Is it not all right? I took the table for Cartwright, and he sold it for 2l.;" I had them taken, and from Ganly's information I recovered the table, about six o'clock.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOWLING. Q. You had been on good terms with Cartwright? A. Yes, and never suspected his honesty - I owed him about 2s. 6d.
   RICHARD MILLER. I am a constable, and took the prisoners into custody - I found on Cartwright 14s., which he acknowledged he had received for the table, and he said Ganly was innocent.
   JOHN FORBES. I saw Ganly with the table on his head between ten and eleven o'clock - I afterwards took him; he said he had been employed by Cartwright to carry it, and hoped he should get into no trouble.
   JOHN RYALLS. The prisoners brought the table to me, Cartwright sold it for 2l.; I had known Ganly five or six years, and I understood Cartwright was going into the same line as he was, hawking goods for sale; they came back, and purchased some goods to go and sell.
 Cartwright's Defence. I had been drinking all night on Saturday, and again on Sunday, with Dolton; he was to pay me on the Monday morning - I went for it, and be put 1 1/4d. on the mantel-piece, and said, "There is that for you."
   WILLIAM EDWARD DOLTON. I had been drinking a little with him - he did not pay more than 6d. or 8d.; I went out about eight o'clock on Monday morning - I returned in twenty minutes, and the landlord said Cartwright had taken away a table.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2062.  JOHN BOWRIE was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 2 saws, value 5s.; I stock, value 2s. 6d., and 1 bit, value 4d., the goods of William George Jones.
   WILLIAM GEORGE JONES. I am a carpenter. On the 16th of October I lost my tools from the yard, in Coal Harbour-street, Hackney-road; I left them when I went to dinner, and when I returned they were gone - the prisoner had come there about ten o'clock in the morning, and staid till I went to dinner, when he went half way with me - I then told him to go and get a pint of beer, and I would bring him some bread and cheese; I went about half-past twelve o'clock, and he was not there - I ran back to where I had been at work, and they were gone.
   CEORGE DEAN. I am a butcher. On the 16th of October I was sitting in the yard at which the prosecutor was at work - I saw the prisoner come and take something out, between twelve and one o'clock; I did not see what he took - he said he was going to help the carpenter put up the fence.
