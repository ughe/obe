   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You told him it would be much better for him to tell the truth? A.  No, I do not believe I did. ROBERT BOWLES. I am in the service of the prosecutor. I heard some thing from him about this—I obtained a description of Smith, and about half-past eight the next morning I saw him in the street, and asked where he was last night—he said, "Playing in King-street"—I asked if he was with Tom  Rabone—he said, "No," that he was at Mr. Haggerty's, an iron shop, in Back Church-lane—I then let him go—I afterwards had some conversation with other persons, and took Smith, and asked if he knew a person named Lloyd—he said, "No"—I asked if he was sure he did not—he said, "Yes"—I asked if he was sure he was not at Lloyd's last night, he said, "No"—I took him to Mr. Lloyd's—as we went along, I said if he knew anything about it, he bad better save a bother, and after that he told me he did go—I took Rabone in Gloucester-place. JOHN BURKE( police-constable H  81.) I remember the prisoners being brought to the station—I heard Smith say he received 3 s . from Mrs. Lloyd, and gave 1 s . 6 d . to Rabone—Rabone did not say anything to that—when I took Rabone to the police-court, he said something, which was taken down—I believe I know Mr. Norton's handwriting—I do not think this is his—I do not know it. NICHOLAS PEARCE. I am a police-inspector. This is Mr. Norton's hand writing—( read )—"The prisoner, Rabone, on being asked whether he wished to say anything in his defence, says, 'I had half of the 3 s . obtained from Mrs. Lloyd.' The prisoner Smith, on being asked whether he wished to say any thing, says, 'I did not have the half-crown, but the 3 s . I did.'" NOT GUILTY.
