MOSES VALENTINE. I am a fancy-card paper-maker, and live in Allerton-street, Hoxton. I have seen the prisoner about town for the last two or three years - he had applied to me for employment: I gave him a job or two - on the 20th of November he came and said he had an order from a gentleman in Cheapside for two reams of pink paper; I told him I had no objection to complete the order for him, but I could not afford to allow him any profit - he said, "I don't want profit, I only want journeyman's wages;" I then agreed to let him have it - he came on the following Monday, I was out; he came on the Tuesday, and I got it completed by the Thursday evening; I was out, my wife would not allow him to take it; he came the next day - he said, "Valentine, are you ready to take this paper to Mr. Wilson's;" I said "Yes, I will go in a moment with you;" I had expected from what he said, that Mr. Wilson would pay me - we went on to Cheapside, he went into the house, left the paper, came out and said to me, "We must wait for Mr. Wilson for an hour;" I said, "Well, I have no objection, suppose we go to a coffee-shop;" he said, "No, I want to go to Fetter-lane, and by the time we come back, the hour will he up;" we went there, he went into a shop, came out and said, "I have to wait twenty minutes," I said, "I will wait no more in the street," I went to a coffee shop, and paid for breakfast for us both; he then said he had a pain in his bowels, he got up as if to go to the bar, but went away, and I saw no more of him till I found him on the Wednesday after at Gravesend.
   THOMAS WILSON. My father keeps a shop in Cheapside. On the 26th of November, the prisoner came, left three parcels tied up, and inquired for my father - I said he would be in in about an hour; he came in about an hour, and took the parcels away - we had given no order for paper.
   THOMAS VANN. I went with the prosecutor, and took the prisoner at Gravesend, on the 1st of December - he said the paper was not all sold, half of it was at his lodging- I went and found half of it - this is it.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he considered he had purchased the paper of the prosecutor.
 GUILTY. Aged 27. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 258.  ELIZABETH GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of December , 1 sheet, value 5s., and 1 bracelet, value 5s., the goods of Elizabeth Franklin.
 ELIZABETH FRANKLIN. I live in  New Tethill-street. The prisoner lodged in another room in the same house, for six months - I lost this property.
   JAMES DEEDY. I am in the employ of Mr. Jones, a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, brought by Lawbridge, on the 6th of December, but we had received information, and gave her into custody.
   MARY LOWBRIDGE. I am an unfortunate girl. I have known the prisoner some time - she gave me this sheet, and told me to pawn it; she said it belonged to herself- I was detained; I met her at the King's Head, in Orchard-street - she works at her needle; I never was at the prosecutor's.
   WILLIAM CLIFTON. I am an officer. I was sent for, and went to the prosecutrix, to ascertain how she had been robbed - I found the window and door fast; I went and got a search warrant - while I was there the pawnbroker's young man came with Lowbridge, and the sheet; I then went and searched the prisoner's lodging, and in the bed I found this bracelet - the key of the prisoner's room door unlocks the prosecutor's door as easy as the key that belongs to it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to another person to ask her to lend me 2s. - she said she had no money, but gave me the sheet to raise the money.
   HONOR GREAVE. I lodge at the same house, and have worked at the army-work for twenty-two years. The prisoner never asked me such a thing, and I never gave her the sheet.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Transported for Seven Years.
 259.  JOHN HANSON was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Ben Sowerby.
 JOHN MOXON. I am in the employ of  Thomas Ben Sowerby, a pawnbroker. On the 12th of November a person came in and told me something; I went out, and saw the prisoner and another boy running on the opposite side - I followed them, and saw them join six others; they spoke a few words, and then ran on - I followed the prisoner; he threw a handkerchief out of his pocket - I took it up, and went and took the prisoner; this is the handkerchief - it is my master's.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that hearing an alarm he ran with others, but was innocent.
 Two witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 14. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Whipped and Discharged.
 260.  MARY HANDLIN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of December , 1 silver spoon, value 2s., the goods of Edward John Howard.
   CHARLES HOLT. I am in the service of Mr. Edward John Howard, a solicitor, who lives in Duke-street, St. James'. The prisoner had lived as cook in his family, but had left it - on the 9th of December she called, and said she had seen my master in the morning, and he had appointed to meet her at three o'clock; I said my master was not then at home, and she had better go into the office, as we are not allowed to let any one remain in the kitchen - one of the clerks then knocked at the door; I went and let him in, and when I returned to the kitchen the prisoner was just going out; I missed a tea-spoon - I took the prisoner's arm, said she must have it, and desired her to give it up without more words - she gave it from her bosom or her pocket; this is it.
 Prisoner's Defence. His wife had got the situation that I held, and he thought I wanted to get back again - that is the reason he says this.
 GUILTY. Aged 21. -    Judgment Respited.
