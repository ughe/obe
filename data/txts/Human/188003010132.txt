Of course I knew there was nothing at the bank to meet it, my account having closed in 1870"—he then said, "I heard that Inspector Andrews had been inquiring about these cheques, and I gave Collier 51. to go and square Field"—Field was the man who cashed the cheque—he said, "As regards the book, Herbert Pugh has been in my employ as a clerk, his' brother Arthur has constantly visited him, and I believe one or the other stole the cheque-book; cannot I arrange the matter with you to destroy Field's cheque-book? come out and have a glass of wine"—I said, "No; it is just as well that people should not see us together"—I then said, "Shall I take down your statement?"—he said, "I will write ft down if you like," and he wrote down this statement, and said, "You can call me as a witness, and I can say what I have written down."
