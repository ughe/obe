THOMAS PAYNE. I am a publican, and live in Shoreditch. The prisoner was brought to my house on the 23d of May; I found my quart pot concealed in his basket.
 HENRY STEBBING. I saw the prisoner take this pot from a butcher's steps; he put a cloth over it, and put it into his basket - I took him with it.
 GEORGE SMITH. I am an officer, and took the prisoner - he has been one of the paupers of our parish.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took it through distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 51. Transported for Seven Years.
 1323.  THOMAS ROURKE and MARY ANN ROURKE, were indicted for stealing on the 3d of June , 1 bat, value 8s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s.; 1 belt, value 6d.; 2 half-sovereigns, 7 shillings. 1 sixpence, and 5 1/2d., in copper monies, the property of Thomas King , from his person.
   THOMAS KING. I am a sawyer. On the 2d of June, about half-past ten o'clock at night, I was in the City-road- I was very much intoxicated, and fell in company with the female prisoner; I had at that time three half-sovereigns, a half-crown, two farthings, and some half-pence - I am not sure whether I had my hat on or not: I agreed to give her 4s. - I gave her half a crown, went to a room with her, and staid about a quarter of an hour; we then went to a public-house, and had something to drink- I there changed a half-sovereign, to pay for gin, and gave her 1s. 6d. more; she immediately ran down a court and I never saw her again till I was at the watch-house - I had had a silk handkerchief in my hat, and another round my neck; one of the farthings was of the old coin, and battered round the edge.
 Prisoner  MARY ANN ROURKE. He met me and another female - he asked us to have something to drink, and said he was ashamed to go into the house, as he had been drinking with his cousin, and had been lying down, and lost his hat; the watchman came up, and said, "Go on women" - we went, and the prosecutor followed us; he said he would give me half a crown, and when we got in, he gave me 2s. 5 1/2d., and said a farthing would make no difference.
 Witness. I had been to see a young man in the City-road - I have no cousin in London; I was going home from the Macclesfield Arms.
   SUSAN SIFORD. I live in the same buildings with the prisoners. On the 3d of June, at twenty minutes before one o'clock in the morning, I was awoke by a noise - I got up, opened my sesh, and saw Mary Ann Rourke at her own door, which is just opposite mine; she looked up at me, and went in doors, leaving the door open; I heard her say to some person, "Eighteen-pence is my due," and a man's voice said, "Here are two shillings, give me 6d.;" she said, "I have not one, you look in your pocket and you will find one;" he then seemed to look and found one - he gave it her, and she said "I am quite satisfied;" she then said, "I am very hungry, I want some bread and cheese and porter;" he said, "It is too late, you can't have it" - she said, "I know a house where I can get it, if you will go with me, and then we will come home and go to ged;" she went out, and the prosecutor followed her without a hat - she ran away from him in less than a second; he stood in the middle of the court - in a few minutes the male prisoner came, went to the bottom of the court, and then returned to his own door, knocked, and said "Jane, are you at home?" he put his knee to the door, opened it, and went in - he staid a minute or two, then came out, and went to the top of the court, where the prosecutor was standing, as if to watch for the woman; he then went near the watch-box - the woman came and went up to the prosecutor, who had fallen on his side - she gave a signal to her husband, who went and put his hand into the prosecutor's right-hand pocket, and took some money out, then into his left hand, and in his waistcoat pockets - they went to their own house, and I heard the man lay down on the table 6s. 6d. and some copper, I think 5 1/2d. and a farthing - she said, "D-n your b-y eyes that is not all the money, I saw more money in his pockets;" the man then went to the prosecutor again, searched all his pockets, turned him over, then went back to his own house, and shut the door - I put on my clothes and went for a watchman, but I could not find one: I came back and got a candle, by that time the watchman had come to the prosecutor; he got him up - I said to the watchman "He has been robbed, and I will point out the person in the morning," and I gave the same statement.
 Q. How do you know it was 6s. 6d.? A. I heard it counted on the table.
 Prisoner  THOMAS ROURKE. Q.What number is your house? A. No. 8; it is opposite, and I could hear what was done - I can see the door, but not into the house; I get my living by hard work, and selling old clothes - I go to fairs with my husband at Easter and Whitsuntide.
   CHARLES CONSTABLE. I was in the watch-house when the prosecutor was brought in intoxicated; another watchman brought in the woman who had seen him robbed - he told such a clear case, that I said "I must go, and apprehend these people;" I went with two watchmen - I knocked at the door; the man looked out of the window, and said,"What do you want?" I said, "I want you, Tom; come, and open the door" - he came down; I said, "It is upon a charge of felony;" I took him to the watch-house, and found this knife and 1s. 6d. on him - I then went back, and took the woman; she was not dressed - she took her pocket to put on, and I took out the money - there was 10s. 6d. in silver, 5d. in halfpence, and three farthings; at first she denied it, but afterwards said there had been a man with her who had lost his hat at Islington - on our way to the watch-house she said, "Constable, don't say any thing about my admitting that I had a man with me who had lost his hat; if you will not, I will give you half a crown."
 RICHARD BLISSET. I am a watchman. I took the prosecutor to the watch-house, and told the officer what I had heard from Siford; we then took them into custody -Siford said she saw the man put his hand intoall his pockets, and then go and search the pockets a second time, and said she heard the woman make use of an oath, and say he had not got all the money.
