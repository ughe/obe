 765. JAMES FRENCH  (42) and THOMAS DAVIS BELL  (32), were indicted for a conspiracy.
   MR. LILLEY conducted the Prosecution. JACOB FITCHETT. I am a traveller in the employ of Messrs. Sears and Co.—the firm, I believe, consists of two persons—I knew French some years ago, but had lost sight of him several years—early in April he came into the Duke of Clarence public-house, where I was, and said that he wanted about 20 l . worth of wine—I took him over to my stores, and introduced him to my stock-manager, Mr. Vickers—some wine was tasted, and an order was given to the amount of 40 l .—he said that he was a provision—dealer, and wanted it for captains, to facilitate business—Mr. Vickers asked him for a reference, stating that the terms were cash and discount, or a bill at three months—when we left the stores I told him that it was a larger amount than I felt justified in executing, and he said that he would leave it in my hands to reduce it to the amount previously stated, 20 l .—we went to the Duke of Clarence again, and an arrangement was made that I should meet him at the Black Prince, Waterloo-road—I did so on Wednesday, and the first thing he said was, "What is the reason my order is not executed?"—I said, "You said that you could give a reference, and we have had no name give to us"—he said, "Oh, my dear fellow, I can give you forty," but he mentioned no name—he asked me bow I should be situated the following morning, and I appointed to meet him at Aldgate Church—I did so, and we proceeded to High-street, Whitechapel—he did not mention the name of any reference during the walk, but when we got within a few steps of the premises of Merry and Nutter, I saw a person of the appearance of a gentleman, standing on the steps, leaning against the doorpost, with a pen behind his ear—French said "There is Mr. Henry Nutter; wait while I speak to him"—he went up to the person, topped with him half a minute, and then turned back to me, and said, "Mr. Nutter will tell you all that you require."—I went up to the gentle-man, and said, "Mr. Nutter, I presume?"—he nodded—I repeated the ques tion, and he said, "That is my name"—I asked him if Mr. French was trust worthy to the amount of 20 l . or 30 l .—he said, "Perfectly"—I said "He might be, in the provision line, but would he be for a luxury?"—he said, "What do you call a luxury?"—I said, "Wine"—he said, "Perfectly, for we as fre quently owe him money as he does us," that he was perfectly solvent, and we need not have the slightest fear—I returned, and made a report to Mr. Vickers.
