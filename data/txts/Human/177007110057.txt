This woman had this dog six or seven years before we came together; I did not care to destroy it, because she had a regard for it; it is very apt to be on the bed, and very often upon my legs, and it snores three times lowder than a christian; I beat the dog off the bed once or twice in the night; in the morning when I awak'd (I don't believe she or I had waked three minutes before this accident happened) I said to her, Hannah get up and light the fire, and make me some tea; upon that she got immediately up; I bid her turn the dog out; her shoes and stocking lay some where about the bed-side; she did not turn the dog out, so I got out of bed; I opened my door and the street door, and then fell a punching the dog in order to get him out; I stood by the door; the dog not seeming ready to come out, this unfortunate knife happening to lie upon my dresser, I took hold of it; I throw'd it with all the force I had in the world at the dog; my wife at this time was stooping, with her head down close to the ground, I believe for her shoes and stockings; she fell down immediately as the knife was thrown out of my hand; her left side was towards me as I throw'd the knife; she was a short woman; I went to her and lifted her up in my arms; she said, Oh John! John! That was all the words I heard her speak. Where I put her down again, God above knows, for the blood came pouring out upon me like a torrent. As soon as I put her down, I cried out, and ran to the next door; some of the neighbours came; I told them I had thrown a knife at my dog, and I believe I had killed my wife; I said to several of them, for God's sake go and fetch a doctor; there was a man so good as to go with me; we went down Grub-street, into Fore-street; I said I knew two doctors there; when we came there it was so early none of them were up; somebody directed me to the Old Jewry to Dr. Sharpe; I saw a young man there, who I suppose was the surgeon's servant; he desired me to sit down; the gentleman would be down soon; I believe the man and I sat down pretty near a quarter of an hour before the gentleman came down; a gentleman came down and ask'd what was the matter; I told him I throw'd a knife at a dog, and was afraid I had killed my wife; he said, I must take her to Bartholomew's hospital; I desired him to come with me, for the consequence was great; when we came to the house there was a great crowd of people; the crowd cried out, ' you have murdered your wife, take him away;' a constable and three or four more laid hold of me; I said you have no occasion to pull and hawl me, I don't want to run away; I made no resistance, then they took me away very quietly to Wood-street compter. I hope your lordship will enquire into my character; I have been watchman in Cheap Ward pretty near seven years.
 Mr. Barton again.
 Court. You hear he says his wife got up undress'd, and as she was stooping down with her left side towards him, he flung his knife at his dog, and stabbed her, and that she was a short woman; Was she?
 Barton. I think she was short.
 Q. Was it possible for the kn ife to miss the dog and strike her in that manner?
 Barton. I am afraid not. - I apprehend that as she was stooping the arm would cover the wound entirely.
 Q. When you saw her then, you apprehended the wound could not be given that way?
 Barton. I gave that as my opinion.
