HENRY ALFRED STACEY (Superintendent of Records in Bankruptoy). The
petition in this matter was presented on 5th May, 1887, by Messrs.
McMorland against the prisoners, trading as W. D. Gordon and Co-
the receiving order was made on 8th June-there is a report of the Official
Receiver of 8th June on the file that shows liabilities 3,0301. and
estimated assets 3831., cash at bankers at Rangoon 301., stock in trade at
Rangoon 1501., office furniture in London 201., book debts at Rangoon
2001., debts at Rangoon 2001.- there is a note by the Official Receiver
appended-the first meeting of creditors was on 29th July, 1887-the
prisoners were adjudicated bankrupts on 1st August-on the 8th Mr.
Stafford Northcote was appointed trustee on behalf of the creditors
public examination of the bankrupts was fixed for 5th August-there is
on the file a transcript of the shorthand notes of Messrs. Gordon's exami-
nation on that day-they filed & statement of their affairs, sworn by
William Gordon on behalf of the firm on 15th July this year.
GEORGE BLAGRAVE SNELL. I am one of the official shorthand writers
to the Bankruptcy Court-on 5th August this year I took notes of the
public examination of William Gordon, before Mr. Registrar Brougham
-I produce the original notes--the transcript on the file is a correct

FREDERICK WILLIAM JORDAN. I live in Sunningdale Place, Suther-
land Gardens, Maida Vale-I first made the acquaintance of William
Gordon at the latter end of December, 1886-he told me they had a
flourishing business at Rangoon-he showed me several large orders and
correspondence from Duncan Gordon to him in reference to the orders
and the business generally, and he asked me if I would like to go out to
Rangoon-I said “Yes," and it was arranged that I should go as
manager-I was to have about 1501. to 2001. a year; I saw him at his
office, Billiter House, Billiter Square-I went to Rangoon--a consign
ment of goods went in the same ship, consisting of tweed cloth, silk,
umbrellas, and some piece goods; I don't know the value, I had nothing
to do with the purchase, I think there were two or three casey-I arrived
at Rangoon on or about 7th April, 1887, having left England about 216
February-I found Duncan Gordon at Rangoon; the establishment there
was a kind of retail and wholesale business, it was one warehouse
divided into the two departments; there were jams, preserved fruits,
pickles, potted meat, lime juice, and some paints ; boots, millinery,
jewellery, and fancy things I could not estimate the value of the retail
goods, but after Duncan left I estimated about 3001. in all-about ten days
after I arrived there, there was a sale by auction by Duncan's directions,
on the premises-all the retail goods were sold and taken away; part of
the money was paid to Duncan; I think the sale realised about 1501.
roughly speaking; the wholesale stock was afterwards sold off by my
direction after Duncan left; he left on 7th May-he said the goods sent
out were not suitable to the market, and he was going to the manufs-
turers at Manchester and Liverpool to buy a better class of goods suitable
