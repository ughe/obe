Cross-examined by MR. FLOWER. Q. Are you sure it is yours? A. It
bears our initials, and is marked like the others that we have.
Andrew Down. I am in my sixteenth year, and am errand-boy to Mr.
Harrison, of Drury-lane. I live in Cross-court, Broad-court, Long-Acre-I
was sitting at the door of my master's house, and saw the prisoner Tregear
passing with a gentleman-he came across the way-I asked him who that
was across the way, if that was his father he said, no, it was a gentleman
that he had been to sell a spoon to, that Edwards gave him-Edwards came
up, and told Tregear to run away-he said no, he fshould go home to his
father, and take the silversmith to his father-I called Edwards back, and
asked where he had got the spoon--he said he had taken it from his
master-he then went away-Edwards came to me after that when I was
shatting up my master's shop, and told me Tregear was taken into custody.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. Had you known Tregear? A.
Yes—we used to live neighbours—when Edwards told Tregear to run
away, he said he would not-he asked Tregear if it was all right, and Tre-
gear said he did not know.
