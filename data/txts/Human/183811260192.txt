   COURT . Q.  Had your brother any communication with you, to autho rise the deposit of this document, when he was in treaty for the wine? A.  No, he had not—he solicited me to purchase on three occasions—I believe the property was advertised for sale, and not sold, at first—I saw that two pieces of freehold land were left out, which belonged to him, and that made me look after it—I informed Mr. Le Grice's clerk, and they were inserted afterwards—when he was able to receive the property he was to have it. GUILTY. Aged.— Confined Six Months.
   NEW COURT .— Tuesday, December  4 th , 1888.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 217. ANN NORMAN  , alias    Streeton, alias   Hathaway; MARY ANN GRAFTON, CHARLES FOSTER, THOMAS GUY  , alias    John Thomp son; and JAMES HOBBS, were indicted for conspiracy.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution . ROBERT NATHANIEL BURTON. I am a corn-factor, and have an office in Bush-lane. I have a cottage at Finchley, which I was desirous of letting—the prisoner Norman called on me at my office, and represented herself by the name of Ann Streeton—she said she had seen my cottage, and wished to take it—we very shortly agreed about the terms, which were 40 l . a-year—she said she had lived at the New-forest, Southampton, for some considerable time, and that she would give me a reference to her late land lord's brother, whom she had a house of there—that he was the best possible reference, as he knew how she had paid her rent, and she referred me to Mr. Hobbs, (the prisoner,) New Manor-street, Chelsea—we came to an agreement, and I wrote it out, stating the terms for three years, for which time she was to take the house—I wanted her to take it for five years, but she said it was impossible, she would not be tied to a house so long—she called for the agreement, and said she must have pos session immediately, the following day or two, as her furniture was on its way from Southampton, and she must have the house to go to at once, which I agreed to—on the second day, when she called to sign the agree ment, she asked if I had a wine-merchant, and I named my wine-mer chants, Messrs. Gill & Co. of Mark-lane—this was on Monday or Tues day, I think on the Friday following I received information, and went down to my cottage on the Sunday morning—I found there the prisoners Norman, Grafton, and Foster—I asked Norman how she could account for her conduct—she said it was all over now, and there was no help for it—I found them nearly destitute—there was no furniture in the house—they had used the kitchen drawers for tables and chairs, and there was a dirty old bed on the floor, which was all I could see—they were taken on the Wednesday, by the policeman, to Edge ware, and I think from there to prison.
