1222. ANN TURNER was indicted for stealing 18 yards of printed
calico, value 10s., the goods of Joseph Norberry; and that she had been be-
fore convicted of felony.
John Adams. I am shopman to Joseph Norbury, of Crawford-street,
Brunswick-square. On the 26th of March I missed a piece of calico from
outside the shop-it is my master's - I had seen it safe the same day.
GEORGE JOHN RESTIEAUX (police-constable E 49.) I produce the calico.
On the 26th of March I saw the prisoner with another girl, in Oxford-street
-the prisoner had this calico wrapped up in her apron-I asked where she
got it-she said, “What is that to you?" - I said, “You know me?"-she
said, "Yes"—she said it was a piece of print she had bought of a woman
-before the Magistrate she said she bought it of a woman in Oxford-street.
WILLIAM MILLERMAN ( police-constable B 95.) I produce a certificate of
the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from the Guildhall at Westmin-
ster-(read)--she is the person.
Guilty. Aged 21.- Transported for Seven Years.
1223. JAMES OSBORNE was indicted for stealing 8lbs. weight of pork,
value 2s., the goods of Henry Stephen Banks; and that he had been before
convicted of felony.
HENRY STEPHEN Banks, tripe-dresser, Church-street, Bethnal-green.
Abont ten o'clock on the 16th of March, I saw the prisoner come and steal
a pig's head from my window-I ran and stopped him-he dropped it.
Prisoner. I was walking down the street; he collared me, and said, "You
have got my pig's head;" he took me a little way, and found it in a bag,
Witness. He had put it into a bag; he was running, and dropped it at his
feet.
JOHN BRADDICK (police-constable R 64.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office-(read)-
the prisoner is the person.
Guilty.. Aged 16.-Transported for Seven Years.
1224. THOMAS PARKER was indicted for stealing 2 shillings, the mo-
nies of John Costello.
John COSTELLO, labourer, Robert-street, Mill-wall, Poplar. I was in bed
on the morning of the 18th of March, and heard money jingle on the floor-
I awoke, and found the prisoner by the side of my bed, with my jacket in
his band, and the money falling out-28. 60. fell out on the floor-he gave
me 6d.-he said, “This is my money"-I said, " It is not" - I got up, and
the two shillings were gone-more men slept in the room, but they were gone
-I had no time to send for an officer then, but gave him in charge at dinner
time the same day.
Cross-examined by Mr. HORRY. Q. He was your bed-fellow, was he not?
A. Yes; bis clothes were on the top of the bed, and mine at the side-I had
48. 6d. in my pocket at first, but after I had 28. 6d. in silver, and some
halfpence in my coat pocket-he did not shake my waistcoat pocket and
hold it up to me-he asked what money I had in my pocket-I said 48. 6d...
and then some money fell, and he said 2s. of it was his.
