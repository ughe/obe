Before Mr. Justice Erle.
1102. JOHN DOBBIN, feloniously forging and uttering a certain will
and testament of one William Musgrave Bowen, with intent to defraud
William Richards.--Other Counts, for feloniously aiding and assisting one
Peregrine Bowen, in the commission of the said forgery.
Messrs. Bookin, Robinson, and PABNELL, conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE WILLIAM WHITAKER. I am a clerk in the Prerogativo-court,
Doctors' Commons. I produce, from the registry there, a will of Willian
Musgrave Bowen, dated 2nd Sept., 1845-probate was granted to William
Richards, Esq.-I also produce a will of William Musgrave Bowen, dated
15th March, 1846; the witnesses to which are John Bell and Jobn Wood
also a letter signed" John Bell," and a printed notice, requiring John Bel
and John Wood to come forward and prove the will.
WILLIAM RICHARDS, Esq. I am a Magistrate of the county of Pembroke,
and reside at Tenby. I knew Lieutenant Peregrine Bowen-William Mus
grave Bowen was under my guardianship when he was last in England,
had been guardian to him fourteen years, ever since the death of his father-
Peregrine Bowen was his half-brother, and was older than he was they were
not on good terms--I never saw the prisoner before this transaction w
brought before the Lord Mayor-the whole of this will, dated 2nd Sept.
except the attestation, is in William Musgrave Bowen's writing--the other
will, of 15th March, 1846, is not in his writing, not any of it--he always
resided at my house when he was in England, and at my father's before me
I have known him all his life-I believe he left England, the last time, on
8th Sept., 1845, a few days after the date of the genuine will—I heard of his
death in the early part of June, 1846-I obtained probate of the will early
in Feb., 1847-I first heard of the other will, dated 15th March, 1846, about
the 15th or 17th of Feb., 1847, and went to see it at Mr. Gwynne's, the
solicitor for Peregrine Bowen, at Haverfordwest-he showed it me, after
some difficulty-it is the one which has been produced-I know Peregrine
Bowen's writing, and believe the whole of this will to be his, with the
exception of the attestation of John Bell—" John Wood" is in his writing-
Peregrine Bowen is since dead.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. The family of Peregrine Bowen
are still fighting the battle about this will, are they not? 4. I believe 80-
they have not withdrawn their opposition to it, to my knowledge-I knew
Peregrine Bowen upwards of thirty years—I did not think much of him
myself-I think he was a clever person-there is an attempted imitation of
the writing of William Musgrave Bowen in the postscript to the second will
-I have not seen Peregrine Bowen for eight or nine years.
MR. ROBINSON. Q. But you are well acquainted with his writing? 4.
Perfectly-I am not aware that his friends or relatives are withdrawing all
claim under that will -- it may be so.
