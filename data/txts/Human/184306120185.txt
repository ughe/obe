EDWARD HOLSWELL. I was working at this house—I remember Leach and Stokes coming before breakfast—after breakfast Leach said he was going to take some cement to another job—I lent him a hand with the cask into the truck—Stokes was there, and lent a hand—he went on the scaffold again to work—I did not see the truck go away. JOHN IRVIN. My father keeps the Tom  and Jerry.  About half-past two o'clock on this day I served Nicholas with three pots of ale—he sat on one side of the fire-place, and Leach and Stokes on the other—Nicholas drank, and passed it round to them and two or three other bricklayers—Nicholas was at work at our house all day, from about eight o'clock till half-past two, when the policeman took him. THOMAS SQUIRES( police-constable K  14.) Between four and five o'clock Leach was given to me for stealing a cask of cement—I said, "You hear what they say, you need not answer any questions if you don't think proper"—he said, "It is all right, it is at Nicholas's for three pots of beer"—I went to the beer-shop, and took Stokes—he said he knew nothing about it—I took them to Nicholas's house, and in the back yard I found this empty cask—I returned to the house again, and under tha stairs found a sack full of ce ment—I went to the beer-shop, and found Nicholas there—I said, "I want you on suspicion of receiving a cask of cement from Leach and Stokes; you need not say anything if you don't think proper"—he then stated that he took a bushel and a half of cement to his job at the beer-shop—I said, "Have you any cement at home?"—he said, "Yes, I have"—I said, "How much?"—he said, "I can't say"—I said, "Have you a bushel?"—he said, "Yes, two bushels, and I generally keep it under the stairs"—I said, "You have a cask full of cement under the stairs now"—he said, "No," if he had some one must have put it there—I did not find any cement at the beer-shop. NOT GUILTY.
 1942. JOHN BRADY and PATRICK HARRINGTON were indicited for stealing, on the   1st of June , 240 pence, the monies of John Newton ; and that Brady had been before convicted of felony.
   MR. WILDE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN NEWTON. I am a chandler, and live in Buckeridge-street, St. Giles's. On the 1st of June the prisoners came into my shop—Brady bought a pennyworth of cheese, and a halfpennyworth of bread—Harrington bought a farthing pipe—I had 2 l . worth of copper in 5 s . packets behind a counter—Harrington walked up behind the side of the counter, and went out with something—I came out of my room, and missed 1 l . worth of copper—Har rington walked out of the shop, and handed something to Brady, who was at the door—I then went to the door—Brady stopped in the passage, then turned round, and faced me—I called out that I knew them both, and it would do them no good—at the instant part of the money fell from Brade.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How long after they had been to buy the bread and cheese did you see Harrington in the shop? A.  Not above ten minutes—I was in the room behind the shop—I saw Brady standing at the door—I swear there was nothing between me and him to prevent my see ing him—I did not see his face after the money was given him—I did when he came into the shop, and at the door—I am sure of that. JOHN ROBERTS. I am a waiter. Between five and six o'clock in the morning of the 1st of June I saw the prisoners near the prosecutor's shop—about ten minutes after I heard some pence rattle on the pavement—the person who dropped them was three or four doors from the prosecutor's—I do not know who it was.
