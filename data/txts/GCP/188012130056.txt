139. FREDERICK BYFORD (35), Stealing a cart the property of
Robert Marr.
MR. POULTON Proseouted; MR. HORACE AVORY Defended.
JOHN RICHARDSON (Policeman P 188). In consequence of information
I received I took the prisoner on 19th November, and charged him with
stealing a cart the property of Mr. Marr-he was then in charge of the
cart-he said, "All right, I have got a receipt, I bought that cart"-he
produced the receipt at the station-he said, "Mr. Stead, the landlord of
the Union public-house, was present at the time I bought it at his house,
and so was James Moss, living at East Ham; they will both tell you 80
if you go to them "-I did so, and they both made oommunications to
me-"Frederick Byford” had been recently painted on the cart, on a tin
which was placed over the former name, which had been burnt out--this
is the receipt, “Spring cart bought of G. Jones, 151. 108. Bottled, G.
Jones"-he said that he did not know who Jones was or anything about
him.
Cross-examined. It is a builder's cart--the prisoner was driving it when
I stopped him-he went from the station to his house, and his wife
brought the receipt-I took the plate off the cart in the evening; Mr.
Marr was with me,the prisoner said, "Jones brought the cart to the
Union public house, and there I bought it"-he also said that he sold
his own cart to Jones for 5l.
EDWARD Brown. I am foreman to Mr. Marr, a builder, and live at 5,
Chichester Terrace, Kilburn- I do not know the prisoner-Mr. Marr had
& shed in Erlam Grove-in August his cart was not in use, and it had
been there about a fortnight-on Saturday, 7th August, about 8 a.m., I
found the shed gates broken down, the door forced open, and the cart
removed—I saw it again on 19th November at West Ham station; my
master's name had been burnt out and painted over, and a tin plate put
over the place with the prisoner's name on it-the front of the cart had
been out-I value it at 10l.; it was worth 25l. when new.
Cross-examined. If I had been offered 151. I should not have refused it
-the name had been burnt out with a hot iron, and a coat of paint over
the place.
Witness for the Defence.
JAMES Moss. I am a master carman-I have known the prisoner eight
or ten months; he bears the character of a hard-working man.
