SARAH ANN GREEN. I am Mrs. Elliott's daughter. About seven o'clock on Saturday evening, the 5th of April, I was in the parlour—there is a window between that and the shop—I saw the prisoner come in, and took notice enough of him to know him again—about two minutes after he was gone I looked at the sixpence in the basin and found it was bad—I put it apart from other money till next day, when the prisoner came again—I knew him to be the same person—I am confident of him—he bought some things of my mother, which came to sixpence, and put down a shilling—I took it up and found it was bad—I told him so, and I told him he had been there the day before—he said he had not, that he had taken the shilling of a young man for some work, and would fetch him—I told him to go and he left the shop—I went a short distance after him—he turned up Booth-street—I saw a constable and directed him to take him—I gave the constable the bad shilling and sixpence. MICHAEL MAHAN( police-constable H  79.) I took the prisoner, and found on him two good sixpences and 1 s . 3 1/2 d . in copper—I received this bad shilling and sixpence from Green. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. This sixpence and shilling are both counterfeit.
   Prisoner's Defence.  On Saturday evening I was not near the shop; I was selling books about seven o'clock. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Six Months .
