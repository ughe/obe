 1328.  THOMAS MABBS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , one tea-caddy, value 1s. 9d.; one reel and spindle, value 2s.; one mouse-trap. value 1s.; one brush, value 1s.; two pair of snuffers, value 3s., and two hammers, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Freer.
   CHARLES WILLIAMS. I am a constable of Hammersmith. On the 25th of August I searched the prisoner's apartments, and found the articles stated in the indictment there-the prisoner was not there. Some of them were found in his bed-room, and the remainder in a cupboard. I found two hammers and a reel in the cellar. Next morning I took him; he said he was in want.
 THOMAS FREER. I am an  ironmonger, and live at Hammersmith, the prisoner was my servant. On the 25th of August I had his apartments searched - He had left me the day before. The things which were found are mine.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALFORD. Q. Did not your apprentice run away - A. No, I turned him away.
   JAMES DIGBY. The prisoner lodged with me. He worked in the cellar, where the things were found.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought the reel as old iron-my master lent me the other things to use in my business. I used to work for him in my cellar.
   JAMES DIGBY re-examined. There was a fight between the prisoner and prosecutor about his not paying him his wages. He had got the prisoner's things in his place. I went to the prosecutor, and told him the prisoner was innocent. He said, "Never mind-if he gets over this I will have him another way."
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. Are you related to the prisoner - A. No. I was outside with the mob, when the prisoner and prosecutor were fighting. The prisoner's wife called out Murder! and two or three rushed into the house.
   THOMAS FREER re-examined. Q. Did you fight with the prisoner - A. No. There was a scuffle - He said he would murder me for charging him with the robbery. He made his escape out of window.
   EDWARD EDGSON. I am a constable. Mr. Freer sent for me in the evening. The prisoner had escaped out of the window. Mr. Freer told me to take him for making a disturbance in his house. I do not think he said he had stolen any thing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1329.  SAMUEL MACEY was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , one handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Battie , from his person.
   JOHN BATTIE. On the 22d of July I was at Sadler's Wells theatre, towards the end of the play. I lost my handkerchief, and 1l. 5s. out of my pocket. I heard some person had been taken up, went to the office, and found the prisoner in custody. The officer produced my handkerchief to me as soon as I missed it.
   WILLIAM WAINWRIGHT. I am an officer. I was in the pit of the theatre, it being a benefit night, and saw the prisoner, and several others in his company, feeling people's pockets. Some person intimated that he suspected the prisoner. I laid hold of him, searched him, and found a handkerchief concealed in his breeches, and one which the prosecutor claimed, in his hat. He said they were both his own, and that he bought the one found in his hat in Liquorpond-street of a boy for 2s., just before he came to the theatre that evening. I put him in the watchhouse. I saw the prosecutor that evening, and showed him both the handkerchiefs and my own-he selected his own from them.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up in the pit.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Life.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1330.  ELIZA MULLENDER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of June , two hats, value 8s.; one shawl, value 2s.; one petticoat, value 6d.; one shirt, value 2s.; one frock, value 18d., and one piece of patch work, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Samuel Atkins.
   MARY ATKINS. I am the wife of Samuel Atkins, who lives in Willow-walk, the prisoner used to bring her work and sit with me. On the 15th of June she came to my house to take care of my children while I was in Clerkenwell prison for debt. I came home and missed the articles stated in the indictment,
   LAUNCELOT WILD. I am servant to Mr. Walker, who is a pawnbroker. On the 15th of June the prisoner pledged a piece of patchwork with me for 18d. - she said she pledged it for Atkins.
   GEORGE WILLIAM KNIGHT DURHAM. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Hollywell-lane. I have two hats and a shirt pledged with me in the name of Atkins.
 JOHN FORRESTER. I took the prisoner in charge. The prosecutrix gave me the duplicates.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was taking care of her house. There was no money to buy her children any victuals. Her husband told me to pledge them to feed the children. She was not in prison for debt.
 MARY ATKINS re-examined. I was in prison for an assault.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
