Before he struck the blow I did not hear him say any thing to her—I said before the Coroner, "He got in front of her, and said, 'I'll do for you,' and then struck her in the breast"—I cannot say if it was the same constable at the police-station door that the prisoner was showing his arm to in Golden Lane—he had some bandages round his arm,—while he was showing his arm, the woman crossed over to the police Station—I also crossed over, and the prisoner stood listening, but did not say anything—the policeman refused to let her in, saying she had done nothing wrong—it was all in a minute or two after we commenced going in opposite directions that the prisoner rejoined us—the woman asked me to go home and sleep with her, saying he would not touch her if anybody was with her—I had been sleeping in a common lodging-house, and I gave the 6 d . I am surgeon to the G division of police—on the morning of 13th September, about one, I was called to the Old Street station, and found a woman lying on one of the benches in a state of unconsciousness from which she never recovered—she died in about ten minutes—the front of her clothing was saturated with blood—there was a punctured wound about five-eighths of an inch in length over the left breast, from which the blood was flowing—at that time I was only able to trace it about an inch downwards, but I afterwards made post-mortem  examination—the knife ( produced ) corresponds with the wound—it had penetrated the bony substance of the third rib, and gone into the left ventricle of the heart—considerable violence must have been used—she died from the effects of that wound.
   Cross-examined.  There was extensive adhesion of the left lung, the result of which was to throw the heart considerably forward on the wall of the chest—with the heart in that abnormal condition it would require a wound of lesser depth and force to penetrate it than under ordinary circumstances—the wound went into the cavity of the heart.
