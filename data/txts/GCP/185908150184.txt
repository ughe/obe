WILLIAM STEWART. I am surgeon to the police force at Woolwich-I
accompanied the policeman, 63, to the dead-house at Woolwich on the
morning of the 14th, the next morning-my assistant saw him before that
I examined the body externally; the only external mark of violence was on
the left side of the neck, where there was a bruise, and the skin slightly
raised; it was below the ear, and behind the corner of the lower jaw-blood
was rushing from the nostrils, but no other external violence-I began my
internal examination by examining the head-I found all the vessels of the
brain congealed with blood, and on the surface were large clots in two
or three different places; and on opening the brain, there was a large clot
on the left side-on removing the brain from the skull, there was also
& very large clot of blood pressing on the spinal marrow-the result that I
drew was that he died from rupture of the brain, from pressure on the
brain, and apoplexy-it might be the result of a blow or a fall on the
ground--the vessels would rupture, the blood would be effused, and death
would ensue from pressure on the brain-if a person had been drinking,
and had received a blow in the stomach, and had fallen down on the
ground, it would be very likely to bring death.
COURT. Q. From the pressure of this blood on the brain, would it find its
way through the nose and mouth ? A. No-probably some of the vessels in
the upper part of the nostril had received a blow-I don't think a blow on
the ear would occasion the death of a man.
EDWARD FEATHERSTONE (Policeman, R 63). I was on duty in High-street,
on Tuesday night, 12th July, about half-past 12—in consequence of some-
thing I heard, I went to the Shamrock beershop, and there I saw the
deceased lying on the ground, and a number of people standing round-after
that, I went in pursuit of the prisoner, and found him in a small cottage-he
was lying outside his bed with his clothes on, apparently asleep, I took him
in charge, and told him that he had assaulted a man in High-street, and
kicked him-he had been drinking.
