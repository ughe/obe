Cross-examined. Q. The deceased was a larger man than the prisoner?
A. He was about as stout, but rather taller-he was no friend of mine,

HENRY Baylis. I live in Church-street, Mile-end New Town. I was
at the Rabbits on the 23rd of April, I did not see the prisoner come in-
he and Bates were there when I got there-I called for a pint of beer, and
sat down at the end of the table--the prisoner and the deceased sat along
side of each other-something arose about Ireland - I heard Bates say,
"Oh, I know you never were in Ireland in your life"-he said, "Yes, I have
been, many a time"-Bates said, "I would not mind laying you a sovereign
you never were in Ireland"- the prisoner pulled out two or three sovereigns,
and threw them on the table-I said “Nonsense, what are you throwing your
sovereigns about in that manner for? you will lose them"-I do not know that
the prisoner was either sober or tipsy, he could walk about very well-I think
he had been drinking-he had been riding-be put the sovereigns into his
pocket-Sacker came and said to me, “Do you know that man ?” (meaning
Bates)- I said "Yes, I do, he rode down along with me, and he rode back
along with me"-Sacker said, "I wish you would take him away, or he
will knock his head through the window"-before I could out with the
words, the prisoner threw the beer in his face--Bates rose from his seat,
and was rubbing his face--the prisoner hit him two tremendous blows, and
sent him on to the table-a struggle then took place which broke the
table--they both fell down right against the carts-- Bates was undermost
- they both got up together, and rushed at one another, and I could not
see anything more, the people all surrounding them-I saw no blow struck
by Bates, they were both struggling together over the table--I cannot say
whether there was any blow or not--I saw no blow struck before the
beer was thrown in his face.
Cross-examined. Q. You were a friend of Bates ? A. Not a particular
friend-I came with him there-I did not bear Sacker keep telling Bates
to be quiet, and not to tease the prisoner-I never said anything to the
prisoner- I did not hear Bates say anything to the prisoner about the
corduroy jacket that he had on, or about his not being a respectable man
-I think the prisoner was rather fresh.
WILLIAM HENRY SACKER. I am neither a friend of the prisoner nor the
deceased-I have known the prisoner about six years- I live at 29, Mile-
end road-on the 23rd of April I was in the yard of the Rabbits-the pri-

call the prisoner an Irishman-the prisoner said he knew that as well as he
did-Bates said it was a lie-the prisoner made no remark-I told him to
take no notice of it - I went to the deceased, and told him he had better
not aggravate the man's temper-I saw the prisoner was out of temper with
