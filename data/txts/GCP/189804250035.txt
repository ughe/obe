CHARLES BURRIDGE (Police Inspector, X). On March 22nd I was in
charge of the station at 3.30, when the prisoner was brought in—I told
him he was charged with assaulting, and robbing Daynton of 2s. 91d at
9.30 p.m. on 21st—the prosecutor had told me I said, “You will be
further charged with stabbing Edward Daynton "-he said, "I know
that; that is the knife you have got in your hand; it has got a hole
through the centre."
Evidence for the Defence.
Thomas Pepper. On March 22nd, about 12.35 a.m., I was with
Rogers, and two friends at the Red Lion public-house in the High Road,
Kilburn-upon leaving, and about 150 yards down the road, Rogers went
to make water-I next saw the prosecutor and Rogers having a few
words—I heard the prosecutor say to Rogers, "If you are a man, take
off your coat, and I will fight you "-Rogers took off his coat, and they
had a scramble--that passed over and Rogers went away as one of our
friends was going in home Daynton came up and went for Rogers, who
pushed him away—I did not see any stabbing nor the prosecutor fall to
the ground—I know he was insensible and taken to the hospital-I did
not speak to a policeman-I was too much upset, and had not the courage
- did not see anybody set on with a belt-I was called before the
Magistrate -I am a carman-the prisoner is a French polisher.
Cross-examined. I went into the Red Lion at 7.15 and left at 12.30-
I was smoking and in conversation—I was about six yards from the
prosecutor and the prisoner when they met-J could see everything-I
did not see the prisoner rush on the prosecutor with a knife.
ERNEST BLANCHARD (re-examined) I do not recognise Pepper-I did
not take off my belt-I do not wear a belt-I was with two others and
there were two more near who had nothing to do with us.
Not Guilty.
