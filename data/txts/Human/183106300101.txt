   JOSEPH FOULKES. I am a turnkey of the Fleet, and have been so nearly twenty years. Rotton was a prisoner there on the 15th of April, and on that day he prouduced a parchment to me, and said it was his discharge - lvey was in his company: they came to the gate together - I just glanced over it, and thought it a very singular kind of a document; I said I thought somebody had been playing a joke with him, that it appeared more like of a writ - they went away, and both came back in about half an hour, with the same parchment, and said it was a bona side discharge; I then took it to the office, and delivered it to Mr. Wiliam Brown, the deputy-warden; neither of them went with me - Mr. Brown said he would examine the book, and give me an answer in a few minutes - he then gave orders for Mr. Rotton and his friend, which was lvey, to be called down into the office - they came out, and lvey said, "There must be a stamp on this;" (meaning a seal) - he went away, as if for that purpose; he returned in about a quarter of an hour - said he had been to the Prothonatary's office, and paid 2s. 8d. for seal; Mr. William Brown went out with the supposed writ to make inquiry - Rotton and lvey remained; Mr. William Brown came back with a gentlemam from the Prothonotary's office - he ordered Rotton and Ivey to be called down, if lvey was still in the house - they both came into the office; I left them there - Rotton was detained; Mr. William Brown said, "lvey, I shall give my officers orders to lock you out," and he was locked out.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you acquainted with lvey before? A. I had seen him several times, as his mother and father worked for several people in the house -Moody was a messenger; I do not remember seeing him going in and out that day - they brought me the parchment three times; I did not look at it minutely the first time - Rotton said, "Here take it, and let me out directly;" he seemed in a great hurry - I knew they brought the same document the next time, by a blot at the end, which seemed something like the Judge's seal; I never quarrelled with lvey or his father - I never had the slightest quarrel with lvey; his father came to me very hastily on the Sunday after this happened.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Is this parchment the supersedas which they brought? A. Yes. that is the blot seal - I took particular notice of this, seeing such a seal; I said,"Why, somebody has been playing a joke with you;" I gave it to Mr.  William Brown- he said there was not a Prothonotary's seal to it.
 MR. EARL. Q. All you know it by is this stamp? A. Yes - lvey told me when he came out of the office, that Mr. Brown said it wanted the Prothonotary's seal, and he went out to get it; when he returned he did not say he had been with Bailey or the attorney - he said he had paid 2s. 8d. at the Prothonotary's office for the stamp; he said nothing about his attorney.
 MR.  WILLIAM BROWN. I am deputy-warden of the Fleet. On the 15th of April this parchment was brought to me by Foulkes, the turnkey - I declined taking it on account of its not having the official seal, and not being property drawn up; it was brought to me by Ivey in about half an hour, with an impression on the back, which it had not before - I understood him that he had got it sealed at the Prothonotary's office; I suspected it was not a regular document, and took it to the Prothonotary's office - Mr. Sherwood came back with me, and Ivey said a clerk had got it sealed at the office in the Temple, and paid 2s. 8d. for it; Rotton was present, he demanded to be discharged, and offered his fees - he was detained.
 Cross-examined. Q. How do you know that is the parchment - have you had it ever since? A. I have not - it is the document originally produced, by the seal and appearance altogether; I can swear to it by the drawing up and the impression on the back - it has been part of the time in my custody; I gave it to Mr. Maule, the solicitor of the Treasury, a few days after - I have seen it several times since; Ivey did not say whose clerk had got it sealed - I knew nothing about Bailey at that time; he was afterwards taken into custody by Ivey, and brought before a Magistrate - that was the first time I heard of him; I then heard he was the clerk who got it sealed - I think Ivey stated, in Bailey's presence, before the Magistrate, that Bailey had got it sealed, and he paid him the money for it- I believe Rotton was supersedable.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When you delivered the parchment to Mr. Maule, was Bailey present? A. I believe he was - I do not think, upon recollection, that Bailey was supercedable, as he had given notice under the Insolvent Act - he was bailable.
 COURT. Q. Was any application made by either of the defendants for him to be admitted to bail? A. No.
 MR. SHERWOOD. I am in the Prothonotary's office. This writ has nothing to do with the Court of Common Pleas one of the seals on it are those of our office; it is not under the seal of the Common Pleas, which it purports to be- the seal here is feigned altogether; the writ is informal, and the seals counterfeit: Sir N. C. Tyndall, whose seal it purports to be under, is a Judge of the Common Pleas, and not of the Exchequer - the defendant was detained under process from the Exchequer.

   JANE SHARP. My husband is a prisoner in the Fleet -Rotton lived in our room in April last. On the 14th of April Ivey was sent for, by Moody, the messenger - he came between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, on the 15th, and Rotton told him he was going to be bailed out, that he had employed Moody, the messenger, about it, with some solicitor outside, who was to do it - he asked Ivey to lend him a little money, as he was very short, and wanted money to pay his fees as he went out; he did not give him any then - Moody came between eleven and twelve o'clock, brought a piece of parchment, and said,"I have brought your discharge, Mr. Rotton;" he said,"Have you?"
