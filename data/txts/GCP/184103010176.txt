MARY JOHNSON. I live with my aunt, Mrs. Fox-I retired to bed
about twelve o'clock-I was the first that came down the next morning-
the shop was then broken open, and the property gone-I bad not heard
any one in the night.
SUSANNAH Fisu. I recollect the prisoner coming to me on the Sunday
morning, between nine and half-past nine o'clock; he tendered me some
farthings- I thought they were bad, he said they were good-he gave me
eight farthings, and asked me to give him 2d. for them, which I did-1
marked one of the farthings-this is it.
MARY JOHNSON re-examined. I missed some farthings from the till, I
cannot say how many-I know two of these farthings produced by Mrs.
Fish-I offered them in the shop on the Friday, and Saturday they were
refused as being bad.
George FORTUNE (police-sergeant K 42.) I traced the footsteps to
the back of Mrs. Fox's premises - I had suspicion of the prisoner, and
compared his shoes with the footsteps—they corresponded exactly, nail to
nail.
JAMES WHITE (police-sergeant K 13.) I compared the prisoner's shoes
with the marks with George Fortune-the property was found concealed in
a dunghill; it was in a putrid state, and could not be produced--the pro-
secutrix saw it.

980. PATRICK MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of
January, 1 ring, value 38.; 1 star, value 31.; 1 sovereign, 1 half-sovereign,
1 crown, 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence, the property of Charlotte
M'Lean: and JOHN MILES, alias Sweeney, for feloniously receiving !
star, part of the said goods, well knowing it to have been stolen ; against
the Statute, &c.-2nd Count, for receiving foz. weight of silver.
LADY CHARLOTTE M'LEAN. I am widow of General Sir Joseph
M'Lean ; I at present live in Wood-street, Woolwich. On the 24th of
December I resided at Woolwich Common-my house was destroyed by
fire that night, and the furniture that was saved was moved to a stable in
the Royal Artillery barracks at Woolwich-about a fortnight after the fire
I saw a star of the Hanoverian order of Knighthood at the Commandant's
house-it was in a chest of my drawers--I had possession of the keys of the
drawers--some of the furniture was afterwards removed from the stable to
Wood-street--the military assisted in moving it-after it had been so
removed I examined the drawer in which the star had previously been, and
it was missing-there were marks of the lock being broken open, picked,
as if the drawer had been forced-I also missed a gold ring which was
cracked, and a purse with some gold and silver in it--I afterwards saw some
pieces of silver at the Commandant's house at Woolwich, which decidedly
belonged to the Hanoverian Order, but there was not the whole of it-
was mutilated.
