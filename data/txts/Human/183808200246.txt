   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  How came you to have the prisoner appre hended? A.  I did not have him apprehended—the first information I had of his being charged was from some other person—I did not know there was a robbery committed—I do not know Stemp—he was taken as a prisoner—he was locked up a week, and then discharged, as I was in formed, for receiving seven quarters of wheat. JOSEPH DANIEL IVES. I am a Thames-police surveyor. About half-past six o'clock on the morning of the 23rd of July I was on duty in the river—I saw a barge at Bellmont-wharf, Nine Elms—some coal-heaven were carrying something up in sacks—I landed at the railroad, and went on the wharf adjoining Messrs. Frances and White's wharf—I could see into the bottom of the barge—I went into a granary, and found some wheat I had been looking for—I then went into the coal-heaver's lobby, and found this tarpaulin, which the prosecutor has identified—it has his same in full length upon it—it was in the back of the lobby, with some sacks in front of it—you could not see it without going into the lobby—I was at the Bellmont wharf between two and three o'clock that same afternoon, and saw the prisoner come on the wharf—he walked up towards me—I asked him if he was the lighterman that brought up the wheat in the morning—he said he was—that he brought it for a Mr. Stemp, and he was employed by a gen tleman he did not know, whom he met at Shad Thames—he asked him if he would take a barge with seven quarters of wheat to Bellmont-wharf, and get it landed for Mr. Stemp, and meet him on the day after at the India-house, and he would settle with him—I asked him what barge he brought it from—he said the Thomas, which was the barge I had seen—he said he did not know to whom she belonged—I asked him whether he had any covering to the wheat—he said "No"—I asked him how he came by Mr. Dudin's tarpaulin—he said he had forgotten that, he did not know it was Mr. Dudin's, but he knew him very well—Mr. Dudin's name was upon it—I asked him whether he could read—he said "Yes," but he did not look outside the barge to see the name—I told him Mr. Dudin's tarpaulin was well marked, and he made no answer—I took him to the India-house and no one was there—he said the tarpaulin was with the wheat-in leaving the India-house, I asked him what he thought of his friend—he said he was led into the trap, and was too old to be caught.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not the wharfinger examined? A.  The stable-keeper was who had taken charge of the wharf—the wharfinger was there-his name is Norton—he was asked whether Stemp was in the habit of having consignments to him on that wharf, and he said he was.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did the prisoner say any thing to Stemp, with reference to the property being consigned to him? A.  Yes; when he said he brought it for Mr. Stemp, Mr. Stemp was sitting there, and I said, "Is that Mr. Stemp?"—he said, "Yes," he turned round and said, "How do you know me, I gave you no orders?" JOHN DUNGATE. I reside in South Lambeth. I was employed at the wharf, to get some corn out of the barge. NOT GUILTY.
