CHARLES LEGGATT BARBER. I am shorthand-writer to the London
Court of Bankruptey-I have before me the Bankruptcy proceedings in
the case of Urban Napoleon Stanger—the date of the petition is 23rd
May, and the adjudication the 25th-the prisoner was sworn and ex.
amined in the usual way on the 25th-I produce , transcript of my short-
hand notes of his examination in support of the petition, and also on the
28th in support of the proof of delt-it is correct-four creditors only
proved debts, the total debts being 1,8607. 188. 3d., and that proved by
the prisoner being 1,5731. 98. 3d. I don't think any statement of affairs
was filed, the bankrupt not appearing—the bankruptcy was annulled by
a decision of the Lords Justices on 17th November, on the ground that
there was no proof that the bankrupt was alive.
Cross-examined. The other creditors are one whose name I cannot
read, 111. 168. 6d.; Mumford, 961. 58.; and Maule, 1791. 78. 6d.
GEORGE PHILIP LENTZ. I am a baker, of Whitecross Street-I knew
Mr. Stanger--I last saw him on Saturday, I believe, 13th November last
year-I was with him and the prisoner in a public-house-I afterwards
went with Stanger to his house ; I saw him go in, and bade him good.
byo and came away, and I have never seen him since I can't exactly
remember whether I went away from the house with the prisoner, or
whether he stopped there-we parted near there and I went away.
Cross-examined. I am not able to say that the prisoner did not go away
with me-we were all on friendly terms that night.
