Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How long bave you known the
prisoner! 4. Two years--I have known his parents nearly seven-I sold
a clock to them two years ago he came to my house occasionally I let
him have the clocks for the people, as I thought it was all right-I knew
he lived with his father-I have seen bim there many times I have not
dined or drank tea there I have been in the habit of doing business with
his father for seven years I never kept my money-drawer locked-I ex-

ever he liked, and so did Rachael Davies, the landlady-there are no ser-
vants—I make my own bed—I have two rooms—I keep my parlour-door
locked, and put the key in the landlady's kitchen-I had seen the prisoner
on the Thursday morning-he never came to my house before Christmas
eve, and three times after-his father has had two clocks and a watch since
I have known bim.
JOSEPH HORMIRE. I am a clock-maker, and live with Ferdererwe
occupy the same room to work in, and pay the rent of the front-parlour
between us--I have a bed-room up stairs--I was there the second time the
prisoner came, when he asked for a Christmas-box, and heard him say he
should like the money very much on the Thursday I met him at the end
of St. Andrew's-row, about half-past four o'clock-he said, "How do you
do !"-I said, “Very well; how is it you have been over bere?" he said,
"I want to see Mr. Ferderer very particularly, about them clocks he sold"
I said, “You have been in our place" he said, "Yes, Mr. Ferderer is
not at home, I will go back with you and wait till he comes home" - the
lodger up stairs let us in I went in, fetched the key from the kitchen,
and let him into the workshop-I found the door locked—I lighted the fire,
and the prisoner sat down close to the prosecutor's drawer-I knew there
was money there I put the kettle on the fire and began working-a
woman in the next room called out to me, “I have got a good fire, if you
want to put your kettle on"-80 I went into the other room and put it on
-I then went up stairs, leaving the prisoner in the work-room-I came
down in a few minutes, and found the prisoner at the street-door, ready to
go out--I said, “ Are you not going to stop till Mr. Ferderer comes home?"
-he said, “No, I had rather go to a public-house and have a pint of beer
and smoke a pipe, and be back in half an hour" I said, " If you want
some beer I will get you some, you can smoke a pipe here, or you can have
tea with me" -he said he had rather go to a public-house, and be back in

went out-it had the usual appearance of having money in it--when I
