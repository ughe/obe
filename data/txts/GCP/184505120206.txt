with the oats? A. About eleven o'clock was a little way from home

good many persons about.

John FIELDER. I am a labourer and live in Gray's-inn-place, Holborn
-I was a stranger to Rendall, but I overtook him driving a wagon near
the Old Ship at Richmond on the 30th of April-he gave me a ride in his
wagon, and after I had been in a few minutes he asked me to drive the
horses and hold the reins-he went to sleep on the wagon-I moved the
tarpaulin and saw some hay under it-I went on to the Plough at Wands-
worth-we got out there to have a pot of beer-we got there about four
o'clock, and remained about a quarter of an hour-when I came out of the
house I saw Rendall talking to Woodland who is the under ostler there-
they were standing under a shelter outside the house where persons sit
down-I went to the wagon again and found the tarpaulin was lying down
flat, and what it had covered was gone-I went into the tap-room again
and soon after Daley, the officer, came in-Rendall was sitting alongside
of me--the officer asked for Barclay and Perkins's drayman-Rendall said
nothing, but the landlord came in and pointed him out-the officer asked
Rendall if he had any hay on his wagon-he said he had none-the officer
said he must go out and see about it.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. You were sitting in the wagon
driving? A. Yes, when I got up into the wagon Rendall went to sleep
- I saw some hay when I happened to lift the tarpaulin-I remained in
my seat-I could see into the road from the tap-room.
HENRY ORGER. I am a tallow-chandler and live at Putney. On the
30th of April, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I was going towards the
Plough public-house, at Wandsworth-I saw Barclay and Perkins's wagon
standing before the Plough-I saw Woodland remove the tarpaulin and
take from the wagon something bulky, which appeared to be hay-he took
it into the stable-I walked on till I was opposite the wagon-I saw him
return and take a second truss of hay from the wagon-he placed them
one on the other in the first stall in the stable, and he locked the stable
door--the horses were not in the stable, they were in the wagon-I took
the number of the wagon, and I met Daley, the officer-I then accompanied
him to the Plough and pointed out Woodland as the person who had re-
