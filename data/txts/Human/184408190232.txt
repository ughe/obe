ELIZABETH PHIPPS. I am the wife of William Sutton Phipps. I thought the prisoner was an unusual size, and told her I thought she appeared in the family way—she denied it, and said she thought she was dropsical-—on the morning of the 3rd of June my husband called me—I went into the kitchen, and saw the prisoner—she said she had got the spasms very bad—she went home—I took the sheets off her bed to give to the laundress, and from the appearance of them I thought she had given birth to a child—something I found in the water-closet confirmed my opinion. CHARLOTTE LAWLESS. I am the prisoner's aunt. On the morning of the 3rd of June she was brought to my house in a cab  —I had before that observed her person to be rather stout, but I thought it was dropsy—she said she was very unwell, and Mrs. Phipps had sent her home for a few days—I went to Mrs. Phipps, and when I got home I accused her of having given birth to a child—she said she had, and had buried it in the garden—she told me who was the father. GEORGE EARL( police-sergeant M  2.) I went to Mr. Phipps's, and turned up the mould in the garden—I found the body of a new-born child—it had not anything on it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was there much earth on it? A.  No, not above an inch and a half or two inches. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Strongly recommended to mercy by the Jury.  — Judgment Respited.

 2216. FRANCIS THOMAS SCOTT was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying John Samuel Read .
   MR. O'BRIEN conducted the Prosecution , JOHN KELLY. On the 15th of July I was in the Blackfriars-road—I saw the prisoner riding down the New-cut—I was about crossing over to the other side—I cried out, seeing a man crossing just at the moment that the prisoner was up to him—I also heard the prisoner cry out after me—he was riding at a very rapid rate indeed, galloping and urging on the horse, beating it on the side of its flank with his hand—I saw nothing in his hand—he had a spur on—he was quite near to the man when I hallooed out—the man was crossing from Rowland Hill's chapel—there was plenty of time for the prisoner to pull up—there was no traffic of carts at the time—he could have seen the man—the deceased had got about half-way across the road—I ran as fast as I could, not knowing which way he would turn, and by the time I got across he was up opposite me, or very near me—I saw him knocked down by the horse.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was there a lad with the old man? A.  Yes—I did not see whether he ran away, I was rather confused at the time. SUSANNAH MARY KNOX.
