747. WILLIAM BEER was indicted for feloniously receiving, of an evil-
disposed person, on the 14th of Jan., 30lbs. weight of lead, value 5s., the goods
of Francis Bursell,
We
Francis BURSELL. I am a plumber and painter, in Cleveland-street, Fitz-
roy-square. On Saturday, the 24th of Jan., I lost some lead from the roof of
the workshop and back parlour-on the following Wednesday, in consequence
of information, I went to the prisoner's house, in Warren-street, St. Paneras,
in the neighbourhood where I reside-I saw the prisoner-I asked him if he
had bought any lead-he said he had- I asked if he could give it to me-he
said he could not, as it was melted down and sold-be said he had bought it
of George Henry Wallington, whom I took with me.
Cross examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. You received some communica-
tion from Wallington ? A. Yes-- he told me he had stolen it—I told him, if
he would tell the truth with respect to it, I would deal mildly with bim-I had
lost a tremendous lot of things-the prisoner told me be had bought some lead
of that boy, and it was melted and gone-he concealed nothing from me
-- he behaved with the greatest possible openness- I did not give him into
custody-he walked with me to the police office, he was admitted to bail, and
surrendered - I have had a good many communications with Wallington since,
and I think he has told a good many untruths.
Nor GUILTY.
(There were two other indictments against the prisoner, on which no evi-
dence was offered.)
