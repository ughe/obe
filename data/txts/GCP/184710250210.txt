John Dudin. I am a master lighterman, and live in Gainsford-street.
Horseleydown. On the 4th of Oct. the prisoner was in my service

the rest in the John barge-I gave no directions as to the use of a skiff-the

Whiting's wbarf-that was not done-Adcock gave me information-after-
wards saw the barge inside the tier-I found the skiff at Prince's stairs-
nothing was in it then-it was in my charge, so that the prisoner could get it
before twelve o'clock-I crossed the water-I saw a boy who I employed to load
the barge, go ashore-the oats were to be delivered to Edward Raper--they
were delivered, but not the five quarters--I have been charged for this deli.
ciency, and had to pay for it-I went to the Thames-police-office, and went
with an officer to find the prisoner-I found him in the George public-house
-I asked him what had become of the five quarters-- he said he knew no-
thing about them.
Cross-examined. Q. Where was the Tennant lying? A. At Prince's
stairs-about half a mile from Whiting's-I only employed the prisoner to
unload this corn-there was a boy employed to turn the meter--the prisoner
had not been long in my employ-he was merely employed by the day-
that is the general practice-Wheeler is occasionally employed by persons
there-that is the way he gets his living - I gave the order the night before
- I have never recovered the five quarters, nor found what was done with
them, nor found Wheeler.

JOHN STRIDE. I am an engineer, and live in Victory-street, New Town,
Deptford. I have known the prisoner about nine years -I was with him in
Oct., abont nine years ago, at the parish Church of St. George's, Bloomsbury,
when he was married to Mary Woolridge-he was then about thirty or
thirty-one years of age--I was one of the witnesses to the marriage--I after-
wards saw them living together as man and wife for three or four years--she
is now alive-I saw her five minutes ago-she is about the same age or rather
older than the prisoner-I had not been there before the marriage-none of
the other witnesses are here-my wife was one I had known the female
rather longer than the prisoner--the prisoner was a carpenter-1 do not kuow
the name of the clergyman who performed the ceremony-Mr. Catty was the
clerk--he was present.
Cross-examined by Mr. WOLLETT. Q. Do you know on what terms the
prisoner lived with his wife ? A. In my opinion he lived on very good terms
- I do not know of any separation-I have heard that it is three or four
years ago since he left her-I then saw them living together very happily, and
comfortably-there did not appear much disparity in their ages - I have
heard of the prisoner allowing her so much a week.
