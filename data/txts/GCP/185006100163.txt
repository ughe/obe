JOHN CROUCH. I am clerk to a gentleman, and live at 9. Bond-street.
Vauxhall. On Wednesday, 17th April, I was at the bar of the Southampton
Arms, between eleven and twelve o'clock, and saw the prisoner come in-1
knew him before-I left as near twelve o'clock as possible, and I believe I
left him there.
JAMES SKUDDER. I am potman to Mr. Hippisley. On the night of
17th April I shut up the shutters, about twenty-five minutes to twelve o'clock
-there was then only one person in the room, and that was a porter-I saw
the prisoner sitting outside the front-door on a seat-I am sure it was him-I
spoke to him-I had seen him in the tap-room, between eleven and twelve-
I bolted the back-door before I went to bed, about five minutes past twe e
-my mistress and the servant were the last persons up--they are not here-
a gentleman who was in the tap-room that night said that he expected some
Alderney cows up next morning, and the prisoner was there at the time.
Ann SIMPSON. I am single, and live at 2, Vale-place, Duke-street, West-
minster-road. On the morning of 18th April, I met the prisoner at Hart's
public-house, in Mount-street--we had something to drink, and he went
home with me he gave me half-a-erown and sixpence, and he also gave me
a cigar or a cheroot-he had given one to some females at the public-house-
he paid 1s. 114d. for his breakfast—he had about half-a-dozen cigars in bis
hand-I saw no papers of tobacco.
HENRY HART. I am barman to my father, who keeps the New Crown
and Cushion, in Westminster-road. On 18th April, between four and five in
the morning, the prisoner came and had half-a-pint of gin, which I believe he
paid for in silver-he then asked if I would oblige him by giving him five
shillings for a five shilling packet of balfpence, but I said I did not want
them-he then broke open a package of halfpence, and spent three or four
shillings' worth treating a lot of persons at the bar, and he smoked some
cheroots or cigars which he took out of his pocket-he left the house a little
after six with Simpson-I did not hear of his swallowing any tobacco
Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing of it.
