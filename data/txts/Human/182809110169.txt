1904. ANN BURFORD was indicted for stealing, on the the 8th of August , 1 tea-caddy, value 8s., the goods of James Ironside.
   THOMAS IRONSIDE. I am the son of John Ironside, a carpenter, who lives on Saffron-hill. I went into the back room on the 8th of August; I heard a footstep in the front parlour - I came out and saw the prisoner going out; I followed and overtook her just outside the door - she threw this caddy from her apron; a person picked it up, and gave it to me - the officer took her.
   JOHN IRONSIDE. This is my property.
   JOHN DENNIS. I am an officer and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went in for one penny worth of milk; I stood by the door, and while I was waiting, one of the cows turned round with great fury - I ran back with fright, and knocked this down against the door; I never had it in my apron nor near it - had I thrown it down, it would have broken.
     GUILTY. Aged 49. Confined Four Months.
 1905.  JOHN BEDFORD was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of July , 1 wooden bowl. value 3d.; 4 half-crowns, 9 shillings, and 15 sixpences, the property of James Smith.
   JAMES GEORGE SMITH. I am the son of James Smith, a porkman, who lives in Bath-street, St. Luke's. I saw the prisoner come to the shop on the 4th of July between three and four o'clock in the afternoon; I was in the back yard and saw him pass round the counter with the bowl of silver in his hand - this is it; it had been in the till.
 Prisoner. He picked it up off the ground, and he took and hit me; I hit the bowl, and it fell on the counter, and he gave it to his mother. Witness. I took it from his hand.
   JOHN COLSON. I took the prisoner and found these four half-crowns, nine shillings, and fifteen sixpences; I said, "Why are you such a naughty boy?" and he said a man gave him 1 1/2d. to go into the shop, and take what he liked.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.
 1906.  WILLIAM ABBOT was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 1 coat, value 5s.; 1 waistcoat, value 2s.; and 1 pair of trousers, value 2s.; the goods of Richard Marshall.
 RICHARD MARSHALL. I am a  blacksmith, and live in Frederick-street. I lent my coat, waistcoat, and trousers, to Edward Potter, on the 21st of July; I was to have five shillings for the use of them for about an hour - I have never seen them since.
   EDWARD POTTER. I borrowed this coat, waistcoat, and trousers of Richard Marshall for the prisoner; I lent them to him - I told Marshall they were for a young man, but I did not say his name - the prisoner never returned them.
   WILLIAM POTTER. I met the prisoner in Weymouth-street, on the Wednesday; he said, the articles were left at a friend of his, and if I went with him I should have them - I went with him to Benjamin's, a salesman's shop, and he said, he had sent a person to sell them there; that he stood in the street, and got the money, 3s. 6d.
   SOLOMON MOSES. I am servant to Mrs. Benjamin; she is a widow - I bought a coat and waistcoat for five shillings of a young man - I did not see the prisoner.
   HENRY RICHARD BUCKERIDGE. I took up the prisoner; he had these trousers on, and said he had sold the coat and waistcoat to a young man, well-known at the Olive-branch public-house, but I could not find him; as he went to the office, he said, he wished he had taken my advice before.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 1907.  MARY CONNELL was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 1 pair of half-boots, value 3s., the goods of John Edwards.
   CHARLOTTE SAUNDERS. I am the daughter of John Edwards; he is a shoemaker, and lives in Bath-street- I was there on the 8th of July, and saw the prisoner come to his shop; she asked if we had any second-hand shoes - after being told we did not sell them, she went away; when she was gone, a young woman gave me information - I went out, and saw the prisoner twenty yards or more from my father's house; I went after her to the end of Providence-street - there I saw her stop and take a pair of boots from her pocket-hole, and put them into her apron; I followed her till she came to Worship-street, and then I went into the office, and spoke to a gentleman, who took her with the boots - he was called away, and Eagles took them.
   THOMAS EAGLES. I received the boots and the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 1908.  EDWARD EDMONDS was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of July , 1 shawl, value 9s.; 1 hat, value 2s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., and 1 pair of overalls, value 2s., the goods of James Lynch.
 JAMES LYNCH. I live in  Oxford-buildings, Bloomsbury. The prisoner lodged at my house six nights in the course of July; he did not tell me he was going, but I got up on the 15th of July to work - I went into my apartment - the prisoner was gone; I missed a shawl, a jacket, a hat, a pair of shoes, and a pair of overalls - I had seen them all safe the night before; I have never seen them since - I met the prisoner about seven weeks afterwards, and said, "I think you have seen my face before;" he said,"Yes, James, I have seen you - I am very sorry for what I have done, and if you will go to work, I will go to work and make you recompence for it;" I said, "If you will go home I will consider about it" - I gave him in charge; he said he had pawned the shawl for 3s., and sold the duplicate for 6d. - the other things he had sold.
   THOMAS PERRING. I took the prisoner - he said he knew nothing about the robbery, but at the watch-house he said he pawned the shawl on Saffron-hill for 3s., and made away with the rest of the property.
 Prisoner. The prosecutor held out hopes to me, that if I acknowledged what I had done with these things he would let me go - I said Yes, to get away, but I know nothing of them; I was only a nightly lodger, and the door was open - any one could get in.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Transported for Seven Years.
