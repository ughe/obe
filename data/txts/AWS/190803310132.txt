JOHN LYONS (prisoner, on oath). I was working in Holloway Road
about four or five doors from the Lord Nelson," when this retired
publican (as I thought he was) came to me and says, Can you do
with some port wine I said it was a funny thing to do with
shoemakers, and I did not know much about it. I thought I might
know somebody. He told me he had two barrels. I said that was
a lot, but if he came with me I might find someone. We walked to
Gray's Inn Road, and there saw Davis with his friend. I asked
them if they knew anybody who could do with the wine, not thinking
they would know any ne. Davis said, "I think old Jaek at the
Griffin' might know somebody who could do with it." The man
who was with me said it was damaged, and when Jack saw it be
said, Yes, it is damaged." I had no more to do with the busi-
ness after I introduced Davis. I left the stuff to them, as I am a
shoemaker.
Cross-examined. I had seen Saville before once or twice; Davis,

seemed to be a gentleman. I saw him in the "Lord Nelson on the

years. ve only heard people say he was a publican. I do know Mr.
