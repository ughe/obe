 Q. Was there another silk handkerchief besides? - Yes, in the jacket pocket. I laid them down along side of me on the bed, the bed was down.
 Q. Did you undress and go to bed? - No; I sat, with the things, along side of her. I sent for a pot of beer and staid there till about one o'clock, and the other parties, they went away; I still had the bundle along side of me for ten minutes after they were gone, and I had occasion to get up to do something for myself, and the prisoner went twice to the door.
 Q. Did you go out of the room? - No, I did not.
 Q. Was your back turned to her? - Yes. When I came forward to the bed I missed my bundle; I asked her for the bundle; and she said she knew nothing about it. With that she went to go out, and I detained her from going out; when she found that I would not let her go out she seemed rather reconciled, and laid down on the bed. I sat up along side of her till I was rather tired, and I happened to fall back myself and drop asleep; with that the first thing that awaked me was her hand in my pocket, and I had about eight or nine shillings in it altogether; on my stirring with that she drew her hand away from me, and pretended to be asleep; I laid there some time and dropped off again, and she went out, and I did not see any more of her till the morning, she left me in the room. In the morning she came in, and I asked her for my bundle; and she said she knew nothing about it. With that I sent for a constable, and a constable came; the the pawnbroker's, and there he found a waistcoat that she pawned.
   WILLIAM MATHER sworn.
 I am a constable of St. Luke's; I produce a waistcoat that I found pledged at Mrs. West's, the corner of Fan-street, Aldersgate-street.
 Q. Who has kept that waistcoat? - I have.
 - sworn.
 I live with Mrs. West, in Aldersgate-street.
 Q. Of whom did you receive that waistcoat which the constable has? - Of the prisoner at the bar, the 3d of July, in the morning, before ten o'clock; I had seen her before; I supposed that she had a husband that belonged to her. The constable called in the morning and I was not in the way, and he took the waistcoat to the magistrate, and has had it ever since.
 Q. To the Constable. Did you examine this woman - Yes.
 Q. Did you find any duplicates? - Nothing of the kind.(The waistcoat deposed to.)
 Prisoner. He gave me the waistcoat to sleep with me all night. About twelve or one o'clock he came and knocked at my door; there are two or three people more in the lodging house; with that they told us we might go to bed, we should be very safe; and when we awaked in the morning we found the door wide open. It was a common lodging house.
 Q. To Prosecutor. On the solemn oath you have taken, did you give her that waistcoat? - I did not.
 Q. Did you give her any money? - Only some money to get some beer.
 Q. You staid with her all night? - I did, when I found I had lost my bundle.
     GUILTY. (Aged 54.) Imprisoned one year in the House of Correction and fined 1s.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
