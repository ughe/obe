 1747.  FRANCIS CRAWFORD and ELIZABETH CRAWFORD were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 shift, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Leonard Clare Matthews and John Matthews.
   LEONARD CLARE MATTHEWS. I am in partnership with John Matthews- we are pawnbrokers, and live in Whitecross-street. On the 7th of September the two prisoners came together, to pawn a gown, at half-past eight o'clock in the evening - I saw Elizabeth leave the counter; she seemed to move about; she said to Francis once or twice,
 "Go, Jem" - that excited my suspicion; she stood against a partition - I went to serve another person; she then stood against the place where this shift was pinned - I went up, and she was covering this boy with her clothes; he had the shift under his frock.
   GEORGE OVERS. I was on duty - I got this shift from the boy.
 Prisoners' Defence (written). I and my brother went with another girl to the shop to pledge some articles, and whilst I was in the shop the girl gave my brother a shift to hold; soon after Mr. Matthews sent for an officer, and gave us in charge.
   LEONARD CLARE MATTHEWS. There was no other girl.
 F. CRAWFORD -    GUILTY. Aged 12.
 E. CRAWFORD -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Transported for Seven Years.
 1748.  FRANCIS SKINNER was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Walter Berdoe.
 WALTER BERDOE. I was on  Clerkenwell-green, between four and five o'clock, on the 14th of September - I was standing opposite the booth at the election, and felt a hand gently withdrawing itself from my pocket; I turned, and saw the prisoner - he said, "It was not me that did it;" the handkerchiefs was on the ground - there was a mob there: I took him, and gave charge of him - two other silk handkerchiefs were found next his skin, and the duplicate of another.
 Prisoner. Q. Can you swear I took it? A. To the best of my belief you did, because no other person, I think, could have taken it, and dropped it there.
 Prisoner's Defence. It is a matter of impossibility for him to swear I did it; I was unfortunately next to him - he turned, and asked a gentleman if he saw me do it; the gentleman said No; he said, I did, and took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1749.  CATHERINE NAGGS was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 1 purse, value 2d.; 1 sovereign, 15 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the property of Thomas Topp.
   CHARLOTTE TOPP. I am the wife of Thomas Topp. On the 3rd of September, between ten and eleven o'clock, I was walking along York-street, Westminster, with my basket - the string of my petticoat broke, and I dropped this purse down - it contained one sovereign and sixteen shillings; a little boy told me the prisoner had picked it up - I went and spoke to her in a potatoe shop; she said,"What does the woman mean?" I told her I understood she had picked up a purse containing my money - I never got it again; it was a dark blue purse - I could swear to it if I saw it; I cannot tell how near she was to me when I dropped it.
   JOHN BARRON. I am going on for nine years of age; I saw the prosecutrix and the prisoner there - I saw the purse drop a little way from the gutter - the prisoner took it up, and put it into her apron; the prosecutrix was six or seven doors off - I went and asked her if she had dropped any thing; the purse was near the colour of my hat - the prisoner put it into her apron.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not accuse a woman who had a dark blue gown on? A. No.
   FREDERICK KINGSTON. I live with a pawnbroker. The prisoner redeemed several articles from his shop on the day in question, and paid me a sovereign; I had known her before.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had left my house at half-past nine o'clock; I went to the pawnbroker's, and took a half-sovereign, not a sovereign - I got a few things out for my child - this woman asked me if I had picked up a purse; I said No; the lad then said the woman was like me, but not me, she had a blue gown on.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1750.  HENRY MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July 1 copper, value 15s., the goods of William Wyatt Grainge , fixed to a building of his.
   THOMAS CROKETT. I am headborough of Hillingdon. On the 31st of July, I was in Mr. Benham's shop, and ironmonger, at Uxbridge, and David Lane brought in this copper for sale - Mr. Benham refused to buy it, and said he had better take it to Mr. Grainge; instead of which he made the best of his way down Page's-lane - a gentleman told me I had better go after him; I went and saw the prisoner with it on his shoulder, and Lane with him - they ran off; the prisoner threw down the copper and ran through a river - I pursued the other, but lost him in a bean-field; this was about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's - I fitted this copper to the place; it matches exactly; the prisoner is a native of Uxbridge.
   THOMAS WILLIAM GRAINGE. This copper is my father's, William Wyatt Grainge- it was in a house which the tenant had left; I did not see it fitted to the place - it had been safe on the 19th of July; the window was broken- I cannot say when it was taken.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw Lane, who asked me to carry it for him, which I did.
     GUILTY. Aged 22 - Transported for Seven Years.
