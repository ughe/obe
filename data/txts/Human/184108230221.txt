I am a police-sergeant. My attention was called on this evening to the accident on the river—I saw Mr. Cotton, and went with him, at his request, on board the Richmond—the prisoner Undey was pointed out as the captain—I asked him if he was captain of the vessel—he said he was—I said, Mr. Cotton, the gentleman with me, had given him in charge, for running foul of a pant he was in, and causing the death of Edward Lewis—he said he had not charge of the vessel at the time the accident occurred—I told him he must go with me.—he said I should take the mate, as the mate had charge of the vessel at the time—Dakers was there, but said nothing then—he said at the station, in Undey's presence, that the captain was on the paddle-box at the time the accident occurred, and he was at the wheel—the captain smelt very strong of liquor—he ap peered to have been drinking—he was a little the worse for liquor.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was he not very much agitated? A.  He appeared so—when a person has been drinking he shows it in his countenance—agitation does not show itself in the countenance so much as drink—it may be visible. KINGSTON MARKS. I am a police-sergeant. I received charge of the prisoners—Undey said he was in charge of the vessel, and Dakers that he was at the wheel.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You admitted them to bail, I believe? A.  I did, about half an hour after they were given in charge—if there had been any appearance of intoxicatign on the part of the captain, I should not have done so—he was not intoxicated, to the best of my be lief—there was no appearance of it, to my knowledge.
   JURY to MR. GARLIN . Q.  Did you observe whether there was a boy on board the steamer, who took the order from the captain to stop the engine? A.  I did not observe any order given at all—all the passengers cried out "Stop her," or something—I heard a number of voices cry out "stop her"—the passengers were all very much horrified, and cried out "Stop her," "Ease her," and so on—as soon as the vessel could be stopped, it was done; as soon as the vessel occasioned the accident, or, probably just before—the persons on board the vessel, and those belonging to it, were in as great horror as the people in the punt. NOT GUILTY.
