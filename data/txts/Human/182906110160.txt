 1364  JAMES DALEY was indicted for feloniously assaulting William Sapsworth , the younger, on the 2d of June , at St. Matthew Bethnal-green, putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 cap, value 2s., the goods of William Sapsworth the elder.
   WILLIAM SAPSWORTH, JUN. I am between seven and eight years old, and live with my father, William Sapsworth, at No. 22, Coalharbour-street. On the 2d of June, I was in Birdcage-fields, near Bethnal-green, and had this cap on my head about eight o'clock in the evening - it was just by my father's house; the prisoner came up to me - he knocked me and my little brother down; it hurt me very much - he did it with his hand; my cap was on my head - I looked about when I got up, and could not find it; he went across the fields - I did not see him again till the second day after; he was then before the Justice at Worship-street - I am quite certain of him; my cap has never been found - it was seal-skin; I have seen him in the fields before, and knew his features - I cannot be mistaken in him.
 WILLIAM SAPSWORTH, SEN. My son left my house between six and seven o'clock in the evening, and went to play in the fields with his brother, who is about two years old - he goes to church and to school; he had a dark sealskin cap on when he left home - I was informed his cap was taken; I went into the fields, and he was there crying- the cap has not been found; I am a labourer in the East-India Company's service.
 ROBERT YOUNG. I am thirteen years old, and live with my father, who is in the Customs. I go to Finsburychapel; I can say the commandments and catechism - God will punish people who tell lies; I was in Birdcage-fields, on the 2d of June, and saw the prisoner: I had seen him before in the fields with other boys; I was sitting by the side of Sapsworth, and saw the prisoner take hold of him, and swing him round - he fell to the ground, and he took the cap off his head, and ran off with it; I ran after him, but he got out of my sight - I gave information to the constable, and he was taken on the 4th of June, near some fields; a sailor boy was with him - he said, "Good by, Tom;" I said, "That is the boy who stole the cap;" I ran and called the prosecutor and the constable, and they took him.
 JOSEPH ROGERS. I am thirteen years old, and live with my parents. If I take an oath, I am bound to tell the truth; I was in the fields, and saw Sapsworth and his brother sitting down - the prisoner knocked him down, took his cap off, and ran across the hill with it.
   THOMAS BRIDGES. I am a constable, and live close to these fields; I received information, and went to Birdcage-fields, and saw the prisoner running away from Mr. Sapsworth - I ran round and caught him; I told him the charge; he said, "I am not the one;" I found a pair of scissors on him - he told the Magistrate he was not the boy who took the cap, but knew the boy who did.
 Prisoner's Defence. The scissors are mine; I am in the silk line; I was not in the field at the time - I was at home at eight o'clock on Tuesday evening. GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 14.
 Recommended to mercy, on account of his youth.

 1365.  HENRY LIGHTFORD and THOMAS WILSON were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of May , 1 diamond pin, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Wick , from his person.
   THOMAS WICK. I am an auctioneer, and live in West Smithfield. On the 18th of May, between twelve and one o'clock in the night, I was on Snow-hill, with a lady - a female was behind me; I saw several persons, and the two prisoners were among them; the prisoners were together, and were at first behind me - they then came in front of me; Lightford shoved up against me - I asked what he did it for: he made no remark, but took the pin out of my breast with his thumb and finger; he went a little way back - I said to him, "You have got my pin - give it me, and go along about your business:" he said, "I have not got your pin;" in the mean time Wilson struck my female servant, who was behind - she called out Watch! and I held Lightford till the watchman came and took him: I do not know what became of the pin - it has not been found- the other persons were further up the bill; after I gave Lightford in charge, Wilson came up and struck me - I think he was two or three yards off when Lightford took the pin.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Do you mean that you gave Wilson in charge? A.Certainly I did, for striking me, and he struck  Catherine Conner, who was with her husband, behind me; there were four of us together - Sophia Aaron was with me: a minute or two elapsed after I lost the pin, before Conner complained at Wilson striking her; we had no thought of taking him till he came and struck me - I saw them together, and three others with them.
 COURT. Q. Do you think Wilson saw Lightford take the pin? A. His back was towards him, he might not possibly.
 SOPHIA AARON. I was with Mr. Wick on Snow-hill. I saw Lightford take the pin from his breast - Wilson was about a yard from him at the time; I do not know whether he saw what Lightford did: Conner gave an alarm when Wilson struck her.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see what he did? A. He passed us to strike her - I turned round.
