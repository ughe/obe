JOHN WILLIAMS. I am the prisoner's brother-in-law, I embarked with him on board the Pathfinder  —I was not present at the conversation be tween him and Mr. Marklew—I heard him say he would take his son out with him, and that he kept the Yarrow Steam packet Hotel, at Port Philip—I did not hear him say any thing about receiving 700 l . or 800 l .—I was present at the Mansion-house when he was before the lord Mayor—I will not know whether what he said then was taken down in writing—I will not be sure whether Mr. Marklew stated there in the prisoner's presence, that he (he prisoner) had said he kept the Yarrow Steam packet Hotel at port philip—I married the prisoner's sister—I think she was going on for seventeen years of ago—I never knew of his having to receive 700 l . or 800 l . in England—I have known him about fouryears, before he went to Australia, and when he came back—he married my sister.
   Prisoner.  It is about two years since I first went out to Australia Witness.  Yes, in 1840.
   MR. MARKLEW re-examined.  I was present at the prisoner's examin ation at the Mansion-house—I do not think what he said was taken down—I stated there, in his hearing, that he had told me he came over here to receive 700 l . or 800 l ., and that he kept the Yarrow Steam Packet Hotel at port philip—the prisoner replied, "it is false," and called me a false perjured villain for stating that he had ever said any thing about the 700 l . or 800 l ., for he never had stated such a thing, and that I should suffer for it on my death bed—he said he never was the proprietor of the Yarrow Steam packet Hotel, and he had never stated to me that he was.
   Prisoner.  I stated that I was never the proprietor, but that I kept the hotel. Witness.  He said he never was the proprietor, but he was always there to manage the business—I am sure he did tell me that he was the proprietor in the first instance—it was on Saturday, the 9th of october, that I gave him the goods and money and it was on that day that he told me he had paid the deposit.
