Mr. Cuckburn. Q. Did he make complaints to you about the state of
his stomach and bowels? A. I heard him mention the circumstance once
when I was in his room at barracks-be stated that he had stones and bricka
in his stomach-his manner was at times very reserved, and at other times
looking very wild and excited, without any apparent cause it was my beliel
that his mind was impaired, from the loss of bis horses and dog-it was my
impression at the time that that had a great deal to do with his change of
manner- observed this striking change of manner after that event.
Q. Did that altered manner and appearance continue until he left the regi-
ment? A. It was not so much after he was released from arrest; at least, I
had not the same opportunities of seeing him-when I did see him I had not
opportunities of observing him so closely as I had before I was not with the
regiment when he left; I was on leave-he made a present of his uniform and
accoutrements to the adjutant of the regiment-I have known parts of an
officer's appointments given, and I have known the horse given, but I never
knew a man give the entire of his things-he gave all-on the very afternoon
of this unfortunate transaction I met Mr. Pate, about three o'clock, in Picca
dilly, and I observed that his manner was more excited than usual-he was
walking in his usual manner, but swinging his arms and his stick a little more
than I had ever seen bim do before-he was in the habit of swinging his arms,
he has a very peculiar gait--every one used to turn round and look at him-be
always carried a small stick; I never saw him without I have frequently
seen him since he left the regiment, always taking bis usual walk between
Duke-street and the Parks- I have met him at different hours taking the
same walk, and always swinging his arms in a peculiar manner, but I never
saw him in such an excited state as he was at three o'clock on the Thursday
-I was walking with a friend at that time, and we both remarked his
appearance.
Court. Q. I suppose you thought his appearance eccentric! 4. I did.
MR. ATTORNBY-GENERAL. Q. Did you speak to him? A. I did; I said,
“How do you do?" and he gave his usual answer, a sort of nod-he recog-
nized me, but in a very wild manner-be usually looked wild, but he never
looked so excited as he did that afternoon-1 have called on him in Jermyn-
street; but finding he did not wish to see any one, I discontinued calling on
him-I have very often seen him in the street, and always spoke or nodded
to him-he always walked very upright, and swung his arms, but not in the
manner in which he has done lately-when I first knew bim be had a peculiar
manner; he held himself erect, and his manner of walking, I think, was
different from ordinary persons, nothing very remarkable-his peculiar
manner has become very much exaggerated lately, so much so that everybody
looked round at him-it was about the same time that he told me about the
poisoning, that he spoke of having bricks and stones in his inside-he was
then quartered at Newbridge.
