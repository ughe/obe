1287. JOHN AUSTIN and HENRY JOHNSON were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the property of Jeremiah Henry Joyce.
   JEREMIAH HENRY JOYCE. I live in Crescent-street, Euston-square. On the 5th of June this handkerchief hung up in my shop on a clothes-horse; a person could not reach it going by - they must have got on the step to reach; I saw it safe between eleven and twelve o'clock when I went out - the prisoners were taken at the time.(Property produced and sworn to)
   SAMUEL JONES. I am a shoemaker, and live opposite to the prosecutor. On the 5th of June I saw the prisoners near his door, about five o'clock, standing on the step; I came out of my shop, and went in at a private door - I came out again, and missed the handkerchief; I went, and told Mr. Joyce's people of it - the prisoners had only got about twelve doors down the street; I did not see them taken.
   JEREMIAH CONNELLY. I am a servant to Mr. Pearse, a neighbour of Mr. Joyce's. I saw the two prisoners running, and Austin putting this handkerchief into his trousers behind him - I ran after them, and they were taken in the middle of Euston-square, about a hundred yards from the prosecutor's; I saw the handkerchief found on Austin at the watch-house - it was claimed by Mr. Joyce.
 Prisoner JOHNSON. Q. Who stopped us? A. Mr. Joyce's lad, and another person took up the handkerchief.
   THOMAS SEABORN. I am a constable. I received the prisoners at the watch-house, and found this yellow handkerchief in Johnson's hat; I searched, and found nothing else - I then ordered him to pull off his boots, and found one handkerchief in each of his boots; I found Mr. Joyce's handkerchief in Austin's trousers - it had this shop ticket on it; this waistcoat, which they said belonged to Austin, was put on the table at the watch-house.
 JOHNSON'S Defence. I passed the shop door, and looked at some of the things; when I got about six doors further, the man took me.
 AUSTIN'S Defence. I saw this at the door, picked it up, and the man came after us.
 AUSTIN -    GUILTY. Aged 28.
 JOHNSON -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 1288.  WILLIAM BERNADEER and WILLIAM CLEASBY were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of May , 14lbs. weight of bacon, value 6s., the goods of Benjamin Barber; and that the said William Bernadeer had been previously convicted of felony.
 LUCY BARBER. I am the wife of  Benjamin Barber: we live in Windsor-street, Islington- we keep a shop, and sell bacon. On Wednesday, the 20th of May, we lost some bacon from our shop - I had seen it about an hour before on the counter opposite the door, not within the reach of any one going by; I missed it about ten o'clock in the morning - I had not seen the prisoners that day; I had seen Bernadeer about before - I saw the bacon at Worship-street; I can swear it was my husband's, though it was dirtier than it had been.
 ELIZABETH BARHAM. I keep a chandler's shop, about one hundred yards form Mr. Barber's, in Windsor-street. I know Bernadeer; he carries hearth-stones about for sale- I had seen him that morning in my shop, and bought some stones of him; there was a person shorter than him sitting at my door - I cannot say whether it was Cleasby.
   RICHARD SKILLERN. I am a Bow-street officer. On the 20th of May, about eleven o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoners in Virginia-row, Bethnal-green; Bernadeer had this bag on his shoulder - I had some suspicion, and stopped him; Cleasby was with him, but he walked away - Bernadeer said it was bacon in the bag; I asked where he got it - he said he had been bird's-nesting in the morning, and picked it up in the field; I took him to the watch-house; he made no resistance.
   WILLIAM KINGHAM. I was with Skillern; I saw him stop Bernadeer - Cleasby walked away, and I took him; he said he was with Bernadeer, in Hackney-fields, when they found the bacon - he said there was another piece all over mud, and if I liked to go and take it, I might.
 RHODA JOHNSON. I lodge at Mr. Barber's. I saw a person standing at the post of the door, about ten o'clock; I believe it was Bernadeer, but I cannot be certain - I saw no one near him.
   GEORGE HENFREE. I am an assistant-beadle of Mileend Old-town. I have known Bernadeer two months; I have a certificate of his conviction, which I got from Mr. Shelton's office - I know it relates to Bernadeer; I saw him then in Court, and heard him plead guilty (read)
 BERNADEER'S Defence. When the bacon was produced at Worship-street - the officer said there was a mark on it, and then the lady swore to it.
 CLEASBY'S Defence. I saw this young man jump into a ditch, and pick up this bacon.
 BERNADEER -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Three Months and Whipped.
 CLEASBY -    NOT GUILTY.
