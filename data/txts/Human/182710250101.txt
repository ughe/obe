JOHN HAINES. I am an officer of this Court. I was present last Sessions at the trial of John Elson, and administered the oath to the defendant, who was a witness.
   WILLIAM FIELD(clerk to H. Buckler, short-hand writer of the Court) read his notes of the trial of John Elson, upon which trial the defendant was examined on the prisoner's behalf, and repeatedly swore he heard Mrs. Donald say, the said John Elson was not the man who had robbed her - for the report of the trial, see Seventh Session, page 741.
   MARY DONALD. I am the wife of James Donald. On the evening of the 6th of September I was at Bartholomew-fair with him; I stood in front of Richardson's booth, and my reticule was got from me - I saw Elson standing near me at the time, and saw him when he was taken; I never said he was not the man.
 Prisoner. Q. What did you say when your husband collared the man? A. I never said a word.
 COURT. Q. When your husband collared the man, how near was he to you? A. He had hold of my arm; if I had said any thing. he must have heard it.
 JAMES DONALD. I was with my wife on the 6th of September, near Richardson's booth; we were making our way through, and a great crowd made a push; we made our way towards the railing - they surrounded us, and one snatched my wife's reticule - Elson passed me at the time, and I caught him by the skirts of his coat - my wife never at any time said he was not the man.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you collar him? A. I seized him, and called for two officers; they collared him.
 MRS. DONALD. I saw the man take my reticule.
 JAMES DONALD re-examined. He was running, and I caught hold of the skirts of his coat.
   BENJAMIN CHARLES MURREY. I am a constable, and was in the fair on the night in question - I saw Elson opposite Richardson's show, and several noted pick pockets with him - I saw the prisoner at a little distance from them - I saw Elson snatch at the reticule; when he was taken, I heard Mrs. Donald say, "That is the man;" as soon as he snatched the reticule, his hand came down with assistance, we got Elson out of the fair - I heard Mrs. Donald say, he was the man more than once; she said,"He took my reticule;" I was there all the time, and never heard her at any time say he was not the man.
   WILLIAM BENNS. I am a constable of St. George's, Bloomsbury. I was in the fair on the evening of the 6th of September; I saw Elson first about an hour before this happened, and again between eleven and twelve o'clock, with a gang of pickpockets; I saw him snatch Mrs. Donald's reticule from her left arm; he made a stoop and put it behind him - what became of it, I do not know; I helped to take him to the Compter; I could hear all Mrs. Donald said - she never, at any time, said, in my hearing, that Elson was not the man.
 Prisoner. It is a very hard case I should be subpoenaed from my work, and then brought here; here is my subpoena. I should not have come if I could have helped it.
 The prisoner received a very good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.

 2224.  JOHN FISHER was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Parker , on the 27th of October , and stealing 1 pair of breeches, value 1s. 6d., and 1 shirt, value 3s., his property.
 JAMES PARKER. I am a  silk-weaver, and live in Wilmot-street, Bethnal-green. On the 27th of October I was in my workshop, about ten o'clock in the morning; my daughter called me down, and I saw the prisoner and my son in the passage; I looked down, and saw a pair of breeches and a shirt, which had been locked up in my room - I laid them on the stairs; my son gave me this key; I sent him for an officer, and when he came, I put this key in the door, and opened and shut it several times - I then gave the prisoner into custody.
   CHARLES PARKER. I am the prosecutor's son. About ten o'clock in the morning of the 27th of October I came out of my father's house, and passed the prisoner within a few yards of it; I crossed the way, and happened to turn my head; I saw the prisoner put his hand up, as if to open the door - he had his hand upon it; he was in for two or three minutes, and then came out with some things under his arm; I had fastened the door myself, and had not left it three minutes - I had not my eye upon the door all that time; I crossed the road and ran after him - I said he must come back to my father's, which he did, and dropped in the passage a pair of breeches and a shirt; another witness came up to the door with a key, and I saw my father try it to the lock of our room door, which it opened as well as our own - it was picked up near where I took the prisoner; he left the door open.
   SARAH PARKER. I am the prosecutor's wife. I was called down by my daughter, and saw the prisoner with this property near him.
   BENJAMIN EVE. I live in Smart's-gardens. On Saturday last I was going out of my garden, and saw Charles Parker take hold of the prisoner, who dropped something, I could not see what; I went up to the spot and picked up this key, which must be what he dropped - I saw it tried to Parker's door.
 Prisoner. I know nothing about the key.
   JAMES ESSEX. I am a constable. I went to the house, and found the prisoner in the passage; he said he was very sorry for what he had done, and he did it through distress. GUILTY. Aged 40. Of stealing only. - Confined Three Months.
