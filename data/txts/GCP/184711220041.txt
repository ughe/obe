67. MARIA HOPKINS, stealing 5 half-crowns, 2 shillings, and I six-
pence, the moneys of James Andrew Wilson, from his person, having
been before convicted.
JAMES ANDREW Wilson. On 6th Nov. I was returning from Paddington,
and was accosted by the prisoner-she forced her conversation on me
missed a handful of silver from my pocket-there had been 20s. or 30s.-it
was all gone but a 4d. piece--she was taken in charge I walked by her
side-the officer found 158. in her mouth, and I picked up half a crown, a
portion of the money which was taken from her,
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What time was it? 4. About one
o'clock in the morning-no one had accosted me but the prisoner and her
two associates--I had had nothing but a glass of grog- I took no notice of
the three, but the prisoner walked with me about ten minutes, from twenty
to forty yards--I was trying to get rid of her, but could not release myself
I did not give her anything-she wanted to know whether I would go home
with her-I merely asked her which way she was going-she said she would
show me I went ten or twelve yards, and then saw a policeman.
Robert SMITH (policeman, 160.) Wilson gave the prisoner into my charge
for stealing money-I saw something bulge out in her check, and tried to
force it out-she said, “Let me go, I will give it up"-she did not give it up,
and with the assistance of another constable I got four half-crowns and
two shillings and sixpence from her mouth-Wilson picked up another
half-crown, which made five.
Cross-examined. Q. How did you get the money? 4. Another man
held her arms, and I thrust my thumb into her mouth, as she appeared to be
swallowing the money-it fell out--this was in the street.
WILLIAM PENNY (policeman, E 140.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction (read-Convicted April, 1844, for stealing a
half-crown from the person, confined one year)-the prisoner is the person.
Guilty. Aged 35.-Transported for Seven Years.
