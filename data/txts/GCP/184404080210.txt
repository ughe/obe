MAGNAY, Mayor.
I have inquired; there is no such firm as Bolte and Co., at Paris--this bill
is not in the same state as when I sent it; a false indorsement has been
added to it, which follows my indorsement to " Bolte and Co., Bremen" -
"Pay to the order of Mr. H. W. Albert, value in account. Paris, 30 Nov.
1843. Bolte and Co."-I indorsed it " In account," because I had an ac
count with Bolte and Co.; and now this false indorsement is “ Value in ac-
count," as if Bolte and Co. had some account with Mr. Albert ; that is very
wrong--these other two bills (looking at them) are part of the same purchase,
and have my indorsement the same as the other-here is on one of them,
“H, W. Albert," and on the other, “J. H. Albert."
Cross-examined. Q. Are you agent of Bolte and Co. at Paris? 4.
Yes, and am well known as their agent there--I heard something of a person
named Frederick--I said I should be glad to meet with him, because his
name was mentioned, and I heard that the prisoner said he had received
the bills from Frederick-I do not know whether Frederick is in custody
in Paris.
FERDINAND Rosing. On a Sunday in Nov, my father gave me a letter
directed to Bolte and Co., Bremen - I put it into the post in the same state as
when my father gave it me.
AUGUSTE THEODOR PLUMP. I am a merchant, of the firm of Bolte and
Co., living at Bremen, and carrying on business there-they have no house
of business or establishment at Paris, or anywhere except at Bremen-Jo-
hanna Rosing is their agent at Paris-we expected a remittance of some bills
from him in Nov. last-the bills did not arrive, and, not receiving them, we
drew on him on account, on the 1st of Dec., for part of the sum-this bill
would have been worth about 651. in English money last Nov.--this indorse-
ment of “Bolte and Co." on it is not my writing, nor the writing of either of
my partners-no person has any authority to draw or to indorse bills of ex-
change for the firm but my two partners and myself-this indorsement of Mr.
Rosing to us, "Value in aceount," means that he stands in account with us,
and so he makes us remittances for it-it is a transaction of account, not a
cash transaction--that is always the way of doing business amongst mer-
chants--these other two bills were never received by our house, nor is there
any indorsement of ours, or by authority from us, upon them the name of
"H. W. Albert" was never known to us--we never had any transaction with
him we know nothing of "J. W. Albert," which is on the other bill.
FERDINAND Davis Blum. I live in St. John's Wood-road, and am clerk
to Konigswater Brothers, of London. We bought this bill of exchange of the
prisoner about the 12th of Dec. last-it is worth about 631. in English money
-- he asked if we would buy it of him we had previously refused to buy a bill
of him, as he was a stranger; and when he brought this be brought a person
to introduce him, whom we had known in business-here is on this bill the
supposed indorsement of " Bolte and Co., pay to order of H. W. Albert, value
in account"-that imports that there had been transactions between the house
of Bolte and Co. and the indorsee-a person in the prisoner's situation would
know enough of the language and practice of bills, to be aware of what it
meant-when the bill was at maturity it was not paid.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you speaking of what the prisoner said to you,
or did he speak to anybody else in the firm ? A. He spoke to Mr. Konigswater
-I can undertake to say that I heard all that passed - I had no other employ-
ment at that moment, and was listening to what was spoken—the name of
Frederick was not mentioned to us by the prisoner when we bought this bill
- I cannot say whether we saw the prisoner at any other time about this bill;
but in speaking of another bill be named Frederick, and gave his address in
Paris.
