skin was very hot-there were several livid marks on the forehead and
side of the head and face-both arms were covered with bruises and livid
marks--there was a contusion on the upper part of the chest on the left

short time afterwards-I made a post-mortem examination; the brain was
healthy, but there were five or six ounces of blood extravasated on the
back-part of the headma severe blow on the front of the head would have
caused that quantity of extravasated blood at the back I do not think it
would be caused by the bruises on the front of the head themselves; they
seemed to be simply pinches I think if the child had been thrown on
the back of its head on a hard substance, that would account for the
extravasated blood- I have seen the bolster in the workhouse-if the
child was thrown with great violence upon one of them, it might perhaps
account for the coagulated blood after a shock of that kind, the child
would appear drowsy and stupified-all the other parts of the body were
healthy-in my judgment, the cause of death was the rupture of a blood-
Vessel, caused by the child being thrown violently against the bed, and
striking its head against a hard substance-none of the other bruises would
account for death, they might accelerate the circulation a little ; that would
be merely accessary.
Court. Q. Might it have been caused by the child falling from the
bed to the ground on its head? 4. Yes, or from its being pitched on an
iron bedstead-the marks on the arms and thighs were isolated, as if from
its being pinched-what I saw might arise from a violent fall, a blow, or
a sudden shake.
Prisoner's Defence. The child fell out of bed five times on Wednesday
morning, and fell on its head each time; it was not sleeping on the double
bedstead, but on the single one when it fell.

1011. JAMES DAUBNEY, stealing 6 yards of linen, value Is. 6d.;
the goods of Thomas Medley, his master.
THOMAS MEDLEY. The prisoner has been in my service fourteen years
-on the night of 30th or 31st March, I gave him the key to lock the
gates, and left-I returned in two minutes, and saw him in the stowage-
room, and saw him take two pieces of linen from a bulk of goods, and put
them in his trowsers-flap- I went to him, walked with him to the gates,
and said, “Jem, you have robbed me," I took hold of him, and gave him
into custody- I took these two pieces of linen (produced) from his
trowsers, and gave it to the policeman-I went with bim to the station,
and then to his house.
