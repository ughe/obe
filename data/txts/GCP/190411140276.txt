Hooley, in his defence on oath, said that he knew nothing of the
Construction Company except what Lawson told him; that he only told
Paine what Lawson told him about the Company and nothing else; thut
he told Paine nothing which he himself did not then believe to be true;
that he had never conspired with Lawson to defraud Paine or the
shareholders in the Construction Company; that he had never represented
to Paine that the Company had earned sufficient cash to pay a divi.
dend of 168. on each share within a few days; that the contract between
Lawson and himself of November 26th was bona fide; that he had never
represented to Paine that Lawson had possession of 245,000 shares in the
Company, or that that number had been issued at that date, or that the
Dublin Distillers shares were worth £5 each, and were readily saleable at
that price; and that he believed the printed documents which Paine sane
were all true.
