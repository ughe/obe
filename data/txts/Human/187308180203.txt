   Cross-examined.  I did not buy soap and candles of the prosecutor, only waste-paper—when Mrs. Castle has come to my place I have seen some thing under her shawl, but I have not known what it was—George Castle's house is thirteen houses distant from my shop—I did not know that she was a laundry-woman—I should have put things under my shawl as she did, I have no doubt. MARY SIMONS. I know the Castles—I went to their house last year, but not to buy anything—I saw a cart standing outside, and things were being put into it—the horse's head was towards Lambeth Walk—Mrs. Castle was inside the shop, and her son, too—I saw some parcels on the counter, and an ordinary carpet bag, such as a gentleman might travel with—one parcel that was in the bag fell over, and I saw that it contained white wax, some of it escaped on to the counter—this was more than a year ago. CHARLES SIMONS. I know the Castles—about three months ago I met George Castle in a horse and cart, he stopped under the tail of the cart, and took out two large cakes of white wax and one of brown—they were in the shape of a bowl—he took them into his shop. SEYMOUR SUNDELL. I am a glass shade and wax flower maker, at 51, Lower Marsh—I have known the woman Scott—about three months ago I bought some wooden cases of her; I noticed a little wax about the mouths of the cases—she afterwards came and asked if I was disposed to buy wax—she brought about 10 lbs. as a sample, she said they mixed it themselves—and so they could sell it cheaper than anyone else—it was white wax—she asked me 18 d . a lb.—from, first to last I bought about 80 lbs. of her at 1 s . 40 d .—I ultimately sold about 22 lbs, of it to Mr. Soames, the traveller to Mr. Field, the remainder was used for my home purposes.
