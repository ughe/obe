 1381.  JAMES CARTER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 1 spoon, value 2s., the goods of Caroline Stanley.
   MARY SAUNDERS. I am servant to Caroline Stanley, she lives at Paddington. On the 16th of July, about three o'clock in the afternoon, I was coming down the kitchen stairs, and saw the prisoner go up the area steps, with the spoon in his hand, at his side - I ran after him and called, "Stop thief," he ran as far as he could to the Park-wall, and I saw him get over - he was caught in the Park, and was brought back with the spoon, which had been taken off the dinner-tray on the kitchen table - I know nothing of the prisoner.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me come down the area? A. No.
   DONALD M'CLEAN(police-constable D 132). I was on duty, and a cab-man called to me, "There goes a thief;" I saw the prisoner running across the Park - I knew him, and followed him, crying, "Stop thief," a gentleman got out of a cab and stopped him, and I drew  this spoon from under his waistcoat.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1382.  MARCUS VINER was indicted for feloniously receiving of an evil-disposed person, on the 14th of June , 6 rabbit skins, value 3s., which had lately before been stolen, he well knowing them to have been stolen. The name of the owner of the property not being stated the prisoner was ACQUITTED.
 1383.  SAMUEL DEAYTON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 7 sovereigns and 40 shillings, of Ann Welsh his mistress, in her dwelling-house.
 MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
   ANN WELSH. I live in the parish of St. Pancras, and keep a dairy- the prisoner was in my employ; on the 20th of July I was called out between twelve and two o'clock - I left Emily Probert and the prisoner there - I saw my cash-box when I went out - I had seen the contents of it safe the night before - it had then 36l. in it - when I returned home I missed seven sovereigns and forty shillings out of a little canvass bag - I had no one but the prisoner to milk my cows, and to carry out my milk; so I allowed him to do it - I afterwards had him taken - I had left my key in the cupboard where this cash box was.
 COURT. Q. Is it your dwelling-house? A. Yes; there was no other person who could have taken the money - the prisoner had been eleven months with me.
 Prisoner. I had bought you a load of hay, and you came out into the cow-shed for half an hour, the money might have been taken then. Witness. No, not that day; I did not.
   EMILY PROBERT. I was left at home with the prisoner- he told me to go to the shed and look for a key; I did, and came back, and said, I could not find it; he told me to go and look into the further shed, where the grass was; I went, and returned, and said, I could not find it; it was the key of the school-room which my mistress had to let - when I went I left no one in the kitchen but the prisoner.
   JAMES WELLS(police-constable S 30). I took the prisoner, he said, he knew nothing about this charge.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1384.  SAMUEL DEAYTON was again indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 1 coat, value 20s. and 1 waistcoat, value 8s. the goods of Ann Welsh , his mistress.
 MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
   ANN WELSH. I live in the parish of St. Pancras- three days after the loss of my money I missed a black coat and waistcoat, of my late husband's; they had been in the cupboard, in my kitchen, in which the money was deposited.
   JAMES WELLS. I took the prisoner - I searched him, but found nothing; but I found, in a box, which he said was his, this duplicate, in a waistcoat pocket - it is for a coat and waistcoat, pawned in the name of Samuel Deayton, 46, Middlesex-street; the prisoner said, he knew nothing about them.
   ROBERT MILTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have the coat and waistcoat, I cannot say who pawned them, but I gave the person this duplicate.
 Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing about these things - I have been at the pawnbroker's, but it was to pawn my own property.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1385.  SAMUEL DEAYTON was again indicted for stealing, on the 24th of April , 1 watch, value 30s. the goods of William Welsh.
 WILLIAM WELSH. I am nephew of  Ann Welsh. I lost the duplicate of a watch in April last - it had been pawned for 10s. at Mr. Griffiths.
   JAMES ARCHER. I produce the duplicate to prove that this watch was pawned; and it was redeemed, to the best of my belief, by the prisoner - when he was taken this watch was found on him.
   JAMES WELLS. I took the prisoner on the other charges - I found this watch on him; he was very desirous for the magistrate to allow him to have the watch again, but he would not - the prosecutor then claimed it; he described it to me before he saw it.
 WILLIAM WALSH. This is my watch - the prisoner lodged with me in the month of March.
 Prisoner. Q. Was your box broken open? A. No; but the duplicate was in it, and it was taken out.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years longer.
