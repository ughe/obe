JAMES JEWETT. I work for Messrs. Phillips on Sunday, 9th March, at
10 o'clock I was sent for to the Victoria Docks--I saw the Richard Nestle
there I remained on board of her from 10 o'clock till 12-I then went
away, and remained away until 7 or 8 in the evening, when I came back,
and remained on board all night-about 3 o'clock, the men who work in the
mud oraft, came and took their craft away, and I shifted the barge I went
and got my breakfast and stopped there till she was unloaded alongside the
warehouse.
COURT. 0. Did you find her adrift A. Yes ; there was nobody on
board; she was floating about promiscuously; the wind was blowing hard
and I could do nothing with her.

Thomas WAITE I live at 23, Bell-and-anchor cottages, Victoria-dock-
road-I am lockman at the Victoria Dock-on Sunday, 9th March, between
4 and 5 o'clock, I let the barge Mary Anne into the dock-I let her out
again on Monday morning, between 4 and 5— young man between 18 and
19 was with her; I cannot swear to him-he had a brown coat on and a
blue cloth cap-1 did not see his face the water was nineteen feet below me
-I am sure it was the Mary Anne-it was the first barge I came in contact
with, and I put my bull's eye on and said, “Hallot is this barge going out
again " and the person in her said, "She is in the wrong doek, I am going
to put her in the London Dock," or "in the river," I can't say which there
were six or seven tarpaulins on her, and one of them was traced up on the
ganwale.
