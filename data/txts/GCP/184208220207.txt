CHARLES TILLY (City police-constable, No. 296.) I took the prisoner
on Saturday week at Mrs. Lake's I went to the prisoner's lodgings she
directed me there--I found the brooch and earrings, a sovereign, and a
handkerchief-in going to Guildhall, she told me she took the earrings and
brooch to go to Greenwich-she owned to taking the 21. 48., but denied
all knowledge of the 31. 10s.
Prisoner. I did not take the three sovereigns and a half.
GUILTY. Aged 35.-Confined Six Months.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
2496. JAMES FRAZER and JOHN ADAMS were indicted for
stealing, on the 31st of July, 4 loaves of bread, value 18.4d.; the
goods of Joseph Simpson, the master of Frazer.
JOSEPH SIMPSON. I keep a baker's shop at Islington. On the 31st of
July, I was going home-as I was near home I saw Adams stooping down
to my area- I crossed the street, and then saw him stoop down several
times-at last he got a 4lb. loaf out of the area- I suppose some one
gave it him, as he could not reach. it from the ground then saw a parcel
in a handkerchief come up in the same way out of the area -he then put
them on his left arm, and went across the road I went to him, and said,
I thought he had some of my property-he said he had not, he got it at
the West end of the town—I took him back to the shop--I then opened
the parcel, and found it contained two 2lbs. loaves, which belonged to me,
and so did the other loaf which he had I had gone out that evening about
seven o'clock, and the loaves were then in my bread-bin in the shop-I
had left my servant-girl at home when I got back she was there--I sent
her for a policeman-I then called Frazer, who was my man, up from the
bakehouse - I told him there was a person outside wanted him-he came
up, and said, “Who wants me!" said, “That person at the door
that was the policeinan-I gave him into custody-Frazer was rather in
liquor-there was a Miss Brown, a lodger, in my house.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. There was nobody in the house
but the servant and a lodger? 4. No, not when I went out-I never
allowed Frazer a set-off for bread- occasionally buy bread of some
neighbours—this happened on Sunday night the 31st of July-I had
not on the Saturday before sent Frazer to a neighbouring shop to buy a
quantity of loaves for me--I swear that I know Mr. Dobell, a baker, in
White Lion-street, Pentonville-I did not send Frazer there on that Satur-
day, to my knowledge-he might have bought a few stale loaves on my
account--we did not want any new loaves-I do not think I sent him to
buy any loaves--I am positive I did not-I cannot swear that he did not
go to Mr. Dobell's for a loaf, but I did not send him he has had dealings
on my account at Mr. Dobell's I do not know that he had on his own
account-be used to sleep in my house sometimes--my shop is always
closed on a Sunday, but we sell a loaf to a customer.
