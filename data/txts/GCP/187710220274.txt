Cross-examined by Fennell. I said at the police-court that I could
tell by looking at the draught of water what the barge held-it would
not hold so much as was represented - I weighed the iron out in barrels.
Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY. Mr. Parsonage, whose private residence
I do not know, represents the Phenix Iron Works-he was the sole
person who represented them up to 29th September he was also the
person who came in after I was a liquidating debtor-I undertook to pay
78. 6d. in the pound-I have not paid the last two instalments-my partner
is not in prison-I think you are on the wrong tack-I suppose he is at
home-complaints have not been made against me from different people
that I am aware of-I am not aware of complaints of our weigh-bridge
until I heard of it at the police-court-I have had no disputes as to over-
charging—Mr. Bowman has not charged me with getting money out of
him for weights which were false-I know him-he may have had a
dispute with my partner, Mr. Whitling-I did not owe Bowman anything
when I compounded we have passed this concern over to Mr. Parsonage
-Whitling and I are still managing it-I had not paid the 901. when the
invoice was brought to me-I think the invoice shows that it was paid on
the 19th-no; I suppose it was paid a week or nine days before, but that
was not before the invoice was brought-the 901. was paid on account of
the balance afterwards our works are at Millwall-I never saw the barge
till the day before the invoice was brought it was alongside the wharf
on the 19th-I never pay by cheque; always in cash-we have no bank-
ing account-we weighed the cast iron turnings, the weight tallied, and
then we paid the amount-I never knew that Porter was getting a profit
out of us—I was present on the 15th, when he admitted that there was a
balance to come to us for insufficient weights, but on the 28th he again
said that the weights were correct-he said so all along, but yet he agreed
to pay the difference-I believed on the 28th that we had been robbed all
through-there had been only from four to six transactions between us
and Porter before I have not sworn that there were only three-I did
swear“I have had two or three transactions with Porter "-the last was
two or three months ago, about May last, but that meant since our failure,
since Mr. Parsonage was introduced-we failed two years ago, and I
think we have had two transactions with Porter since.
Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Mr. Whitling is the City

not call ourselves partners-he has the lease of the works-my wages are
101. a week, and Mr. Whitling's the same-sometimes we take it weekly
and sometimes monthly; we have no regular account-we go through
our accounts at 60, Gracechurch Street, I have not the wages-book here,
