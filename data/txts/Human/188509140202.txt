Other Counts  for conspiracy.( GIOVANNI DOMPE was also charged in the indictment together with the prisoners, but the COMMON SERJEANT quashed the indictment as against him.
   MESSRS. GILL and BODKIN Prosecuted. LOUIS ARISTIDES SINDOUX DUTAP( Interpreted ). I am a journalist, for merly of Namur, now living at 32 1/2, Greek Street—I saw an advertise ment to the effect, but not in the same words, as this one. ( This stated that servants of both sexes were wanted for England, that English was not necessary, and that expenses would be refunded; application to be made to Barnard,  21 A, Noel Street, Berwick Street, London;  30 centimes to be enclosed for an answer . ) I wrote to Barnard and received this letter in reply. ( This had a printed heading, French Registry Office, &c., J. Bar  nard, director, 21, Noel Street, and stated that they had several situations as professors in schools, with salaries from  100 to  200 francs per month, with board and lodging, or as French correspondent with  75 to  300 francs a month, or as correspondence clerk and bookkeeper at a firm of diamond merchants at  400 a month, and requesting him to send references and  10 francs for inquiry fee, and adding they would charge  5 per cent. on the first year's salary as commission . ) I sent references and 10 francs to Barnard, and received this letter from him dated 10th July. ( Stating that in eight days they would be able to put him into communication with the diamond merchants who required a clerk and bookkeeper, and who they hoped would accept his services . ) In consequence of that I came over to England on 25th July, and went to 21, Noel Street two or three days after my arrival, but saw none of the prisoners there—I was told to call again, and I went again next day, when I saw the three prisoners—I asked for Mr. Barnard; Launet came forward as if he were he—he said the place for which I came had been taken two or three days before, but that they had a place at that moment which would suit me, and to which I should be introduced; that was to be the director of Mr. Dompe's club at 40, Charlotte Street, near Fitzroy Square—I went there with Linden, who called himself Durant, and after having a glass to drink he introduced me to Dompe—after doing so he said, "Now I have intro duced you to Dompe, and if you suit him we shall speak of the matter to-night"—we did not speak of business that day, but next day I went again with Linden to the place, and then Linden spoke to Dompe by himself, after which he informed me that Mr. Dompe expressed now some objection to my getting the place, and that he asked a deposit of 10,000 francs, but that through his intervening with Dompe on my behalf Dompe would be satisfied with 3,000 francs—I told him I would write home for the 3,000 francs and try to get them—Linden said, "Remain here and write the letter," and then he left—I afterwards went to Noel Street, where I saw Linden, and said Dompe did not require a servant, but somebody to take charge of the club, with 3,000 francs deposit—Linden said, "If your father should object to pay the money from hand to hand without depositing it at a bank, let your father
