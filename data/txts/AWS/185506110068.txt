and entering the dwelling house of William Phillips, in the parish of St.
Luke, and stealing therein 1560 scarfs, value 3111., and 144 lbs. weight of
silk, value 1971.; the property of said William Phillips and others.
MEssrs. BODKIN and RIBToN conducted the Prosecution.
WILLam PHILLIPs. I live at No. 4, South-place, Finsbury, and am a
silk manufacturer ; there are two houses adjoining each other; I reside in
one, and the business is carried on in the other; there is a communication
internally. On Friday night, 13th April, I examined the premises-they
were all closed up and fastened-next morning my man sent a message, and
I went and found the next house in confusion-the door of the weavers'
entrance was unfastened, and three other doors opened--the top room Was
kept open by a stool, it was never locked--the two rooms had been entered
by a centre bit from the inside--a person could get to the inside from the
window which opens on to the parapet-that window was open-the next
house but one was unoccupied at that time-persons could get to the roof of
the unoeeupied house, there is a parapet five feet nine inches wide from the
window of my house-there were footmarks in the gutter, and some tow
which had dropped off the thieves' feet-there were footmarks in the
warpers' room-the door of the warehouse had been broken open, the
drawers opened, and 130 dozen scarfs and 100 or 200 lbs weight ofsilk missing
amounting to between 5001. and 6001-I also missed from the warehouse a
canvas bag, containing waste-I had seen it the night before-it WaS partly
filled-the contents had been turned out on to the floor.
