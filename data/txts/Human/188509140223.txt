I live at 13, Surrey Road—on 6th May the prisoner came to lodge at my house, and his wife remained till July 1st, but he was taken before that—I gave him notice to leave, as he used to come home at 2, 3, and 4 a.m.—when the police came they found two house-breaking jemmies.
   Cross-examined.  He never wore a blue serge coat or jacket to my knowledge, nor have I seen his hand tied up with a bandage.
   Re-examined.  He went in and out for days together without my seeing him at all—I sometimes saw them pass the kitchen area. ALFRED WARD( Police Sergeant L ). On June 27th, before 1 a.m., I saw the prisoner at the corner of George Street, Black friars Road, with another man; they looked up at a pawnshop door and at the window, but on seeing me they moved away rapidly, and we lost sight of them—I saw them again at 1.15 at the corner of Surrey Road—Grey and I went to them; Grey got hold of one, and I got hold of Barber, and said "I am a police officer, and I take you in custody for loitering, supposing you to be about to commit a felony"—he said "You have made a mistake; I am going home"—I asked his address; he refused it—I said "We happen to know your address from having watched you on Wednesday night"—I went with Boswell and Grey to 13, Surrey Road, and found a box, which I unlocked with one of four keys which I found on the prisoner, and which was indicated as being his by a prostitute with whom he lived.
   Cross-examined.  I have been over 17 years in the police—I have not often seen a jemmy  like this; it is of superior manufacture—Jenkins, who was with him, has been dealt with in another place, and sent to prison for not reporting himself. FRANCIS BOSWELL( Detective Sergeant L ). I was with Ward when he took the prisoner—I went with him and Grey to the prisoner's lodgings, and found three jemmies  and two door wedges, a knife, a hammer, and some plated spoons—I opened the box with a key which Ward handed to me, and saw another box with a number of pawn-tickets in it—I returned to the station, and said to the prisoner "What is your name and address?"—he refused to give it—I pointed to the tools—he said "You have helped me, go and help yourself." GUILTY. He then PLEADED GUILTY ** to a conviction of felony at this Court in the name of James Reid in June, 1878.— Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
