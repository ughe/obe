JOHN WHEBLE (police-constable R 300.) On the 26th of Aug., at half
past six in the morning. I was going home from off duty, and I met the pro-
secutor in the Greenwich-road-he could give me no description of the
prisoner, but said if he saw her he should know her-I asked if he could
show me the house where they had been, and he did-he said he had been
robbed of 158.-I inquired of the persons who keep the house where they
slept, and they told me where the prisoner lodged.
Cross-examined. R. Did you go to the prisoner's house then ? A. Yes
-I told her what she was charged with she showed me a work-box, and
produced some money-she said it was hers, and all she had it was 138. 2d.
-she put it into her pocket, and I did not feel at liberty to take it.
REBECCA BENSON. I searched the prisoner, and found on her three half-
crowns, four shillings, two sixpences, and two fourpenny pieces.
Prisoner's Defence. He gave me 58., and I had put 8s. by for my rent; I
told him I could not take him home as I lived with my brother; the week
before that my brother earned 168., and since he has been a widower he gives
me his money to keep; the prosecutor never counted his money before me;
we went up to the Rose and Crown, and had some gin and shrub; I had to
get up in the morning to get my brother's breakfast, and I left the prosecutor
at four o'clock; when the policeman came I went down in my flannel petti-
coat and let him in, and gave him the money that I had.

2359. JOHN DODD was indicted for stealing 1 watch, value 41. ; 4 watch
keys, 1l. ; 2 watch-guards, 1l. ; 1 pair of boots, 58.; the goods of James
Fortune, and I cap. 48.; the goods of Edward Jessop.
JAMES FORTUNE. I am a bombardier, of the Royal Artillery. On the 16th
of Sept. I left my barrack-room, at Woolwich, at half past one o'clock-I
had a box in my room which had my watch, and keys, and guard in it-I
locker, my box-when I returned about three o'clock, my box was broken
open, and my watch was gone I went out of the barracks, and overtook the
prisoner-I said to him, "Jack, give me my watch"-he said nothing, but he
gave it to me-he bad got half way between Woolwich and Greenwich-he
told me his reason for taking it was that I had vexed him I had not vexed
him, nor said anything to him.
EDWARD JESSOP. I am a gunner of the artillery. I left the barrack-room
about two o'clock that day-I left my cap in the barrack-room-I returned
about four, and my cap was gone-this is it-it has my name written in it.
DUNGAL M'CLEOD DAVIDSON (police-constable R 289.) I took the prisoner
- I told him his brother soldiers gave him in charge-he said, "You may
take me"- I took him, and then he struck me several blows on the head, and
kicked me very violently I called for assistance, and we took him to the
