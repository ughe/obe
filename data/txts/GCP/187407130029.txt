JAMES HOLMES. I am manager of the Old Rose public-house, St. George's
Street on the night of 12th June the prisoner and deceased were there
drinking together with a third man- they were quarrelling-I told them
to go outside, which they did I stood at the door to keep them from
coming in-I saw the deceased strike the prisoner two or three times and
want to fight him; they were not violent blows--the prisoner said if he
gave him one blow that would be enough for him, and he hit him one blow
under the nose on the lip; he fell and caught his head against the side of
the kerb-I went in and saw no more--they had been drinking, but were
not drunk.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. The deceased had previously been
quarrelling with another man-I did not hear you say "What is the use
of two old pale quarrelling"-I did not hear you call for a pot of beer and
say “You had better quarzel with that I would not serve you nor the
other houses either the deceased went on the top of the hill, and said
"Come on, you are no man if you don't put up your duke that was
before you struck him-you were on the lower part-there are stones there
sticking out of the ground-he took every advantage of you.
| JOSEPH BRANSDEN. I am a cheesemonger, of 58, St. George's Street
on 12th June, about 8.15, I was in the Old Rose, and saw the prisoner and
deceased there and another man who the deceased wanted to guarrel with,
in faot they were all quarrelling together I went away and returned again
a little after 9 d'olook-the prisoner and deceased were then outside the
house quarrelling again-the other man had gone away the deceased
struck the prisoner three or four times, and wanted him to go in the road
and fight the prisoner eventually went into the road, it is a steep hill-
they put up their arms to spar, and the prisoner hit the first blow, hit the
the deceased under the nose, and knooked him down, his head struok
against the kerb, and there he lay insensible,
Cross-examined. The stones are very awkward there it was not dark;
there was plenty of light from the public-house-I don't suppose you wished
to fight, but you did you struck him, and he fell straight down with his
head against the kerb, and there he lay as if he was dead.
WILLIAM LYTHR (Policeman K 325). On the morning of 13th June,

him with assaulting the man-he knelt down on his knees and kissed the
