Cross-examined. 6. How long were you outside the door A. Six or
seven minutes-nine persons went in during that time I went in a minute
or a minute and a half after Marks-he closed the door and I remained
outside-he then opened it and I went in-I may have stopped in the shop
two minutes-Dobinson said, "Do not stand there; come in and shut the
door "-that was not to let the police see that he was drawing beer, I
suppose--I had never seen him before I came out, leaving Marks behind
-Marks said to me, “Will you have half-a-pint of beer!"-I declined the
invitation and went outside - I saw nobody buying apples or eating them
I did not ask for any tobacco -I did not hear Marks do so I do not see
anybody here to-day who went into the shop-Dobinson was fined 51, on
my evidence and 28. more-before I went in I was standing twelve or
fourteen feet from the shop--the people could not have seen me as they went
in-no trouble was made about my coming in--that was the first time I
had been in the shop, or that Marks had, to my knowledge--there were no
children there at all-I saw nothing of the sale of any ginger-beer-the
door was closed before I went in--the size of the shop is about ten feet, and
a portion of it is taken up by the counter, which is in the centre, running
from the street-door to the passage-I saw beer drawn---there were three
barrels standing there in the passage on the right as you go in--the parlour-
door is on the left and was out of my view--I saw the passage door--I will
undertake to say there was no woman at the parlour door Marks said
to me," Ned, will you have half-a-pint} -I said, "No," and went outside
--that was said in Dobinson's hearing-Marks was not above a minute in
the shop before he let me in the door was not closed after I got in- I did
not tell Dobinson when he came outside that we were going to have a
summons out—I knew none of the men-the case came here by Mr. Traill's
direction-we were ordered by our inspector to go before the Magistrate at
the police-court-that is all the account I can give you of how this prose-
cution was got up-no attorney was employed in it to my knowledge.
Court, Q. Were you and Marks examined before the Magistrate in the
prisoner's presence! 4. Yes, she heard what our evidence was.
autes Witnesses for the Defence.
GEORGE SMITH. I am a labourer in the service of Mr Thoms, a charcoal
manufacturer at Rotherhithe-on this Sunday, I saw the two policemen out-
side Dobinson's shopthey were not in uniform-I knocked at the door-it was
opened I believe by Mr. Dobinson, and I was let in-I asked for a penny-
worth of hair-oil-there were then other persons in the shop I cannot say
how many-I am positive there were not nine-I did not notice any
children-while I was there, Dobinson was called to the door, and one of
these men said, "Do you know you are doing wrong?"-they took out a card
or a pocket-book, and then I turned myself round to Mrs. Fuller and Mrs.
Dobinson, and said, “There are two policemen at your door”-I did not
hear Dobinson make any answer to that I saw no beer served while I was
there, or being drunk-I did not notice any barrels - there were jugs about,
