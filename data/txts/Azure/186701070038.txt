"In Chancery. In the matter of the Companies Act, 1862, and in the
matter of the Joint Stock Discount Company Limited. Deposition of
James Freeling Wilkinson, called on behalf of the liquidators ; sworn
the 17th October, 1866; filed 12th November, 1866: was the
managing director of the Joint Stock Discount Company Limited in
January, 1866. In the latter part of the month I resigned my offee of
managing director. In that month there had been rumours of the finan-
cial position of the company, and Messrs. Quilter, Ball, and co. had been
occupied in investigating the accounts of the company. The general
meeting Of the company was held on the 31st of January, 1866. I was
present. A committee of investigation was appointed at that meeting,
and after their appointment the committee had daily meetings, and so had
the board I believe. Johann Gustaf Carl Pontus Kleman was a customer of
the company, upon a loan and discount account, which account commenced
soon after the company formed. The nature of our dealings with him
wu that he would bring bills to us for discount like on ordinary merchant.
The company debited him with the cash advanced by the company on
bills or otherwise, and credited him with the bills when received, or any
cash he might pay. On the 31st of December, 1865, the company wu in
advance to Kleman to the extent of 30,0001. This was a balance unse-
cured and carried on to the next year. At the end Of January, 1866,
that balance had increased to the sum of 108, 7891. 48. Id., which was un-
secured. None of the advances made to Kleman after the 3rd Of February,
1866, were, I believe, brought specifically to the notice of the directors.
The advances appear in the loan ledger, marked T', and in the Advance
ledger, marked T5. The advances which had been made to Kleman
tween the 1st of January and the 1st of February, 1866, which increased
the balance to his debit to the sum of 108,7891. 48. Id., were not specifi-
cally brought before the board of directors, nor were any advances gene-
rally speaking brought before the board, but there was a book which
contained every eh-eque which was drawn, and for whom it was drawn,
always upon the board table ; in other words, every director Who signed
a cheque could know for whom it was signed. The directors were in the
habit of leaving cheques signed in blank, that is to say, without any num-
ber or amount being filled in, and without the name of any payee being
put on the counterfoil. Ordinarily the director who was in the weekly rota
for signing cheques when he came to the offce would go upstairs and
sign a lot of cheques,and then go away. I believe that the cheques for
the advances which appear to Kleman's debit in the loan ledger were
signed in the above manuer. I refer to the account Of Messrs. Capper,
Escombe, and Lawson, at page 226 of the loan ledger, and as to the item
on the debit side under date 28th August, 1865, I say that sum must have
been advanced to them with my knowledge and authority, but I do not
recollect for what purpose the advance was made. I had no account with
them personally, but I had done business with them as brokers. I can't
recollect that I was personally indebted to them in the year 1865. The loan
ledger was not nor were any books kept under my superintendence. On the
28th August, 1865, a cheque for 8601. was paid by the company's bankers
out of its funds. Looking at the counterfoil of the cheque No. 7660 at the
bankers' pass book, marked 5 L, and the account of Capper, Escombe, and
Lawson in the loan ledger, I have no doubt the sum of 8601. was paid to
Messrs. Capper, Escombe, and Co. by the company on the 28th August,
