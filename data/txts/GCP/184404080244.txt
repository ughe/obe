JANE ARNOLD. I live in Rope-yard-rail, Woolwich, and am nearly eigh-
teen years old. I have been keeping company with the prisoner nearly two
years-on the 3rd of April, about half-past nine o'clock, he came to my mo-
ther's house and stopped till half-past ten o'clock-when he left I followed
him to close the door-he said he was going home to his mother's at Poplar
on Thursday, and asked if I would write to bim-I said I would by Mon-
day or Tuesday-he wanted one before then, and asked if I had picked
up with any body else while he was away-I said, "No"-he said was I
going to give him up ?-I said I was-he wished me good night and went
away-he came again almost directly after, and knocked at the door-my
mother opened it, and asked him to come in-he said no, he wanted to speak
to Jane-I went out to him, and he said “You are going to leave me?"--I said,
"Yes, George"-he said, “Oh you are, are you?" and he up and hit me
with a knife across the neck-it was not a deep cut, it only just went through
the skin-1 was not much hurt-Mr. Downs attended me I was going to
give the prisoner up because he had got into bad company.
Prisoner. I had my arm on her shoulder; I was cutting my finger nails
with a knife, and in putting my arm out to prevent her falling it scratched her
neck. Witness. He would not have done it, only he was in liquor; I believe
it was not done intentionally, only to frighten
me
CATHERINE ARNOLD. Last Wednesday night my daughter, who is a
servant, came home about eight o'clock--the prisoner came about half-past
nine o'clock, and stopped an hour or so-he was in liquor I said, “ George,
we want to go to bed"- my husband had gone to bed-he jumped up and
went out, and my daughter also-they had some words outside, and my
daughter seemed very agitated when she came in-in two or three minutes
he knocked at the door, and said he wanted to speak to Jane--he was very
agitated, and looked very wild-I said, “George, come in"-he said, “No,
I want to speak to Jane"-my daughter went out-in a few minutes I heard
a scuffle, and heard her say, "Oh!" or "Oh mother!"-I jumped out of my
chair, and saw her throat was cut and bleeding-I ran to the door and said,
“Oh George, what have you done!"-I rushed in doors, bolted the door,
ran up stairs and told my husband-he ran down in his shirt, and went to
look for a policeman-a doctor was sent for the prisoner had always be-
haved very kindly before, and she is still very fond of him.
GEORGE Downs, surgeon. I was called in to examine the prosecutrix's
throat-there was a cut on the left side of the neck about two and a half inches
long, merely through the skin-it was quite superficial.
Amy HOOPER. I am single, and live in Rope-yard-rail. Between ten and
eleven o'clock at night the prisoner applied to me to lend him a knife, which
I did he was intoxicated - I did not know what he wanted it for it was a
small dinner-knife-I knew him before,
WILLIAM Watson, labourer. I met the prisoner just at the dock-yard
gate about half past eleven o'clock the morning after this, and asked what he
had been doing-he said he did not know-I asked what made him do it-
he said he did not know-then he said he meant to do it to himself, but the
knife turned.
WILLIAM GLADWIN ( policeman.) I apprehended the prisoner on Satur-
day, the 6th, two days after it happened-I told him it was on suspicion of
cutting a young girl's throat-he said it was done accidentally, he was stand-
ing at the door with her paring his nails with the knife, that he had his arm
round her neck, and hearing somebody coming he went to save her from fall-
ing and cut her neck.
