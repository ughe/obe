 2526. DENNIS M'CARTHY was indicted for receiving, on the   15th of Aug ., 6 balls of hemp, value 2 s  .; 1/4 lb. weight of nails, 2 d  .; 3 leather backs for boots, 2 s  .; 2 leather fronts for shoes, 1 s  .; and 1lb. weight of leather, 8 d  .; the goods of Henry Snead , of a certain ill-disposed person, well knowing them to be stolen. WILLIAM FREEMAN. I am shopman to Mr. Henry Snead, and live in Tot hill-street, Westminster—Took has been in the service—about ten o'clock on the 15th of August, I found a pair of soles in his trowsers when he was going home—I went with him to a house in Bedfordbury—the prisoner lives there—he opened the door—I went into a room on the first floor—the prisoner said it was his room—it was searched, and six balls of hemp, five soles, some nails, three boot-backs, and some other things found—I believe them to be my master's, but would not swear to them—the policeman continued the search, and was tying up these things—the prisoner said, "You have no oc casion to search any further, the devil a farthing's worth more is there in the place than what you have got"—I never saw him at my master's.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You only know these from circumstances? A.  Yes—I cannot say when I had seen them safe—8 d . is not much under the value of the soles to sell, but if I went to buy them, I should have to pay 14 d . or 16 d . for them. ROBERT TOOK. I am thirteen years old. I have been in the prosecutor's service—the prisoner met me one day in New-street, Covent-garden—he said, "Can you get me a bit of leather to mend my shoes with?"—I said, "I will get you a bit if I can"—he said, "Don't let any one see you"—next day I gave him a pair of soles—he gave me 8 d . for them—I then brought him a half sole—he gave me 4 d . for it, and said, "Try to bring me some more if you can"—I did so, and he gave me 6 d .—he repeatedly told me to get in re—all these things are Mr. Snead's property.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not he refuse to take goods of you? A.  No—I met him by the Boccius lamp—he kept a stall there—he knew my father was a shoemaker, and lived up a court there. WILLIAM CHAPLEN( police-constable B  118.) I found these things in the room.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 43.— Confined One Year .
 2527. WILLIAM NOCK was indicted for embezzlement. WILLIAM PRITCHARD. I live in High-street, Poplar—the prisoner was my shopman—when he sold things he ought to account to me directly, and enter it in a book—I was in the shop on the 21st of Aug.—he left about eight o'clock that morning—there is no entry of 7 s . 6 d . that day—nor have I had it.
   Prisoner.  I entered his service not on a salary, but to have ten per cent on the profits, which made me consider myself a sort of partner—and he agreed I should take the ten per cent, every week—a few weeks after I said I did not want it every week, and he authorized me, if I wanted money to take it, and put it down in the book. Witness.  He went away directly he bad re ceived this—he came back on the Wednesday evening drunk—I said, "About this 7 s . 6 d ."—he said, "It is all right, I always keep these clear"—I did not give him authority to take money—I had given him notice to leave, but his time had not expired.
