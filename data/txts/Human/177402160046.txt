For Dickenson.
 - Wilkinson. I am a printer: Dickenson was apprentice to me; he behaved extremely well; he had a very good character, and was extremely useful; he acted so politely and civilly to all the customers , that they all spoke of him with very great commendation. I was in the country for four months together, on account of my illness, and he had the principal management of my business, and behaved vastly well to the customers; he had not been absent but about six days during the time of this unfortunate accident.
 - Nowell. I have known him five years, he always behaved well, and had a good character.
 For Laremore.
 - Madden. I have known him from his infancy; he was an honest sober lad.
   John Prior. I live at Uxbridge ; I have known his father and him three or four years; he had always a good character.
 For Smith.
   Richard Bream. Smith lodged with me three years together; she always had a good character.
   Susannah Smith. She lodged with me half a year; she has a very good character; her mother goes out to nurse people in their illness, or lying-in, and she has an extraordinary character.
 For Garth.
 - Powell. I have known him some time, but his brother is more particularly my acquaintance; he had that opinion of him that he set him up in his business.
 For Norbury.
   Thomas Nowell. I have known her four or five years; I thought her a diligent honest girl; I never heard of any reflection upon her.
 - Norbury. I am her father; she never offended me before in her life; I wonder how she could be so deluded.

 182, 183. (L.)  WILLIAM OWEN and JAMES BUGDEN were indicted; the first for stealing 24 lb. of bohea tea, value 4 l. 4 s. the property of James Longden, Dec. 2d; the other for receiving the said goods, well knowing them to have been stolen. ++
   James Longden. I am keeper of the George inn; I had the custody of the tea and was answerable for it; the prisoner was a servant of mine at the time it was lost.
   Samuel Spencer. I directed my servant, John Rhodes, to carry 24 lb. of bohea tea to the George inn.
   John Rhodes. I did carry such a kind of package, and delivered it to Giles, the bookkeeper of the inn.
   Richard Weston. The prisoner being in custody owned having taken several parcels at different times, but particularly this 24 lb. he sa id he took it out of the George inn yard.
   Thomas Compton. I heard him confess that he took it out of the George yard.
 Prisoner's Defence.
 I never made any confession.
 OWEN,    Guilty. T.
 BUGDEN,    acquitted.
 184. (L.),  JOHN KIDWELL was indicted for stealing 18 lb. of beef, value 4 s. the property of George Tyler, Feb. 5. ++
   George Tyler. I am a butcher: I was informed the prisoner had taken some beef from my stand: I live in Leadenhall market.
   Catherine How. I saw the prisoner take a piece of beef from Mr. Tyler's stall; he was pursued and taken.
 The prisoner called three or four witnesses to his character who seemed to know but little of him. Guilty of stealing to the value of 10 d. W.
