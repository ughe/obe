   Q.  How would the assistance of counsel which had for its object the pre paration of that deed, interfere with the question, whether you should act for a party giving, and a party taking? A.  Because counsel were instructed to prepare a deed for all parties, which is very frequently the case in the pro fession.
   Q.  Did you ever say to Miss Constable, "You appear to be a stranger, have you no friend but this Blackwell; had you not better consult somebody on your own behalf?" A.  If I did not not say those very words, I did to the effect—I can go no further than my memorandum and my recollection—I asked her if she wished us to act for her, and she said, "Yes"—I am not sure whether I asked her if she wished anybody else to act for her—I cannot say—we did act for her afterwards—I made myself a trustee in the deed—I did not receive any money on its execution—I obtained money on it after wards, the costs of it—that was not 150 l .—I think the costs relating to the deed itself were about 60 l . or 80 l .—I forget the amount—there were some other costs—that money was paid on the completion of the security, from the prisoner to Newhall—I do not know who paid it—my clerk settled it—what hand actually paid the money, I do not know—the agreement was a security from the prisoner and his sister to Newhall, and on our handing over the deeds, we were paid our bill of costs.
   COURT . Q.  By whose direction did you hand over the deeds to Newhall? A.  As the solicitors of the prisoner and his sister—I have no written direc tions—it was a general direction when we were instructed to settle the agree ment—there was an agreement to deposit, to which Mr. and Miss Blackwell were parties, and Newhall—I have not got it—I placed this deed in New hall's hands when the agreement to deposit was executed.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  As between Blackwell and Newhall, you were not concerned for both those, I suppose? A.  No, we were concerned for Blackwell and his sister—the agreement was the authority for handing over this deed to Newhall.
   Q.  On your oath, had you not verbal instructions from the prisoner to hand over the deeds to Newhall, and among them this deed in blank? A.  Not from the prisoner alone, but from the prisoner and his sister, on the terms mentioned in that memorandum, which are very particular as to Miss Blackwell, because her interest is different, but still kept in view—Mr. New hall's solicitor prepared an agreement to deposit, as an equitable security on this property—we were instructed by the prisoner and his sister to deliver over the deeds to Newhall with the agreement, and not without it—we were not justified or authorised in parting with these deeds except with the agree ment, when the matter was closed—we were then justified in parting with them, by our instructions as solicitors to the prisoner and his sister—we had directions not to dispose of the deeds without the agreement—we were di rected to deposit the agreement and deeds also.
