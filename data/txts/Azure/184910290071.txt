1896. FREDERICK MILLS, feloniously uttering a forged order for
payment of 91. 5s., with intent to defraud John Von Der Hyde.
MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN VON DER HYDE. I am a tobacconist, of 80, Lower Thames-stret,
On Saturday, 22nd Sept., the prisoner came to my house—he said he wanted
we cigars to send to a friend in the country—I think he said at Birmingham
—I do not think he mentioned the party's name—I do not recollect what
quantity—they were not selected—this check was sent up to me—I came
down and saw the prisoner—I said it was not usual to take checks from a
stranger, Mr. Wallis and Mr. Tuke might be very good men, but I did
know them, neither did I know him, and therefore I could not take the
check—he said he knew the young man at the counter very well—that wu
Baker—I said, If Mr. Baker knows you, that is a different matter; I do not
know was not in the counting-house at the time—I went out
of the counting-house and spoke to Baker—I said, If you know him, and
know him to be a respectable man, and like to take the responsibility of the
check, it is all very well, but certainly shall not send the cheek
came into my possession.
Cross-examined by MB. MEW. Q. You were up-stairs, and the first inti-
nation you had of the prisoner being there, was the check being sent up ?
A. Yes; my clerk came up—I was not present when the prisoner asked for
tbe cigars—I do not know what quantity he asked for—I did not intend to
rod the cigars, but I understood they were to be sent to a man in Crooked'
lane, whose name was Arrandorf or some such name, whom we did not bear
very favourable accoupt of afterwards—this is the check—it is made payable
to Mr. Tuke—if any person were to come to me who was a perfect stranger,
and offer me a cheek, I would not take it.
ROBERT BAKER. I am sbopman to Mr. Von Der Hyde. On Saturday
morning, 22nd Sept., I saw the prisoner there—be told me he wanted from
ten to fifteen pounds of cigars—he produced the cheek, and wanted me to give
him the difference—I referred him to the counting-house—be mentioned tbav
he knew me, and Mr. Von Der Hyde came and asked me if I knew him—
I said, Yes, when I was living io the Borough six years and a half ago ; he
vas a customer of mine for tobacco and Von Der Hyde said if
I liked to give him the change I might, and I gave him ll. 148. dd., and took
the cheek—he told me to send the cigars to Messrs. Arrandorf, I think it was,
at No. 2, Crooked-lane—we did not send them, and the prisonerdid not come
to make any inquiries about them—I took the check to the banker's, gnd
was refused.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known the prisoner? I
should say about nine or ten years—I never heard anything against his
character—when he called at our house he had a letter in his hand, I did
not look at it—I cannot say that I know his handwriting—I cannot say
whether this check is his writing; he gave an address at the counting-house
—l did not make any inquiries at that address—I believe there was a person
came into the counting-house who said that that was his address.
