MARY JANE BONNER. I am barmaid at St. George's Tavern. On Wed nesday,24th Feb., Dutton came in for half a pint of porter—I served her; she tendered a bad sixpence—Mr. Hill came in at the side door, and I bent the sixpence with my teeth, tod told Dutton it was bad—Mr. Hill took her by the shoulder, and I put the sixpence on a sideboard, and afterwards gave it to Hornsley. JAMES HORNSBY( Policeman, L  193). I was sent for from St. George's Tavern, and Dutton was given into my custody—I found two shillings and a halfpenny, in good money, in her hand—I told her she was charged With uttering a bad sixpence—she said that "she was not aware it was bad"—I received from Mr. Hill two bad sixpences, one of which was broken—I saw 7 1/4 d . in good money, found on Dutton at the station. WILLIAM PALMER( Policeman, L  125). On Wednesday, 24th Feb., I was called by Mr. Hill, and took West—I took from her left hand sixpence—goingalong, she was rifling her hand in her bosom very much—the sergeant came up, and took a black bag from her right hand—I asked her where she got it from; she said that Dutton gave her 2 1/2 d ., to mind the bag for her at the Elephant and Castle. GEORGE RUDD( Policeman, L  9). I assisted in taking West, who was struggling with Palmer—Mr. Hill took hold of her left hand, and took this bag from it, containing fourteen counterfeit sixpences; eight were sealed up in a piece of paper with this postage stamp, and the others in a paper which was broken opean at one end—she said nothing—I took a basket from her, containing a penny leaf, two ounces of Epsom salts, two German sausages, and two black puddings. WILLIAM WEBSTER. These two sixpences are counterfeit—in this parcel of fourteen, here are seven of 1846, all of one mould, and from the same mould as one of those uttered by Dutton, and eight of 1856, from one mould, and from the same mould as the other uttered by Dutton.
   West's Defence.  The bad money was given to me by Dutton.
   WEST  — GUILTY.**— Confined Two Years .
