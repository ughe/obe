JOHN CHAMBERS was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of November , 1 pair of trowsers, value 3 s  ., 1 waistcoat, value 2 s  .; 1 shirt, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; shawl, value 3 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  .: the goods of William Peatling , and that he had been before convicted of felony. MARY PEATLING. I am the wife of William Peatling; we live in Caro-line-street, Stepney, and let ready-furnished lodgings. The prisoner came and took a lodging for a constancy on the 28th of November, he took it by the week, and stated that he worked at Limehouse—he was only there one night—he left on the 29th—as he did not return in the afternoon, I went into the room, and missed the articles stated—I have never seen them since. WILLIAM SLADDEN( police-constable K  50.) I took the prisoner on an other charge, on the 31st of December, at the East India Docks—I men tioned this robbery to him—he said he knew nothing about it. JAMES MALIK( police-constable K  99.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—( read )—theprisoner is the person—I was at the trial. GUILTY. Aged 23.— Transported for Fourteen Years. (There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)
 637. WILLIAM CAPES was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of January , 2 chairs, value 1 l  . 2 s  ., the goods of John Owen . GEORGE TREW( police-constable H  125.) On the 12th of January, about six o'clock at night, I met the prisoner at the corner of Old-street-road carrying these two chairs on his head—I asked where he was going to take them—he said, to a man down Shoreditch, but he did not know his name nor where he was. THOMAS OWEN. I live with my brother, John Owen—his shop is in Old-street-road—these two hall chairs are my brother's—I saw them safe in the shop at five o'clock, and missed them soon after six.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 22.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Six Weeks.
