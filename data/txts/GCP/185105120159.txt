Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. What did you do with the horse and
cart? A. I went up and down the market; I let it stand at the Bull nearly
an hour I was commissioned to buy it for Mr. King-he gave me 71. ; I got
it for 61. 158.-Mr. King afterwards sold it to Mr. Barker,
Mr. Cockle to John Fitch. Q. Describe the cart that you saw? 4.
It was dark-green, picked with white on the wheels—there was a game on
the shaft, but I could not read-it was a smallish grey pony-I was spoken
to about it on the Monday following a great many had put up at our house
between the Wednesday and the Monday.
JAMES Coomes. I am a horse-dealer. I was at Romford Market on 30th
April- I wrote a receipt that day-Bray was present when I wrote it-be
gave the name of John Williams, and I wrote it on the receipt-he gave his
address, Adam's-place, Southwark-I said Southwark was a large place, what
street was it Bear?-he said Redcross-street-I saw the horse, it was a grey
borse, and the name of John Court was on the shaft of the cart.
ROBERT FENNING (police-serjeant, M 4). On 30th April, 1 took Bangham
in charge for buying a pony and cart, knowing them to be stolen-I found
on bim this receip-on Friday, 2nd May, I took Chamberlain- I told bim
the charge was for being concerned with Bray in selling a pony and carthe
said he got his living in a different way from that-I found the horse and
cart in possession of Mr. Standford, in Kingsland-road-Mr. Court told me it
was there, and he went with me.
Cross-examined by Mr. COCKLE. Q. When did you receive information ?
4. On Wednesday, from Mr. Court, jun. I did not then learn Chamberlain's
address, I discovered that the following morning-he was not in custody-I
took him at his own door, on the Friday-I believe he is a housekeeper, be
keeps a private bouse-I went in the house, but not to the back premises
be has been out on bail.
Thomas RICHARDS (police-serjeant, M 10). I apprehended Bray, on 2nd
May, in the Mint, on a charge of stealing a horse and cart, and selling it-ho
said he never sold one, he never bad one to sell.
JAMES COOMES re-examined. This is the receipt I wrote (read—“Re-
ceived, April the 30th, 1851, of Mr. John Bangham, the sum of 6l. 158., for
a grey pony, a cart, and a harness. John Williams, his mark.")
JOHN DELANEY (police-serjeant, M 18). I produce a certificate of Bray's
former conviction-(read---convicted August, 1849, and confined six months)
he is the person-I have known him these five years— he was tried in this Court
on 19th Aug. last, and acquitted. (Chamberlain received a good character.)
BRAY-Guilty. Aged 26.- Transported for Ten Years.
GHAMBERLAIN-Not Guilty.
