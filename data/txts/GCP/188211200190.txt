ARTHUR EDWARD TWIDDY. I live at 211, High Street, Borough, and
am a wholesale provision merchant-John Waite Bendall was formerly a
customer of mine--on 10th June, 1881, I was managing the business;
he owed my father money, and in liquidation assigned him the lease of
the house in question in Crystal Palace Road, formerly called the Ross-
more Road- the assignment is endorsed on an agreement for a lease
between the British Empire Company and John Waite Bendall for 99
years, I think, from year to year for 15 years, and then at the expiration of
that time the tenant may have the option of a lease for 84 years. (One of the
clauses of the agreement was read, stating that Bendall was not to carry on any
trade, business, or manufacture without the consent of the company in writing.)
Mr. Bendall carried on the business of a grocer and cheesemonger on
those premises, and used to sell bottled beer to be consumed off the
premises—I had supplied him with the groceries—he left the premises;
I cannot say when-I endeavoured to let the premises before he went-
I advertised them for sale, and spoke to Johnson about them-Johnson
called on me about the end of March last year, and said he had called
about the shop in the Crystal Palace Road -he said he wanted to make
it a central depôt for bottling beer, as he had a number of off-licence
houses, and would supply them from there I told him he could not open it
as a beershop, as we could have let it to Mr. Miller only the British Empire
would not strike the clause out of the original lease--I said he could
carry on a bottled-beer trade there the same as Bendall, and in con-
junction with a grocer's business—he did not say he wanted it for a
beershop-nothing was said with regard to draught beer-I showed him
the agreement with the superior landlord-he read it, and said it was
quite satisfactory-he read it right through-I don't know that I pointed
out that particular clause-I did not give my consent to a beer-engine
