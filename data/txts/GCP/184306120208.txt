1966. WILLIAM McNESS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of
April, 6 spoons, value 31., the goods of Thomas Quarterman.
GEORGE KILNER. I am waiter at the York hotel, Bridge-street, Black
friars, kept by Mr. Thomas Quarterman. The prisoner came there on the
24th of April, between three and five o'clock, and had a glass of wine and a
biscuit-he took his seat at a corner table—the dinner table was laid, and
these six large spoons were on it.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. What do you know these spoons
by? A. By the mark of “N” on them, the initial of the late landlord of the
hotel-I do not know what his name was—I do not know them by any other
mark-they are the spoons that were used in the house.
CHARLES WILLIAM PARR. I am in the service of Mr. Brown, a pawa-
broker, in Fetter-lane—these spoons were pawned there on the 24th of April,
between four and six o'clock, by the prisoner-I am sure he is the
person,
Cross-examined. Q. Did you say before the Magistrates, that he was the man
who pawned them ? A. Yes~(looking at his deposition)-it was not stated that
the prisoner pawned them-I have not said since I have been here that if
any body would give me 31. I would go away-I have mentioned about 31.-
I did not mark the words I said.
JAMES TIDMARSH (police-constable M 51.) I produce these six spoons
marked with a cross on the back, and the letter "N” in front- I found them at
Mr. Quarterman's hotels-four at one hotel and two at another.
GEORGE TOMKINS (police-constable M 166.) I received charge of the
prisoner, on the 26th of May, for stealing plate.
Not GUILTY.
Jury half Foreigners.
