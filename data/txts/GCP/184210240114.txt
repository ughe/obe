2888. WILLIAM LOVELL was indicted for stealing, on the 12thd
October, 4 bushels of oats in the chaff, value 108.; the goods of Joke
Grove.
John Grove. I live at Harmondsworth. About five o'clock on Wed.
nesday evening, the 12th of October, I was in my barn- I had four
bushels of oats safe in a sack then-they were black oats-they were
threshed, but not cleaned-there were garlic-seeds and other seeds among
them-I missed the oats and sack next morning these are the oats.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Garlic is a flower which
grows in almost every oat-field in the country? A. It grows in a great
many--these oats are my own growing.
Thomas DUGGIN (police-sergeant L 28.) I received information, and
went to the prisoner's stable about twelve o'clock on Saturday night, the
22nd of October-he returned from London with his cart while I was there
- he is a jockey-man-I asked him if he had heard of the prosecutor losing
a quantity of oats-he said he had-I asked if he had any objection to my
looking into his stable--he said he had not-I asked if he had got my
oats-he said he had not--I searched some time, and he came and turned
a truss of hay off this sack of oats I asked what he had got in the sack-
he said mixture for his horses--I put my hand in, and took a sample out
-I had got a sample at the prosecutor's, and they seemed to me to be the
same-I asked where he got the oats-he said, between a peck and small
bushel he got from an ostler at the Chaise and Horses at Hounslow-I asked
if they were black oats-he said yes I asked where he got the remainder-
he said he bought a sack at Mr. Warne's, a corn-chandler, at Brentford-
I left him in charge, and went to the ostler, and then to Mr. Warne.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe with the oats you found a mixture de
beans and bran? A. Yes-the prisoner keeps horses-from a bashel to
bushel and a half was found in the sack.
JOHN WARNE. I have seen the oats produced-I did not sell them
the prisoner-I have not sold him any since the 12th-I sold him a sack
on the 5th or 6th-I am sure these are not what I sold him.
Cross-examined. Q. Has he been a customer of yours? A. He may,
but not very often-I have not had this description of oats in my posset
sion for twelve months.
John BURTON. I sold the prisoner a feed of corn which had white ose
in it.
Cross-examined. Q. I suppose black oats occasionally come into your
possession ? A. Yes I am ostler boy-we have our corn at Mr. F)
bread's-1 sell these oats on my master's account-I pay my master.
NOT GUILTY.
