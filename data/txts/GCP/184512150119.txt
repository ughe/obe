Bridget Lloyd's Defence. My mother-in-law asked me to go down the
street to get an iron belonging to a lodger; she had a cloth cap with a gold
band, which she pledged, and treated me to some gin; she afterwards got
another cap-when we got to Wellington-street, she said, "Stop till I come
back;" she went away, and returned with these things rolled up under her
arm; she said she got them from a woman she had sold the cap to; she gave
them to me to carry, and the policeman came up as she was going to take
them from me again ; the prosecutrix can speak to my character.
MRS. MITCHELL re-examined. I knew her four years ago, in Ireland, in a
respectable situation, and she bore a good character; I have not known her
since.

316. SARAH MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of Dec.,
8lbs, weight of beef, value 5s., the goods of George Peppercorn.
JOHN JONES. I am in the service of George Peppercorn, a butcher, in
Broadway, Deptford. On Friday, the 12th of Dec., about three o'clock in
the afternoon, I saw the prisoner take a piece of beef off the block in the
shop, and put it under her shawl-I knew her before, and am sure she is the
person-there was no one else in the shop-I called Grace--we went after
her, and brought her back--she took the beef from under her shawl, and
asked Grace to forgive her.
GEORGE GRACE. I went after the prisoner, and brought her back to the
shop-she had a piece of beef concealed under her shawl-it had been in the
shop, and was my master's property.
MICHAEL THOMAS (policeman.) I took her into custody - she said she had
not taken the heef.
(The prisoner begged for mercy.)
GUILTY. Aged 30,- Strongly recommended to mercy.--Confined Seven Days.
Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
