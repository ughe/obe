 2112.  ANN FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; 2 shillings, and 1 sixpences, the property of James King.
   ANN KING. I am the wife of James King, a tailor- we live in Long-alley, Moorfields. I had got a little drop to drink, and got into the watch-house; the prisoner was there, and stripped these things off me - I saw the apron on her.
   WILLIAM BARTLET. I am a Policeman. I was at the watch-house; the prosecutrix was brought in drunk; I took the prisoner there about noon, for a riot, and breaking a window; she had been drinking - when I let the prosecutrix out she complained that the prisoner had her apron on, and she missed a handkerchief - the prisoner gave her the apron, and she took the handkerchief out of the prisoner's bosom.
 Prisoner. I picked it up after the prosecutrix went out- four of my family have died since I have been here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1930.  JOHN FRANCIS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 2 pewter pots, value 18d., the goods of Andrew Morris ; and I pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of George Kerridge.
   RICHARD MORRIS. I keep a public-house in Piccadilly. I found two pots at the office, with my name and sign on them.
   GEORGE KERRIDGE. I keep a public-house in Belgrave-square. I found the prisoner in custody with a pot of mine.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I am an officer of Marylebone. On the 8th of August, about seven o'clock in the morning, I received information, and watched by a cellar in Carey-street, St. Giles' - I saw the prisoner go to the cellar, pull the flap open, and go down; I went down after him - I could hardly, see it was so dark - I said, "What is your name?" I believe he said Francis - I said, "Come to the light" - he threw his hat off, and I found two pewter pots in it; I found a string tied round his waist, with another pot suspended to it, concealed; I proceeded to search the cellar, and he tried to escape - I pulled him back, and in the cellar found this frying-pan, with metal sticking to it.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2060.  FREDERICK FINLEY and ANN, his wife, were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , 2 pairs of shoes, value 2s., and 2 caps, value 1s. the goods of John King.
 JOHN KING. I am a  dealer in marine-stores, on Saffron-hill. I missed these things from my shop, on the 18th of August - I had seen them two or three days before; the prisoners lodged in my house, and slept in a little parlour, and would pass through the shop to go out - they lodged about three weeks with me; I found the things at Loscombe's, No. 113, Saffron-hill, on the 20th, and knew them by the marks - they are children's caps; one pair of shoes is sown all roundthe other has a patch in the heel.
   Frederick Finley. Q. Did you ever go by any other name? A. I went by the name of Wilson, in the army, for twenty-five years; I am illegitimate, but that was my father's name - I receive a pension in that name, but I was Christened by the name of John King; I was bail for Mr. Pollock in the court of King'sbench, for 35l., - I can bail for 500l. if I choose; I had lost a quantity of goods, and refused to let you into my house that night, but told you to go to bed, and next morning you left; you once lost your child, and I said it was probably through your own drunkenness.
   ELIZA LOSCOMBE. I live at No. 113, Saffron-hill. On the 16th of August the male prisoner sold me a pair of shoes for 5d., and on the 18th another pair for 5d.; I received three child's caps, a woman's cap and tippet from the female prisoner for 8d.
 Prisoner. Q. Are your goods exposed for sale? A. Yes, outside and inside the shop; I live thirty-six doors from King.
   JOHN GOODMAN. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 21st of August; the man said he did not care, he should easily get over it - I found them in an uninhabited house, in Castle-street, Saffron-hill.
   Frederick Finley. Q. How was it uninhabited? A. It was not rented by any tenant; a person had leave to be in it, and he was cohabiting with her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   Frederick Finley's Defence. I have endeavoured to elicit from the prosecutor, the fact of a serious quarrel between us, in consequence of his accusing me of having intentionally lost my child - in consequence of this, I availed myself of the information I had of his having bailed Pollock in the name of King, and justifying in the name of Wilson, and informed Mrs. Pollock of it, and she refused to pay him 10s. for having been bail; I believe she wrote to say, if he attempted to make the claim, she would see into it - he received the letter on the Monday, and made this charge; I admit selling the things, but distinctly at his request; his wife as he calls her, not supplying him with funds to procure liquor; if I had stolen them, I should not have sold them so near his house.
   FREDERICK FINLEY. -   GUILTY Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
   ANN FINLEY. -   NOT GUILTY.
