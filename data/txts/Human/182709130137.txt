 SEVENTH DAY. THURSDAY, SEPTEMBER 20.
 Third Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1919.  GEORGE PATTERSON and JAMES WOODYER were indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 35lbs. weight of lead, value 5s., the goods of the   St. Katherine Dock Company , and fixed to a certain building.
   WILLIAM WINTER. I am a rope-maker. On the 17th of August, about a quarter before nine o'clock in the morning, I saw Patterson take this lead off an empty house in Star-court, and put it in at the window. I did not see any other person.
   MICHAEL HARRAGAN. I live at a public-house near St. Katherine's Docks. On the 17th of August I saw Patterson take the lead off the top of the house, No. 9, Star-court; he said to me, "Go round, and see if you can see any body;" I said, "No, I will go and see if I can see any body to take you;" I told Sitgrave.
   WILLIAM SITGRAVE. The house this lead was taken from was in possession of the St. Katherine Dock Company. Harragan came and told me he saw the lead taken- I tried the front door, and could not get in - it was bolted - I ran round to the back door, and saw the two prisoners about fifteen yards from the back door. I secured them both; Patterson said I was no officer, and should not hold him; if I let him he would walk quietly; I let go, and he ran away - I followed, and took him; I went on to the house, and found this piece of lead doubled up in the parlour. I compared it with the top of the dormer - it fitted exactly; Woodyer was there, but did nothing.
 PATTERSON'S Defence. I was not in the house at all - I was going to work; this man came and asked if I had seen the master come down.
 PATTERSON -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 WOODYER -    NOT GUILTY.
 1920.  ANN RANDALL was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of September , 1 coat, value 12s.; 1 waistcoat, value 3s., and 1 pair of breeches, value 15s., the goods of William Halt.
   SARAH HALT. I am the wife of William Halt; we live in Turnmill-street. On Saturday night, at eleven o'clock, I put his clothes in the passage; and missed them when he asked for them on Sunday - the prisoner visited one of the lodgers.
   JANE BROWN. I keep a hair-dresser's shop. On Sunday morning, about seven o'clock, the prisoner came and asked if her husband had been to get shaved; I said I could not tell; she asked if she might leave a bundle till he came, as it was his clothes, which he wanted to take into the country; but if he was in liquor, I was not to give them to him - she put them on the bench; she afterwards came with the officer, and had them away.
   CHARLES VINCENT. I am an officer. Halt sent for me- I took charge of the prisoner; she took me to Brown's, where I found the clothes; she said she took them out of a lark to bother them.
 Prisoner. I only did it to frighten Mrs. Halt, as I thought it a wrong place for them.
 SARAH HALT. I offered her 5s. to tell me about them, and she said she knew nothing about them.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Confined One Month.
 1921.  ELIZABETH SELLIS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 blanket, value 7s., the goods of Henry Moriarty.
   JANE MORIARTY. I am the wife of Henry Moriarty; we live in Tash-street. On the 26th of July, about half-past ten o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner in our passage - I saw her take this blanket off a line in the passage, where it hung to dry - she put it under her shawl - I was going to take hold of her, and said, "You have stolen a blanket;" she used a bad expression, and said she had not got it. I stood talking to her some time - she then dropped it, and ran away - she was quite a stranger.
   MATHIAS WELHEN. I am an officer. I heard a cry of Stop thief! and saw the prisoner running - several people were following her. I secared her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I never saw it till it was in her hand.
     GUILTY. Aged 43. Transported for Seven Years.
 1922.  ANN (THE  WIFE OF ISAAC) SOLOMONS was indicted for that she, on the 28th of August , at Christ-church, feloniously did receive, of a certain evil disposed person, 1 watch, value 6l., the goods of Joseph Ridley , which said goods had been lately before, (by the said evil disposed person.) feloniously stolen, she well knowing the same to have been stolen, against the statute.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   JOSEPH RIDLEY. I am master of a ship. On the 17th of August I was robbed of a watch - I do not know the person who stole it - I am sure it was not the prisoner.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. Where did you lose it? A. I do not know - I lost other property at the same time - I had been drinking, and was off my guard.
