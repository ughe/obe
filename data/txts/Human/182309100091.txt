 1190.  JANE VANCE was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , five caps, value 5 s.; a ring, value 4 s.; a shirt, value 3 s.; a gown-body, value 2 s.; a half-handkerchief, value 2 d., and a piece of ribbon, value 6 d., the goods of George Huggett.
   SARAH HUGGETT. I am wife of George Huggett. We live in the Commercial-road. The prisoner was acquainted with me, and used to do needle-work in my front room. She was backward and forward every day. On the 9th of August I missed these things, and went with a constable to her lodgings, at Stepney. She has a blind mother, who she supports by her labour. I think she was in distress. I found three caps, a gown-body, and handkerchief there. She was crying all the time. I found a sheet in pawn.
   GEORGE RAVENOR. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Ratcliff, The prisoner pawned a shirt for 4 s.
   ELIZABETH MANN. I live in New Gravel-lane. The prisoner gave me two caps.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   CHARLES READ. I found the prisoner at her lodgings. There was every appearance of distress there.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.

 1192.  WILLIAM WARE was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , a bugle, value 50 s., the goods of William Dale.
   WILLIAM DALE. I am a musical-instrument seller, and live in the Poultry. On the 14th of August, at night, this bugle was in the shop. I missed it next morning about ten o'clock; and on the 21st found the prisoner at Benton's, in Holborn, and took him.
   JAMES BENTON. I am a pawnbroker. On the 15th of August, between eleven and twelve o'clock at noon, the prisoner pawned this bugle in the name of Ware, for 30 s. I never saw him before, but am certain of him. He came again, in about a week, to sell the duplicate to a person. Mr. Dale was there with the officer, and took him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM CULVERWELL. I am shopman to Mr. Dale I saw the bugle safe, near the door, at nine o'clock in the evening. A person must have got into the shop to get it. I missed it a few minutes after nine o'clock. I had seen a man about the shop for an hour and a half. He was of the same size and appearance as the prisoner.
   GEORGE SMITH. I keep a sale-shop in the New-cut. On the 20th of August, the prisoner offered to sell me the duplicate of a bugle for 10 s. He took me next morning to Benton's to see it.
   JOHN LIMBRIC. I went with Mr. Dale to the pawnbrokers. The prisoner said he bought it of a man in the street, and did not know where he lived.
 Prisoner's Defence. I became acquainted with a man at the Jerusalem Coffee-house. He brought me this bugle, covered with a handkerchief, and told me to pawn it for 30 s. He said he was going to sea, and the duplicate would be of no use to him. I might have it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1193.  THOMAS WIGGINS was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , a handkerchief, value 2 s. 6 d., the goods of Abraham Luck , from the person of Martha Luck.
   MARTHA LUCK, a child, twelve years of age, not appearing to understand the nature of an oath, was not examined.
   ABRAHAM LUCK. I am father of Martha Luck, and live in Charlotte-mews, Bloomsbury. I know nothing of this circumstance.
   DANIEL HONE. I am headborough of St. Giles's. On the 29th of July, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I heard the cry of Stop thief! in Castle-street, opposite my own door - I immediately looked out, and saw the prisoner running with a handkerchief in his hand; he ran about thirty yards, then threw it down, and the little girl ran to pick it up. I saw the prisoner stopped, and only lost sight of him as he turned the corner - he was the only person running. I brought him back to where the girl had picked the handkerchief up. She said in his presence that she had it loose round her neck, with her hand on it, and he took it off - he said he picked it up.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in Museum-street, and saw it on the ground; I picked it up, and heard the cry of Stop thief! dropped it, and ran to see where the thief was.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Life.
