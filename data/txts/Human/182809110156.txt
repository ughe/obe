 1851.  JANE GIBBONS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 purse, value 6d.; 3 half-crowns; 2 shillings, and 4 half pence, the property of Elizabeth Collins.
 ELIZABETH COLLINS. I live in  Crown-court, Strand. On the 7th of September, this money was all in a purse, on the table, in the lower room, when I went upstairs; a little after eleven o'clock at night; I sent my servant down before me, and she came up and said the purse was gone, and the prisoner likewise; she had been sitting in the same room with me.
   MARY THOMAS. I went up stairs with the prosecutrix; she sent me down for her gloves and purse; the purse, gloves, handkerchief, and the prisoner were all gone - the prisoner had been in the parlour with the prosecutrix: it is an hotel - there had been no other person there till the money was missed; there were no gentlemen in the parlour.
   SAMUEL GILES. I am an officer. I took the prisoner into custody, about twelve o'clock, on the night of the 7th of September; I asked what money she had got - she said only a few coppers in change for a sixpence; I searched her, and found a glove, with two half-crowns, two shillings, and five half pence in it - I took her in the same house; I asked her where she got this money, and she said at a house, next door to the Lyceum - I went there and saw two servants, but they said she had not been there: the prosecutrix told me that one of her half-crowns was a new one, and one of these is new.
 Prisoner. When I left the house I had only change for a sixpence, but I did not say when I came back that I had no more.
   SAMUEL GILES. I asked what money she had, and she said only a few coppers in change for a sixpence; and when I found the money, she said she had got it next door to the Lyceum.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, denying having had tne purse, and stating that she heard she she was accused, and immediately went to the house.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1852.  JOHN GILBERT was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of Alexander Bateman.
 ALEXANDER BATEMAN. On the 5th of August, my coat was on the top of a load of pots and pans, which I was driving; I had got to  Tottenhom-court-road, about half-past six o'clock in the morning - a man came and asked if we had any horse-cloths; I said "No, we have got some great-coats" - I then got up, and my coat was gone; he said "Come along with me," I went and saw the prisoner running with something under his arm - we followed him, calling Stop thief! he was stopped - I did not see my coat in his possession; he dropped it - I saw it again in about five minutes: Heaves picked it up and gave it to the watchman.
   WILLIAM HOOPER. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and saw the prisoner running before several people; he had nothing then, but they said he had stolen a coat - I stopped him.
   THOMAS HEAVES. On the morning in question, I was going down Tottenham-court-road; I saw the prisoner and another person standing against the shafts of this waggon- they crossed over, and I saw something under the prisoner's arm, which I thought was a horse-cloth; I asked the prosecutor if he had any horse-cloths - he said no, but he had some great-coats; he looked up and said there was one missing - we then followed the prisoner and his companion, who were going on towards the corner of Gower-street: the coat dropped from one of them, but I cannot say which - the prisoner's companion had something under his arm: I did not see them taken anything from the waggon - I picked the coat up; this is it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you ever known this boy before? A. No - I had lost sight of them.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Three Months.
 1853.  JOHN HEDGE was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 hammer, value 6d., and 1 trowel, value 1s. 6d., the goods of James Coker.
 JAMES COKER. I lost a trowel and hammer, on the 29th of August, from a house in  Brunswick-street, Hackney-road; while I was at work, I saw the prisoner come, between six and seven o'clock in the morning, and take them off the bench - he ran off towards Hackney-road; some of my friends stopped him - my tools were found on him: I was in the next house, and saw him through a hole in the wall, and pursued.
 Prisoner's Defence. I work for Mr. Pledge - I was walking up the street and saw a mob; I ran towards them, and a man came and asked if I were the thief - I said "I don't know what you mean," and as we were going along, a carpenter said "That is not the lad who dropped the tools, but I must take him back to Mr. Coker," who knows me very well, and has a spite against me.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Whipped and Discharged.
 1854.  THOMAS DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 12lbs. weight of ham, value 16s., the goods of Richard George Spice.
 RICHARD GEORGE SPICE. I am a  ham dealer. On the 21st of July I saw the prisoner take part of a ham from my shop - I gave an alarm, and he dropped it on the step of the door; he went out at one door and I at another and took him; he had run along the window, which is about a yard and a half.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not swear you could not positively say it was me? A. No I did not.
   JOHN CONDER. I was sent for, and took the prisoner in charge - and in going along he feigned intoxication, and said it was a lark.
 Prisoner. I said I thought they were having a lark - I came from my work and it began to rain; I was them going past the shop, the gentleman came out and took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined One Month.
