WILLIAM LAWRENCE. I am a police-inspector. I was at Marylebone
Office on the 14th of December, when the prisoners were in charge--I
noticed the shoes of Rowe—I observed that they exactly corresponded
with the description that Stebbings gave me—there were two nails out on
the inside of the right foot, on the toe, and two large nails in the centre,
and part of the tip was off—when the prisoners were about to leave the
office, I desired Glasscock to take their shoes off—I said that loud enough
for Rowe to hear—I saw that shoe again afterwards, and there had been
several alterations in it—one of the large nails in tbe centre had been
removed, and the tip taken quite off.

road, or come by the Canal—they both had bundles—I asked Amon

Can't a man carry a bit of mutton through the street without being

go—he resisted—I then drew my staff, and said I should be obliged to
