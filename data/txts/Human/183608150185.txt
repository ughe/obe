On Friday evening, the 15th of July, I was in the shop—the prisoner Marsh came in for a 1  d . worth of tobacco—I told him my master did not deal in that—and then he asked for a penny pie—I served him—he gave me a sixpence—I showed it to master—it turned out to be bad—I told him it was bad—he said he had taken it for his work—I gave it him back and he left. ANN BRADY. I live at Deptford. On the 15th of July, I was assisting Mrs. Owen, a potato-dealer there—two young men came up to the shop shed—I do not know them myself (I showed them to Mr. Males the officer) they gave me sixpence—I gave it to Mr. Males, where I went to get it changed—I took it back to the young man, and they gave me a good one—they bought a 1 d . worth of potatoes—they went away and Males followed them. WILLIAM MALES, Jun. I was at the shop door—Ann Brady pointed out Marsh, and John Carthy to me—Brady gave me a bad sixpence—she took the bad one back and they gave her a good one—she brought it to me—the two young men went away together. WILLIAM MALES, Sen. I live at Deptford, and am the father of the last witness. I recollect Mrs. Brady coming to my son for change for sixpence—I followed the two young men—I saw them first go to widow Townsend's, the baker's—one of them went it—I followed them after he came out, to the Black Horse Bridge, they were then joined by the prisoner Thomas Carthy—they then went towards Rotherhithe—I sent to William Badham, and he joined us at China-hall—when he joined us the three prisoners separated, and one went inside Mr. Mumford's shop—after he came out they fell into company again—they went two hundred yards and then went to a bun-shop—one went in—he came out and they joined again—I went into the bun-shop, came out, and followed them—I saw Marsh and John Carthy go into a pork-shop—Thomas Carthy did not go into any shop, to my knowledge—I still followed them over Mill-pond Bridge—I sent to the station-house that I wanted more assistance—they joined again after they got through St. James's-gate—we and the policeman then surrounded them, put them into a baker's-shop near Lilliput-hall, and took them to the station-house—I searched Thomas Carthy, and found on him four base sixpences, and 2 s . 10 1/2 d . in copper. WILLIAM BADHAM. I am a police-constable. I joined Males in pur suit of the prisoners—when they were taken, I saw Thomas Carthy stoop down, and do something to his left foot—when we got to the station-house, I examined him, and found three pieces of metal on him—three counter feit sixpences fell from his shoe—I found a file and knife on John Carthy.
