955. JOSEPH HUSTON (38), Forging and uttering a warrant for
the payment of 121. 108., with intent to defraud.
MR. BROUN Prosecuted.
MONTAGUE HARRIS. I am a jeweller of York Lodge, Kennington
Park-Mr. Theobald introduced the prisoner to me in May last year
--the prisoner said he had some coupons which he wished to have
discounted which belonged to the South London Tramways Company,
and the interest was due on 1st January, 1884-these are the coupons
(produced)-I said "I can advance the money, but it is a most unusual
thing for persons to detach coupons before the money is due "-he said
"They belong to my aunt, she gave them to me to get cashed to provide
for my wife and family”-I asked for one to take to the Company's
office to ask them whether they were genuine- I cannot identify the one
I took, but I saw them all together-I made inquiries-I afterwards drew
this cheque (produced) for 171. 108.; it has been paid-- I gave it to Mr.
Theobald ; the prisoner was aware of that because he did not part
with the coupons till I gave it to him.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I do not keep & shop-I know the
Punch Tavern- I do not go there to business, I dine there--the cheque
was made payable to Theobald-I held a promissory note for 17.51.-
I did not lend the money upon that, I took that as collateral security-I
have known Theobald as a clerk-you are the first client he has intro-
duced to me you waited about outside the house, I saw you-I paid the
coupons into my bank on 1st January, when they became due- I could
not find you-I knew they were not right about seven days afterwards
-I did not threaten to give Theobald into custody-I did not meet him
at a tavern in Fleet Street the morning he was given in custody-I said
to Theobald "You can give him into custody if you like " - I was
reluetant to sign the charge sheet because I do not like the trouble of
these things—I do not know if you received the money except from what
Theobald told me--I saw you give the bonds to Theobald, and then you
showed me a duplicate and said “They are right, or else a pawnbroker
would not lend money upon them "-I sent you a letter, but found you
had removed the terms of the letter were that the bonds were forgeries,
and asking you to pay the money-it might have been to the effect that
the promissory note for 11. 54. was duz-it stated "the coupons, my
collateral security, are proved to be forgery."
