JOHN AVERY. I live at 32, Charles Street, Oakley Street, Lambeth, and am a labourer in the employ of the Val de Travers Asphalte Com pany—on this Tuesday I was at work at the bottom of the Scaffolding, inside the enclosure—I had pulled up a pail full by the pulley, the prisoner would have to empty it and let down the empty pail—there is a scaffolding up above, with a scaffold-pole with a pulley, for the purpose of being worked; I saw the empty bucket hanging over, it was lifted over the boards and gently lowered—there is a difference between lifting it over and throwing it over—I did not say I saw the pail thrown over—when it was lifted off the boards he did not call to me below—I was on the look-out—I was lowering it—I lowered it about 15 or 20 feet, and it then struck against a scaffolding-pole and fell over, I called "Below"—I could not see if the chain was round the handle, it was too far up—a careful man would put both hooks through the ring—the bucket fell directly it touched the scaffolding—it is a zinc pail, about 20 lb. weight.
   Cross-examined.  We were only using one hook, I was at the bottom, and it is impossible for me to say whether the chain was round the handle or not—I have been with this Company, off and on, for two years—the prisoner is a steady, hard-working, respectable fellow, and very careful—all he had to do was to take off the full bucket, put on the empty one, tilt it over the ground, and then I lowered it with the rope. THOMAS FORD( Policeman M  73). I was Coroner's Officer at the inquest—I saw the prisoner there; he was sworn—I heard him give his evidence—after the verdict I arrested him on the Coroner's warrant—he was bailed.
   The Prisoner's Defence before the Coroner. "On Tuesday last I was asphalting the top of the school in Webb Street; we were using iron pails. We use them with a rope and pulley for taking asphalte from the ground, about 48 feet from the ground—there is a chain with a hook at the end of the rope—the chain is taken round the handle of the pail and then attached to the hook. I was at the top about 4 p.m. I hooked an empty pail on to the rope; I then threw it over and Avery began to lower; it had gone down about four feet, when agust of wind came and knocked the bucket right against the puddick, and the bucket was unhooked. I looked round, and saw the bucket separate from the rope and falling. No buckets had fallen before. I have asphalted the tops of other schools, and I never saw an accident before. The hook is supported by a spring, a single pulley only, one bucket going up and down at a time. My duty was to unhook the full pail and attach an empty one in its place." NOT GUILTY. The Jury stated that stronger precautions ought to be taken in future to prevent accidents.
