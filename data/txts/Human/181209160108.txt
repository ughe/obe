 London jury, before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 842.  ELIZABETH BROWN was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 6th of July , two pair of sheets, value 2 l. the property of William Wilson.
   MARIA WILSON. I am the wife of William Wilson. My husband keeps  the Three Pigeons and Stars, in Hatfield-street, Goswell-street, St. Luke's. The prisoner was a lodger in my house. I lost two pair of sheets. On the 6th of July I missed them. The prisoner lived up two pair of stairs in my house. She came to us as a married woman. She cohabited with a man we supposed to be her husband.
 Q. Had the man the same access to the house as the woman - A. Just the same. On the 6th of July I saw the prisoner going out with a bundle, between eleven and twelve o'clock.
 Q. How lately before had you seen the sheets - A. At ten o'clock the sheets were safe. One pair of sheets were in the next room to where the prisoner lodged. She lodged in a two pair of stairs room. The other pair were in the club room. The door was locked, but I had left the key in the door.
 Q. What was the value of these sheets - A. Two pounds.
 Q. At what time did the man go out to work - A. At five o'clock in the morning. He was not at home that day after I saw her go out with the parcel.
 Q. You saw her go out with a parcel - A. Yes, I did. She stopped, and had a glass of gin at the bar . I did not suspect her then. It was large enough to have contained these articles. It was covered over with an apron. About twelve o'clock that day I went up stairs and missed the sheets. She did not return to my house. On the next morning she was apprehended.
   WILLIAM MATTING. I am a constable. On the 7th of July I apprehended the prisoner, between nine and ten o'clock. She was sitting at a public-house door . I took her to Mrs. Wilson's, and searched her. I found upon her this houswife with three duplicates in it. I went to the pawnbroker, and discovered the sheets.
   THOMAS DAVIS. I am a pawnbroker in Brick-lane, Old-street. The prisoner pawned two blankets with me.
 Q. That is not in this charge .
   ROBERT UPSALL. I am a servant to Mr. Williamson, 30, Barbican. On the 6th of July the prisoner pawned a pair of sheets, with me. I advanced five shillings and sixpence upon them.
   JAMES HARRISON. I am an apprentice to Mr. Read, pawnbroker, Redcross-street. I produce a pair of sheets pawned at our house. I do not know the person of the prisoner.
 Prosecutrix. They are my property.
 Prisoner's Defence. I pledged a pair of sheets and a pair of blankets.
     GUILTY, aged 33. Confined Six Months in the House of Correction, and fined 1 s.
