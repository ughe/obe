THOMPSON, M.D. I am a nephew of the late Mr. Richard
Constable. I knew his handwriting very well-I certainly think the signa-
ture, Richard Constable," to this deed, is not his writing--there is less of
flourish in it than he always used-it is certainly an imitation of his writing
but it is clearly not his-I have not a doubt about it.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. You have seen that before, have
you not? A. I saw it here on Friday- I looked at it for some seconds or
minutes, as I have looked at it now I do not think I ever saw my uncle sign
a deed-it does bear some resemblance to his writing-I never should have
suspected it to have been his, if I had seen it-I should certainly say it is
clearly not bis-when it was put into my hand I had been informed that the
question was, whether it was a forgery or no forgery-it bears some resemblance
to his writing ; not a strong resemblance: I think it is an imitation can
swear I think it is not my uncle's writing this paper marked (C) is a good
deal more like his writing-I should believe it was his, except that it is
“Richard" at length, and forty-nine times out of fifty it was "R" only-I
think it is his, with that exception- I cannot certainly say whether I ever saw
him sign Richard"-it bears a very close resemblance to his writing-this
paper marked (A) I should take as his writing-1 bave no doubt about it
the word “Constable" is very like his, I think it is his writing.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. I believe you live at No. 129, Piccadilly? A. Yes
-my uncle resided at Lewisham formerly, and latterly at Kensington--he
was the brother of the late Mr. Abraham Constable, who lived at Lewisham
many years, and was a magistrate there-this property originally belonged to
him-he built the house.
