 323. JANE BUTLER was again  indicted for stealing, on the   9th of December , 2 sheets, value 3 s  .; 2 blankets, value 6 s  .; 1 pillow-case, value 2 s  .; and 1 bolster, value 2 s  .; the goods of Samuel Clouting . ELIZABETH SEABOULD. I am the wife of Frederick Seabould, of No. 7, Martha-street, St. George's. On the 14th of September, the prisoner took a room of me, as a married woman—she left on the 27th, without notice—the person she lived with, left the Friday before—I went into the room, and missed one blanket and one sheet—these are them. HUGH HENRY CAMPION. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a sheet, which I received from the prisoner. JOHN HOPE. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have a blanket, pawned in the name of Jane Hasket—I cannot tell by whom.
   Prisoner.  I own the things were pawned by me, but the man was with me—he left me on the Friday, and I went on Monday to look for him. GUILTY. Aged 24.
 324. JOHN SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of De cember , 3 dead rabbits, value 3 s  ., the goods of John Bewley . CHARLES MALLARD. I live in Chapel-street, and am a labourer in leadenhall-market. I was near Mr. Bewley's shop on the morning of the 17th of December. I saw the prisoner take some rabbits off the rail—I told Thomas who ran and took him. WILLIAM THOMAS. I am shopman to Mr. John Bewley. I ran after the prisoner, and took him—I found these three rabbits on him, which are my master's.
   Prisoner.  You found nothing on me—you ran back, and found the rabbits. Witness.  No; I found them on you—you dropped them. GUILTY. Aged 40.— Confined Six Months.
 325. JAMES TILLETT was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of De cember , 1 tea-kettle, value 4 s  .; the goods of Peter Pige . STEPHEN LAWRENCE. I am an extra-man at Mr. Peter Piges. On the 12th of December, this copper kettle was on his stall—I missed it when I was told of it—I pursued the prisoner, and saw the spout of the kettle poking out  under the tail of his coat—I stopped him, and asked him whether he saw any one with a copper kettle—"Copper kettle" says he, "Copper kettle! no"—I collared him—he gave me the kettle—I let him go, as I did not like to tackle  him in a lonesome place—I brought the kettle back, and told the policeman—we went and took him.
