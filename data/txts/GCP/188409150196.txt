MR. SAUNDERS Prosecuted; MR. Raven Defended.
DAVID Ving. I am a blacksmith, living at Kennington Cross-on the
23rd of August, about a quarter or 20 minutes to 11, I was in the
Westminster Bridge Road, when the prisoner and another man came up
to me-the prisoner came right up to my face and put his hand up anil
threw me over--I fancy I was kicked up, because I had a great lump on
my ley-the prisoner broke my chain and took my watch ; this is the
other part of my chain-when I got up the prisoner was running down
the street-I don't know where the other went to-I was lame before this
with rheumatics-my watch was worth 51-I have no doubt the prisoner
is the man who took it.
Cross-examined. There was another man there; I did not recognise
him as it was done so suddenly-I have not seun the prisoner before to
my knowledge—this was at the corner of Charles Street, on the public
pavement-there was a lamp at the corner, and there I saw the two men
standing as I crossed the street-I believe it was the other man that
tripped me up-I shouldn't say the man who assaulted me had any
whiskers -I ain not very short-sighted; I can see better with glasses-I
had my glasses on at the time--I did not notice that he was wearing a
pin-I a positive he had a billycock hat on, I believe a black one
-I believe the other had a black hat on, I could not swear to it-both
of them had dark trousers on I know nothing of the prisoner's character
or that he holds a driver's licence.
By the COURT. Neither of them spoke-there were a lot of people
looking on, no one interfered for a week aiterwards I suffered a great
deal of pain; I had a lump as big as an egg under my kzee, and my leg
was grazed and my hands cut.
