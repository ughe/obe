OscaR FREDERICK HANNINOTON, grocer's assistant, 47, Tonidge Road,
Thornton Heath. On August 22, 1906, in reply to an advertisement
in the Daily Telegraph," I went to see prisoner at Puzey's office, 20,
Bueklersbury. Prisoner was there by himself, except for the office
boy. He said he had just been lucky enough to secure the sole
ageney for the Adler Cars ; he had three or four cars coming into
his garage at Church Street, Barking Road; that he really did not
care for my money, but he wanted a younger fellow to study his inte-
rests whom he could put down at the garage, and that Saturday week
was the day fixed for the arrival of the motor-ears. He showed ine
all Mr. Puzey's books, and the names of some large business people
he had done business with. He said he lived down at Woolwich, where
he had got his ear. I spoke to him about the money, and said I
should not be able to get it just then-the advertisement asked for
2250. He said if he could have a security from me so that he knew
I should study his interest that would do. I told him I could let
bim have E10 or 220. On August 25 I received a telegram telling
me to meet him at the garage, Church Street, Barking Road, and to
bring E10 with me, which I did, taking with me my mother's cheque
for 210. I was to have E2 10s. a week for my services when we
started and the orders came in, and a commission guaranteed ac
22 10s. a week, making E5 a week. On August 27 I commenced my
duties at the garage. No business was done whatever. One or two
trial cars came down there, but there were no clients for them at all.
I received letter (produced), dated August 29, from prisoner, and on
the 30th I handed prisoner my mother's second cheque for E10. He
then asked me to sign the letter produeed, undertaking to pay 2230,
which I did. I left about Oetober 7. During my employment I only
received a cheque for E1 1s., out of which I paid the carpenter who
was at work there. I received no wages and no commission. I saw
prisoner at 381, Mansion House Chambers, and told him he had not
kept his promise, and no business had been done, and I was so much
out of pocket that I could not possibly think of staying. He said I
could not leave him as I signed the agreement. I told him it was
not a proper agreement as it was not stamped. He said he had got
it stamped after and had signed it. I am 22 years old. At the
time I parted with my money I quite believed prisoner to be a partner
with Puzey
