MR. PHILLIPS addressed the Jury, and called
Henry Day. I am a surgeon and live at Acton. I was called on to
attend the prisoner first in 1824, when he came home from school in an
insane state of mind-there was no obvious cause for the derangement
except his too intense study at school-I attended him on that occasion
for about six months-his mind was in a complete state of aberration the
whole time, with some trifling intervals-I think his mind on the whole was
on pious feelings, but occasionally obscene and profane-these circumstances
do constantly mingle in persons in this state of mind I was next called on to
attend him in October, 1826, until October, 1827-his mind was then in a
very similar state to the former period he had always a keeper with him-he
was not in a fit state to be left alone for one moment his mind was very
similar to what it was at first-pious feelings, mingled with incongruous ideas
--his person was occasionally very filthy, and at other times he was earnestly
desirous of being very clean when his mind was at the worst he was
exceedingly filthy--that is frequently the case with persons afflicted with
madness-he was occasionally misehievous towards the close of it he had
something similar to epilepsy-I attended him again from 1888 to July,
1884—there was no material deviation then, he was as before he was
occasionally so ill as to have two keepers--a person named Gracey, as far
as I can recollect, was one-he was attended by Sir George Tothill, who
was a physician at Bedlam, but is now dead he was occasionally very
loquacious, and at other times very taciturn-he broke in the panel of a
door on one occasion, and he would throw any thing into the fire-be
would sometimes call himself St. Jerome, and address me under the title of
St. Jatnes --he had a straſt waistcoat on occasionally, but not always-
there was always a mark of intellectual derangement about his eyes, closing
them for a long time oecasionally, during which time he would never speak
-his laugh was idiotic. vario to

Q. When there is a tendency to aberrations of this kind, will circum-
stances, slight in themselves, sometimes call it forth suddenly ! A.
Undoubtedly-I ever considered the prisoner to be in that state of mind
any exciting cause would do it--the general state of his mind was of that
character-it was never to be considered a responsible one, if excited by
any cause.
