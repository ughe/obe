 2511. ANN HEWITT and SARAH BENNETT were indicted for steal ing, on the   24th of Aug ., 1 petticoat, value 1 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Robert Watt ; and that Hewitt had before been convicted of felony; to which
   BENNETT  pleaded GUILTY.— Transported for Seven Years . MARIA STEVENS. I live in Clerkenwell. On the 24th of Aug. I was passing the prosecutor's in Exmouth-street, and I saw the prisoners together—I saw Hewitt take a petticoat from the side of the rail at the side of the shop, and put it under her shawl—they both walked away—I gave informa tion—they were taken, and the petticoat found on them.
   Hewitt. Q.  Did you not say at first it was Bennett took it, and afterwards say it was me? A.  No. JAMES HARMAN. I live with Robert Watt, in Exmouth-street. I received information, and ran after the prisoners—when I got close to them Hewitt took the petticoat from under her shawl, and threw it in at a door way—this is my master's.  2512. RICHARD WEST was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of Feb ., 7 pairs of trowsers, value 15 s  .; and 2 waistcoats, 5 s  .; the goods of Fanny Payne . FANNY PAYNE. I am a widow, and live in John's-row, St. Luke's. About one o'clock, on the 28th of Feb., I gave the prisoner seven pairs of trowsers and two waistcoats to pawn for me—he did not come back—I have never seen either them, or the money since.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I got a drop of drink and lost them. GUILTY. Aged 66.— Confined Nine Months .

 2514. ELIZA NIGHTINGALE BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of June , 4 blankets, value 2 l  .; 2 sheets, 14 s  .; 1 snuff-box, 6 d  .; and 1 printed book, 1 d  .; the goods of John Forrester , her master: and ELIZA BASSETT BROWN , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen. JOHN FORRESTER. I live in Compton-street. The prisoner Nightingale worked for me—I missed a sheet from the bed on which she slept, and the blanket from the bed underneath—I asked her where they were—she made no reply—I pressed for an answer, and then she said she took them to her mother's to wash.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOWLING . Q.  Did you say it would be better to tell the truth? A.  No—she was never required to wash the bedding of her own bed—this snuff-box and book are mine—they are worth about 7 d . THOMAS HOOKER. I am a pawnbroker—I produce a sheet and blanket—I took in the blanket of Bassett Brown.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  She had pawned a great number of things, and taken them out? A.  Yes—I have known her about twelve months.
