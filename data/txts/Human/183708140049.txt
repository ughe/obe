 1814. ROBERT JONES  , alias    Croxon, was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of July , 1 watch, value 2 l  .; and 4 seals, value 1 l  .; the goods of Elizabeth Stagg : and HENRY JOHNSON  , alias    Willis , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. ELIZABETH STAGG. I am single, and live with my mother, at Avon, in Wiltshire. On the 28th of July I was at Reading—three or four boys were standing together, and one pushed another towards me—he laid hold of my frock and handkerchief, and my watch was gone immediately afterwards—I had seen it safe just before—this is it—it was lost at Reading, in Berkshire—I do not know the boy again. MARY BOORNE. I am the wife of James Boorne. I was at Reading on this occasion, walking with Elizabeth Stagg, but I do not know who took the watch. RICHARD TEBBS. I am shopman to Mr. Trail, of Turnmill-street, Clerkenwell, a pawnbroker. On the 1st of August, the two prisoners came to the shop—Willis offered a gold watch to pledge, and asked 2 l ., for it—I asked him where he got it—he said his mother lent it him—I knocked for my master, and went to the office for a constable—while I was gone they left the shop and left the watch—as I was coming back I met the two prisoners—I said, "There they are," and the officer took one of them. JOHN READING. I am a constable of Reading. I heard of this robbery, and had Willis in my custody on suspicion—he was discharged on the 29th of July. GEORGE GRUBB. I live at Reading. I was there on that day—I saw Croxon push up against the woman, and take the watch and seals—Jasse was with me—he is not here.
   Jones.  He was with us, and he first began to talk about taking it. Witness.  No, I did not. GEORGE JONES. I live at Reading. I saw Croxon take the watch—I do not know Johnson, the other prisoner. JOHN NESBIT. I received the information that the watch had been offered to pawn—Tebbs pointed out the prisoners—I took one, and the other was pursued and taken—I took him.
   Jones.  We were altogether, and there was a lady coming along; George Jones said he saw a watch shine, and asked if we would take it; I was pushed up against the lady, and I took it.
   Johnson.  I was not there; only this boy and another brought the watch and asked me to go and pawn it—I went, and said that my mother lent it me—they told me they found it.
   JONES *— GUILTY. Aged 17.
   JOHNSON  †— GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Two Years; Two Months Solitary .
