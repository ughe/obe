MR. SLEIGH. Q. Is your work very heavy, and requiring horses of
peculiarly good condition to do it? A. Not very heavy-they have to go
long distances--Mr. Edward Peek seldom or ever comes down there—Mr.
Frean is the partner-he would be consulted in the business, but nothing
would be done without my being made fully cognisant of it-if there had
been any to be bought or sold it would have come under my super-
intendence.

work for the prosecutors-early in March the prisoner called at my place
and asked me if I knew any one who had got a horse to let on hire - I told

trial-I first had a bay horse-I drove him to Waterloo station and back with
milk to the premises, and then returned him, as he did not suit me I went
round a day or two after, and he sent me a grey horse, which he asked 10%.
for-I tried it, and gave him 91. 108. for it ; 31. 108. first, and then 61.-I paid
the 61. on 22d March ; this (produced) is the receipt for it-on 2d April he
came to me, and told me he was disappointed in a horse coming in, and asked
me if I could lend him the horse to work for the firm at 78. per day, and
next morning he went to my stables for the horse I sent to the firm for it
next day, but did not get it back-the brown horse did not suit me, and the
prisoner sold it in my presence to Mr. Coleman for 91. 108, and allowed me
Il. for my trouble and for the introduction after I had paid for the horse,
and got possession of it, the prisoner came to my stable one morning, and
said, “Do not say anything about having paid for the horse until quarter
day, and then I shall have replaced the other horse."
