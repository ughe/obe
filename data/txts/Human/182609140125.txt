   JEMIMA MARGON. On the 5th of July I met the prisoner in Brick-lane; he asked if I should go to the fair - I said, No - he said he should go, and before he went he should cut his work out of the loom; he said it was bad, and troubled him; on the Wednesday following he came home, while I was at work, and I asked him if he was not sorry for what he had done; he said No, and for two pins he would cut all the work out, and then he should only have three months imprisonment, and get away from his master.
   ROBERT DAVIS. I took charge of the prisoner - I asked if he had cut his work in the loom - he said, "Yes, because it went so bad."
 Prisoner. I have always done my best towards my master - and when I had bad silk I was always taken before the Magistrate - and he said I was lazy when I could not do my task of work - it was very bad indeed.
 SAMUEL GRAVENOR. It was the best silk I could get, but he was always altering it, and therefore it was disabled from working as it should do, he was so fickle minded.
 Prisoner to JOHN VINCENT. Q. Did not you know that I could not get on with the work? A. You said so several times - it was a new harness and not quite settled - if he had made three quarters of a yard, it would have done very well.
 JEMIMA MARGON. I know he complained of his work, and I went with the boy to look at it - it appeared to be bad.
 SAMUEL GRAVENOR re-examined. Q. Supposing the work to have been wrong, could it have been put to any use? A. Yes - it could have been made in another loom; the harness was new, and every thing in a complete state; the value of the silk, spoiled, is about 8l.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Twelve Months.

 1604.  WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 2 baskets, value 5s., and 21 loaves of bread, value 12s., the goods of James Rutland.
   DAVID JOHNSTON. I am journeyman to Mr. James Rutland, of Old Burlington-street. I was out on the 26th of June, and left my two baskets in Bond-street, I was absent about twenty minutes serving customers, and when I returned the two baskets, and 21 loaves were gone - I found the prisoner with one basket, in St. Giles'.
   JOHN MENELAUS. I am a journeyman baker. I met the prisoner in St. Giles', between one and two o'clock on the 26th of June, he was selling this bread, and taking the money for it - I had seen Johnston - I asked the prisoner where he came from - he said he came there every day - I looked into his basket, and compared the bread with a loaf Johnston had given me - it corresponded exactly; I took him to the master's.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ALLEY. Q. Did not he tell you a person of the name of Flood gave it him to carry? A. No.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer - I took the prisoner, and produce the basket, which he had; I have 4s. 10d., which he said was the produce of the bread he had sold.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not he tell you a man named Flood gave it him to carry? A. He said a man gave it him - he mentioned no name.(Basket produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had never been in London before - I am a baker, and I met a young man who employed me to carry his basket, which I had done two or three mornings.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1606.  MARY LEARY was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 1 gown, value 5s., the goods of Joseph Harris.
   ANN BUNN. My husband is horse-keeper to Mr. Biggins, of Swan-yard, Westminster. On the 18th of July I was passing Mr. Harris, the pawnbroker, in Tothill-street, Westminster- I saw the prisoner open his door; I went home to my own house; the prisoner followed me into my own room; she asked me for King's-head-court - I said, I did not know it; she then pulled this gown from under her apron, and said, she had bought it for four shillings - she wanted to sit down; I told her I was going out; she went away, and I went out - I had never seen her before - she appeared much agitated, and I went and told Mr. Harris; his young man followed and took her; he took the gown from her left hand.
   JOHN ROBINSON JACKSON. I am in the employ of Mr. Joseph Harris; I went with the witness after the prisoner; I took her in Broad-way, Westminster, and said, she had a gown belonging to Mr. Harris; she said, she had not; I took it from her left hand - I had hung it on the doorpost myself.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked up the gown, and inquired if any one about knew who it belonged to.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Confined One Month.
