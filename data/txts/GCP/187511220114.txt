COTTON, Mayor.
FOURTH COURT.-Tuesday, November 23rd, 1875.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Esq.
26. JAMES BROUGHTON (19), PLEADED GUILTY to stealing 26 handker-
chiefs and other goods of Samuel Lewis, his master-Judgment respited. 27.
SIDNEY MARSHALL (40), to unlawfully forging and uttering an order
for the payment of 91. 168. ; also an order for 91. 58., having been before
convicted of felony--Ten Years' Penal Servitude. 28. FREDERICK
MATTHEWS (17), to stealing a chest of tea of George Roper and another.
having been before convicted of felony-Eighteen Months Imprisonment.
29. JOHN WHITTEN (30), to stealing 12 skirts, of the value of 21. 78., of
William R. Sutton, having been before convicted of felony-Seven Yeart
Penal Servitude. 30. SARAH BARTLETT (65), to unlawfully keeping a dis-
orderly houso-To enter into recognizances to appear. And 31. WILLIAM
CRAWFORD SMITH (42), to feloniously forging and uttering an order for
four guineas with intent to defraud. Recommended to mercy by the Prose
cutor - Judgment respited.
32. In the case of RICHARD WHEELER (40), indicted for unlawfully in
flicting grievous bodily harm upon William Holditch Stephens, the jury found
him insane upon arraignment-Ordered to be Detained till Her Majesty's
pleasure be known.
33. WILLIAM HOLLAND (26), stealing a purse oontaining 141
and a receipt for goods of Hope Duff, from his person. GUILTY-Seven
Years' Penal Servitude.
34. WILLIAM KELLY (18) and ALEXANDER LAWSON (20),
stealing fourteen pieces of skirting, value of 121. 126. 11d., of Theodoro
Crewdson and another.
KELLY PLEADED GUILTY.
MR. THORNE COLE conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD POTTERILL (City Policeman 116). On the 5th November, about
half-past four, I was on duty in Wood Street with Westwood-I saw the two
prisoners and two others not in custody, one of the latter wheeling a barrow-
he up-ended the barrow, and the two prisoners and another went into the pas-
sage of 39, rolled a bale of goods into the barrow, and Kelly wheeled it away
into Silver Street, accompanied by Lawson and the others- I had known
both the prisoners before-when they got into Silver Street an alarm was
given ; Kelly instantly dropped the barrow, and all of them ran away, Kelly
running down Wood Street, where I apprehended him-the other men
escaped I saw Samson after that on the 15th November about 1.30
in the daytime in St. Paul's Churchyard - I told him I was a police-
constable, and I should take him in charge for being concerned in stealing a
bale of goods from a doorway in Wood Street-he said "You have made a
mistake this time; I was not there"-I took him to the station-house-
he was asked his name and address, and he said "I have no fixed place of
residence."
GEORGE WESTWOOD (City Policeman 707). I was with Potterill-his
evidence is perfectly correct.
HENRY HOOPER. I am a warehouseman at Mr. Crewdson's-on the 5th
November a bale of goods was produced to me containing fourteen pieces of
skirting-it belonged to Theodore Crewdson, and was valued at something
like 127. 128.--- it was placed in the passage on the ground floor on the
the morning in question, and it was missed about 4.45 in the afternoon.
