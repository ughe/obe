WILLIAM COCKRILL (police-constable L 108.) I was on duty in Westminster-
bridge-road, about a quarter-past two o'clock in the morning of the 22nd of
April, and saw Shambrook in company with three prostitutes, at the foot of the
bridge about a quarter past three o'clock, in consequence of what I heard, I
went to No. 15, Duck-lane, Westminster, and in the front room up stairs I
found the two prisoners, and four other women-the two prisoners and two
other women were on the bed-I said to my brother constable, “That is
Dwyer"- I took her off the bed, but found nothing on her-my brother
constable was searching Shambrook-he was very violent, and said he would
not be searched-we brought him out, and felt something in his fobhe
said it was his own property-we asked him to produce it-he would not
-I was searching a girl, I left her, and came and took hold of his collar
and left wrist, and my brother officer took the watch from his fob.
Cross-examined. Q. What made you say, "That is Dwyer?" A. From
information I had from a woman named Clifton, who keeps the brothel
-I never saw Shambrook before I knew Dwyer well-Shambrook had
been drinking, but I should not take him for a drunken man.
