No; I thought the prisoner belonged to the first or second regiment of Life Guards, and went with her to the Regent's barracks the same night - when I went into the parlour she was sitting close by the prisoner on the bench; the lights were not lighted more than twenty minutes before they went - the witness was not drunk; she was the worse for liquor.
   ISAAC MOOREY. I am waiter at the Little Cannon. I remember the prisoner and the witness being there - they came in together; the prisoner asked her what she would take - she said, "I won't have any thing to drink, I would rather have something to eat;" the prisoner said bring in a pint of ale at all events - he pulled off his gloves and gave me 1s., and said fetch us some beefsteaks; I took them in, and saw no more for an hour, I dare say - another pint of ale was then called for, and I took that in.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see or hear any thing like a watch about her? A. No.
   MATILDA PARLETT re-examined. I did not sleep in the Fleet Prison the night before; I was at a friend's, No. 8, Halton-street; I am sure I had the watch when I got up, and fastened it with the tape as usual; I had looked at it in the day - the reason I said I would defy any man to take it from me was, because I did not know what character he was, and I thought it would deter him - my friend is not here at whose house I slept.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am quite innocent.
   ANN EDSON. I lived at the Goat and Compasses. On the 24th of August I served the prosecutrix, and a soldier who was with her, with two pints of ale; I cannot swear the prisoner is the person - the man had a red coat on; I have no doubt of the prosecutrix's person.
 COURT. Q. What time was it? A. Half-past twelve o'clock when they came, they left at half-past one - the house is at the corner of Fitzroy-street; I saw something about this in the newspaper, but had no idea I should be wanted; I had a subpoena last Wednesday - I had no conversation with the prosecutrix; she was in the back room - she had a white shawl on with a narrow border, and a white bonnet with coloured ribbons; I observed her face - my master and his two nieces live there; I served them with what they had.
   WILLIAM BOWER. I am a private in the First Regiment of Life Guards. On the 24th of August I was at the Regent's-park barracks - the prosecutrix came there that evening, she was what I call drunk; the landlord of the public-house asked for the officer of the guard - I said there was no officer, there was a corporal; he said that would not do; I sent the orderly man to the adjutant - they staid two or three minutes; the prosecutrix reeled up against the table; the adjutant asked her if I was the man - she said No, he had no pouch-belt on; he asked her what time he left her - she said at half-past nine o'clock; I then went to the guard-room, and it wanted a quarter to nine; she said she was positive it was half-past nine - the landlord said you mean half-past seven; she said No, half-past nine.
   DANIEL PENNY. I am the corporal. I was called in by the last witness; the prosecutrix appeared to me to be very much the worse for liquor - I heard her complain to the adjutant that she had lost a watch, and some soldier stole it at the Little Cannon; he brought forward first the orderly man and then the centry - she said he was something like the orderly man; she staggered about very much; the adjutant said, "I will show you every assistance in my power - leave your address with the corporal;" and while he was writing she frequently caught hold of the table to steady herself.
   GEORGE MILLER. I am a quarter-master at the Knightsbridge barracks. On the morning of the 25th of August the prosecutrix came there, and stated she had lost her watch - the prisoner was taken off the parade, and searched in my presence, but no watch or duplicate was found; she then gave her address, "Parker, 16, Britannia-row, Islington;" she wrote it herself - this is it; I saw her write it.
   MATILDA PARLETT. I gave him the address, but I did not write that - it is not my writing.
 COURT. Q. Look at it; tell us whether it is your writing? A. Yes, this is my writing - but I thought if I could get the watch without my father's knowledge I would - I did not remember it before.
 MR ADOLPHUS. Q. Who is Parker? A. They live at No. 16, Britannia-street; they are laundresses - I do not know what part of the house they occupy; I am not much acquainted with them.
 MR. ADOLPHUS to  GEORGE MILLER. Q. Have you made inquiries for them? A. Yes; there is no laundress of the name of Parker, there is one of the name of Sawyer; the prisoner has been ten years and a half in the regiment, and had a good character.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1769.  SARAH BANKS was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 sovereign, and 3 shillings, the monies of Frederick Timberman , from his person.
   FREDERICK TIMBERMAN. I am a sugar-baker. On the 30th of July I was going home, at half-past one o'clock in the morning, from a public-house where I had been spending the evening; I met the prisoner and another woman at the corner of Lambeth-street; they asked where I was going; I said, "Home, and it is almost time for you to go home;" the prisoner then put her hand into my pocket and took out 1l. 3s.; she went across the road - two other girls came round her; I saw her trying to make away with the money - the watchman caught her, and found some silver on her, but could not find the sovereign; I was sober.
 Prisoner. I had been drinking at the White Lion public-house, and he came and asked if I would have any thing to drink; he called for liquor three times, and asked if I would take a walk with him - when we got out he said he had been robbed. Witness. I was at no public-house with her at all; I do not know how much money was found - the watchman is not here.
     NOT GUILTY.
