WILLIAM WOODWARD( policeman, B  157). On Sunday, 17th Sept., about eight o'clock in the evening, I went to Lindfield's house, 24, Prior-place, East-street—I was in plain clothes—I saw a young man there, named Wil liam Lindfield—I asked to see Mrs. Lindfield in private, and he took me into a parlour adjoining the shop—I said I came from a young woman, Eliza Wilson, living at Norwood—she asked how she was—I said she was very bad, she was in a feverish state—she asked whether she had taken the medi cine she had sent by her brother, and put her feet in warm water—I said I did Dot exactly know—I asked her who brought the young woman to her house, and she said a person living in York-street, who sold oysters—I asked her if her name was Dryden—she said she did not know, she had not seen her above twice—she said the person that brought her had given her some pills and a bottle of savin water, which had caused a stoppage in the bowels and the water—I told her I would call again in the morning, and let her know how the young woman was—I then went away—I called again the next morning, about ten, I was shown into the parlour—she asked me how the young woman was—I said, "She is very bad; you are not aware who I am?"—she said, "No"—I said, "I am a police-constable; consider your self in charge for using a certain instrument on Eliza Wilson, and procuring abortion, and thereby endangering her life"—she then said, "The only instrument I used on her was to draw off the water, and 1 can't recollect whether it was three or four times," she produced this instrument ( produced )—I then asked her if she had received any money from the young woman—she said she had had 50 s .; 8 s . 6 d . she had given the woman that brought her, 2 s . she charged for medicine, and she had returned Eliza Wilson two sovereigns—this letter was produced by her to the Magistrate, and it has been in my possession ever since ( produced ).
