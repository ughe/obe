MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
PHILIP CAPON (City-policeman, 71). On Saturday night, 2nd July, I
received information, and went to Mr. Capp's warehouse in Moor-lane, about
20 minutes put I I o'clock—I beard something inside like persons moving—

and the other at the back of the premises—I went to the lower door which
is down the steps in a sort of area—tbe door was nailed and fastened inside,
shored up,with a piece of wood—I partly forced the door open, and heard the
sound of persons going up the first flight of stairs—I examined the lower

up the next flight of stairs, from the first to the second floor—I looked about
the first floor, and then went up to the second floor—when got there I saw
the prisoners, one of them threw up the window, and they both went out
—l afterwards examined the door in the area—I found it had been forced
open—it bad been nailed, and the nails were broken down—there was in the

day—I did not find it—I examined the place again on the Tuesday, and
