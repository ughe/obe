   Q.  Will you swear there was not 1000 l . overdue, and unpaid at that time? A.  I should say it was not exceeding 1000 l .—I cannot swear that, without you will allow me to see my ledger—there was Coster's account then not wholly paid.
   Q.  Did you, or the prisoner, say that you thought 800 l . would be a good sum to put in the deed? A.  I am not positive—I cannot say whether I did or not—I do not remember proposing 1000 l ., and the prisoner saying, "No, put in 800 l ."—I only stated to the prisoner in passing, that I thought if all they said was as available as they represented it to be, as Miss Constable and the prisoner both stated, independent of the property which was at Hunting don, and in Wales, that I thought when I was making out these copies, 1100 l . was a sufficient sum for passing over the estate from one gentleman to another, and Miss Constable replied that they could prove still further receipts if I wished to copy them, but it being then near twelve o'clock at night, I felt uneasy at my wife waiting my return—I might have proposed to have 1000 l . put into the deed, but I cannot swear it—the prisoner might have said that he thought 800 l . would be better—I did not on that say that I thought two sums of 400 l . each would be better than 800 l .—I will swear that positively—I never suggested two sums of 400 l ., or any thing to that effect—I never proposed the division of the 800 l . into two sums—I never pro posed that the 800 l . should be divided—I will swear that to the best of my knowledge—I believe I did not—if I must swear one way or other I should distinctly say no—I can go no further—I am a man of property now—I do not see that I am bound to say to what amount, my creditors may be here after influenced by it—I have been insolvent—I have never been through the Insolvent Court—I have not been insolvent twice—my father-in-law became security, and I paid my creditors off myself—I asked them to give me time, after which I paid them interest.
