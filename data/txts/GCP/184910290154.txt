I repaid Mr. Butcher the money, and went to 10, Clifford's-inn, asked for
the prisoner, and he was not at home- I went a second time, and he was not

policeman, and saw the prisoner—that was the first time I had seen him
since the 22nd Sept.-he ordered me out of the place-I said to the
policeman, who was with me, " This is the person; I give this man in
charge"-when he told me he was an attorney I believed him, or I should
not bave given him the money-I had known him to do business for Mr.
Butcher for about ten months, and saw him with papers tied up with red tape,
and he was always talking of law-he said nothing on 22nd about being
clerk to an attorney.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. R. Are you a gentleman ? A. No:
I have just got enough to find myself a bit of bread; what you call a poor
gentleman-I have been a private in the army--my son did not collect my
rents—he has robbed me of hundreds of pounds, but I never charged bim-
I have known Mr. Yorston nine or ten months, and seen him occasionally-
I never before had occasion to ask him if he was an attorney-I thought I
would ask him when my child was likely to be sent out of the country-he
had been convicted before, and was taken up a very few days after he came
out of gaol.
MR. M Mahon. Q. When your son was about to be transported, you
were particular in asking the question ? A. I asked whether he was an
attorney, because I would not have it said, after my son was gone, that I had
not done my duty.
JAMES BUTCHER. I live at 117, Waterloo-road, and know the prisoner and
Mr. Winston. Mr. Winston came to my shop on Sunday, 23rd Sept., and
asked me to give Yorston 25s. to ask for a mitigation of the punishment of
his son-they had been talking about it in my shop on the evening before-
Yorston came on Monday, 24th I gave him 258., and he gave me the
receipt, which has been produced- I do not recollect whether I showed that
to Mr. Winston-- I did show him the letter—the name in the receipt is
Vincent, but we call him Winston-I told Yorston he was to go to Clerken-
well, and find a policeman, whose number and letter I told him, and he
would tell him all about it I did not tell him where the trial was to take
place-he said he had a cause to attend at the Insolvent Debtors' Court, and
he would attend to this if he possibly could-on the Tuesday evening
received the letter from him which has been read—the prosecutor called on
me on the Tuesday evening, and again on Wednesday morning-I gave him
the receipt and he paid me the 25s.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known the prisoner? A. About
three years—I have employed his master, Mr. Townshend-I knew perfectly
well that he was an attorney's clerk-I believe that was generally known
I cannot say whether Mr. Winston knew it-I think Winston has been in
the house when the prisoner and I have talked about my business with
Mr. Townshend, but he walked away when we talked about business—I do
not recollect Mr. Winston saying he thought the man might have said he
was an attorney's clerk--I am quite sure the prisoner was clerk to Mr.
Townshend at that time.
