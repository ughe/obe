I heard no more of her till Thursday evening again, when I received this note; then she complained only of the constipation of the bowels, and I sent her two ounces of salts by a young man who brought this note. Dryden did not say she had given her savin; it was the young woman said that, when I took her up stairs privately to examine her. It was the third time the deceased came to me I drew off her water." Dryden says, "She (deceased) had no savin water from me. I gave her rive-leaf grass, ground-ivy, agrimony, and pil. cochioe, which are fine things for the stomach. She asked me if I knew a midwife, and I referred her to Mrs. Lindfield." Orpin says, "I have been out with the young woman several times, but as to giving her poison it is wrong; whatever she took she got it, and took it of her own accord; I neither supplied her with money nor bought it myself; she said something must be done; she came to me, and asked me to give her 30  s . to get her some stuff, but 1 told her I would not have anything to do with it, and 1 have not seen her since.")

 2444. ADOLPHUS SOLITA , feloniously killing and slaying Robert Woodrow .
   MR. PLATT conducted the Prosecution . FRANCES SMITH. I live at 1, Elliott's-place, Lambeth, with my sister, who is the mother of the deceased boy, Robert Woodrow—he would have been thirteen years old next Jan.—he came home on Saturday evening, 9th Sept., from Mr. Randall's, his master, a boot and shoemaker, at Camberwell—he sat down on a chair, and complained of his head aching—I asked what made his head ache, and he said a boy had struck him at the back of his head, and he said to me, "Aunt, feel what a lump I have got?"—I felt a lump, about the size of a nut, behind his ear—I did not think it of any great importance; I thought it was only a cold—he told me his nose had bled at his master's—he went to bed—he awoke me in the night, and his nose was bleeding violently; it poured out all over the bed, I suppose it was more than ten minutes before I could stop it, he went to bed again, it bled again about ten o'clock on the Sunday, and again twice that day, equally violent—he complained of pain in his head—he went to his employment on the Monday, and every day up to the Thursday, but he complained, and was getting worse every day—on the Thursday he came home earlier than usual, and said his head was so bad that he could not do anything at his masters—he went again in the morning, but came home directly, and went to bed—at went again on the Saturday, but he was still very bad, and his master let him come home very early in the evening—he was then very ill indeed, still com plaining of his head—he never went to work after that—his master asked him to come on the Monday, and he went, but could not stop—on the Thursday following we called in Mr. Thomas, and he attended him till he died, which
