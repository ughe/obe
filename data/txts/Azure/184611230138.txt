178. MARY ANN EDMONDS was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief,
value 18., the goods of William Judd, from his person.
WILLIAM JUDD. I am a cap-maker, and live in Vincent-square, West.
minster. On the 9th of Nov., at half-past eleven o'clock at night, I met the
prisoner at Lambeth—she came up to me, and said, Where are you going!"
—l said, " Home"—l felt something move from my pocket—I turned, and
saw my handkerchief going, and saw her putting it under her shawl—I asked
her several times for it—she would not give it me, but abused me—the
stable came up—she pulled it from under her shawl, and said, Don't give
me in charge"—she wanted me to take it back, but I would not—I did
take any liberty with her.
Prisoner. He did, and gave me 3d. ; he gave me the handkerchief to go
down a turning; I said I would not be bothered with him nor his handke
chief.

he had been robbed of his handkerchief—the prisoner said he gave it her with
3d.—she said on the way to the station, Do forgive me for the sake of my
NOT GUILTY.
179. CHARLOTTE WAYLAND was indicted for stealing 2 rings, value
58. ; and 1 breast-pin, 48. ; the goods of James Newman, her master.
JAMES NEWMAN. I am a carpenter, and live at Mordake. About nine
o'clock on Sunday evening, the 16th of Nov., I gave the prisoner two ring
and a gold pin to put into the top drawer or the bureau for safety—she wu
in my house to take care of my family—I have missed the articles since—
tbe pawnbroker has them—these are them—I never saw them after I gave
them to the prisoner.
Cross-examined by MB. PAYNE. Q. You said she was in your house
take care of your family? A. Yes—I am a widower—I was not about to
be married to the prisoner—I had made a promise of marriage—she had
management of my place—she was there about eleven weeks—I sent for be
brother to dinner on Sunday, and asked his consent to be married to her—I
corrected one of my children—the prisoner and I had a dispute, and she let
my house on Wednesday night—when I went to her she said she had the
property safe, and I should have it in an hour—she did not say the money
had been used for the support of the family.
COURT. Q. Was it used? A. I do not know—I do not know what she
has embezzled—she has run me 51. in debt.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Were you not to be married in Nov.—and did you not
say it was unlucky to marry a second wife in the same month that you did
the first wife? A. I might say so—her brother is the sexton of Mortlake.
GEORGE SPENCRE. I am a pawnbroker. Two rings and a pin were
pawned with me, to the best of my belief, by the prisoner.
NOT GUILTY.
