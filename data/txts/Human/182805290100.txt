 1304.  THOMAS JEFFRIES was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 20 quarters of malt, value 60l., the goods of John Lyall and others.
 In October Session Parsons and others were convicted of this offence; the prisoner was included in the indictment, - as none of the witnesses could identify him as being concerned, Mr. Churchill withdrew from the prosecution.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1305.  ROBERT HUGHES was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of May , 1 carpenter's rule, value 2s., the goods of William Nosworthy.
 WILLIAM NOSWORTHY. I am a  carpenter. The prisoner came to me last Saturday, and asked for a job; I said I did not know of one - he stood at the bench, and asked for something to drink - I said I did not want anything, and desired him to walk away; he stood muttering something; I went to the end of the bench. and saw him go out; he was shuffling at the side of his thigh - I went after him, and said, "You have my rule;" "I have" says he, and he gave it me.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I was rather in liquor.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1307.  WILLIAM WALTON was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of May , 2 live fowls, price 5s., the property of David Brown. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1308.  MARY WARREN was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of May , 1 gown, value 2s., the goods of Robert Alexander Jones.
   ELIZABETH JONES. I am the wife of Robert Alexander Jones - we live in White Hart-court, Castle-street. I lost a gown from behind my room door up one pair of stairs - there was a child ten years old in the room, who gave an alarm, and the prisoner was taken in Cecil-court.
   JANET MURRAY. I saw the prisoner leave the house; a man stopped her, and I saw this gown found on her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   RICHARD MILLER. I took the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 42. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1309.  WILLIAM WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of May , 2 asses, price 8l., the property of Thomas Fisher.
 THOMAS FISHER. I live at Watford. I  keep asses, and supply families with milk; I lost two asses on Thursday night or Friday morning last from Watford-heath; they had been accustomed to be turned out there for about a month - I had had one of these about five weeks; the other about six weeks; they were with four others of mine; I saw them all safe on Thursday night at nine o'clock; I know nothing of the prisoner; I saw them again on Friday evening and knew them to be mine.
   HENRY FOX. I am toll-keeper at Westbourne-green gate; I saw the prisoner on Friday morning, at half-past four o'clock, riding on one of these asses, and another person with him on another; the other man came and offered me 2d., but the toll being 4d. I refused to take it; he said sooner than pay 4d. he would go back, which they did; the prisoner was riding the smallest of the two asses. A gentleman named Roach came at eleven o'clock and asked if I had seen any asses; I told him - I saw the prisoner near the gate the same afternoon and knew him to be the man; he was secured; I have seen the donkeys since, and know them to be the same they were riding; I had not known the prisoner before.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. The prisoner was brought to the Police-office on Friday with the donkeys, which the prosecutor claimed; I was directed to take charge of them, and I have taken care that no other person should have access to them; they are at the door.
 THOMAS FISHER. They are my property.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 1310.  ELIZABETH THOMAS and EDWARD FERNLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of May , 2 shirts, value 15s.; 1 hammock, value 5s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; 1 palm, value 8d., and 1 needle case, value 1d., the goods of John Francisco. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1311.  MARGARET LUSH was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of May , 1 gown, value 5s., the goods of John Harigan.
   MARY HARIGAN. I am the wife of John Harigan, and live in George-gardens, Drury-lane. On the 28th of May I brought this gown from Whitechapel, and laid it on my bed; the prisoner was with me; she came down while I went to get a candle, and when I returned she was gone and the gown too; I knew her by sight, she asked me if she should come down with me, and I said Yes.
   JOHN PROSSER. I live at a pawnbroker's, in Gray's-inn-lane; I took in this gown of the prisoner on Thursday afternoon.
 MARY HARIGAN. This is my gown; I never gave it to her to pawn; when I took her on Friday she threw herself on her knees, and took her oath that she knew nothing about the gown.
 JOHN HARIGAN. I have known the prisoner by sight about a month; I never allowed her to pawn anything or treated her.
   THOMAS SMEE. I am a constable. I took the prisoner and asked her if she had pawned the gown; she said Yes, but did not say where; I found by the prosecutor that it was at Mr. Creed's, in Gray's-inn-lane; I took the prisoner there and they identified her; she was very much intoxicated, and I think she did not do it willingly.
