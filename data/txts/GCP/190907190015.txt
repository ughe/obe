Inspector SYDNEY BUTTON, L Division. I was on duty at Konning.
ton Road Police Station when prisoner was brought in just after mid.
night of July 7; he appeared quite calm and collected; he smelt of
drink; he had a wound on the left side of his head. I told prisoner
that the woman he shot in Waterloo Road was dead; he was about to
speak, and I cautioned him; he then said, "She robbed me at Tabard
Street last Friday; I met the two women to-night; we
went and had sausages; I want(ed) to shoot the two women, and when
I came out I fired at them."
Cross-examined. Prisoner had & peculiar dazed appearance.
EVEREST LAVALLE, Divisional Surgeon, said that he saw the prisoner
about 12.15 a.m. on July 8. Prisoner's manner was oalm and col-
lected, but he was in a dazed condition, and under the influence of
drink; he had a slight cut on the scalp; this might have been caused
by the blow from Vandersluis's stick. Witness assisted at the post-
mortem examination of Archer; the cause of death was bemorrhage
from a bullet wound of the lung.
Cross-examined. Prisoner's appearance was consistent with his
having being continually under the influence of drink for six weeks.
Detective Inspector WILLIAN EUSTACH, L Division. On my rond-
ing over to prisoner the charges (murder of Archer and attempted
murder of Vanderaluis) he said, "I did not intend to shoot the man
it was jammed.” On examining the revolver ( Browning pistol) I
found it was jammed with an empty cartridge. I went to the room
* waich prisoner had been staying at the "Trafalgar Hotel," York
Rond; there I found a travelling trunk and portmanteau. In the
trank were two boxes containing 41 cartridges; a licence issued in
South Africa to possess & Browning pistol: & naturalisation paper
issued at Johannesburg; and some discharges showing that prisoner
had worked in the merchant service as a fireman.
Cross-examined. There were 19 discharges, some marked "good,"
some very good," none "bad"; there were also several characters
from engineers of vessels. Prisoner arrived in England on May 18,
and since that time had been continually under the influence of drink.
Prisoner had on him a watch, no chain.
WILLIAN CHARLES SAVAGE, manager of the Trafalgar Hotel," said
that prisoner occupied a room there from July 3 to the morning of
the 7th. On the evening of the 7th the handed witness £9 to take
care of; he was then partly drunk.
WILLIAN EUSTACE, recalled. On prisoner's arrival in England he
put 250 into the Post Office Savings Bank; this was drawn out in five
sams of £10, the last on July 6.
