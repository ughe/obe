HANNAH HELENA BAGLEHOLE I am a widow, and reside at Keppel-
place, Brompton. On 3rd April, about 20 minutes before 3 o'clock in the
afternoon, I got into a Royal Blue omnibus in Billiter-street-I was going
to Pimlico—the prisoner got into the -omnibus after I was seated—le sat
next to me, on my right side I had my pocket on my right side the
omnibus was crowded after the prisoner got in I was annoyed by his
pressing against me I endeavoured to get from him, but he still followed
me, and pressed against me—he raised up my dress on my right side, and
picked my pocket of my purse I had my purse safe when I got into the
omnibus, at the bottom of my pocket-I carried the money to pay for the
omnibus in my hand-there was in my purse & Post office order for 16
after the prisoner had taken up my dress I wished to leave, and I desired a
man to stop the omnibus, and the moment I was going to leave, the prisoner
had fastened his hand in my pocket, and tore the gathers out of my dress -
I said, “You have picked my pocket"-he has a very large hand, and he
dropped the purse at the right side I did not see him throw it down, but
he had his hand in a position to cover the purse-I was considerably
agitated, and so I am now the prisoner ran away, I ran after him; he
crossed the road, and went between the coaches-the conductor pursued
him, and he was taken-he said, “ Pray don't give me in charge ; look at
me, look at me"-I did not wish to give him in charge, but a gentleman
desired me to do so, for the benefit of the publie—this is my purse, the one
I had in my pocket-my son Charles had it all the time he was in Calcutta,
and I covered it with a piece of my dress.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Are you quite sure that the
prisoner put his hand into your pocket ? A. Yes, because it was fastened
there when I rose up-he looked at me in a particular manner-I did not
see him drop the purse.
