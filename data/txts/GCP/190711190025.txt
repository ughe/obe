Inspector ALFRED WARD, B Division. I went to 11. Belgrave
Square on October 9 and examined the house. There was no indics
tion of forcible entry. Steps were taken to circulate the loss of these
articles, and I subsequently received certain information. On Octo
ber 21 with Sergeant Jones and another officer I went to No. 1,
Down Place, a turning off King Street, Hammersmith, where pri
soner Stapleton lives. Keeping observation on the house about 12
o'clock in the day we saw prisoner Hawking enter. At one o'clock
prisoner Stapleton came out, accompanied by somebody, and went
into a public-house in King Street. When he came out he returned
to his own address by a circuitous route and got back about 1.30. At
three o'clock he came out again with a jug, purchased some beer, and
went back again. Observation was continued until four o'clook, when
both prisoners left together and walked along the street until they
were opposite a publio-house known as the "Palace of Varieties." As
they were about to enter the house I and other officers stopped them
I said, "You are suspected of committing crime." Stapleton did
not speak. Hawkins said, "I do not know what you mean. I do
not know him," pointing to Stapleton. I then gave instructions that
Hawkins should be taken to Hammersmith Police Station.
which was done by Police-constable Phelps and another officer who
happened to be passing at the time. I then proceeded with Staple
ton to No. 1, Down Place. On the way I said, “I have reason to
believe you are in possession of valuablo miniatures which have been
stolen." He said, "I have not got anything at all. Hawkins brought
me some miniatures to sell about a week ago. He has taken them
away again." Just before reaching the house he said, "The minis
tures are not here. Hawkins has broken them up. I came out to
fetch some beer and on my return I found him destroying them. I
do not know what he has done with them." I made a note of that
as soon as I got into his room. I made a search of the house, and in
& room in the basement behind a large picture on the mantelpiece I
found the whole of these miniatures, some in their frames on one
side and the remainder on the other. The miniatures were hidden
by the picture, which is 4 ft. or 5 ft. square. In a disused gas stove
in the rooms I found some of the frames which had been mutilated.
On the sideboard I found other portions of the frames. I searched
