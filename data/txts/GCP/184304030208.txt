Cross-examined. Q. When did the prisoner come into your service!
A. At the end of 1839-he came on trial first, and then he became my ap-
prentice- I had a partner named Thomas Hutchinson-we have dissolved
partnership-he is alive and well, I believe-we ceased to be partners on the
19th of last April--I have understood that would be an end of the prisoner's
apprenticeship.
Q. Did the prisoner talk of leaving you after he was free? A. I believe I
first gave him warning about a fortnight prior to his giving me warning to
leave, which was in Feb.-I did not induce him to stay by offering to raise
his wages- I will not swear I did not-he may have made two journies in a
week, and sometimes one, after these customers--the number of distinct per-
sons he would have to call on would depend on the number we have-he
would bave to call on forty, fifty, or sixty in a day-he might probably ons
Monday have to call on two hundred, to collect these sums of 1s., 1s. 6d., ar
6d., or half-a-crown. .
Q. Has it not happened sometimes that the prisoner has paid an excess of
money from what he has received ? A. Not on these days-it is very pro-
bable that sometimes he might have paid more than he received-such a thing
might happen as that, out of these two or three hundred items-he has omitted
to put down the 1s. or 6d. in one column, but has put it to another person,
and that would account for the excess--the columns in this book represent
customers- this book lasts six months-here are different columns, in which
the entries ought to be made-such a thing might have happened that the
entries have been made the next day, but it was unknown to me- I do not
recollect your putting to me at the office an enormous number that had got
under the wrong head-I had not time to examine this book every night-
it has been left to my clerk-the prisoner might have paid me sixpence on
the 26th of May-I will not venture to swear he did not pay me the sixpenco
-I generally received the money at night; if not, the first thing in the
morning--I have an account of the money I received—I do not enter every
night myself what money I receive from each ; but if not, I do it in the mom.
ing--sometimes it is done by the clerk-there were three examinations of this
case before Mr. Trail, the Magistrate.
