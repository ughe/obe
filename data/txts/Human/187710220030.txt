I am married, and live at 26, Eagle Street—on 3rd October I was working for Mrs. Turner, and heard the prisoner come downstairs—I know him and know his voice—he went to the door several tunes and said "Open the b——door"—he pushed it and went into the room, and we heard him walk across the floor—he stayed in the room a quarter of an hour, and then I heard him go upstairs, and my attention was then called to smoke coming out of the room, and a little girl came and called out, and I took some pails of water and threw over the fire—the bed was all piled up in a heap by the fireplace and blazing—there was a little fire in the grate but very small, not enough to have caused the bed to catch fire—it was a dead fire, merely a few  cinders—the floor was burnt where the bed was, and also part of the fireplace, the wainscoting. JOHN HEALEY. I am a bootmaker, of 24, Red Lion Street—the prisoner is my landlord—on 3rd October he came into my shop a little before 12 o'clock and stopped there some time until he saw Mr. and Mrs. Lintott go out, and then within a minute he went towards the kitchen, and was absent about a quarter of an hour, and came back and eat down in my shop again, but said nothing till some people passing saw smoke coming from the front kitchen, and I said, "It looks as if the house was on fire, Leach"—he said, "It does"—Lintott's little girl then went through, and came back and halloed out, "Oh, Mr. Leach, the house is on tire"—he said nothing, but sat there till the alarm was given, and then I told him to go out, and I locked my shop door and went down to the kitchen and found the door open and a great smoke and a blaze—I could hardly get in for the smoke, and I told the women to fetch some water—thu mattress and bedding were all piled up against the fireplace, level with the mantelpiece—I soused water over it and turned it over—there was a bundle of wood between the mattress and the floor; not the whole bundle, it was opened and it was burning with the mattress—there was a mass of blaze altogether—the floor was burnt and part of the mantel-piece and the wainscoting—after I put the fixe out I came upstairs and saw Leach standing on the threshold, where some people were accusing him of it, and he said "I mean to burn the b——lot out," and with that he went away—he has been in the habit of drinking on and off all the time I have known him, about eight months—I can't say that he was drunk on this day.
