2229. HENRY PLACEY , stealing 6lbs. weight of sausages, value 5 s  . of, the goods of William Parker , his master; and WILLIAM SIMMONS felo niously receiving the same.
   MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM PARKER. I am a sausage-maker, at High-street, Southwark—Placey was in my employ. On 26th Aug. I delivered him about 1 1/2 cwt. of meat, some beef, and some pork, to chop it, season it, and bring it into the shop in the shape of sausages—I delivered him the last quantity about one o'clock, and between two and three he was brought to me by the policeman arid a basket, containing about six pounds of pork sausages—I have no doubt they were mine by the style of them.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE Q.  Was the meat cut? A.  Yes—my sausages have a different look to most sausages. WILLIAM GRIST( policeman, M  223). On 26th Aug., about half-past two o'clock, I saw Placey come out of the shop—he looked rather bulky in front—he went into the Black Horse and Crown, into the private box, in front of the bar—Simmons was there, with an empty basket and a cloth in it—in about seven minutes Placey came out and went to a watering-place—he had two jugs of beer in his hand—he set them on the ground, and appeared to be undoing his trowsers—Simmons came out with the basket, and put it towards Placey, while he was in the watering-place—I saw a number of sausages turned into the basket—Simmons covered the cloth over them and was going away—I took Placey, and found on him 5 l . 12 s . THOMAS SWAINE( policeman, M  106). I saw Placey go to the public-house, come out, and go to the watering-place, and Simmons after him—he turned some sausages into this basket—I took him with the basket as he was going away—I asked what he had got—he said he had nothing—I found these sausages in the basket.
 (The prisoners received good characters.)
   PLACEY  — GUILTY. Aged 22.
   SIMMONS  — GUILTY. Aged 23.
   Recommended to mercy by the Jury.— Confined Three Months .
 2230. ANN DAY , stealing 1 watch, value 5 l  .; the goods of John Palmer , from his person. JOHN PALMER. I am a gentleman, and live at Walworth. On 7th Sept., about twelve o'clock at night, I was taking a little walk—I had had sundry glasses of grog—I called a cab  in Holborn—I was accompanied by two females, who asked for a ride—the prisoner was one—we rode to the Elephant and Castle, and there had three or four glasses of brandy-and-water—we were then walking along the Walworth-road—I missed a small Geneva watch—I accused them—they both denied it—they were taken to the station—this is my watch.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  You were very drunk? A.  I was a little fresh  —I bad been to a sporting-house, Spring's, in Holborn—I had my watch when I left the cab  —I had a friend with me—he left me, and then I had the two ladies—I had not been in any house, but merely to have some brandy-and-water, I would not like to swear it positively—one of the women took my watch—next morning I was not labouring under the effects of drink to any effect—I knew what I said before the Magistrate. JOHN BAKER( policeman ). I found the prisoner and prosecutor together about three o'clock in the morning—the prisoner and the other woman were searched, but nothing was found.
