Grobecker's Defence. I did not acknowledge myself in any way to be the

that the word, "ground-landlord," was not used at the conversation-- I can
not say I have many witnesses, as I did not anticipate that out of so
straightforward a transaction any charge of a criminal nature could arise
I might imagine I could not pay the bills, and be arrested, but could not
imagine a proceeding of a criminal nature would be brought against me,
and therefore I have not evidence as to all the conversation which took place
at the interview ; however, I have evidence of what did take place, and
though it may be received with suspicion, still I will call that evidence.
WILLIAM GROBECKER, Jun. I remember Mr. Griange and Mr. Moote
coming to my father, in September last. As near as I can recollect, it was
about the 26th-I was in the dining parlour, on the ground floor, near the
window, which is three or four feet from the ground-the window was open-
I heard part of the conversation which took place I heard a knock at the
door the servant went to the door-I immediately afterwards beard some
body passing through the passage I saw my father approach the door-Moore
and Griange went out of the back door, and met my father under the window
-Moore introduced Mr. Griange, who said, “Mr. Grobecker, my business
is very short with you; Mr. Moore has seen bricks in my field, which he
seems to think will suit him to perform a building, which he has contracted
to do" he then said, “Mr. Grobecker, have you any objection to become
security for these bricks ?"-my father said, "No; I consider myself quite
secure" -he then inquired what credit was to be given-Mr. Griange said,
"Two months"--my father said, “That is short: cannot you make it three,
or I think it will be better to make it three, and then all will be right
-Mr. Griange said, “We will see about that, Mr. Grobecker," and they
then left.
