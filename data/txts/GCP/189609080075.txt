September, 1893, this registered letter, address to my firm, was
received at our office-it was opened in my presence by my direc-

had scores of his letters - the Mr. George Boulton referred
to in this letter was then the senior partner & ny firm; he has
since retired through ill-health. This letter contained the passage :
4 As Mr. George B., as and for his firm, published tw forged letters to me
of Miss E. F. B.'s honoured name of dates July 20th and September 11th,
1892, and as he knew when he published these letters that they were
certainly lies, if not forgeries of Miss E. F. B.'s fair ha nd, I certainly
decline to meet Mr. George B. in the manner his letter indicates." The
letter further stated that he would meet Miss B. in the presence of two
dergymen, whom he named, if they consented; asked whether Mr. George B.
and Mr. James B. were still members of the firm, and whether the firm still
acted for Mrs. Emma Boulton, and concluded with the postscript : "The
letters referrred to were forged, to pervert the course of justice. Will Mr.
George B. say from whom he received them L C.") On receipt of that
letter, I took the advice of Counsel (I had previously taken the advice of
Chancery Counsel), and a summons was taken out at Clerkenwell Police
court for criminal libel, and the case came on before the Magistrate the
prisoner was represented by Messrs. Boulton and Co., and by Mr. Besley,
the Counsel—the case was committed for trial to this Cout, and a true bill
was returned on October 16th, and the trial was fixed to take place on
October 21st, but the prisoner did not surrender to take his trial-after
that I received a number of communications and pamphlets from him from Soul
Boulogne, through 1894, 1895, and 1896—I received this letter of July
29th, 1894, from Boulogne. (This informed them, in order that they
Oh might have no difficulty in proving publication, that he wrote and published
the pamphlets, " Honour Proclaimed,” and “My Lady's Honour," and they
might be included in the indictment, particularly as they said the first was
a libel on Mr. George Boulton, and the second was like the first, and that
he was making arrangements to surrender at the Septentber Sessions,
and should require Mrs. Emma Boulton and Miss Boulton as
witnesses in regard to Miss Boulton's alleged letters of July 20th and Sep-
tember 11th, 1892, and in regard to other parts of the alleged libel.) I re-
ceived, among the other pamphlets, these : "Oliver for Roland," and
“Forgery on the Court of Chancery"_this letter of August 29th, 1894,
is in the prisoner's writing from 1893 down to the other day a warrant
was in existence, and in the hands of Inspector Leach; the prisoner was
arrested at Dover, and brought to London.
