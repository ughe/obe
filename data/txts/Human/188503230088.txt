   Re-examined.  The Wilsons were convicted at this Court of uttering counterfeit coin at the February Session ( see p.  639). JAMES SCOTT( Detective W ). On 19th January I went to 17, Clipston Street—this box was shown to me by Harriet Enger in the front room, first-floor—I broke it open and found in it these four double moulds for half-crowns, three double moulds for florins, and two triple moulds for shillings, some plaster-of-Paris, a portion of a battery, some clamps, sand, bottles, and acid ( produced )—no keys were found on the prisoner—Young was arrested on 16th February, no keys were found on her which opened it. MARTIN MCNULTY. I live at 3, George Court, Oxford Street—I was a patient in the Cleveland Street Asylum in January and February, and on 14th January the prisoner was admitted, and occupied a bed opposite mine—he had a beard and moustache when he came in, but they were shaved off in a week by the barber at his request.
   Cross-examined.  You had a very bad cough, and used to spit—you had to wash yourself in bed. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I saw this box two Sessions ago—all these articles are used in making counterfeit coin; the moulds are very good.
   The prisoner in his defence complained that he had not been placed with other persons to be identified. He stated that the articles found were not his, that the box was Mary Young's, and he did not know the contents, and when he went to the Infirmary he determined to have nothing more to do with her; and if the moulds had been his he could have broken them up before going away; and that he had his beard shaved off as it was an encumbrance when ill in bed.
