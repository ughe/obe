 281. CHARLES FIELD , THOMAS EVANS , DANIEL CARROLL, and DAVID BENTLIFF were indicted for stealing 1 box, value 2 l  .; 5 shifts, 5 l  .; 9 petticoats, 4 l  .; 2 night-gowns, 4 l  .; 1 pair of stays, 2 l  .; 4 pairs of drawers, 4 l  .; 42 pairs of stockings, 15 l  .; 45 handkerchiefs, 30 l  .; 8 veils, 90 l  .; 10 pairs of cuffs, 3 l  .; 6 wreaths of artificial flowers, 2 l  .; 17 napkins, 4 l  .; 19 doyleys, 1 l  .; 46 towels, 1 l  . 13 s  .; the goods of Sir Richard Paul Jodrell , Baronet.—2nd Count, stating them to be the goods of James Butler .
   MR. FLOOD conducted the Prosecution. SARAH BALLS. I am lady's-maid in the service of Sir Richard Paul Jodrell, baronet. The family were at Brighton in Oct. last, and left on the 28th, by an early train—I assisted in packing up the luggage, particularly Lady Jodrell's wearing apparel, in a large black leather trunk, with brass nails—I put in some veils, some dozens of silk stockings, from thirty to forty pocket-handkerchiefs, worth four or five guineas each—the whole was worth about 200 l .—Charles Crocker, the second footman, locked the box—I saw the box in the drawing-room after it was corded—I have not seen it since—I came by the same train as the family, and the servants were to follow—two men and two maids came by the same train as the luggage.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  How large was the box? A.  I do not know exactly—I have seen others as big, and with brass nails—it was like a great many trunks—I did not see it when it quitted the house—I should say it was not uncorded to put anything in—one of the housemaids had the key—most of the things belonged to Lady Jodrell—there were some very fine damask dinner napkins, for her ladyship's own use—I did not see either the napkins or the doyleys put into the box—when I last saw them they were in the room where the box was, and were to be put in. FANNY HEARNDON. I am housemaid at Sir Richard Jodrell's. On the 27th of Oct.
