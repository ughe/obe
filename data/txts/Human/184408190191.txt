   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Homewood had been in your brother-in-law's service two or three years? A.  Yes, I believe he was re spected by all that employed me—he was about taking a shop and forge of his own, of a man named Burrows—I went to Burrows—I did not persuade him not to let Homewood have the shop, nor tell him he would not have the rent—I did not say, "I will give you the same rent that he will, and will pay you now" nor that I could afford to keep it shut up, and he could not—I did not ultimately take it—I believe Homewood did—I did not say, if Homewood's wife had not been so insolent to me, I would not have brought the charge—I did not say so at any time to my knowledge—Mrs. Homewood wished the charge brought—this is my name to my deposition—it was read over to me—( looking at it )—I have said, that if the prisoner Homewood's wife had not been insulting to me, I should not have preferred this charge—I cannot recollect that—I have sworn to the best of my knowledge to-day—I do not know which is true—Mrs. Homewood came to me, and first begged me not to do it, and then she met me in the street, and said, "Now we will see it out if you like"—she has been several times to me—I did not say to Nor ris's wife, "You can send the shoes to my place, I will give you so much"—she said, "I don't know what I shall do with my little business, for my children are starving"—I said, "If you have anything you can send it me, I will allow you the profits out of it"—the prisoners were in prison four or five days—they were bailed by the Magistrate—I did not give Norris into custody immediately—I saw these shoes hanging in his shop quite exposed—any one could see them—they had only been there about one day—I gave Norris in charge the same afternoon, and Homewood the same day—he was discharged on the 6th of July—I have borrowed shoes—it is a very uncommon thing—I have borrowed shoes of my brother-in-law—Homewood lent them to me when he had an order to do so—Kent was present when Homewood said to Norris, "Say you borrowed them"—Home wood has got a forge now, and is working as a farrier, nearly half-a-mile from me—I do not think he has got any of my customers—he said, "I have neither sold nor made away with your property"—I said, "You have; here is a proof; some of my own make, I made a few days ago"—he denied it—I said I would send for a policeman, and Homewood said to Norris, "Say you borrowed them"—I was within five or six feet, walking away, and Home wood Raid, "Master, come back, don't be spiteful, I will tell you all about it"—I had some beer in the course of the day at the Oxford Arms—I had none with the prisoners—Homewood was not in my service after I made the charge.
