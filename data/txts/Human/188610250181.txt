1113. DANIEL DACEY  (22) , Robbery with violence on George; Samuel Cherubin , and stealing 11 s  ., his money.
   MR. SANDERS Prosecuted ; MR. HUTTON Defended. SAMUEL GEORGE CHERUBIN. I live at the Cottage, 600, Commercial Road East, and am a clerk—on 30th September, about 11.30, I was going home, and went into a public-house in the High Street, Borough, and on leaving I counted my money; I had 11 s . exactly, which was in my right-hand trousers pocket—I turned into a narrow turning, and while I was unbuttoning my trousers I was attacked by three men from behind; they must have followed me—the prisoner was one of them; he threw his left, arm round me, and took hold of me by the throat, and another took hold, of my left arm, and the third took hold of my right hand with his left hand and placed his right hand into my pocket, and I felt him take my money—I never released the prisoner—I called out; generally to them, particularly the one taking my money, and said "You scoundrel"—the, two men ran away, and a policeman came to my assistance immediately, while I was still on the ground—the three of them threw me to the, ground; it was quite a melee  —the prisoner said "Let go"—I was not aware there was a constable, but as soon as I saw it was I let him go, and the constable, assisted by another one, took him to the station, and L then found my money was gone—I was very much hurt, and I felt the effects on my throat for three weeks—I was in bed next day when the constable called; I have recovered now—I had not seen the prisoner before to my knowledge—I had had a glass, but I knew perfectly what I was about—I walked to the station with the constable.
   Cross-examined.  I put out my hands and seized the first person I could lay hold of, particularly the one that held me—I live from a mile and a half to two miles from this public-house—I had left home in the afternoon, and had been to a wharf where I had been formerly engaged—I had no drink there—I then went to another wharf on this side of the water, and I had a drink of mild and bitter, there with the chief clerk—after that I went over the water; I was in company with other friends—I might have had five or six drinks during the evening, but I should say I was a sober man that evening—I had no whisky; I never touch spirits—if the constable says I was the worse for drink I should say he was mistaken; I was excited—I was at the station no longer than it would require to take the charge; not half an hour—the station was about a quarter of a mile from where this took place—I did not walk beside the constable, I walked behind—he saw me at the station, and I had a conversation with him there.
   Re-examined.  I was naturally excited and dazed at the injuries I had received. JOHN WATERMAN( Policeman M  329). I was on duty in the High Street, Borough, about 12 o'clock on the night of 30th September last, and from information I received I went to St. Margaret's Court, a turning out of the High Street, and saw the prosecutor struggling with three men, and I saw him fall; he was about 12 yards up the court—there are some houses at the other end—I went up to him, and the two men saw me coming and started off down the way they came—the prisoner fell down on the top of the prosecutor; I went and pulled him off—the prosecutor had hold of him here , very tight, and when he saw I was a constable he got up and said, "They have robbed me,"
