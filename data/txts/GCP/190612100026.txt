AMY CATHERINE Durr, the wife of Edwin Alexander Duff, 22,
Onslow Gardens, Kensington. On November 6 I purchased a postal
order for 21s. (produced). I sent my footman for it to 94, Fulham
Road. I retained the counterfoil and sent it in an envelope ad
dressed to the office manager, Haymarket Theatre, by my butler to
post. I had no acknowledgment.
WILLIAM HEAD, butler to Mrs. Duff. I posted the letter in ques
tion about 11 a.m., in the pillar-box at the corner of Onslow Gardens.
WILLIAX HENRY LEVERTON, office manager, Haymarket Theatre. I
had a telephone message from Mrs. Duff on November 6 booking two
seats, and afterwards a message from her saying she had sent a postal
order and had not had a receipt. I told her I had not received it,
nor had I.
ANNIE HORTON LEVERTON, supervisor, Post Office, Piccadilly, 1.
saw Gavin in the office on November 6, about 2.10 p.m. He pre-
sented this order for 21s., which I cashed. It was already filled in.
JOHN CAMERON, assistant inspector of messengers, G.P.O., 8.W.
district. I know Miss Leverton. I received instructions from a
clerk in the office to follow Gavin from the counter at 2.10. He went
into Wells Street and then into the "Three Crowns" public house,
where he stopped about three minutes. There was a postman with
him that I do not know. I think his number is 810 S.W. (Gavin
stood up.] That is he. I followed them into Regent Street, and
Randall went into the branch office, No. 21. Gavin stood at the
corner of Regent Street and Jermyn Street. Randall came out and
joined Gavin, and they went across Regent Street into Jermyn Street
towards the Haymarket.
JOHN RANDALL (prisoner, not on oath) said he did not deny cashing
the orders, but they were given him by Gavin ; that he was trying
hard for work, and left the Army with a good character after 12
years' service; that he tried in vain to get employment, and had to
take to selling newspapers ; Gavin asked him to cash the orders
and that he gave him a drink for doing so; that he made no secret
of it, and did it in the presence of everyone, and did not know they
were stolen ; that he asked Gavin why he did not cash them himself,
and he said, "It looks so bad for a postman to go in and cash them."
and he said, "All right." It was the first time he had been in trouble.
Verdict, Guilty.
