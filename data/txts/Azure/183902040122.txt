and permitted him, on one or two occasions, to come to the house, when
we had things to clean up, believing him to be honest—he was there on
the 25th of January—the plate in use was kept in a box, with green baize
to it, in the pantry—the prisoner bad access to the pantry, and had an op-
portunity of seeing it there—on Saturday morning, the 26th of January, I
was reading in the newspaper of the loss of Mr. Platt's plate, in Russell-
square—I went up to my master, and pointed it out to him ; and, in conse-
quence of his directions, I went down to the pantry to bring up the spare
plate, and found it was gone—I suspcted the prisoner, and gave information
to the police—the plate is worth from 151. to 201.—1 had seen it safe in the
pantry about a week before—I had no occasion to refer to it in the mean
time.
