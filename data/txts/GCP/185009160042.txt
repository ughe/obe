William Cleaver. I live at 40, Eccleston-place. On the afternoon of
3rd Sept. I was at my door, and saw Stevens bowl the loop against Fox's
leg-he trod on it, and turned it upward, and put bis hand on the top-
Stevens said, “Leave go, or else I will give you a slap on the ear"-For
said he would give him another-Stevens put himself into a fighting
attitude, and Fox the same, and struck Stevens on the breast-I did not see
Stevens strike him--Mr. Harwood went between them, and parted thetn ; and
when he bad got them nearly apart, Mrs. Stevens came up, seized Fox by the
breast, and drove his head against my shutters -Hayes then ran up from the
beer-shop, about two doors off, and seized on Fox, with Mr. and Mrs. Stevens
- he hit him with his fist as fast as he could; I could not count the blows-
there was a cart there, with the shafts lying down, without any horses, and as
Fox was walking backwards he fell over the shafts, with his head on the
ground and his heels upwards-it is a Macadamised road-be got up, and
tried to keep them off him-three or four more ran out from the beer-shop,
and closed in with the Stevens's-the mob gathered round, and hid the fight
from me, and I saw no more-when Fox got up he seemed confused and
frightened, and very red in the face.
