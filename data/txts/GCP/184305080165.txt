Joseph Frost. Some years ago I was carrying on business in Clerken-
well, as a brass-founder, in partnership with my brother Isaac I placed a large
number of sovereigns in this field-they were my own private property that
I had drawn out of business some of them were in a jelly pot or jar, and
some in a pot-I pointed out the ground where this money was found that
was where I deposited it.
Ann COOPER. The prisoner lodges with me-he is hard of hearing at
times, when he gets a cold-he and his family were in great distress--1 lent
his wife 18.-he has been a honest man for what I know.
THOMAS MALLETT, I am chief clerk at Clerkenwell Police-court. The
prisoner made a statement there on the 8th of April-it was taken down in
writing, and read to him-he did not sign it-(read)—"The prisoner says,
'I never saw the pot after the money was shared ; neither did I share the
money. The money was given into my hands ; a sovereign was put into
my hands; fifty-one altogether; not one half-sovereign, that I know of was
among them ; fifty-one sovereigns altogether were put into my hands; I
lost them all, or else they have been stolen from me."
Not Guilty.
1648. JAMES JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of May,
1 purse, value 58.; 1 half-sovereign, and 2 shillings, the property of Stephen
Nott; from the person of Martha Nott.
MARTHA Nort. I am the wife of Stephen Nott-he lives at an hotel in
Oxford-street-on the 7th of May, I and my husband were going in a steam-
boat from Hungerford pier to the Tunnel pier-there was a great rushing
behind me-I felt a pull at my bag in which I had a purse and in it a half-
sovereign- I turned and saw the prisoner take his hand out of my bag --he
was close to me, I said, "What business have you to put your hand in my
bag?"-he said, "I beg your pardon, you must be mistaken"-I said, "My
purse is in your hand-my purse was in his hand-I took him by the
collar and called to my busband, and said, “This man has taken my purse"
