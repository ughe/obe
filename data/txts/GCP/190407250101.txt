WILLIAM JONES. I have been the timekeeper and storekeeper for
eleven years, and live at 15, Hanlon Street, Deptford it is the duty of
the watchmen to see that the store room where the solder was kept was
locked-after all the hands are gone there is no reason for going in there
--the partition that divides the store room from the passage is about
T ft. high; anyone would have to get over that if they did not go through
the door--I keep the key from 8 8.m. till 7 p.m.--I had been missing
Bolder for some time, and I reported it to the manager, and a box to hold
it was made about twelve months ago-it was a box with a lock and held
about 30 cwt., but sometimes, there being too much, we had to put some
of the solder on the top--we were very busy at the time the robberies
Were taking place, and we were using from 30 cwt. to 2 tons weekly-on
April 12th there was about 15 ewt. in the box, which would do ordinarily
until the 18th-on April 15th I found that the lock of the box had been
broken, and that we had almost run out of solder, and we had to melt
down all the scraps to keep the business going the value per ewt. of
solder is about 678. or 688.-I informed the firm of what had happened-
on June 3rd, as instructed by Mr. Clark, I hid myself at 8 p.m., which
was closing time, in the store room-Goshawk was out on his rounds, and
Thundow was in his van in the yard close by—after about an hour and a
half Goshawk came in at the door, which I had received instructions to
leave unlocked, looked round, and took a bundle of solder from the box,
which was unlocked-I informed Mr. Clark of it-Smith was in hospital
