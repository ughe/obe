HENRY GOODWIN re-examined.  The prisoner said nothing about Miss Grey at the station—not till next morning before the Magistrate—he appeared sober—I do not know Miss Grey—the prisoner requested me to ask my assistant if he knew her—he is not here.
   Prisoner's Defence.  Miss Grey had been a lodger of mine, and owed me 14 l .;, she paid me in dribblets; Mr. Goodwin told me he had made inquiries of his man, and there were two women in the shop at this time. I am an up holsterer at Speilinghurst, near Tonbridge-wells; I came to Deptford for some money owing to me. The inspector sent the policeman out, he was away two hours, and when he returned he said there was such a person in the New-town, but he could not find her; an officer there said he could find her directly. The officer speaks very falsely; I received the shawl from Miss Grey's hand. GUILTY. Aged 54.— Confined Three Months .

NATHANIEL KING was indicted for stealing 2 tame pheasants, price 35 s  .; 6 tame pigeons, 12 s  .; 7 tame fowls, 14 s  .; 2 ducks, 5 s  .; and 9 pears, 6 d  ., the property of Thomas Perronet Thompson . JOSIAH VINCENT( police-constable R  196.) On Monday morning the 8th Jan., about half-past two o'clock, I was on duty in Church-street, Deptford, and saw the prisoner crossing Church-street, on the footpath of the railway, towards town, with a large bundle at hit back—being an unseasonable hour I went after him and asked what he had in the bundle—he said that he was a servant, that he had just left his situation, and had got his dirty clothes; that he had met with a friend and had taken a glass—I asked if they were his own—he said, "Yes"—I took him to the station, and found the bundle contained two live pheasants, four live pigeons, two dead ones, seven dead fowls, and two dead ducks, and no dirty clothes—I showed the things to Waterhouse.
   Prisoner. Q.  What distance was I from you when you first saw me? A.  About thirty yards. JOSEPH WATERHOUSE. I am gardener to Col. Thomas Perronet Thompson—he had some pheasants, pigeons, fowls, and ducks, safe on the evening of the 7th Jan.—I missed them between eight and nine o'clock on the 9th—I have since seen them in Vincent's hands, and know them to be the same—they were kept in the stable and loft—the stable was not locked.
   Prisoner. Q.  When I lived with Col. Thompson, were these things kept in the stable? A.  In the summer they run loose, but in winter they were closed—I forget whether Col. Thompson had pheasants while you were in his ser vice—the fowls used to be locked in the hen-house, and the key taken to a drawer kept by young Mr. Thompson—I can identify all the poultry as my master's—I know the pheasants by a ring round their necks, and the cock pheasant had lost its tail—they are English ring pheasants—I am quite sure of the fowls and ducks—the yard-gate was open till ten o'clock—I do not know whether it was locked after that.
