1238. THOMAS BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , a pig's head, value 2 s., the goods of John King.
   JOHN HILL. I am servant to John King, a butcher. On the 12th of July, this pig's head laid on the shopboard outside the window. I missed it, and upon turning round saw the prisoner with it. I found it on him about sixty yards off.
 The prisoner pleaded Distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1239.  JOHN NORMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of July , a tea-caddy, value 8 s., the goods of John Lowen.
   JOHN LOWEN. I am an upholsterer, and live in Whitechapel-road. I had a tea-caddy standing near the door - my man gave me information about one o'clock on the 15th of July, and I missed it - the prisoner was brought back with it under his arm - I gave him in charge. I asked why he took it; he said nothing.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Confined Two Months.
 1240.  GEORGE SOLLOWAY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , a hat, value 3 s., and a handkerchief, value 1 s., the goods of Henry Howes.
   HENRY HOWES. I am a coachman. On the 1st of July, I lodged at the Sportsman, public-house, High-street, Mary-le-bone. About one o'clock I took off my hat and put it on the tap-room table, with my handkerchief in it. I went out for half an hour, and on returning they were gone. Nobody was in the room when I went out. I found it before the Magistrate two days after. My hat was new.
   JOSEPH PARKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Cambridge-heath. On the 1st of July the prisoner came about six o'clock in the evening, and offered to pawn a hat, which I refused, as it was wet. He went to the door, returned, and offered a handkerchief, which I took in in the name of Soloway.
   HENRY HOWES. I have no mark on the handkerchief.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1241.  JAMES SIMMS was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of September , 14 ozs. of tea, value 2 s., the goods of the   United Company of Merchants trading to the East Indies.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to belong to persons unknown.
 MR. WALFORD conducted the prosecution.
   AARON SAUL. I am a labourer in the service of the East India Company. On the 3d of September the prisoner was employed to carry hoops to mend the chests. I saw him looking at several chests, and at last came to one which was very much broken. He stirred the tea up with a small stick, and put his right hand into the chest several times, and then into his pocket. I informed the elder.
   WILLIAM GIBSON. I searched the prisoner. I could find nothing. He denied having anything. I made him unbutton his trowsers, and found he had a large pair of pantaloons under them, with a bag sown where the pockets should be, and there I found 14 ozs. of tea. The bag would not be felt in rubbing down. He had 16 s. 6 d. a week, and works from eight to two o'clock only. I have lost the bag.
 Prisoner's Defence. It was not a bag; it was a pocket.
   AARON SAUL. It was above a foot long.
 GUILTY. Aged 41. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 1242.  JOHN WILLIAM ROGERS was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of June , two coats, value 8 l.; a waistcoat, value 1 l., and a pair of trowsers, value, 2 l. the goods of George Charles Coward.
 No evidence. NOT GUILTY.
 1243.  JOHN POWIS and JAMES KNIGHT were indicted for stealing, on the 30th of March , two knives, value 6 d., and five forks, value 5 s., the goods of Judy Brown, and ANN KNIGHT was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to be stolen.
   JUDY BROWN. I live in Rosemary-lane. On the 30th of March. I found a chest broken open, and two knives and five forks gone; four of them were silver handled; also two brushes and three tumblers. Powis used to come to the house to see his grandfather, but I never saw him after missing the things.
   ABRAHAM ELLIS. I am a headborough. On the 7th of July, I took Powis, and asked what he did with the things he stole from Mrs. Brown - he said voluntarily, he would take me to James Knight's where he had left them; we went and found another knife there, and two knives and five forks in the cupboard. I told her to bring her son to me when she came home, and she did in the evening.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1244.  RICHARD SUTTON was indicted for stealing on the 12th of July , three caps, value 3 s.; a pair of pockets, value 1 s.; a hammer, value 3 d., and a gimblet, value 3 d., the goods of John Holt.
   JOHN SMITH. I am a publican, and live in St. George's Fields. On the 12th July, I was at the Duke of Bedford public-house, Old Gravel-lane, talking to Holt, and saw the prisoner enter the yard, and as he went out, he took these things off the line, put them under his waistcoat, and went into the tap-room - we went and took them from him.
   JOHN HOLT. I was in my yard with Smith; he gave me information. I saw the property found on the prisoner in the tap-room - he was tipsy.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 GUILTY. Aged 50. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1 s. and Discharged.
 1245.  MARY STURCH was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , a purse, value 1 s.; a sovereign, and 17 s., the property of Charles Upstill.
   ELIZABETH UPSTILL.
