   MR. CAARTEEN . Q.  Has the prisoner been in the habit of visiting your coffee-house? A.  I have seen him in a few times, perhaps five or six times, generally with some one else; he was with two more that night—I did not know either of them.
 (  The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate was here read: "I am not guilty of it; I was not there; I was not in the coffee-shop; I was in bed; I never saw the coffee-shop keeper before yesterday.") JAMES FROST re-examined.  This is the tablecloth I found in the hat. MARIA CONLEY re-examined.  I know this tablecloth, from my using it the last sixteen months—there is no mark on it—here is the fellow one to it ( producing it, ) only one is longer than the other—I can swear this is my employer's tablecloth—it has been in daily use, with this other one, and two more—they are the same patterns, and the same person made the two, the porter's wife, who was there before I was—they are hemmed in the same manner—two tablecloths were taken away—the other one is not found—these boots belong to Mr. Rimmer—I know them, from so frequently taking them out of the cupboard.
   Cross-examine. Q.  Is Mr. Rimmer here? Q. No; those gentlemen are both single—I have never had a mistress—I did not see the porter's wife make these tablecloths—there were four tablecloths in use—I did not wash them—I laid them occasionally on the table for sixteen months—there is no mark on them.
   MR. CAARTEEN . Q.  Did you give them out to wash? A.  Yes, and received them, and examined them, when they were brought back—besides that, I laid the cloth, and have such knowledge of them that I can swear to them. Witness for the Defence. MARGARET GILL. I live at 3, Abbey-place, Smith-street, Hercules-buildings; my husband is a slater.
