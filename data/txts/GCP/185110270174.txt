ALFRED FRANCIS re-examined. This bill is the prisoners's writing-it is
"Paid, S. Ansell."
ELIZABETH Beach. My husband is a builder. He deals with Messrs.
Francis for cement-I know the prisoner as warehouseman at Scotland-yard.
On 11th Sept. I paid him 7s. 60.-I got this receipt from him for 73. 71d.
GEORGE WILLIAM JUDD. I am cashier to Messrs. Francis. I have been
there twenty-six years, the prisoner was warehouseman in my masters'
employ-it was his duty to sell the goods of Messrs. Francis in the yard -
he had a check-book, and when he sold goods it was his duty to make out a
check as a bill and receipt if the goods were sold for cash, and give that to
the party who bought the goods; and if they were sold on credit, the
goods were merely filled in-the counterfoil remained attached to the book
it was his duty to make out a weekly stock-paper-it consisted of the goods
that had been sold during the week from the Friday to the Thursday night-
he was to enter in the day-book the money and goods sold on credit-he
brought the stock-paper and books to me every week-I used to look at the
account and receive the money from him-the amount of cash in the stock.
paper I compared with the amount in his day-book, and if they agreed I took
the balance from him-he paid me at the time—the day-book was the voucher
to me for the moneys that had been received by him-considered that every
thing ought to be entered in the day-book- I looked at the day-book to see
what had been received by him-I have the day-book here- I have com-
pared it with the stock-papers from Jan. to Sept.- I have found that the
stock-papers agree with the day-book- I do not find any entry in the day
book of 1l. 10s. received from Mr. Keith, or 10s. 6d. from Laycock, or
7s. 7d. from Beach.
Cross-examined. Q. How often was this book brought to you ? A. Every
Friday night, almost invariably-it has been on Saturday-I do not recollect
that it ever went two weeks-the money received is, on both sides of this book,
made up a total at the end of the week-it was by this book and the stock-
paper he paid me the money-the book and the stock-papers agree as to the
amount-he gave me the stock-paper and what appeared there-he occasion-
ally left the book with me-from 23rd May till 11th June I was absent-Mt.
Friend was there then-the prisoner has come sometimes on Friday evening
and sometimes on Saturday—he has come when I was not there-in one or
two instances I think he left the book with the wharf-clerk; but when I had
the book the paper was in it, and I found the book and paper to agree-he
might have left it with one of my fellow-clerks, but I do not recollect such an
instance--this book is in his handwriting—there were no other clerks at
Scotland-yard who might have made entries while he was there-there was
no one but himself-if a large quantity of cement were delivered he would
get assistance.
JURY. Q. How often did you examine the counterfoils and the stock-
paper with the day-book? A. I examined the cash at the time it was no
part of my duty to examine the counterſoils with the book--that was done by
a clerk at Nine Elms-he is not here: the book is.
MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. When he gave these bills to the customers, was it
his duty to enter this money in the day-book ? A. Yes; I was sometimes out
when he came with money-I compared the day-book and stock-paper with
the balance of money that had been left-I did that in all cases, and found it
correct-I received from him, during the period I have mentioned, the sums
that appeared to be entered in the book--the whole of the money entered in
