ROBERT MORRIS sworn.
 I am servant to Mr. Langdale. On the 9th of June I went with a constable to the prisoner's lodgings in Hosier-lane, Smithfield; I found a cask and funnel there; they are Mr. Langdale's property. The constable Mr. Bush came and told me of the prisoner; we went after him but could not find him; he was taken afterwards.
   JOHN BUSH sworn.
 I am a constable of Farringdon Ward Without; I took the prisoner on the 9th of June, between nine and ten o'clock. I found this cask and funnel, and two other casks, in his lodgings.
 Did any person live with him? - Only a woman he cohabited with as his wife, as he told me; about an hour after I had been to his lodging, I was informed where he was and went and took him.
 Court. How came you to go to his lodgings? - Mr. Langdale's servant came to me and said there was some of their liquor in the prisoner's room.
   WILLIAM BRIGGS sworn.
 I live in Hosier-lane, No. 29, in the same house with the prisoner. Upon the 7th of June, the day on which Mr. Langdale's house was beset, a great deal of liquor was brought by some persons, I did not know who they were; I told the landlady it was wrong to let it come in. She said she did not like to let it come in, but had not seen Cubbage, and he was so desperate a fellow she was afraid to speak to him. On the 7th in the evening, about eight o'clock, I met the prisoner on the stairs with a copper funnel and some kind of spiritual liquor in it; I let him pass on, and I went out. I hope if any body comes to swear for him you will please to observe them for they must be forsworn.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I never saw the funnel; I know nothing of it.
 For the prisoner.
   MARGARET CURRY sworn.
 The prisoner has lodged three months in the house where I lodge; concerning Mr. Langdale's affair I saw all the liquor brought in; none was brought in by the prisoner that I saw, and I believe I saw it all brought in.
 Who was it brought in by? - By a strange young man I never saw before nor since to my knowledge.
 How came he to bring it to your house? - I am only a lodger in the house; I sat at the door from seven till eleven o'clock, and saw it brought in at different times.
 Whom was it brought to in the house? - I do not know; it was found in the prisoner's apartment, I believe he was not at home at that time; I did not see him; he was out all the time the liquor was brought in I believe.
 Cross Examination.
 You was not in his room? - No.
 How can you swear he was not at home then? - I sat at the door from before seven till near eleven o'clock, in the course of which time the prisoner neither went in nor out; I saw a strange young man, who I had never seen before nor since, bring in a great cask of liquor; I believe that is the cask which is produced; he went a second time and brought some in a pail; he came a third time with some in the copper funnel; he put it to my face two or three times and wanted me to drink. I refused; I said I thought it a strange thing to bring liquor home in an open funnel. I asked him which way he stopped the funnel? He said he put a bung at the bottom of the funnel; I believe it is the same funnel; it was of the same colour; it was bent like this funnel; a few minutes after the last of the liquor was brought in the prisoner came in all in a sweat and said, He had been helping Mr. Langdale remove some of his things for fear the fire should reach his place.
 Prisoner. Mr. Woollaston I said.
 Witness. Mr. Woollaston's. He said for fear the fire from Mr. Langdale's should reach Mr. Woollaston's place he was removing some of his effects.
 What time did he come home? - Between seven and eleven; he followed the last liquor in. I was then sitting at the door in my own chair; the alarm was so great I could not stay in my apartment.
 Is Mr. Woollaston here? - No.
 Where did the prisoner go to after he came home? - I do not know; he went up stairs.
