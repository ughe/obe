 Cross-examined. I work for Cockerell. Calcutt was not there a second. About June 11 I made a statement to Sergeant Ferrier that I had no recollection of anyone being with Cockerell or Calcutt that night. Cockerell afterwards recalled it to my memory. WALTER TROTT(prisoner, on oath), recalled. Cross-examined. The turnover in my business has been about £60 to £70 a week for the last 18 years. It has decreased since the Inquiry. My shop is rented at £60 a years, and I keep a carman and a shopman. The profits are £3 to £4 a week. Calcutt was a customer before I went on the Board, and became a bigger customer since 1904; I have known him ten years. Stammers, Gould, Warren, and myself stood for election on one ticket. I and Grimes were great friends after my election. I do not know where he is. He never did as he liked with me. I did not follow him, I was not called "Grimes' dog." At Christmas, 1905, Mrs. Calcutt paid me £5 2s. 6d. Calcutt never paid me anything. After Calcutt's arrest he called when I was out, and I met him afterwards with Heward. He said nothing about £16. His object was to make up a case against the Guardians, so as to clear him self. He came to entrap me. I twice borrowed money from Haseltine when on Spitalfields Market—that was about the Inquiry time. I was never in Calcutt's house with Grimes and Loftus or with either of them. Calcutt never spoke to me at the "Salisbury," about the drainage job at the laundry or said Knight wanted to take too much off. I voted for the 1s. 6d. at the full Board; there was a lot of argument about it. Knight would not pay more than 1s. I never discussed the question of Barker paying £300 or £400 on a contract at Spooner's. I was introduced to Barker, junior, at the banquet given by him on the laying of the foundation stone of the ex tension building, just after my election. I have tried to get contract from the Bromley Guardians, but have not succeeded. Calcutt did not introduce me to any Guardian on the Bromley Board. I only know one whom I knew before as a personal friend. Symes was going in for the Nurses' Home contract, after Barker, junior, had been refused. I met Symes one Friday about 4 p.m., coming home from Stratford Market. Grimes took me to him. Symes said he was going to tender. I said I should vote for the lowest man. I never said I would influence the con tract his way for a consideration; he has said that falsely out of spite. I never heard that Symes wrote to Thacker to complain of it. Warren called a meeting about an anonymous letter from somebody saying he had been recently visited by two members of the Board who said they were in a position to influence a contract. I never thought it was from Symes or that it alluded to me. The letter was long after my visit to Symes. It is not true that I telephoned to Symes. After I saw him Symes did meet me outside my shop. I said I was very busy and would see him later. I did not again see him. I never wore the overcoat given me by Calcutt, it was knocking about the shop; the carman used it or I might put it over my shoulders on a wet morning.
