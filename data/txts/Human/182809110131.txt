 1775.  SARAH LAMBETH was indicted for embezzlement.
   MARH ELTON. I am the wife of John Elton; he is a varnish-maker. The prisoner lived with me to go on errands, she was to receive money for me, and to give it me as soon as she received it.
   MARY BROCKLESBY. The prosecutrix washed for me for many years; I paid the prisoner a sovereign, on the 15th of August, for her mistress.
   MARY ELTON. She never paid it to me; she went with a basket of clean linen and never returned - I went after her, and found her on the 17th, in the Pancras-road; I got an officer and her took her - she told me she had bought some clothes with the sovereign; she had been in my service three weeks - I gave her victuals, drink, and lodging, and some clothes: she had received half a sovereign from Mrs. Brocklesby which she brought me.
   RICHARD COOPER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner; she said she had the money and bought some clothes - I found her old clothes at a house in Dyot-street, where she had been: she was an unprotected girl, and this good woman took her in out of charity.
 GUILTY. Aged 16.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury.
     Judgment Respited.
 1776.  DAVID EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of August , three 10l. bank notes, the property of John Evans.
 JOHN EVANS. I am a  cheesemonger, and live in Clothfair; the prisoner is my eldest son of ten. On the 10th of August, I had a pocket-book, which contained three 10l. Bank notes, and a number of cheques; I missed it about ten o'clock that morning - it had been in a kind of teachest or caddy, in a closet, in my bed-room; the caddy was locked - it was taken from my bed-room up to the attic, and the nails were drawn: my son had been in my house from Whit Sunday till the 10th of August - before that he had been in the work-house; he got into the work-house and then got away - I have seen two of the notes in the possession of the constable; he sent back the pocket-book and the cheques by a porter.
   ROBERT DALLON. I am a constable. I was on my rounds on the 10th of August, and went into the Plough public-house; I received information there, and waited until the afternoon, when the prisoner came, and I took him into custody - I took two 10l. notes from him and seven sovereigns and a half; I told him what I took him for - he looked very stupid and strange, but gave up the notes very willingly; I asked him how he came to do so, he said he was very sorry - he did not deny it.
   JOHN EVANS re-examined. Q. Are these two of the notes you lost? A. Yes - they have my writing on them; I knew they are what I lost - my son has laboured under mental derangement; he was at Bethnal-green for nine months, and then at St. Luke's for a twelve month - I believe he was at this time labouring under mental derangement.
   ROBERT DALLON re-examined. Q. Did you not take up another person on suspicion? A. No - I understood he was acquainted with a girl in Denmark-court; he offered her the notes, and she refused them.
   ALEXANDER CULLEN. I am landlord of the Plough - the prisoner came there on the 10th of August, and called for a glass of porter; he then asked me to send this book to W. Smith - I got a man to take it; I had never seen the prisoner before.
   FRANCIS EVANS. I live with my father. My brother has been out of his mind for two years; I believe he is out of his mind now - he has been discharged as a lunatic. NOT GUILTY, believing him to be insane.
 1777.  CHRISTOPHER MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 28 lbs. of rope, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Blyth.
   DAVID STEVENS. I am clerk to Mr. Thomas Blyth, who is a merchant and ship owner. On the 8th of August, between four and five o'clock, I was superintending the landing of some goods on the wharf; Driscol told me there was a boy in the warehouse taking some rope - I went there, and saw the prisoner with it; I asked what he was going to do with it; he said to take it to his master - I asked who his master was; he said William John Brown, a rigger, at Wapping; he had moved the rope five or six yards.
   MICHAEL DRISCOL. I am a labourer. I saw the prisoner take a piece of rope from the inside of the warehouse - he was bringing it out towards the gate; this is the rope- I saw him on the premises five or six days before, and I turned him off.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I thought I saw a ship, which I expected to go on board of; a man asked me to carry this to Narrow-street, Wapping, and said he would give me 6d. I took it up, and this man came and asked where I was going; I said to my employer's, a man of the name of Brown, the foreman then came, and said I should not go off the premises till he saw my employer - I was going to find him, and he called me back.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury- Confined 3 Months.
