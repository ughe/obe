1976. ANN HARRISS was indicted for stealing 1 5 l  . Bank-note, the monies of Thomas Whitewood , her master. MARTHA WHITEWOOD. I am the wife of Thomas Whitewood, of South-street, Greenwich—the prisoner was in my service. On the 9th of Aug., a gentleman called to pay me some rent—he paid me a 5 l . note and 17 l . in silver—I put it all into a pocket-book, and placed it in a sideboard-drawer, in the parlour—the prisoner knew of it—I said before her that I had received it—she had access to the room—the drawer was left open—next day I placed the pocket-book under lock and key, without examining it—I do not know whether the note was in it then or not—next day, Wednesday the 11th, I went to the pocket-book, and missed the 5 l . note and 3 s .—nobody but the prisoner and my daughter knew anything about it—a constable was sent for, and the prisoner said, in my presence, she had not seen it—on Friday, the 13th, she came into the kichen, and asked me if I would forgive her if she paid me back the whole of the money—I asked her if she took the money—she said, "No"—I said, "Can I receive of you what you say you have never taken of me? No, I cannot do it"—in about a minute she came again, begged me to forgive her, and said she had taken the note and 3 s .—I had told her that day, that if she had been tempted to take it, if she would replaced it, I would forgive her—I said nothing about her confessing, only about replacing the money—I sent for a policeman in the evening, and gave her in charge for something else—she is an orphan—I had no character with her—mine is the first place she has been at. GEORGE GOODE. I took the prisoner on Friday evening at Mr. White wood's—I told her she was charged with stealing a 5 l . note—I cautioned her that I should tell the Magistrate what she said—she was anxious to tell the truth, and said she had taken the note and burnt it, and had taken other things besides.
   Prisoner.  I am guilty. GUILTY
 1977. ANN HARRISS was again  indicted for stealing 1 pair of ear-rings, value 5 s  .; 3 yards of satin, 5 s  .; and 2 1/2 yards of silk, 5 s  .; the goods of Thomas Whitewood , her master. MARTHA WHITEWOOD. I missed a pair of ear-rings, and two pieces of satin—I knew nothing about these things when I said I would forgive the prisoner if she replaced the note—she said she said had taken the ear-ring, and I should find them at Foy's, at Deptford, with a few more things—I went there and found them—I did not miss the satin until she acknowledged taking it. GEORGE GOODE. I produce a pair of ear-ring which I got at Mr. Foy's in Collier-street, Deptford.
   MRS. WHITEWOOD re-examined.  These are my ear-rings—they were lost from an ornament on the fire-place in the parlour. GUILTY.  Aged 19.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutrix  . Judgment respited.
