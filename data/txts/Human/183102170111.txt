 615.  SARAH SHAW was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of January , 2 pairs of boots, value 3s., the goods of James Davey.
   ELIZA DAVEY. I am the wife of James Davey- he keeps a shoemaker's-shop. On the 7th of January the prisoner lodged in the house, and slept in a private room which went into the shop; she left us without notice, and we missed two pairs of boots.
   JOHN MARLOW. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Golden-lane. I have two pairs of children's boots, pawned by the prisoner, on the 7th of January.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 53. - Confined Three Months.
 616.  THOMAS WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of January , 28 quires of paper, value 1l. 5s., the goods of Charles Roworth and others.
   CHARLES ROWORTH. I am a letter-press printer, and am in partnership with my two sons - we live near Bellyard. The prisoner is a total stranger to me. On the 20th of January I heard a noise - I came down, and found the prisoner in custody of one of our men, who had stopped him going out of our premises with some paper.
   EDWARD LYON. I am in the employ of Mr. Roworth. I was returning to his premises, and met the prisoner carrying this paper; I followed him about fifty yards, and asked what he had there - he said some paper, and that he was going home; he requested me to take it, but I refused- he came very quietly back with me, and acknowledged that he had taken it from the middle of the warehouse.
 MR. ROWORTH. This is our paper - the prisoner pointed out the place he took it from, and pleaded distress.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was cast away, and travelled up to london for employ, but could not get any.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Confined Three Months.
 617.  MARY WOODCOCK and MARY SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February , 26 yards of cotton, value 1l., the goods of James Brown.
   WILLIAM SHEATH. I live with Mr. James Brown, a linen-draper, of Shadwell. On the 10th of February the two prisoners came to his shop together, and looked at some printed cottons - I was standing by, and noticed that Smith held up several pieces rather higher than persons generally do, which concealed Woodcock very much; they still kept on together, and after some time I missed a piece of cotton from the counter, containing about sixteen yards - I looked for the Policeman, who came and found it up Woodcock's clothes, and another piece hanging to her tail; there was nothing found on Smith.
   WILLIAM ROBERT GARDE. I am a Police-constable. I went to the shop, and took the prisoners; I found this piece of cotton between Woodcock's legs, and as she was going out of the shop I saw about a yard of this other hanging behind her - I found no money on either of them.
 WILLIAM SHEATH. These are my employer's property- they had both bartered and looked at the goods; they pretended to buy a dress, and said they would leave 1s. on it.
 WOODCOCK -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 618.  ROBERT PASSINGHAM and JOHN RAYNER were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 3 handkerchiefs, value 15s., the goods of Daniel Gibbon.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST. I am a Police-constable. On Wednesday evening last I was in Coventry-street, St. James', and saw the two prisoners at Mr. Gibbon's shop window - he is a hosier and glover; I saw Rayner go into the shop, while the other stood at the window - he returned with something in his hand, which he gave to Passingham, who put it under his coat on the left side; Hobbs took Rayner, and I took Passingham - I found these three handkerchiefs under his coat.
 Cross-examined by MR. CARRINGTON. Q. About what time was this? A. Between seven and eight o'clock - it was dark, except from the gas-lights; there were no persons looking into the window at that time but the prisoners.
   THOMAS HOBBS. I am a Police-constable. I was with West, and saw the two prisoners looking in at the window- I saw Rayner go into the shop, take hold of the handkerchiefs, and give them several tugs, till he got them down- he brought them out, and gave them to Passingham, who took them backwards.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Are you quite certain as to the person of Passingham? A. Yes, I am; it was all done in less than five minutes - I was close to them- there was a waggon stopping close to the door, and I whipped round it and caught them.
   THOMAS CAFFIN. I am a shopman to Mr. Daniel Gibbon. These three handkerchiefs are his property, and were hanging at his door - I saw them safe in the afternoon, and missed them in the evening.
 Cross-examined by MR. CARRINGTON. Q. Have you any mark on them? A. No; I think we had the pieces of these handkerchiefs for three months - we sold five of them.
 The prisoners received a good character.
 PASSINGHAM - GUILTY. Aged 15.
 RAYNER - GUILTY. Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. - Confined 3 Months.
