PHILIP MILLER. I am a baker, and live at Plumstead-common. On Saturday, the 15th of February, I was at Mr. Davis's shop, and saw the prisoner there with another man—I saw the prisoner take a smock-frock off a pile on the counter, fold it up, put it under the frock he had on, and go out with the other—I told Mr. Davis, and went with him after them to the Shakspeare public-house—MR. Davis fetched a policeman, and went in after them—I waited at the door—I did not lose sight of the prisoner all the way from Mr. Davis's to the Shakspeare public-house, which is about a hundred yards—I did not know what became of the other man—I did not see him at the public-house. EDWARD PALMER( police-constable R  231.) I went with Mr. Davis to the Shakspeare public-house, and saw the prisoner—I asked if he had any thing about him—he said, "No, that is my bundle that lies there"—I took it up, and these two smock-frocks were both in it together, one of which Mr. Davis pointed out as having been sold, and the other as being stolen.
   Prisoner.  I had the 2 s . 3 d . frock on the table, and the other was at the other end of the room. Witness.  They were both together on a settle in a comer—I did not see one on the table. JAMES THOMAS MELLISH re-examined.  These are the frocks—they belong to Mr. Davis—it was the 2 s . 3 d . one I sold to the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  The other man had one. Witness.  There was only one sold, and that was to the prisoner—I am not aware that I ever said it was to his companion—( looking at his deposition )—this is my signature—it was read over to me—I meant to state that I sold it to the prisoner.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 33.— Confined Six Weeks.
 979. ROBERT ELLIOTT was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of February , 1 spoon, value 2 s  ., the goods of Benjamin Smith , his master. JOHN DOWSON. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Skilton's-lane, Green wich. On Friday, the 28th of February, in the evening, the prisoner came into my shop with a silver tea-spoon—he laid it on the counter—I was busy at the time, having no one else in the shop—I suspected it was stolen—I took it up, looked at it, and was not able to make out the initials—the prisoner said, "It is old silver"—I said, "I suppose you want to sell it then?"—he said "Yes"—I asked his name—he said, "Smith, my mother sent me with the spoon, and the name of Smith is on it"—I put it on one side a little time, went to the door, and called an officer—I then told the prisoner I thought he had either found or stolen the spoon—he said he had found it on Deptford-bridge—I said the spoon appeared to have been used where there was a family—it seemed to have been bit—I asked him on which side the initials of "Smith" were—he said, "On the back"—it was "W. S." on it, which I believe are the initials of the person—I said it was Mr. Benjamin Smith's, the proprietor of the wharf—he said it was not, and I gave him in charge—he said afterwards his name was Robert Elliott. GEORGE HARRISS( police-constable R  158.) I was called into the shop—the prisoner was having some conversation with Mr. Dowson—I asked him his name, and he said, "Smith"—that his mother's name was Smith—I asked where he worked—he said, for Mr. Smith, at Deptford-bridge—I said, "How do you account for the spoon?"—he said he had found it on Deptford-bridge—I took him into custody, and received the spoon from Mr. Dowson.
