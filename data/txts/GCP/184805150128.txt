1374. JOHN MURPHY, stealing 9 bagatelle balls, value 98.; the goods
of William Ironside.
William Ironside. I keep the Three King's, Clerkenwell-close---I had
some bagatelle balls safe on 21st April - I missed them next day-these are
them.
Cross-examined by Mr. Briarly. Q. How do you know them? A.
Here is one with a plug out of it-I never saw one so before-I saw then
again on 29th-I never saw the prisoner in my house to my knowledge-1
cannot identify half that come.
WILLIAM HATSWELL. I keep a beer-shop-I bought these balls of the
prisoner on Saturday, 22nd April.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you know him? A. By seeing him two of
three times before-I bought them in a beer-shop kept by Nicholls, my
son-in-law, who bas been tried for assisting certain thieves.
Thomas GRUNDRY (policeman, 178 G.) I took the prisoner-he said he
knew nothing about the balls, and had not sold them to Mr. Hatswell.
Not Guilty.

1375. JOHN BARRY, stealing 2 half-crowns and 3 pence; the money
of George Jackson, and others, his masters.
ROBERT Giles, I am shopman to George Jackson and Sons-the prisoner
was their errand boy-on the morning of 27th April I watched and saw him
take a key out of his waistcoat-pocket, open the till and take money out three
times I told the cashier he went to the prisoner, who gave up half-a-crown
and put it on the counter-he was told that was not all-he then took three
pence from his right-hand pocket-he was told that was not all, and took
another half-crown from his stocking and gave that up-I said he had better
give up the key-he produced it-it fitted the till.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. How old is he? A. Thirteen in
June-he has been two years in our service-I believe he was as good a boy
as there was in the place I am afraid he has got into bad company.
The prisoner received a good character, and a witness engaged to emplos
him. GUILTY. Aged 12.-Confined Ten Days and Whipped.
