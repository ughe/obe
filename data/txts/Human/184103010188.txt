 Rev.  JOHN HORTON . I am Rector of St. George's, Southwark, I subscribed this register of marriage—the parties were married by me—I am not able to recognise either of them—I signed this book, but not the copy. WILLIAM STEELE. I am parish-clerk of St. George's, Southwark. I wrote this copy of the register, it is a true copy—I was present at the marriage, but am not able to identity the parties—a copy of the register is generally given at the time of the marriage, and that is signed by the minister—a person applied to me for this copy, (I think it was the prose cutor,) and I wrote it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  When did you give this copy to any person? A.  On the 11th of January, 1841—I do not know who—it was in the vestry—I think I delivered it to this gentleman, (Mr. Clothier, senior,) I know he is the prosecutor, but I cannot undertake to swear he was the person—I was at Union-hall police-office about a fortnight after I delivered the paper—I did not remember at that time to whom I delivered it—I think there was only one person when I delivered it. FRANCES BELL. This is my handwriting as a witness to this marriage—I have no recollection of the parties. EDWARD COLLIER. I am a printer, and live in Fenchurch-street, City. I know William Hugging, he is in Court—this "William Huggins" in this register, I presume to be his—I have seen him write frequently.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You merely presume it? A.  I do not presume, I used a wrong expression—I am satisfied it is his—perhaps for three years I saw him write every day, in passing through the shop, from 1825 to 1827 or 1828. ROBERT SAVAGE. I live in Johnson-street, St. George's-in-the-East, and am a tailor. I know the prisoner—she came to live next door but one to me, just about Christmas, 1836, with a person named Johnson, that she represented as her husband—he died there, three or four months after they came there, and was buried, I suppose—there was a funeral pro cession, and every thing of that kind—I know Huggins, he lodged in the same house previous to Johnson's death—I have seen him there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know any thing more? A.  Mr. Huggins continued to live there with her till they went away, some time in August—I never heard of their being man and wife at that time—I do not in sinuate any thing of that. SAMUEL CANHAM. I live in Oxford-street, Mile-end, and am an un dertaker.
