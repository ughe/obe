John Johnson. I am shopman to Mr. Thompson of Grosvenor-tow.
Pimlico. On Friday, the 2nd of December, between five and six o'clock
the prisoner came to our shop with his wife-I knew him before as 1
constant customer at the house-he bought a silver watch, a chain, two
seals, a key, and a ring; a hat, a jacket, two shifts, and five rings--the
whole amounted to 81. 16s. 6d.-he also redeemed articles to the amount
of 1/ 48. 9d., which were in pledge, making in all 101. odd-his wife gave
him a note for £100, and he produced it to me--I asked him how he came
by it-he said that it had been forwarded to him by a solicitor from
Ireland, that a rich uncle of his had died, and left him thirty or forty
houses, and some acres of land, and he was going the same evening to the
Cove of Cork in Ireland --I handed the note to Mr. Smellie the foreman,
and be gave me change out of it--this is the note (Looking at it.)
Cross-examined. Q. Can you tell whether this name, "James Skerritt,"
was on the note when you took it? A. No, I cannot -- the prisoner's
name is on the note--that was not done in my presence-it was not on it
when handed to me--it was indorsed afterwards--I know the address the
prisoner gave--I was not certain whether he lived there it is the address
he always gave-he has frequently pawned things and redeemed them at
our shop-1 was perfectly acquainted with him.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. You knew him by the name of Coveney very well!
A. Yes--if he had given any other name, I should certainly have known
it to be false.
