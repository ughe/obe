   MR. METCALFE . Q.  Are there often forced sales? A.  Yes. BOLE HARMS( City policeman , 288). On Tuesday evening, 2d March, about 8 o'clock, I was on duty in the Old Bailey, and saw a pony and cart down Fleet Lane—there were two persons with it, one of whom was the prisoner, I have no reason to doubt—I stood for a minute or two, and the prisoner passed me near the gaslight; and four or five minutes afterwards Howes sent for me—I told them I should detain the canvas, and let it remain in his shop till they sent the money—I detained the prisoner, and was with Foulger when he was taken—I have kept the canvas ever since the 17th.
   COURT to JAMES HOWES . Q.  How came the canvas to be left with you on the 2nd? A.  Because I agreed to buy it—I was to pay for it when they brought it in, but when it was brought to me in rolls, I refused to take it—Iexpected it was remnants—the prisoner went away with the pony and cart, leaving the canvas at my house—I had only told him that he was to come back for the money; he did not do so—I never saw him again till he was in custody.
   MR. METCALFE . Q.  Did you tell him when you would pay him? A.  Yes, as soon as they brought it; but as it was different to what they represented, they left it, and I went to the police. GUILTY. †— Confined Two Years .
