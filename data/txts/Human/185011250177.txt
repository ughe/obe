 161. WILLIAM RHODES , stealing 30 sovereigns, 20 half-sovereigns, and 1 10 l  . bank-note; the property of Robert Calton . ( See June Session. )
   MR. COCKLE conducted the Prosecution. ROBERT CATTON. I am a linen-draper's assistant—in April I lodged in Sutton-street, Soho. On 10th April I saw the prisoner about eleven o'clock in the morning, in the parlour of the Canterbury Arms, in Lam beth—he came in with Anderson—they both spoke to me—there was a game of skittles to be played between me and one of the name of Jones—the prisoner said he would take the chalk, and see that the game was played fair—I played one game for 3 l ., and then Jones made me a bet of 50 l . that I could not knock the skittles down in nine throws—I said I would bet him 50 l .—I had not the 50 l . with me, and I went to 3, Sutton-street, Soho, where I lived, and asked George Williams, my landlord, to lend me 50 l .—I got thirty sovereigns, twenty half-sovereigns, and a 10 l .-bank-note—I returned to the Canterbury Arms with James Chick, who I had met in St. George's-road—he went with me to borrow the money, and returned with me to the Canterbury Arms—when I got back, it might be twelve or half-past twelve o'clock—I found the prisoner, Jones and Anderson, and one of the name of Strong in the parlour—they sent for some sausages, and two or three bottles of wine—we then went in the skittle-ground—I put down 40 l ., and one of the party said, "You had better play for a bottle of wine before playing for the 50 l .
