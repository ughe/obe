CHARLES BROCK was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the counting-house of William Kitson and another, on the   19th of January , at   St. George the Martyr, Southwark , and stealing therein 1 cash-box, value 30 s  .; 1 pocket-book, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; I piece of parch ment, value 1 s  .; 12 sovereigns, 16 half-crowns, 80 shillings, 46 sixpences, 60 groats, 60 pence, 100 halfpence, and 15 l  . bank-note; their property.
   MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM KITSON. I am in partnership with Mr. Robinson, as soap boilers, in White-street, Borough—the prisoner was in our employ for rather better than a fortnight. On Wednesday the 19th of January, my cash-box contained twelve sovereigns, a 5 l . Bank of England note, 8 l . in silver, two 5 s . papers of coppers, and the lease of our premises—one packet of copper con sisted of pence, and the other of halfpence, and the silver consisted principally, of shillings—I locked the cash-box up in my private desk in the counting-house, about half-past three o'clock, when I left—the prisoner was then at work behind the counting-house, only separated from me by a slight par tition and glazed work, so that be could see into the counting-house, and I could see over the premises—he was melting fat at a copper exactly be hind the partition, within about eighteen feet of it—he could see every thing that was going on in the counting-house—I went Co the premises about twelve the next morning, found my partner there, and received in formation of the robbery—I called all the men together—they were all on the spot except the prisoner, who I did not see again till he was in custody at Union Hall, on the Friday following. RICHARD ROBINSON. I am partner with Mr. Kitson. I remember his leaving the premises on the Wednesday afternoon—I locked up the count ing-house myself about half-past four o'clock—I locked the door quite safe—there is a window to the counting-house, which opens on hinges like a door—I will not say whether that was fastened, but it was closed, and it is generally fastened when it is closed—I then left the premises—the pri soner was then employed behind the counting-house—I could not see any one employed there besides him at that time—I went to the premises again between ten and eleven next morning—I found the counting-house door open—the lock had been forced back, the small window was also open, and Mr. Kitson's desk forced open, and the cash-box taken out—I had all the men brought down, and pointed it out to them—the prisoner was among them—I told them to take notice of it, and that I should make further inquiries when Mr. Kitson came, and that I expected him in a short time—the prisoner, when he saw it, held up his bands as if to express his surprise at the robbery—I sent them all to their work, and in about three quarters of an hour or an hour Mr. Kitson came—I told him the circumstance, and had the men called down again, but the prisoner could not be found—he had left the premises, and did not return—there were marks on the count ing-house window, as if made by greasy fingers, and also marks outside and inside the door, as if some instrument had been applied to force it—it
