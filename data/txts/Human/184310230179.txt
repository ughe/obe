MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. BENJAMIN CARR. I am a horse-hair manufacturer, and live in Banner-street, St. Luke's. Fletcher and George Meadows worked in my factory—I rent a place in Golden-lane, where the hair was taken from—on the 16th of Oct. I received a letter—in consequence of that I conferred with the police—the next day I saw a bag containing some hair—it had been taken from Golden-lane—Richard Meadows had a right on the premises at Golden-lane—Fletcher had no business there whatever—I had ordered my foreman on Tuesday morning to let George take this hair to Golden-lane, to have it carded by a steam-press I have there, and to bring it back—he had no right to dispose of it.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  How long has Fletcher been with you? A.  About eleven years—he bore an excellent character—four or fire months back he asked me to let him have a bale of hair for his son to work at home—I let him have it—he has bought hair of me five or six times—I have two places of business—I rent a steam-press in Golden-lane—the fore man was stationed in Banner-street.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Richard was in your employ? A.  He had been for three or four years, but was not at this time—I should have had no objection to his calling on his brother there. JAMES BRANNAN( police sergeant G  20.) In consequence of directions from the prosecutor, I went on Tuesday, the 14th of Oct., to a court leading into Golden-lane, where I could watch the entrance leading into the factory—I saw Fletcher standing at the corner of Arthur-street-leading to the prosecutor's factory—I pointed him out to Cole—he had no appearance of bulk about him—he went towards the factory—in three-quarters of an hour he came from the entrance of the court—there was no thoroughfare, so that he must have come from the factory—he had a bag nearly full—he passed me with it in Play-house-yard—I watched outside a public-house—he went in, and Redman followed George and Fletcher into the house—I had seen George three or four times before Fletcher went into the house—(I had first seen George com ing from the prosecutor's residence in Banner-street, about 300 yards off—he went to the factory)—they staid in the public-house three or four minutes—Redman came out before them—George came towards me, and went towards the City, and I followed Fletcher with Redman—he turned round in Chis well-street, saw me, and went into a public-house—I followed, told him I belonged to the police, and wanted to know where he got this hair—he said, "I bought it in the Curtain-road"—I said, "How came you this way from the Curtain-road?"—he said, "Oh, this I bought in Smithfield"—I said, "Which way did you come?"—he said, "Through Long-lane and Barbican"—I asked if he had called anywhere—he at first said no—I asked if he did not call on some one in Golden-lane—he said, "Yes, I did"—I found this hair in the bag.
