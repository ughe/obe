THOMAS WINCH( police-sergeant N  40.) I produce an examined copy of the register of marriage between William Holmes and Maria Budd in Oct., 1829—I got it from the first wife—I went to the parish church at Teddington, and examined it with the register, in the clerk's room at his private residence, where the books are kept—they are not kept at the church—when I went about the certificate of the second marriage the book was fetched from the church to the clerk's house for me to examine it by—I had never seen the clerk before—I went to the church; the people opposite told me I should find the clerk at the house, and he showed me the book—there are several blanks in this certificate—they were so in the register. JANE HEDGES re-examined.  I knew the clergyman at the time—I know he was the officiating minister—he attended the church in his usual dress—it was before twelve o'clock. WILLIAM GUNTON. I am a carpenter and joiner, and live at Kingston. I was occasionally employed as journeyman by the late William Turvey, who acted as parish clerk of Ham church—he was a master carpenter—I have known the prisoner eight or nine years—in 1840 I was at work at Turvey's house, and saw the prisoner and a woman named Harriet Talley, whom I knew before, go into Turvey's house; after remaining there some time they came out, and went with Turvey towards Ham parish church, which is about fifty yards from the house—I do not know whether they went into the church—they afterwards came back to the clerk's house, and went over to the Jolly Gardeners beer-shop. JANE TALLEY. I live with my mother, Harriet Talley, in York-street, Rotherhithe. I have known the prisoner about twelve months—he and my mother lived as man and wife—they were so for aught I know—they used the same room. THOMAS WINCH re-examined.  I produce a paper purporting to be an examined copy of a marriage in Ham church—I got it from the clerk's house—he professed to be the clerk of Ham church—I do not know whether he was to—he showed me a book—I examined this copy with it—the clerk is since dead—he was one of the attesting witnesses to the marriage. ELIZABETH NYE. I am a pew-opener at Ham parish church. Mar riages are celebrated there, and have been so for many years—it was first a chapel, but is consecrated as a church—I have been pew-opener there nine years—I was present, in August, 1840, at a marriage between William Holmes and Harriet Talley—I cannot recognize the prisoner—there was some little dif ficulty in putting the ring on the bride's finger, but I did not see what it was—it was put on the left hand—the bride had on a dress of the clerk's daughter—the party went from the church to the clerk's house, and then to the Jolly Gardeners—I recollect hearing the banns put up three successive Sundays in the names of William Holmes and Harriet Talley—the Rev. James Hough is the clergyman who married them—the clerk is since dead—I did not know Talley before her marriage, and did not know her name, or whether she was single or a widow—she was described as a widow, and was asked in church by the name of Harriet Talley—I recollect that—the registers are usually kept in an iron chest in the vestry; but the clerk being very ill in bed, and not able to get up, and the clergyman being from home, they might be sent for to his house.
