HENRY RIDLRY. I am twelve years old, and live at 14, Windsor-ter-
rece. City-road. On 5th Oet.,, I was going on an errandi for my motheruf
and met the prisoner at the corner of Chiswell-street-he said. "My little
map, will you run with this to Whitbreads', I will give you 2d." I said,
"What, sir?" on getting over to the other side of the way with bim he
brought a letter out of his pocket--this is it ( produced)--he said, if they
asked me where it came from I was to say " From Mr. Hugh Hughes, of
Camden-town"-be gave me the letter-I was to come back to bim at the
same place took it to Whitbreads' counting-house--the clerk took it of
me, in a few minutes Mr. Martineux came and put some questions to me
I gave him an account of how I got it-they took me to the station, and to
the police-court-I was remanded to prison, and afterwards let out on bail on
Monday morning--this happened on Saturday at twelve o'clock, or a little
before-I had never seen the prisoner before I saw him again about a
month afterwards at Baggnige-wells police-court in one of the cells
knew him, and have no doubt he is the man
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY.. . Did the policeman take you there
to see him? A. No; one of the officers of the Court he told me the
man had been found, and I was to go and look at him.
