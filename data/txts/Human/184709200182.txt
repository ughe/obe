WILLIAM HEARNDEN. I am carpenter, and work at Mr. Brann's, at Lee, next door to Mr. Lock's. On Saturday afternoon, the 7th of Aug., between three and four o'clock, I saw the prisoners with another man, opposite my employer's—I ordered them out of the field—they came towards the church-yard—two of them went in and one stood outside—I did not see any more of them till the Tuesday following. FRANCIS HOSIER HART. I was passing the churchyard on the 7th of Aug., at half-past three o'clock—I saw Williams come from the direction of Mr. Lock's house, across the churchyard. THOMAS TAYLOR. I was locked up in the station-house, waiting for bail—the prisoners came in on the Tuesday—they said they were locked up for stealing a gold watch—Williams said Jones climbed up the spout and chucked  it out, and Williams sold it to George Gray, who kept a fence  in Mint-street Borough, that there was another one in it, but he was not there, he was gone into the country, that they went down on the Tuesday with the intention of screwing the drum , meaning, "to rob the house, "for one to get I and wait till the people were gone to bed, and then let the other in to rob the house.

WILLIAM JAMES STUBBINS was indicted for stealing 20 knives and forks, value 14 s  .; 1 roasting-jack, 12 s  .; 2 pairs of snuffers 2 s  .; 2 brushes, 2 s  .; 1 broom, 3 s  .; 1 strainer, 1 s  .; 1 pair of scissors, 3 s  . 8 skewers, 1 s  .; 1 cork-screw, 1 s  .; 2 sauce-ladles, 7 s  .; 4 spoons, 4 s  .; 6 knives, 4 s  .; 1 razor, 1 s  .; and 1 dressing-case, 4 s  .; the goods of John Wilson Newhall , his master. JOHN WILSON NEWHALL. I am ironmonger, and live in Powis-street, Woolwich. The prisoner was in my service since last March—I lost a variety of things—I went with an officer and the prisoner to his house—we found there his wife, and he acknowledged it was his house—we found there all this property produced—I will not say the whole of it is mine, I believe it is—three or four things I will positively swear to.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  What do you swear to? A.  I have known this dressing-case ten years—it happened not to be a saleble article—it is marked by myself, in my own writing, and by a man whose writing I can swear to—I can swear to these toilet-snuffers, and to this hair broom, which is marked in my private character by my apprentice, who is now in my shop—here is a brush with the private mark scratched out—I could almost say my private mark is here, but I will not—it is not possible for me to say I missed all these articles—it is merely because I saw them four articles that I swear to them—I can produce duplicates to some of then—most of them are marked—three person sell in my shop—we went to the prisoner's house—he took us into the room where these things were—we made him believe we wanted a pig-trough—he had no idea we wanted these things.
