WILLIAM BROWN( Detective ). I went, with other officers, to 34, Watson Street, Plaistow, on the evening of March 30th—when I knocked, the door was opened by the female prisoner—when she saw me she attempted to close it—I said I was a police officer, and must come in—she let three dogs out, which ran at our legs, and started barking, but we pushed her on one side, and went into the kitchen, and then upstairs—I heard some one going out at the back window, and afterwards saw the male prisoner brought in—the female prisoner began to cry, and I said, "Don't upset yourself"—afterwards I took part in finding these things.
   Cross-examined.  You did not ask us to come into the house. HENRY MARSHALL(738 K. ) I was in the back garden of No. 32—I did not hear anything said by the female prisoner, or any remark made to her. WILLIAM RYAN(819 K. ) On March 30th, about 10.45 p.m., I went to this house—in the kitchen I saw the female prisoner—she was trying to get by us through the door, but I stopped her—I told her she could not go out, and that she would most likely be charged with having pos session of a mould for the manufacture of base coin—she said, "Who has put us away?" WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These four moulds are for the manufacture of counterfeit florins—the other articles are all connected with coining, and part of a coiner's stock-in-trade. JOHN LING. I am an estate agent, and have the letting of 34, Watson Street—the house was let to the male prisoner in November, 1903—I have collected the rents since—they were regularly paid by Mrs. Phillips every time I called, but once.
