Second Session, 1875–76.
ANNIE DUKE. I am barmaid at the Winchester Music Hall, Suffolk
Street, Borough-1 was there on the 3rd December, when the prisoner and
Hird were there, I received two bad shillings from them in payment for
some beer and gin,
WILLIAM LANs (Policeman MR 26). I took the prisoner into custody
on the night of the 3rd December, about 50 yards from the Winchester
Music Hall-he said "I suppose I can square you for this, a couple of bob
will do for you "-I produce one bad shilling received from Miss Duke.
STUART RAMSEY (Policeman M 167). I took Hird into custody-I pro-
duce one of the bad shillings.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. The two shillings produced are both bad, and of
different moulds.
GUILTY.
The Prisoner also PLEADED GUILTY to a previous conviction for a like offence.
- Five Years' Penal Servitude.
91. HENRY HIRD (21), a like offence.
MR. CRAUPURD conducted the Prosecution.
ANNIE DUKE. I am barmaid at the Winchester Music Hall-I served
the two prisoners on the night in question-they tendered me two bad
shillings--they were afterwards taken into custody.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I suppose there were five or six of your
own class in the house - I did not know the money was bad at the time I
took it-I will swear to you two who passed the bad coin.
STUART RAMSEY (Policeman M 167). I took the prisoner into custody
- I found upon him seven sixpences and 38. 6d. in bronze-he said, "You
will find no bad money on mo"-I produce the bad 16. Miss Duke gave me.
WILLIAM LANE (Policeman MŘ 26). I took Taylor into custody-I
found upon him two sixpences and 91d. in bronze.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. Both shillings produced are bad and of different
moulds.
Prisoner's Defence. "I had been out selling herrings and[baddocks, and
that is how I came by my money. I never had a bad shilling about me.
I get my living by costermongering, and that is how I had so many half-
pennies.
