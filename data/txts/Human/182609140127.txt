 1608.  JOHN MACK was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of August , 1 hat, value 7s.; the goods of James Melville , from his person.
 JAMES MELVILLE. I am a  carpenter. On the 13th of August I went to the Crooked Billet public-house, Wych-street, St. Clement's, about half-past six o'clock in the morning, and remained there with a friend; we had a little porter. I got rather drowsy, and set my hat on the table, and went to sleep; when I awoke it was gone; I saw it again on the head of another person, on the 21st of August.
   THOMAS JONES HICKS. I am a porter. I called in at this house on the 13th of August; I put my hat on the table; the prisoner and another man came in; the prisoner wanted to drink with me, and we had another pot of porter; I had been very ill a long time; I put my head on the table, and soon afterwards I felt the prisoner's hand in my pocket; I told the landlord of it, and asked if he would suffer strangers to be robbed in his house; my hat was then gone; and I told the prisoner I would not go out without it; he then sent his companion into the parlour, and took Melville's hat, and put it on my head, and pushed it down; I do not know where they got the hat, but they turned me out altogether, and said, what did I want more - I went home.
   OWEN MURRAY. About half-past one o'clock, on Sunday, the 13th of August, the witness came into my house, and sat down on a seat facing the bar; the prisoner and some other person came in, and sat down in the box where he was; the prisoner sat on one side, and the friend on the other; they asked him to treat them, and they drank two pots of beer - Hicks then laid his hat on the table, and seemed to be dozing; he awoke in about five minutes, and said to me, "Landlord, I have lost my hat" - I came outside, and said, "If you have played any tricks with the man's hat, give it him, and don't have a piece of work on a Sunday about it" - the prisoner and his companion then went into the parlour, and brought out Melville's hat, and put it on Hicks's head; Hicks took it off, and said he would not have that; I thought Melville was then gone; I went into the bar, and was busy serving, when Hicks cried out, "Landlord, here is conduct; he has got his hand in my pocket" - I took the prisoner's hand out of Hicks's pocket, and said, "I will not let you be in the house any longer;" the prisoner and his companion used bad language, and I walked them out.
   SAMUEL GILES. I am the officer. I took the prisoner on Monday night, and afterwards found this hat on Hicks's head.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1609.  HENRY JONES and JOHN BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 1 book, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Michael Gandy , from the person of Christopher Gandy.
 CHRISTOPHER GANDY. On the 19th of August, about one o'clock, I was in  St. James's Park, near the Horse-Guards; I had a spelling-book in my coat-pocket; I had just left school; the officers told me I had lost my book, and they would take me to the boys who stole it; I had not seen them near me.
   THOMAS ROBERTS. I was with Bertraun, and saw the two prisoners behind the prosecutor; I saw Jones take the book from his pocket; I went up, and took it from under Brown's coat, and gave it to Bertraun; Groom and Bertraun fetched the prosecutor, who owned the book; I had watched them for a quarter of an hour.
   JOHN GROOM. I am a street-keeper. I saw the prisoners close behind Gandy; I saw Jones take something from his pocket, and pass it to Brown - I went and took them; Bertraun brought Gandy, who owned the book.
   ANGELIOUS BERTRAUN. I was with the two officers. I saw the prisoners behind Gandy - I suspected they had taken something, and asked him; he said he had lost a book; I brought him back, and saw the book taken from Brown.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 BROWN'S Defence. My fellow prisoner is quite innocent - I did it myself.
 The prisoners received good characters.
 JONES - GUILTY. Aged 15.
 BROWN - GUILTY. Aged 19.
 Recommended to Mercy - Whipped and Discharged.
 1610.  WILLIAM JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 1 watch, value 2l.; 1 watch-chain, value 14s.; 2 seals, value 25s.; 1 watch-key, value 7s., and 1 ring, value 5s., the goods of Joseph Southwell , from his person.
 JOSEPH SOUTHWELL. I am a  journeyman tailor, and lodge in Compton-street. I was in Leicester-square on the 10th of September, about one o'clock in the morning; I had passed the evening at a public-house in Carnaby-market; two persons came up to me, and I thought they were the same trade as myself - one of them spoke, but I cannot tell what he said; I was the worse for liquor - we walked and talked together, and all at once one of them ran away; I put my hand to my pocket, and my watch was gone. I had it safe just before.
   JOHN EVANS. I am a watchman. I was on duty in St. James'-street, Haymarket - I heard the cry of Stop thief! and saw four or five persons pursuing the prisoner down Whitcomb-street; I took him round the street, and met the prosecutor, who appeared to have been drinking; he said he had lost his watch.
