   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Were you drunk, or sober? A.  I had been drinking a little, but was not drunk—I was not sober—I saw the prisoner on the Saturday following—he was not in custody then—I went with him to several places to find Colonel Johnson—I went with him through Highgate on Saturday—I told him I must go back to the bank, and could not go on—I went with him on the Sunday to Colney-hatch—I did not find a Colonel Johnson there.
   MR. WILKINS . Q.  Why did you go to Colney-hatch? A.  He told me he had received this 20 l . note of a Colonel Johnson, at Colney-hatch—he knew the road till we got near there, and then he had forgotten it—I knew what was going forwards on the night I lost my notes—they were safe in my right-hand trowsers pocket when I got into the cab. JOSEPH STEPPING. I live in Fetter-lane, and am a tailor. I was with the prosecutor at the Ben Jonson in Great Wild-street—I fetched a cab  for him about a quarter before eleven o'clock—before he left he took his purse out, and had two 20 l . notes, which I had seen before, and a bill of exchange, or something of that kind—he put the notes back into his purse, and the sove reigns at the top of them—he put his purse into his pocket, and buttoned it up—I told the cab-driver to drive him to No. 16, Passmore-street, Pimlico. HENRY SHRAPNELL WHITHERS. I am chief cashier in the London and Westminster Bank, in Lothbury. On the 1st of May this cheque was presented—I paid it in two 20 l . notes, and 10 l . in gold—this is one of the notes—( looking at it )—I took the numbers and dates at the time—this note had been issued from the Bank of England that morning, and I paid it about half an hour afterwards to the prosecutor. WILLIAM HIGMAN. I am a clerk in the Bank of England. 100 20 l . notes were issued on the morning of the 1st of May, to the London and Westmin ster Bank—this is one of them. GEORGE THOMAS HUGGETT.
