 157. HENRY JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of Nov ., 4 handkerchiefs, value 10 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 pair of shoes, 85.; the goods of Joseph Johnson . JOSEPH JOHNSON. I live in West-street, Stratford. The prisoner is my brother, and he occasionally lived in the same house with me. On Thursday evening, the 21st of Nov., I came home, and went up stairs to put away a little money in a box—I found my box had been broken open, and missed from it three handkerchiefs—I came down, and missed a pair of shoes and another handkerchief—I went to the station, and gave infor mation, and the prisoner was taken by a policeman—the policeman took a handkerchief from the prisoner at the station, in my presence—it was one of those which I had missed from below stairs—it was a cotton one, and I have the fellow one to it on my neck now—it is my mother's misfortune to be blind, and she is lying on her bed—any one can get into the house without being seen.
   Prisoner . There is my sister-in-law there, who is not out of the house five minutes in a day.
   Witness . She lives there, but she was in a room up stairs, and has her husband to attend to. SAMUEL BENTON( police-constable K  49.) I took the prisoner, and told him what he was charged with—he said he had not taken anything—he had a handkerchief round his neck—I took it off—he said it was his, bnt it was claimed by the prosecutor. JOSEPH JOHNSON re-examined . This is my handkerchief—it is hemmed with white cotton, the same as the one I have round my neck—I bought the two and my little niece hemmed them for me—there is no name or mark to it—I do not know that my niece ever did anything for the prisoner—he never had anything of his own—he has done no work for four years—he has got a job now as a bricklayer's labourer, and will not do it—he sent another person to do it.
