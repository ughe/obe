WILLIAM JOHN BARTON. I am the husband of Henrietta Barton—I saw a woman in the shop on December 22nd—my wife showed me a coin and a mark on the slate—I saw the woman go out of the shop and I followed her—I saw a police constable named Armsby, whom I did not know before—I made a communication to him, and pointed out the two prisoners to him.
   Cross-examined.  Until the coin was scratched on the slate there was nothing to attract my attention to it; it had a good ring on the marble counter. ALFRED ARMSBY(746 K. ) About 3 p.m. on December 22nd I was in plain clothes in the part of Romford Road which runs through Forest Gate, when William Barton said something to me and pointed out a man and a woman to me, whom I followed along the Romford Road towards Strat ford—I saw them meet another man and the three of them then entered the Pigeons public-house and remained there ten minutes—Detective Pitchley overtook me shortly after they had been pointed out to me by Barton—on coming out of the Pigeons they walked to Forest Gate and went along Woodgrange Road and turned into Osborn Road—they remained then for a minute or so and then the third man left—I did not see anything happen before he left—the two prisoners then went into the Fox and Hounds in the Broadway, Forest Gate—they then came out—the male prisoner kept looking round—when they got into Strode Road we arrested them—I said to the female prisoner, "I am a police officer and suspect you of having counterfeit coin in your possession "—she said, "I have not any "—I said, "I shall take you to the station," and I took her there—she said nothing on the way—directly I spoke to her she pulled 10 1/2 d. in bronze out of her right-hand jacket pocket and said, "That is all I have—on being charged with uttering she made no reply—she produced no money in my presence at the police station—I saw her taken into a room by the female searcher, who I saw come out of the room and hand something to Detective-Sergeant Mercer, but I did not see what.
   Cross-examined.  There was no bad money, to my knowledge, passed by them at the Fox and Hounds.
