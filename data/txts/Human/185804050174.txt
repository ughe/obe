SARAH ANN SLADE. About the month of May, 1856, I was living with Mrs. Cox—I was present when the prisoner left a bundle; the bundle was tied round in a sack and a handkerchief—I looked, and saw a blue dress, I can only speak to the dress by the colour—this is the dress my mistress wore continually—I afterwards saw my mistress give some money to Charlotte Parker.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did the prisoner leave the bundle with you or your mistress? A.  With me—he merely told me to take care of it; he did not ask me to put it anywhere—I could see the dress without untying the bundle—Icould see there was a blue dress, and that made me look. CHARLOTTE PARKER. I am a single woman, living at No. 2, King Street, Deptford. I remember, about the time in question, seeing Mrs. Cox at the door of her house; she showed me a dress—that is it—I knew the prisoner before this—in consequence of what Mrs. Cox said, I went to him; I asked him whether he wanted to sell the drees—he said, "No," he was going to make it a present to some one; he then said if I wanted it, I might have it for 11 s .—the bargain was ultimately struck for that sum—the dress was for Mrs. Cox—she afterwards gave me the money, and I took it to the prisoner, and paid him for it. ALFRED JOHN CROUCH( Policeman, R  92). On 17th March I took the prisoner into custody—I said to him, "I have seen Mrs. Cox, and she says that you sold her a dress for 11 s . about twenty months ago, and that dress was stolen from Mrs. Knight's, in Church Street—he said, "I know nothing about it"—onhis way to the station, he turned round, laughingly, and said, "You know that was not the only thing stolen at that time"—I then cautioned him that he need not say anything, but anything he did say I should repeat.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you quite sure he said "stolen," and not "lost?" A.  No, "stolen"—he said stolen " about  that time," not " at  that time;"that is the only difference. NOT GUILTY.
