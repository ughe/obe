ELIZABETH GARDNER. I am the wife of William Gardner, who keeps the
Queen's Head at Barking-on 11th August the prisoner came about twelve
at night for & pot of ale, and gave me a bad shilling in payment-I said,
" This is a bad one;" he said, “I am very sorry," and put his hand in his
waistcoat pocket and gave me another; I said “This is a bad one also "-I
asked him why be treated me like that, and he said he did not know that
they were bada policeman then came up and said to him “You know it
was bad, because you have been trying at it at Mrs. Milton's, down the
street_I said to the policeman, "You had better take him "-I had laid
the 28. that the prisoner gave me on the counter, and be had taken them in
his hand—the policeman took them away from bim on the step; I after-
wards saw him mark them I had seen the prisoner in the evening before
this; he came for a quartern of rum, about half-past 10 or 11; he gave me
ls in payment; I put it in my pocket-I did not notice it at that time-

had got a bad 18. and found one-I marked it, bent it, and gave it to police
constable Godwin—I had several shillings and half-orowns in my pocket
with the bad 18.-I cannot say he gave me that.
