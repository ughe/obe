 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. How long has he been in your employ? A. Five months - I have a lad named Champness, who has been my cashier some time - we have occasionally found errors into which he has fallen - we have discovered mistakes among the shopmen, which have been shortly afterwards rectified; the lady came afterwards, and paid 3s., which is regularly passed to account - he gave a ticket of the last payment - there had been a quarrel between the prisoner and Champness; the prisoner struck him.
   ANN HARLOW. I went to the shop on the 12th of August, and bought goods amounting to 14s. 1 1/2d.; I paid the prisoner 11s. 1 1/2d., and said I had not sufficient with me, but I would bring the other 3s.; when I went in, I wished to be served by the prisoner - some other persons came in while I was there - this is the bill the prisoner gave me; it is 14s. 1 1/2d.; it has no receipt to it.
 Cross-examined. Q. You did not desire that the part not paid should be marked on the bill? A. No; when I went back, I went to the prisoner, and paid him - two ladies came in to look at some crape shawls, after I was served - I do not recollect whether he attended to them before he made my bill out - he took the 3s. to the cashier the moment I gave it him, but the first money he did not take for several minutes; he was looking for a sheet of paper to put the goods in.
   THOMAS BROWN CHAMPNESS. I am cashier to the prosecutor. On the 12th of August the prisoner brought me this cheque of 7s. 1 1/2d., which he paid me.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been in the service of the prosecutor? A. Fourteen or fifteen months- I am apprentice there - Mr. Hall has complained of errors in my cash accounts; I do not recollect his saying he would turn me away - errors have been made by me, or the shopmen; an explanation has been given afterwards - I quarrelled with the prisoner, and he struck me; but I was not set to watch him particularly - there were a great many people in the shop - I saw two other ladies come in, but I think that was after the ticket came to me; I received the 3s.; I did not see Mr. Phillips present - I did not hand the money over to Mr. Phillips; I believe Mr. Phillips and all the rest were present when the prisoner was charged with this.
 COURT. Q. Has there been any charge against your honesty? A. No, there have been mistakes.
 MR. BARRY to MR. HALL. Q. I believe you had a good character with the prisoner? A. Yes, for three years - I had engaged him as a foreman, and had the greatest confidence in him till within a few days.
 COURT. Q. When did you tell him of this? A. About two hours after it occurred - he denied it altogether, till the lady was brought over, and then he admitted it - the cashier slipped out after the lady, and asked what she had paid; when the lady was brought back, the prisoner admitted it, but did not say how it happened.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do not you know he had served other ladies before the charge was made? A. Yes, he served thirty-four customers that day - it was about nine o'clock at night when I spoke to him.
 JURY. Q. Was the lady sent for? A. Yes; I had been over to inquire the particulars, and then brought her to the shop - he said, "I admit the lady paid me 1ls. 1 1/2d." he did not say he meant to pay the 4s. at some other time, nor would any young man be allowed to do so - that is my peremptory order.
 COURT. Q. Suppose he had wanted 4s. for any purpose? A. He should have taken the whole money to the cashier - it does not appear by his book that the other ladies were served - the sums just above and below this are 1s. 10d. and 1s. 4d., and then here is a cheque for 5l. 12s.; the 7s. 1 1/2d. is not in the right place in his book; it is added afterwards.
 MR. BARRY to  THOMAS BROWN CHAMPNESS. Q. Was the cheque given with the money at the time? A. I believe it was a minute or two after he paid the money.
 Prisoner. Q. Has not Mr. Hall entered the amount of your deficiencies to your father both before and after I was in the house? A. Yes - I believe you did say there were 3s. more to pay; I do not recollect that you were attending to any ladies at the time - you have at other times given your money without a cheque, when engaged with a second person - on the Monday night, I entered 1s. 9d. to your name which did not belong to it.
 Prisoner. Q. Have you not left your desk totally unattended to for a quarter of an hour? A. No; I may for ten minutes - I do not think I was down stairs for twenty-five minutes while Mr. Hall was at Margate.
 Q. If a young man has given you a cheque, and it has been wrong, have you not asked how much he gave you last time? A. Yes; you have set me right - you were engaged with two ladies when you brought the 3s., but not when you brought the 7s. 1 1/2d., to my knowledge.
 Prisoner to MRS. HARLOW. Q. I believe at the time I was serving you, these ladies were sitting by you? A. Yes; you served them, and while you served them, tied up my parcel.
 JURY. Q. When you were brought back, what took place? A. The prisoner denied having received more than 10s. 1 1/2d. - I said, "Either you or I must have wronged Mr. Hall;" he paused for a while - I again said,"Either you or I must be guilty; I will take my oath I paid you 14s. 1 1/2d." - he said, "Well, then, I admit I have kept it;" Mr. Halls said, "Goulbourne denies having taken more than 10s. 1 1/2d. of you;" I saw him take the first money to the desk, and heard him say "The lady will bring you 3s."
 MR. BARRY. Q. Did not you say he was hunting for some paper? A. Yes; and the crape shawls for the ladies to look at were then on the counter, but I think the bill was made out before they were on the counter; I think the ladies applied to another young man first, and the prisoner said "If they will wait, I will attend to them;" the money laid on the counter at the time - he should have taken the bill up to the desk, but he did not; he gave it to me.
 The prisoner, in a long address to the Court, positively denied all intention of keeping the money; stating that it arose from a mistake on his part, and that the prosecution was the result of vindictive motives on the part of Champness.
