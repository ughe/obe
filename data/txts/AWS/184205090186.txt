Letter to Mr. Marklew.-" Wednesday morning. Sir,-You will no
doubt feel much surprised at having a note from me, but I write to you
with the hope that however culpable I may appear, yet on hearing some
circumstances from me you will find I am not guilty. I have arrived in
London with the sole purpose of letting justice prove the truth and
though I would fain the matter might be settled if possible in a different
way yet should you not be enabled to listen to what I can say in palliation
of what has occurred, I am determined to abide by the laws of the country.
Allow me to say, Mr. Marklew, that you are indeed very much mistaken
in supposing I am the villain stated in the public papers. I can prove, I
trust, to your satisfaction, if you would hear me coolly and dispassionately,
that no intention ever existed in my mind of injuring any one party.

state that you are very much deceived in judging me as you do. 1 enclose
this in a similar note to Mr. Williams, begging him to be kind enough to
present it to you, and all I entreat of you is, to beg you will hear me.
W. SNow.'
