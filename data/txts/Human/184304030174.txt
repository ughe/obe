1351. MARGARET CONNOR, and JAMES CONNOR, were indicted for feloniously receiving, on the   17th of March , 1 watch, value 2 l  .; 1 watch-guard, 15 s  .; 1 locket, 1 s  .; one pair of boots, 6 d  .; and 1 cap, 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Spong ; knowing them to have been stolen. ANN SPONG. I am the wife of Thomas Spong, and live in Buckle's Cot tages, Upper Clapton. I found Elizabeth Loader, took her into the house, and befriended her—she suddenly went away, on the 17th of March, while I was gone out—I missed a watch and chain, and locket—she would have an opportunity of taking them—I never saw either of the prisoners before—Loader never came back.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How do you know the watch? A.  I have had it ten years—here is "Robert Stroud" on the back of it, and one of the hands is turned up at the end—I have the key I wind it up with. WILLIAM HOWARD. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned on the 18th of March, by Margaret Connor—she first asked 10 s ., and afterwards 15 s .—I lent her 15 s .—she said it was her husband's—I asked what he gave for it—she said he bought it before marriage, and she could not say—she was sorry she was obliged at last to part with it, in consequence of the illness of her husband—this guard-chain was pawned by James Connor, on the 20th of March—my brother asked him if it was silver—he said he bought it for silver.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you known Margaret? A.  Nearly six months—the prisoners are husband and wife—they bear a very good cha racter—James is very ill, and Margaret gets her living by keeping a coffee-stall. JOSEPH NIFTON( police-constable D  118.) On Monday, the 20th of March, I went to No. 20, Carlisle-street, where the prisoners lived—I saw Margaret first—I said I came about a watch she had pledged—she said the had pledged it; she found it in the Edgeware-road—she produced this cap, and said she found this watch-chain and locket in the cap on the morning of the 18th, close against her coffee-stall—I asked if she knew a girl named Elizabeth Larey, or Loader—she hesitated a few moments, and at last said, "No"—James then said, "We don't know anybody"—Margaret produced the dupli cate of the property—I told her she had done very wrong in pledging them, if she had found them—I took her into custody—James being very ill, I did not take him—after the examination the next morning I went back to the pri soners' lodgings with my brother officer, and found this cap, bonnet, boots, and shawl, which she said Loader wore when she absconded.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it after James said, "We don't know any one," that Margaret said she did not know? A.  No, before—she made the reply first.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
 (The prisoners received a good character.)
   MARGARET CONNOR ,— GUILTY. Aged 47.— Confined Three Months .
   JAMES CONNOR ,— NOT GUILTY.
