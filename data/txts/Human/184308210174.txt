HENRY CHARLES BARKER( police-serjeant 11 H. ) I went to the prisoner's house with Byerley—I said to the prisoner, "You have some ironmongery you have offered for sale to this man"—he said, "I have nothing here but what is my own"—Byerley pointed out these things—I found these in the bottom drawer, and four sash-weights in the bedroom—while I was searching the drawers I heard a scuffle and saw the prisoner halfway out of the street-door—the other officer had hold of him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know Byerley? A.  I have known him nine years—I took him to Worship-street—he came to me about eleven at night, and I told him to go and look at the things—he shewed me a piece of paper with the prisoner's address—I did not go with him to the prisoner till the Friday night. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined Six Months .

GEORGE FRY and MARIA LANGLEY were indicted for steal ing, on the   15th of February , 1 table cloth, value 1 s  . 3 d  .; 1 flannel shirt, 1 s  .; 1 candlestick, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 flat iron, 4 d  .; and 1 blanket, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Jeremiah Lyons : and that Langley had been before convicted. MARY LYONS. I am the wife of Jeremiah Lyons, and live in Long-lane, Ratcliffe. The prisoners both lodged at my house—when they went away I missed a blanket, a table-cloth, a flannel shirt, and a flat-iron—these are them.
   Fry.  I have a pension from the East India Company—it was due on the 22nd of August—there was 2 l . 12 s . due to me. Witness.  He owes me 3 l . 12 s .—the key was sent home by another lodger, and I found all the things gone—there were not any things borrowed of me.
   Langley.  Every article here has been lent to us. Witness.  No, they have not—I lent her the flat-iron to iron with, not to get a quartern of rum on—the flannel shirt was not lent to you to pledge with a piece of diaper. GEORGE MURRAY( police-constable 68 K. ) I took the prisoners. The duplicate of the flat-iron was given me by Langley, and two duplicates of a table cloth and a flannel shirt were given me by Mrs. Lyons—Langley said what she had done was under the direction of Fry that Mrs. Lyons had given her permission to pledge these articles. THOMAS ALLEN. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a table cloth and a flannel shirt, which were pledged by Langley in the name of "Fry, 7, High-street"—they correspond with the duplicate I have.
   Langley. Q.  Do you remember my separating the shirt from the diaper? A.  No—but I recollect Mrs. Lyons saying, when she came to make inquiries, that there was a flat-iron, a shirt, and some other articles, but the table cloth she had lent to Langley—I did not tell the magistrate that the prosecutrix said that. MARY LYONS re-examined.  I did not tell Allen that I had lent the cloth to Langley—the policeman was with me. GEORGE MURRAY re-examined.  I went with Mrs. Lyons—I did not hear her tell Allen so.
