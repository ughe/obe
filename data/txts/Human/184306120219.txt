1977. WILLIAM ROOKE was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of June , 2 petticoats, value 4 s  .; the goods of Elizabeth Holbrook . ELIZABETH HOLBROOK. I live in Portpool-lane. On the 2nd of June I had some petticoats drying—I took them out of the tub, and hung them across the chair—the prisoner came in with a board—he took it in—I went in, and they were safe—he came in from where they were three times, and the third time he came with the petticoats in a bundle, under his arm, running with water—they have not been found.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I never saw any petticoats, or touched them. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Four Months .
 1978. WILLIAM KING was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of June , 1 coat, value 1 s  .; and 1 jacket, 2 s  .; the goods of Samuel Ward . SAMUEL WARD. I live in London-lane, Hackney. Between three and four o'clock, on the 10th of June, I had a coat and jacket safe in the window—I received information, looked, and missed them—these are mine. JOHN READ( police-constable N  171.) I live in High-street, Homerton. I was on duty in Church-street, Hackney, about half-past three o'clock on the 10th of June—I saw the prisoner drop this coat and jacket, and run off—I took him without losing sight of him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was walking across London-fields; I had not been in Church-street. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .
 1979. ELIZABETH FAGAN and ALICE FAGAN were indicted for stealing, on the   8th of June , 3 bottles, value 3 d  .; 1 1/2 pint of ginger-beer, 4 d  .; 1 dead lobster, 1 s  .; 3/4lb. weight of pickled salmon, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1lb. weight of mutton, 6 d  .; 1/2lb. weight of cheese, 4 d  .; and 1lb. weight of bread, 1 d  .; the goods of Richard Pryce , the master of Elisabeth Fagan. RICHARD PRYCE. I live in the Strand; the prisoner Elizabeth was my maid of all work. About seven o'clock, on the 8th of June, I was called up by the police, who produced these things—I believe the ginger-beer to be mine—I asked Elizabeth, who is Alice's sister, to put on her bonnet and come to Bow-street—she agreed, and said her sister had drawn her to take these things—I made no promise to induce her to tell me—these things, are worth, altogether, 3 s . 6 d . CHARLES HENRY BAGNALL( police-constable F  31.) About half-past six o'clock this morning I stopped the prisoner Alice in Pickett-street, about half a mile from the prosecutor's—she had a basket—I asked what she had them—she said some empty bottles and other things—I asked where she got it—she said from 430, Strand—I knocked at the door—it was opened by Elizabeth, who said, "Good God, what is the matter?"—Alice said, "The policeman stopped me"—Elizabeth said, "I did give her these things, and I will give you 2 l . if you will say nothing about it," and then she said, "I will make it up 5 l ."
   Elizabeth Fagan.  I never said so.
   ELIZABETH FAGAN  — GUILTY. Aged 28.— Confined Nine Days .
   ALICE FAGAN  — NOT GUILTY.
   Sixth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
