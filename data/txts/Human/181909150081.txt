I ran out, and took him in about two hours at the Hoop and Grapes, public-house, Ratcliff-highway. Next day the bundle was brought to the office.
   JAMES CRUTCHER. On Tuesday evening, the 14th of September, about ten o'clock at night, I was coming up Old Gravel-lane, heard the cry of Stop thief! and stopped the prisoner in the road - I gave him to the watchman. The constable of the night found a shawl between his waistcoat and shirt, which the prosecutrix claimed.
   WILLIAM SUMMERS. I am a headborough. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house - he denied all knowledge of it. I found a shawl under his jacket, and the other things in a bundle at the Jolly Sailors, public-house. He said he left the rest of the property there.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor left me in the house; Scott gave me the bundle, asked me to leave it at the Jolly Sailors, public-house, and told me to sell one shawl. I sold it to a Jew, and gave her the money.
   ELIZABETH SCOTT re-examined. It is false.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Transported for Seven Years.

 1358.  SAMUEL BEWS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of August , one coat, value 12 s., the property of George Dawe.
   GEORGE DAWE. I live in Montague-square; the coat was on the rails, in the area, before the housekeeper's window - the gate was shut. Soon after, I was informed that it was gone, ran out, and secured the prisoner with it under his coat.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   ANN WHEELER. I was in the housekeeper's-room, and saw the prisoner hook the coat up with a hook.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1359.  JOHN BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , one coat, value 2 l., the property of James Bannister.
   JAMES BANNISTER. I live in Goodge-street, Tottenham-court-road, and am a hackney-coach driver; the prisoner was in the habit of coming to me every night. On the 11th of August, he came and said he was very hungry. I told him to ride behind my coach till I could get some money, and then I would give him something to eat. I got an eighteen-penny fare, and bought him some meat. While he was at the public-house, he was looking at my coat, and said it was a good one - I said it was, and if I lost it I should be ruined. He said,
 "Give it to me, and I will put it on the box." I gave it to him, and he set off with it. I did not see him again until the 11th of September, when I found him at Lambeth workhouse. We were brought up together.
 Prisoner's Defence. I put it on the box.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1360.  JOSEPH BELLAMY and ELIZABETH COLLIS were indicted for stealing, from the person of John Curtis , one watch, value 3 l.; three seals. value 1 l., and twenty-five 1 l. bank notes, his property.
   JOHN CURTIS. I am a smith, On the 10th of August, between eleven and twelve o'clock in the day, I was in company with Collis at a house in Homer-street, Paddington- Bellamy came up. I put my hand into my pocket to pay for some beer, and accidentally pulled my notes out, and replaced them again. Being fatigued, I laid my head on the table, and went to sleep for about ten minutes, then awoke and found them both gone - I was sober. I missed all this property, which was safe when I laid my head down, for I took it out of my fob, and put it into my breast-pocket. The notes were in my breeches-pocket.
 Q. How long was you there with her - A. From two or three o'clock in the morning; Bellamy came into the room between eleven and twelve o'clock in the day - I was up then.
 Prisoner COLLIS. Q. Was you not in liquor - A. No. I met her in Oxford-street.
 Q. Did you not offer to sell the notes for 2 s. 6 d. each, saying, they were bad - A. No. When I pulled them out I said they were not good ones, as I thought they would not try to take them - Bellamy asked me to sell them. I lost a silver hunting-watch and three seals,
 DAVID PLANT. I am a private in the third regiment of Guards. On the 11th of August, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I met Bellamy; he asked me to carry a small parcel to the barracks for him, which I did, and put it into my box. On the 16th or 17th, Butler said he was requested by Bellamy to ask me for the watch; I then opened the parcel, and gave him a hunting-watch out of it - it was in a handkerchief. Nothing else was in it.
   ISAAC BUTLER. On the 16th of August, Collis came and asked me to get from Plant a watch and seal; on the 17th I asked him for it, he gave me the watch, and said he would bring the seal on the following Saturday. I gave the watch to the prisoner, Collis, about nine o'clock that evening.
   WILLIAM BROWN. I am a coachman. Collis gave me a watch to pledge, and I sent my fellow-servant, Burgess, with it.
   WILLIAM BURGESS. I received the watch from Brown, and pledged it with Welton for 1 l.
   WILLIAM WELTON. I am shopman to Mr. Ross, who is a pawnbroker. Burgess pledged the watch with me on the 18th of August.
