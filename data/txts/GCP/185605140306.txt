Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. What are you? A. A distiller
and farmer I have distilled in France, Belgium, and Germany, I am not
established in any business- I get money sent from home, at Stettin--it is
one year and eight months since I was in Stettin-I have received money
from home during that time I received a letter from the son of the prisoner,
and through that I knew he was in Paris - I was in Valenciennes when I
received the letter through the prisoner-I wanted to come to London, in
hopes of getting something to do in the distillery-I was living in Paris,
in Rue d'Amsterdam, near the railway-I do not know the number, it was
20 or 30–I lived alone I was a lodger-I do not know the name of
the persons I lodged with—I lodged with them two days the prisoner was
lodging in the Rue de la Paix I remained two days after 19th Jan. I
then went to Valenciennes—I do not know exactly when I arrived in
London ; either in March or April—the prisoner fetched me from the
station when I arrived, and took me to the public house where the things
were found I could pay my lodging at the public house.
Q. Will you swear that the landlord did not detain what little you had
for payment of the lodging in King William-street, and that he did not say
that he would turn you out of the house unless you paid, and then the
prisoner became security for you? A. Yes, the arrears that there was-
the evening before the prisoner was taken, the landlord threatened to keep
my things, to pay the arrears I was not the person who went twice to the
house in King William-street to ask for the prisoner's things.
Q. Will you swear that you did not go to that house on 17th April, and
demand Lenden's things, and were they not refused you? A. I do not know
the dates on the day before the apprehension of the prisoner, I took a letter
to the house and demanded Lenden's things I did not go again on the
morning of the 18th-the carpet bag 1 lent to the prisoner was the only one
I had I had a courier's pocket besides-I was before the Magistrate when
the prisoner was examined—T have been to Newgate every day to see him
I am now living over Westminster-bridge. I do not know the name of
the street-I do not know the name of the person I am living with
MR. LILLEY: Q. Was the place at which you lodged at Paris an hotel:
A. A furnished lodging-I lodge opposite Astley's Theatre, at the first coffee
house on the left hand over Westminster-bridge — I was not examined
before the Magistrate when I was at the police court - I have my passport
and certificate at my lodging-what I have said about the carpet bag is
true I have not said anything in order to favour the prisoner.
