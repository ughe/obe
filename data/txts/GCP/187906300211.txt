Cross-examined by Mr. Fultoy. I am giving this information in the
interests of public justice I did not plead guilty to the Old Kent Road
burglary-I gave this information after my conviction-I had previously
told the detective that if I was convicted I could give them valuable inform-
ation, and if not convicted I might not have given the information--3
convictions of felony were proved against me at the Surrey Sessions, on
6th May-I was convicted at Slough under the Prevention of Criininals
Act, as an habitual criminal, and 2 previous convictions were proved
against me--Mrs. Chandler was present at my conversation with Jones,
but I do not know whether she heard it I heard her examined at the
Southwark Police Court-she said she did not hear the conversation-I am
not aware that any one was present at the conversation between Patterson,
Jones, and myself, at the Elephant and Castle--it was at the publie
refreshment bar.
Cross-examined by Emma Workman. I saw you at your house in con-
versation with Patterson and your husband—I did not tell the police of
this because you gave evidence against me.
"By the Court. Mrs. Workman was a witness against me in the Old
Kent Road hurglary case.
WILLIAM CHANDLER. I am undergoing penal servitade in Pentonville
Prison—I was sentenced at the Surrey Sessions, April 9th, 1879, and had been
in Wandsworth Prison from 5th November, 1877, to 3rd August, 1878
I knew Patterson seven or eight years agoI did not know him personally,
but a man named Charles Wells, of Camberwell, showed him to me I
have known Workman and his wife for some years the witness Harding
lodged in the name of Baker at 14, Cork Street, and he was lodging there
in August, 1878—I have not seen Baker at my house in company with
either of the other prisoners except Workman-Workman has been there
hundreds of times, and visited Harding (or Baker) there a good many
times— Baker and I were out housebreaking together almost daily about
the middle of August, 1878, I saw Patterson and Workman talking
together at the Station Tavern, Beresford Street, Walworth Road, but I
did not hear what they said when we came away Workman said, "Do
you know that party ?” I said, “I have seen him before;" he said, " That's
Pat, my brother-in-law. That's the party that's putting up the place in
Westminster Bridge Road "_"putting up" a place means to commit a
burglary or robbery-Baker, Workman, and Dowthwaite, alias Rogers,
were all out housebreaking together last year-Baker is the cleverest man-
I have five or six times heard Baker and Workman, at my place, talking
about Maudsley's place, Westminster Bridge Road, and concocting how to
do it-I met Workman by the Crown, London Road, coming from Water-
loo, about the middle of August-I said, “Hallo! Arthur. What's yer
game?" he said, “I have just been down to see Pat." We went inside
and had something to drink, and he said, “I've just been down and
fetched these from him," showing me two keys, one a pipe key and
the other not-I did not notice, I was a disinterested party-I said,
“Is there two safes, Arthur!” and he said, “Yes, sometimes they
put the money in one and sometimes in the other, so, to make sure we have
had two made" I said, “Well, if you open them with keys, Arthur, they
will think it is some one about the place "-he said, “ Pat will leave a few
weeks before the job comes off, and they won't suspect him "-_in August
