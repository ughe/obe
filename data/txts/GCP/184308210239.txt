WILLIAM SANDFORD. I am foreman to Mr. Beard, and live in Red Lion-
yard, at the foot of Westminster-bridge-it is the yard behind the prosecu-
tor's premises-the back gates were open on the night in question, as my
master was out--my attention was attracted by the policeman running about
- he told me to stand back, wbich I did, till he had taken two of the pri-
soners-be called for a light, and I brought one-I picked up in the yard
three spoons, a constable's staff, and some cigars--I gave them to the ser-
geant No. 3.
WILLIAM SIMONs (police-sergeant L 3.) I received some articles from Sand-
ford, which I produce-I found some cigars, and I found a jenmy in the coal-
cellar-I dug it out of the coals-I found two german silver forks and a spoon
in the bar-parlour, and a pair of plated sugar-tongs-the life-preserver was
found on the cellar steps- I tried the jemmy to the bar window, and it fitted it.
Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. Where in the cellar did
you find the crow-bar? A. Under the coals, nearly in the centre of the
cellar-I found it about twenty minutes after I first came up-I did not
go into the house the first time I went through the kitchen, down the
stairs, into the coal-cellar-there might be, perhaps, a ton of coals in the
cellar.
MATTHEW FRENCH WAGSTAFF. I am surgeon to the police-force-I saw
Mr. Mackintosh at the station on the morning, I was sent for-he appeared to
have a contused wound on the temple-it was done by some heavy instru-
ment--this life-preserver would have done it-there is danger attending a
blow on that part--the eye has been discoloured and swolled—it is very
slightly so now.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Was it not such a blow as might
have been given by a man's fist? A. I think not-it must bave been some
heavy instrument--the skin was not broken, but there was that appearance
to lead me to suppose it was some heavy instrument-it is possible a violent
blow with a fist might do it.
