 1932.  JOHN BUCKLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 basket, value 2s.; 9 live tame rabbits, price 9s.; 5 live tame fowls, price 7s. 6d., and 1 live tame duck, price 1s. 6d., the property of William Cockerton; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   WILLIAM COOKERTON. I came from Suffolk to London on the 30th of July with a basket of rabbits and fowls, but I do not know the number; I believe there was a duck in the basket - they belonged to George Bread- I should think they were worth a sovereign; when I got to Whitechapel church I heard a cry of Stop thief! I got down, looked at the hind part of my waggon, and missed the basket.
   JOHN SEFFORD. I am a Police-officer. On Saturday morning, the 30th of July, I was in Whitechapel; the prisoner left a cart, then came to me, and said, "What do you think of these waggoners? they would not give me a ride to Spitalfields:" I said I would not be under an obligation to them - he then asked if I had seen two men with baskets - I said No; he then crossed over the road - I stood at a door, and watched him - I saw him go to this waggon, take the basket, and run round a corner; I pursued, he dropped the basket: I pursued to Colchester-street, and found him squatting down behind an old boiler - I sprang my rattle, and another officer came, one went round one way, and the other the other; we took the prisoner - he then pretended to be very drunk, and suffered himself to be dragged out; I then took up this basket.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me take the basket? A. Yes; I had seen you watching the carts the morning before.
   WILLIAM ARGENT. I took the prisoner last December; I saw him tried here, but I have not a certificate of his conviction.   GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1933.  JOHN MONK was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 chaise, value 20l., and 2 spring blinds, value 1l., the goods of William Scurry.
 MR. HEATON conducted the prosecution.
   MARY SCURRY. I am the wife of William Scurry- he lives in Hunter-street, Brunswick-square. On the 27th of August my child's chaise was in the parlour, and the blinds laid in the room - I missed them between one and two o'clock; I saw the prisoner about two in Brunswick-square - the chaise is worth 20l., and the blinds 1l.; the pannel of our front kitchen door had been taken out, and in the prisoner's pocket we found a key, which fitted the lock of the area gate.
   JAMES COOPER. I was visiting at the prosecutor's. I received information, went out at the street door, and saw the prisoner dragging this chaise, seventy or eighty yards from the house; he attempted to make his escape, but another person came up, and he was taken - we found on him three skeletons, and four other keys, a screw-driver, and two knives.
   JOHN CLINTON. I assisted in taking the prisoner - this key found on him fitted the area gate; I saw the prisoner scuffling with Cooper, and secured him - the kitchen door had been entered.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I have a wife and four small children - I did it from want.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1934.  JOHN MASON and RICHARD SHAW were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 1 shilling, 1 sixpence, 1 penny, and 6 farthings, the monies of James Faley , from his person.
   WILLIAM SEABROOK. I am a farmer, and live at Abbots Langley, in Herts. I was in Covent-garden on the 16th of July, and saw the prisoners follow a person, who gave his name James Faley- he is not here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1935.  WILLIAM BARBER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 pair of boots, value 4s., the goods of Benjamin Lambert.
   EDWARD AUSTIN. I am servant to Mr. Benjamin Lambert, of Old-street-road. On a Wednesday in September the prisoner came to his shop, and asked the price of some blacking - I said 4d. a bottle; I saw him take a pair of boots off a nail at the end of the counter, and put them into his hat - he then asked me if we had any cheaper blacking: I said No; he walked out - I ran to the door, and called my master, who was down stairs - the prisoner was taken to Worship-street.
   JOHN MORLEY. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner, and found the boots in his hat, on Wednesday the 7th of September.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A boy took the boots off a nail, I ran and took them from him, and they took me; I said,"Here are the boots, but I did not steal them."
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Three Months.

 1936.  GEORGE BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of William Gwyther , from his person.
   WILLIAM GWYTHER. On the 8th of September I was in Parliament-street, between ten and eleven o'clock, waiting for the procession; I did not feel this handkerchief taken - but it was held up, and I claimed it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You did not see it taken? A. No.
   JAMES WILD. I am an officer. I was on duty on the day of the Coronation, and saw the prisoner with another man, attempting to pick several pockets, and at last he took this handkerchief from the prosecutor - he put it under his waistcoat; I told the prosecutor of it.
 Cross-examined Q. Had you known him before? A. I cannot say I had; he did not come to me, produce the handkerchief, and say he had found it; I said it was a bad job for him - he never said a word; I was in plain clothes.
 The prisoner received a good character, and a witness promised to take him into his employment.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Weeks.
