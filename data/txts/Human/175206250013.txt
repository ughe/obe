She seemed to speak as though I should not have to do with her for that; I said I did not desire it I only wanted to go to bed for the conveniency of the bed, and said, give me my money again. Presently she said, you shall give me half a guinea; she slapped my face and gave me several blows. Then I came to my senses. She said, I will have your money, and there are them in the house that will prevent your telling tales, if you don't give it me, and affected a hem, calling Dick. She put her hand into her pocket and said, if you don't give it me I'll rip your guts out directly. I told her I was as strong as she; upon which she said, I should be murdered if I did not comply, so I pulled out all I had in one pocket, which was half a guinea and four or five pennyworth of halfpence, and laid them on the table ; she scrabbled it up, put it in her pocket, and said, she would have more, and that I had a guinea more. I told her I had not, and desired her to give me the halfpence again for a breakfast, but she still insisted upon having a guinea more or I should never go out of the house. At last I got out by swearing I never would take her before a justice or meddle with her. I immediately got a watchman, who talked to her as she looked out at the window. She told him he was no constable. Then came a beadle, to whom an old woman opened the door. They took the prisoner to the Roundhouse. The woman of the house offered me my money again.
 Prisoner's Defence.
 The gentleman made me a present of it. After he had what he desired of me, he said he would have it again, or he'd swear a robbery against me.
   William King. I live in the Old Bailey. I was at the house next door to where the prisoner lives, and going out to make water I heard her voice, (I knew her before.) I also heard a man say, give me my halfpence; she answered, did not you give it me? he said, I know that, but if you don't I'll swear a robbery against you.
   Mary Smith. I opened the door and let the prisoner and that man in that night.
 Q. What is her name?
 M. Smith. I can't tell her sirname, her Christian name is Jane. I have known her about a fortnight; she lived there at that time.
 Q. Who keeps the house?
 M. Smith. I can't tell, the gentleman lives in Westminster.
 Q. What are you?
 M. Smith. I am a chairwoman and washerwoman.
 Q. Who lives in the house?
 M. Smith. There are two men and their wives. I was in the passage and heard him say to her, what will you have? she said, half a guinea; he said, there it is for you, take it. I know no more.
     Acquitted.

 362. (M)  George Ballentine, was indicted for that he, together with William McCuller, on Mary wife of  Robert Steel , and against her will, the said Mary did ravish and carnally know, May 12. ++
   Mary Steel deposed, the prisoner took her as she was going to work about three o'clock in the morning from near a watch-house where he found her to a lodging-house in Well-street, and carried her up stairs, where were two beds; in one lay two women together, and he flung her down upon the other bed and lay with her against her will; that she resisted and cried out all in her power; that he tore her cloaths, kept her daughter in the room, and that M'Culler kept her from going out of the room to call in any assistance for her mother.
   Elizabeth Steel, her daughter, confirmed the mother in all, except that of being only on the bed. She said, the prisoner and her mother were in the bed covered under the bed cloaths about two hours.
 The prisoner, in his defence, said, it was a contrivance of one Westwood, in order to extort money from him, who called  Oliver Smith the constable of the night; who deposed, that he and Henry Keys, one of his watchmen, found the prisoner and a little girl with her which he supposed to be the daughter lying sleeping in an alley with their gown tails over their heads, and took them to the watch-house. That from thence the prisoner and M'Culler went with the prosecutrix and her daughter.
