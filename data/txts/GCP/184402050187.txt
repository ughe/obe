WILLIAM THORNTON. I was horse-keeper to the prosecutor in Aug. and
Sept.--I was at Fulham on a Wednesday when my master was absent-
I saw the prisoner at the house just before twelve o'clock-Mrs. Smith was
up-no one else was in the house-I was in the tap-room, and Mrs. Smith
told me it was time to go to bed I went up stairs to go to bed in my room-
after I went up stairs the prisoner followed Mrs. Smith up stairs to her bed-
room-I saw Mrs. Smith draw the curtains and put out the light.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you never said one word about this to any
one till two days after Mrs. Smith left? 1. No.
JAMES GRAY, tobacconist, Drummond-street, Euston-square. On Sunday,
the 12th of Nov., the prisoner came to my bouse with Mrs. Smith-they
asked for apartments for a few nights-1 let them the first floor--they lived
there as man and wife-they were found in bed at my house, by the officer
and Mr. Smith, at five o'clock in the morning--the prisoner brought three
packages, which were directed to some person in Dublin-they returned to
my house the same day as they were taken in custody, and left in half or three
quarters of an hour there was a hamper there, which was taken away to
Liverpool, to be forwarded to Dublin.
Cross-examined. Q. Mrs. Smith was very fond of the prisoner? 2. Yes,
EDWARD Brown. I am a cabman, and stand in the rank in Drummond-
street-on Sunday evening, the 12th of Nov., the prisoner came and asked
if I could drive to Fulham-I said, “ Yes"-he said he wanted some luggage
brought back-I drove him to Fulham-he went to a house—be put a
hamper and portmanteau, a canvas bag, a gun-case, and a deal box in the
cab-be told me to drive on about twenty yards from the house, and he would
come to me--he came, and told me to make the best of my way home to
where I came from, which was Mr. Gray's house in Drummond-street-next
day I went again with him to Fulham, and set him down about 200 yards
before he got to his father's house-he told me to pull down to the door and
bring the baggage away– I received from him a large chest and a hat-box-
he ordered me to drive back, and stop at the same place that I had come
from-I said “Yes," and he told me to take the left-hand road-I left him
at Gray's.
SAMUEL SMITH. I remember my wife leaving home by my direction-
I had before that deposited with her some silver; I cannot say what amount-
I should think from the appearance of the bag, the last time I saw it, there
must have been 71. or 81.- this is the bag-this shilling I can speak to--
was not aware that at any time before this my wife had parted with any of
this money to the prisoner- I traced them to Drummond-street, and the
prisoner took this bag out of his box-there was another little bag in this
bag-it was my habit to trust my wife with the care of the money when I
got to Drummond-street I saw this bag, and said I had no doubt of its being
mine, but I was in such an agitated state that I hardly remember what did
transpire-I do not know what has become of the little bag-it was given up
to the prisoner, he claiming it-my initials were on it-it was the bag that
I had had in my house, and had delivered to my wife to take care of my
money in- I have been missing money for a length of time, and complaining
of it-I expected to find 2001. in the house, which I did not-the prisoner
said the money was his.
