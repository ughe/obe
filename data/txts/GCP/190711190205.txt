HERBERT PAVELEY, van salesman, 53. Princess May Road. Stoke
Newington. At a quarter past eighs on November 1 I was londing my
van outside the warehouse, 61, Charterhouse Street. I saw defendant
there making water beside a whsel between the vans. I next saw him

see him come back the second time, I was called out of the ware
house, and the goods were on the ground.
JOSEPH BUCKLER, Market Constable 49. About 25 minutes to nine
in the morning of November 1 I heard whistle on the north side of
the poultry market. I went across and saw a crowd assembled and
prisoner struggling with Parkin. He told me the man had stolen
some goods from a van in Charterhouse Street. I took him across to
Charterhouse Street, some goods were shown to me, and it was sbsted
they were stolen from a van. I told him I should take him into cus-
tody for stealing woods. He said, "It is a mistake, I know nothing at
all about it." I look him to Snow Hill Station, where he was charged.
In answer to the charge he made no reply. I was told in his presence
that he had stolon the goods. He did not say anything about boys at
the time. It is a common practice for men to make water against vans
there, but they are stopped; I believe some have been reported and
summoned for committing a nuisance.
