MR. GIFFARD. l. What is your age? A. I am seventy-seven and a half.
@ Was the balance-sheet, which you saw, detailed or general, signed
by the auditors ? A. I think not; I won't say positively; I think it
is very probable that it was—I have no positive memory one way or
the other-I don't recollect anything about the word "detailed "-there
was a balance sheet in a very rough state laid before us; that was the
balance sheet prepared by the manager and secretary-I think there
were less figures in the final one-it was & short time before the half-
yearly meeting that I saw it-(looking at the detailed balance sheet again)
I can't say whether I ever saw this document before.
JURY. Q. Had you any knowledge of the forged and bankrupt bills
being in the item of 207,0001.? A. I did not know anything about them
till all of a sudden we found these things were in existence; I had no
knowledge of them before.
COURT. . Did you understand that there were overdue bills in that
item? A. No, I had no suspicion that such a thing was done-there
might be overdue bills which were of some value, as we supposed- I did not
know whether there were bills of bankrupts-bills are supposed to be of
some value for a long time; in that way I might consider they appeared,
but certainly not at their value as put upon paper-I did not consider
they appeared in any other form than this, that some value is attached to
a bad bill, if it is only 18, in the pound, and that is customary sometimes
in banks, I believe ; at least so I understand
JURY. Q. Were you at any time aware that forged bills and bankrupts'
bills were included in that 207,0001. ! A. Certainly not-bills which are
not paid when they become due, may be paid afterwards; they may pay
108. in the pound, or something I did understand that they were valued
at something, but in no other way; if I had had a suspicion of it I should
have been frightened out of my senses.
COURT. l. Did you know that the weekly statements contained the list
of all the bills that had accumulated since the 38,0001. was written off!
A. I believe no such information was given to us-I did not know it-I
never gave my sanction to anything of the sort-I certainly did not believe,
nor bad I the least suspicion, that bills that were good for nothing, were
brought there at their full value-I did not know in what way the over-
due bills were treated in the weekly returns; and if I and my colleagues
had known I think we should have stopped it.
Q. Did you ever give directions that forgeries and bankrupts' bills should
be only estimated, and not put down at their full value ! A. I never gave
any particular directions about it, but I always understood that was the
way in which it was done in all banks—the bills always were estimated-I
never saw it done in any account in the bank.

quently became a director of the London and Middlesex Bank-I was not
