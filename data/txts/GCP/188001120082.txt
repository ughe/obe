EDWIN CHAPLIN. I am & wine merchant, and trade under the firm of
Morgan and Co. at Hamburg - I am the prosecutor in this case-I first had
transactions with the defendant in 1866-between 1869 and 1876 I had no
business transuctions with him; in 1876, 1878, and 1879 I had-I supplied
goods in 1878, and those bills were outstanding to the extent of 1,2001.
ou receiving the order of 14th January I wrote to Mr. Norris the letter of
the 18th-I received a reply from hin, and on the 29th I received a letter
after his interview with the defendant-after rejeiving that letter I executed
the order which the defendant had sent me on 14th-the goods were shipped
in London on board the Ann Duthie for Sydney-the usual credit is six
inonths from the date of the mate's receipt one of these six bills had been
Tenewed in December; the first would become due early in March-I drew
the bill for these good after his bankruptcy, simply to give it in evidence-
I came to London for the purpose of attending the meeting of creditors on
27th March, and found the meeting was postponed, and notice of postponis-
ment had been given only a few hours, late the evening before-I instructed
my solicitors, Messrs. Wontner, and returned to Hamburg- I was induced
to execute the order of 14th January, by the letter I received from Mr.
Norris, dated 29th, the contrast between the letters of 20th and 29th
showed such a total change in his mind I was unwilling to supply the
goods before, but after Mr. Norris had made inquiries at my request, and
wrote the letter of the 29th, I thought it safe to execute the order I have
not received any dividend, and do not expect any-I have been bothered
to prove twice, once under the insolvency, and once under the bankruptcy;
I did prove.
Cross-examined. The goods were not parted with till the mate's receipt
was received I selected the goods in Hamburg for the performance of the
order, and put them on board a vessel to send them to England_I sent an
invoice to the defendant, and informed him that I had shipped the goods
on his account in performance of his order I knew that they were to be
put on board a vessel to be named by him-Mr. Norris, as my agent, shipped
the goods for him.
CHARLES JOSEPH HITCHINGHAM. I am & clerk to John Brown and Co..
bankers, of Abchurch Lane-I produce a bill for 871. 88. 1d. due 21st
February, 1879; it is a three months bill from 18th November, 1878,
accepted by G. F. Dickenson-it was payable at Prescott's-it was presented,
and dishonoured; the notary's ticket is attached to it.
RICHARD EVANS. I am ledger-keeper at Prescott's the defendant had
an account there; it was closed on 28th January last year, and has never
been reopened-31. 98. 6d. was the amount drawn out on that day to close
the account ; that sum had been standing to his credit from the 22nd.
Cross-examined. I know that the account had been attached-up to that
time he always had a large and good account with us; he had a fair balance
generally--he was making large payments to us in November and December,
and up to the time the account was attached; from that time it was not
thought advisable to pay more money in.
Re-examined. The date of the attachment was 8th January 431. 48. 6d.
was then standing to his credit.
