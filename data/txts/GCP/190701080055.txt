in the account supplied to me. As to Leftwich, I have put 121
trusses of bay in the original statement, and that it appears as 12
trusses in the analysis is the fault of the copyist. The sale to Lipton
of February 24 was omitted from the original account, so does not

account, and the same observation applies to others. I cannot speak
as to the quantity consumed by the horses. As to damaged stuff, I
have always seen some in the stores that has not been used. I
should say it had been there some months. It was of value, as at
times it could be mixed with good stuff. The entries of the stuff that
went away on March 22 and 23 are respectively in Battey's hand-
writing and myself. They read all that went out. I appear to have
been on duty early that morning. That is all that I sent away.
Mumford used to leave overnight instructions as to vans going to
ccrtain customers. A van never left the yard without an entry being
made. As regards the straw, the two bales of rye otraw to Dickerson
on February 17 is not in the goods account, nor are the 36 trusses of
straw on February 24. (Witness was further examined in great de-
tail on the particulars of the account.) As to the statement that
large sums of money were taken to the market out of the safe by
Mumford, I never knew him to keep any large amount of cash
in his safe. He has sent me once or twice to take money from a
little canvas bag that he kept to take out money, but I never knew
bir to have more than three or four sovereigns and some silver in it.
He used to go or send to the bank and cash chequee payable to sell.
I believe I have sometimes taken out £40 in gold. It was a practice
to enter in the toll book more goods than were in fact purchased
on the market. If I had really nothing to enter I would enter
perhaps 10 loads. The toll was 7d. for hay and 1d. for straw-a
very small matter. The object of that, I suppose, was that Mr. Mum-
ford wished to pay his fair share of the tolls, and, not having much
to enter, he entered more. The toll book is not, therefore, & trust-
worthy record of the business done on the market. When I put
down unintelligible or fictitious names I make them up often out of
my own head. I have known of goods bought on the market on the
farmers' vans being transhipped on to Mumford's vans without their
going to the market and being weighed.
