852. ELIZA HARRISON, MARGARET HOARE, and CAROLINE
ALBURY were indicted for stealing 35 yards of ribbon, value 11s., the
goods of Catherine Clark.
WILLIAM CLEAR. I live with my uncle, in Blackman-street, Borough.
On the afternoon of Tuesday, 2nd Feb., I saw the three prisoners at our shop
-I afterwards watched, and saw them go into Mrs. Clark's shop-I then saw
two of them come out, and the other one shortly afterwards--it was then
about four o'clock-I watched them for more than an hour.
WILLIAM CLARK. I am the son of Catherine Clark, a widow, who carries on
the business of a draper, in Waterloo-road. On the afternoon of 2nd Feb. the
three prisoners came into our shop-Albury asked for some lace-I showed
her some-she said there was none to suit her-there was a collar on the
counter-one of the prisoners took it in her hand-my mother saw them from
the parlour window-she came and took it from her-they then left the shop
-Clear came in, and from what he said, I looked into the window, and missed
some ribbon, which I had placed there about half an hour before it was not
on a block, but loose-Clear and I then followed the prisoners--they went
to the New-road, and stopped at Mr. Lawrence's shop--we saw them take
something from there--they then went into a kind of recess- I went for a
policeman--we found them together, and charged them with having taken
the ribbon--they said they had not taken it, and did not know anything
about it-I saw the ribbon in Hoare's hand-she said it was not mine-
gave them in charge when we got to the station, I did not see the ribbon
again-I knew the ribbon-it was my mother's there were thirty-five yards
of it, as near as I can guess.
Cross-examined by MR. MELLER. Q. You had some suspicion about these
women? 4. My mother bad we took them about ten minutes after they
left our shop-1 do not know what was done with the ribbon--the prisoners
were searched --some wafers were found on them-we had only that one piece
of ribbon in our window-I saw the ribbon after I brought the policeman-
I said in his presence. That is my ribbon"-the prisoners were taken to the
station, and no ribbon found-Hoare had it in her hand in the street.
WILLIAM WELLS (police-constable L 84.) I heard Clark speak about the
ribbon - I did not see any-I took the prisoners to the station, but no ribbon
was found.
Hoare. They were the strings of my bonnet which I had in my hand.
