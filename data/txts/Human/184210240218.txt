JOHN MOIR( police-constable M  207.) The prosecutor gave Barrett into my charge—on her statement of the transaction, I went with her to a lodging-house, in Kent-street—she went in, came out, and gave me a description of the prisoner, and the part of the room he was sitting in—I went in and took him into custody—I brought him out—there was very little light where he stood, and the prosecutor said he thought he was not the man, but afterwards, when I took him into the house, where there was more light, he identified him, and gave him in charge—he made no answer to it, but said he thought he was taken for a robbery, which occurred with some girls, a few nights previous—the prosecutor pointed out to me the spot where he was knocked down—it was not more than eight or ten yards from the house where I took the prisoner—there is a lamp directly in front of the house—there was sufficient light to recognize any one—more so than where the prisoner stood when the prosecutor could not iden tify him—the light from the lodging-house window throws a better light a little way off than close to it, and there is a gas light to a public-house adjoining—when the prosecutor expressed a doubt, I said, "Come into the house and look round, there are plenty of them here"—he came in with me and looked round, and after scrutinizing the prisoner more, he said he was the man.
   Prisoner , He did not; he said it was a man a head taller than me that robbed him. Witness.  Not after he saw you in the house—Barrett told me that she had seen the pad in the prisoner's hand. THOMAS PHILIP DAVIS re-examined.  Barrett told me she had seen the pad, when she first spoke to me of the man that robbed me—I told her I had lost one, but not before she told me she had seen it—I had not talked to anybody about losing it—the prisoner was by my side when he trippd me up—I saw his face when he stooped down—I am sure I had my money about me at the time I was knocked down—I had seen it safe about ten minutes before—I had had no fall before I was knocked down to the best of my knowledge—I have no recollection of falling—I am certain the pri soner is the man that knocked me down—I said at first that it was a taller man, because it was dark there, but when they took him into the light I knew him—the house where the prisoner was taken is about six or sewn doors from where I was robbed—he was dressed very shabbily when he knocked me down—it was not far from a lamp, and a few shops were open—I have often been robbed before—I have not charged several people with robbing me—I have let them go—I charged a man that was tried many years ago in the New Court, and he was transported for fourteen years I believe—I never charged any one but the prisoner with this robbery—a little while ago I lost a 30 l . Bank of England note, some sovereigns, and a Bank Dividend Warrant—I went to the station about that—that is the only time—I have 1300 l . in the Three per Cents., and have a good bit of money at times—I take very little care of it.
