   COURT . Q.  Can you be mistaken, could it be faintness or any other cause? A.  No, she was laughed at by the men.

No, it was not faintness—I could not be mistaken, it was too evident—she stood up in the court-yard, against the railings, to recover herself—she could not stand—I saw her drink brandy twice, as well as porter, yes terday, at the public-house. FRANCIS JOHN HOLLOWAY. I am a sail-maker. I came to the Old Bailey yesterday with a friend, to see a person named Evans, a stranger, who had come up about a situation—I met with him in the Old Bailey—I saw Mrs. Beagley cross the road from the Pitt's Head public-house over to this Court—she appeared to me in a beastly state of intoxication—she fell down on one knee, and was pulled up again by the man with her—I believe she was intoxicated.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Were you in the public-house? A.  I had been in it, but was coming away—I was not in it while she was there—I am not acquainted with the prisoner's father—I saw him here this morning—I have spoken to him—I spoke to him four or five days ago for the first time in my life—it is since the sessions have been on—I heard from a friend of mine that he had a daughter here—I was along with Mr. Silvester—the father told me about his daughter's case.
   COURT . Q.  Do you take an interest in it from what he said? A.  I took an interest so far as this, I came with Mr. Silvester, who took an ac tive part in the prisoner's defence. NOT GUILTY.
