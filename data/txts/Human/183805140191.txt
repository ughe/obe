   Q.  Have you been paid for coming up before the Committee? A.  Yes—I cannot tell you how much—I cannot tell whether it is 7 l . 8 l ., of 9 l ., or what it is—I am sure I cannot tell how long I was in town—it was some where about nine or ten days, I believe—I came up by the coach—I paid my own expenses up—Mr. Murray paid them down—I cannot tell what I received for myself besides my expenses up and down—I was boarded at a public-house, and did not pay—we received our expenses separately-every witness received their own.
   Q.  Did you receive the money which was paid you, besides what you were paid for coming up and down? A.  Yes, I believe I received it on Saturday night—I did not go back with Emma Harvey—I was on the top of the coach on Sunday—I cannot tell what day of the week I came up—I was paid in sovereigns—I cannot tell how many I had—it was not twenty, nor fifteen, nor twelve, nor ten—I swear I had not ten—I tell you I had eight or nine—I cannot tell which—I got no wages for the time I was in London—I earn 7 s . 6 d . a day when I work—I work regu larly for Mr. Mason—I have earned 7 s . 6 d . a day many times—the 8 l . or 9 l . was to include my expenses down, and while I—was here—I mean loss of time—the eating and drinking was paid for.
   Q.  Are you a voter? A.  Yes, a freeman—I voted at the last election for Mr. Miller and Mr. De Horsey—I did not vote for them before, because Mr. De Horsey never came before—I voted for Mr. Miller three times be fore—I never received a farthing for my vote in my life—I have never been in trouble in my life—I am a married man—my wife has not had any money, to my knowledge—I never found her in possession of money I was not aware of—I was canvassed by Mr. Miller at this election—I was not paid for standing at the top of the stairs—Mr. Cork placed us there—he asked me—I do not know how many voters Mr. Mason has in his em ployment—it ebbs and flows—sometimes he has between twenty and forty—I have known him to have one or two hundred freemen.
   Q.  Did you see 5 l . paid to the freemen? A.  Is that a fair question to ask?
   MR. KELLY . Q.  you say you never received any money at all for your vote at any time? A.  At no time—I received the 8 l . or 9 l . from the solicitor when I went down—I do not know the precise sum, whether it was 7 l ., 8 l ., or 9 l .—I arrived in town on Thursday, and remained till the following Saturday week—I have a wife and family—I paid my coach hire up to London myself—it cost me between 2 l . and 3 l .—I was in town nearly a fortnight—I paid ray coach hire down, out of the money Mr. Mur ray gave me—the rest I had for loss of time—I am quite sure, after Mr. Miller left the Castle, I never saw him there again that night.
