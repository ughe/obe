 Rockall's Defence (written). About eight o'clock on the evening in question, I was hastening home, through Holborn, to my father's house, when to my surprise, I was taken into custody on the present charge; and what added to my surprise was, that the prisoner Brown was taken as an accomplice.  143.  RICHARD KING was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS WOODWARD. I am a furniture-broker, and live in Carnaby-street. The prisoner was my porter for fourteen or fifteen years; he was occasionally authorised to receive money for me, which it was his duty to account for immediately he returned - on the 26th of August, I packed up two mahogany night-tables, and directed him to take them to No. 15, Bruton-street - the bill I gave him was 2l. 8s., but I believe there was a mistake, and the lady paid him 2l. 7s. - he ought to have returned to me immediately, where I had other work for him to do; but I saw no more of him till the officer took him on the 22nd of October.
   ELIZABETH SMITH. I am housekeeper to John Woolmer, Esq., of Bruton-street. On the 26th of August, the prisoner brought the two tables to me, and I paid him 2l. 7s. - this is the bill.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating, that he had gone into a public-house and fallen asleep, during which time the money was taken from his pocket. - Peter  John Dorrington gave him a good character, and engaged to employ him.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. - Confined Two Days.
 144.  EDWARD JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of November , 1 child's waggon, value 15s., the goods of James Vine.
   JOHN HIGGINS. I saw the prisoner on the 19th of November, with a child's waggon; he asked a person at the corner of the street, if she would buy it - he then asked me; I asked him the price; he said 8s. - he stood talking a few minutes, and then said I should have it for 1s. - he said he would not sell it so cheap, but Smithfield-market was not open; and he told me to buy it soon, as he wanted to go back to steal some brooms - he then went on, and I followed him; he went into Mr. Swale's shop, and came out, and he said he had sold it for 5s. - he told me he had sold three dozen brass cocks, at Elme's, in Playhouse-yard, for 1s., and they had told him if he did not take that they would send for the policeman, and have him taken - I went there, and while I was there three boys came running in with a dressed ham, which they sold for 2s., and they were to have one pound of it for their supper - the policeman came, and had the ham.
 Prisoner. I did not say any thing to him. Witness. Yes, he did; and he asked me to go with him, and steal a till, and said he would give me half the money - he told me the shop he had stolen the waggon from, but when the prosecutor came, he did not say a word.
   WILLIAM SWALE. I keep a shop in Coppice-row, Clerkenwell. I was sitting in my shop when the prisoner came with this waggon - he asked if I wanted to buy it - I said "No;" he said it was a very good one - I asked what he wanted for it - he said 6s.; I said, "I bought one, and it broke down" - he said this would not do so; that his father had made it, who lived at No. 24, Willow-gardens, Hoxton - I asked him if his father repaired such things; he said, "Yes," and I told him to call any time, and he should have the other to repair; he said his father would be glad of the job - I bought this one of him.
   JAMES VINE. I keep a toy-shop, in Hedge-row, Islington. This is my waggon - I lost it on the 19th of November.
     GUILTY. Aged 12. - Transported for Seven Years.
