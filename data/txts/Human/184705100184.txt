ELIZABETH LEONARD. I am the prosecutrix's daughter, and sister to the prisoner. I remember the prisoner coming in drunk on Sunday evening, at a quarter past six o'clock—I was taking tea with my mother—she asked him to sit down—he did not sit down—he said, "If you were like a mother you would let me get a chair myself"—he asked if she would have anything to drink—she said she would rather have a pint of beer for her supper—he got very angry, and said, "How would you look if I were to upset the table?"—she said, "Don't do that, have a cup of tea"—he upset the table—there was a knife laying there—he had it in his band—he went towards the fire-place with it, where my mother was—he went towards her—he did not do anything, he only went towards her—my mother got it out of his hand—this is my mark to my deposition—it was read over to me—( read  —"The prisoner took up the knife, went towards her, and made an attempt to stab her with the knife in the chest")—I did say that my mother saved herself and got a cut—the prisoner left the room, and returned, my mother took up the poker, and said if he did not go she would use it—he caught hold of her hand, and she did not use it—he struck her, and she struck him with her head and made his nose bleed—he knocked her down. EDWARD VOKIN( policeman. ) On the 11th of April I was called to the house, and found Mr. Leonard standing, leaning near the banisters, bleed ing from the nose and mouth, and the prisoner standing alongside of her, bleeding too—I asked her who had been beating her; she said her son—I asked him what made him do it, he said, it was a family affair,—he was drunk.
   Prisoner.  I stated to you that I had been struck in the face. Witness.  Not a word about it. BEMERSIDE HAYWARD. I am house-surgeon at St. Bartholomew's Hos pital. On the 11th of April Mr. Leonard was brought there—I examined her—she appeared very much injured—there was a swelling on the side of her head, on her wrist, and the whole side of her body—it appeared to be from repeated blows—there had also been blows on the back of the head—I considered her in danger for a short time—she had inflammation in the side, being in a precarious state of health.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have been with my mother nine or ten years, and did all for her that laid in my power; I sat down to breakfast with her on Sunday morning, afterwards went out and met a friend, and remained with him till about half-past five o'clock; I went home, a few words ensued; I was struck in the face, and having had a little drop of drink, I was provoked to do it; if I had not had the drink I should have had command over my temper; I am sincerely sorry for what has happened, I have been a teetotaler some time.
