2171. WILLIAM MALEY was indicted for stealing 1 sovereigo, 1 shil-
ling, and 1 sixpence; the monies of Ann Elizabeth Rolfe, from her
person; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
Ann Elizabeth Rolpe. I am a servant--I live in Duke's-court,
Union-street, Borough-on the 4th of Oct., at half-past eleven o'clock at
night, I was standing on the pavement, where there were some people
looking at a man—the officer told me something-I put my hand into my
pocket and missed a sovereign, a shilling, and a sixpence, which I had had
safe not a minute before, wrapped in a small piece of paper-I cannot
swear that I saw the prisoner.
23
ROBERT BRANSFORD ( police-constable M101.) I was on duty in Union.
street, in the Borough-I saw the prisoner lift the prosecutrix's dress about
a foot from the ground - saw bis hand in her pocket-hole I watched
him--one of his companions told him of it, and he left her I went and
spoke to her, and he disappeared-I took him about an hour afterwards.
Prisoner. I was going home that Saturday night, about eleven o'clock:
I passed by a crowd ; I was going home again, and the officer came and
said I had picked a lady's pocket.
JAMES BOCKHAM ( police-sergeant L 37.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction at the Surrey Sessions-(read-Convicted 18th
March, 1844, confined twenty-one days, solitary)-I know him to be the
person-he has been in custody five times besides. s
Guilty. Aged 17.--Transported for Ten Years.
2172. DAVID ROWELL was indicted for stealing 1 loaf of bread,
value 8d., the goods of Jonas Rowley, bis master, since deceased.
EMILY REBECCA ROWLEY. I resided with my father, Jonas Rowley-
he was a baker--I have been accustomed to transact his business-the
prisoner was in his employ-I kept an account of what the prisoner deli-
vered to Mrs. Freeman-I got the account from the prisoner-I copied it
in the waste book, and then it was copied in the day-book-on the 7th of
June the prisoner had one quartern loaf for Mrs. Freeman-it used not to
be paid for- it was booked-he ought to have delivered that one loaf and
he did not.

Cross-examined by Mr. HUDDLESTON. Q. You only recollect it from
the entry in the book ? A. I made the entry in the waste book ---my
father used to copy it in the ledger, and it is in the ledger now-it is from
seeing it in the book that I know I gave it bim on the 7th of June-I
could not charge my memory with it-the waste books were always de
stroyed as soon as they were filled up and entered in the ledger-I made
a great many entries in a day--the account used to be given to me after
the prisoner had taken the bread-Mrs. Freeman only had bread twice a
week--I went before the Magistrate on this case on the 25th of August -
nobody served in my father's shop but him and me-my mother was con-
fined.

book with the waste book, but I can, from my own memory, state I gave
