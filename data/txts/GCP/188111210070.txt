Cross-examined. I do not know in what part of the world Germany is,
I can't say whether it is in Scotland—I have never been to school-I
can read a little-I believed the cheque came from Germany, though
"London" is printed on it-I saw who it was signed by-I do not also
do a little in the betting line-1 generally go once a year to the Oaks
I have never had betting transactions with Kunz-Newmarket is my
native place-I saw a horse called Petronel there in the town last July
Meeting, but I do not think he ran at that meeting; that was the first
time I was there—I don't think I ever made a bet on Petronel-I never
had a bet with Kunz in my life, nor has he come and made bets for
other people—I was at Brighton Races once; I did not back White-
chapel there I have known Kunz six or seven months; he used to
come and have a glass of ale-this 131. 138. was my master's money-
the other cheques I changed for Kunz were always right-I never saw
Black before to my knowledge.
Cross-examined by Kuns. I did not give you 111. 118. only, but the
whole 131. 138.-it was on the 5th that you asked me to change a
cheque from Germany-you repaid me the 21. loan, and gave me 28. for
the Christmas goose club.
Re-examined. I change from 20 to 25 cheques every week-I had lent
Kunz 58. and 108. at a time, and the 21. belonged to me-I should not
have lent it to a man who was not a customer-it was not a betting
transaction.
GEORGE GOSLING (Police Inspector x). On 6th October, about 12.30,
I met Kunz in Paddington, and said "Mr. Kunz, there is a warrant
lying at the station for your apprehension for obtaining 131. 138. by false
pretences;" he said "I have been away, Mr. Gosling, for some time,
and when I came home my wife told me there had been a summons
brought, something about a cheque, but I did not get it in time to
attend the police-court. Billy Black owed me some money, and told me
it was all right, and I thought it was all right”-he said nothing about
Germany.
Cross-examined by Mr. GRAIN. Kunz did not say that he had borrowed
the eheque from Black-I believe a letter was received from Mr. Lewis
Lewis saying that he should surrender Black, and I believe he came
voluntarily to the station; I was not there.
Cross-examined by Kunz. You are very respectable, and have never
been charged with anything.

of the ledger made by myself. (Mr. GRAIN objected to this, as the Act
