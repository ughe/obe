519. CHARLES MILLARD was indicted for stealing 4 shillings and 2
sixpences; the moneys of John Bailey, from the person of Alfred Bailey.
ALFRED BAILEY, I am ten years old in Sept. ; I live with my father and
mother at 29, King-street, Smithfield. On 4th May I met the prisoner at
the corner of Farringdon-street-he said, "Have you got any money?"-I
said, "Yes"-I had four shillings and two sixpences-he said, "Shall I put
it into a piece of brown paper for you ?”– I said, "Yes," and gave it to him
- he gave me a paper which I thought contained the 58., and went away-I
went to a shop about five minutes after, and discovered it was only a penny
in the paper-I described the person to my mother and at the police station
--I saw him again the next evening in Fleet-street-I told my father, and he
had him locked up-he was not pointed out to me by anybody.
CAROLINE BAILEY. I am the last witness's mother. He came home and
told me what had happened, and described the person who bad the money-
I was going down to the station the same morning--I was looking about,
and saw the prisoner standing by the Sunday Times Office, and from what the
boy had said, I took a deal of notice of him, and while I was looking at him
he was gone.
Edwin HILLS (City policeman, 81). I took the prisoner in consequence
of information from the boy.
Prisoner. Q. Did not you ask the boy if I was the person, and he said he
did not know? A. I did not hear him say so—we were on the opposite side
of the way, and the boy pointed you out to me--I asked him if that was the
person, and he said, “That is him."
Nor GUILTY.

harbouring Taylor.
MR. LILLEY conducted the Prosecution.
