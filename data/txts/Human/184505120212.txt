1222. WILLIAM LEWIN was indicted for feloniously and knowingly uttering a forged receipt for money, with intent to defraud John Oakley .
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN OAKLEY. I am a black-lead manufacturer. The prisoner was in my employ as a packer—on the 11th of March I sent him to Mr. March, for some labels—he was to pay for them—I gave him a sovereign and j written order to get them—on the following morning he produced the labels with this bill and receipt, with Mr. March's name to it—I should have required a similar receipt to this from him on his return.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  What was the nature of the business he had to do? A.  To pack black-lead at my house—it was piece-work—I paid him every Saturday night—the course was to make out an account for the work he had done in the week.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Had he any right to appropriate this money for wages? A.  No; he brought his bill in every Saturday night, and was paid. JAMES MARCH. The prisoner came to me, on the 11th of March, and brought an order from his master, in consequence of which I delivered him 15,000 labels—I did not receive any money from him fort hem—I knew him as the servant of Mr. Oakley, as he had been before—the upper part of this bill is mine, "15,000 bills, 18 s . 9 d ." but this "Paid, J. March," is not mine, nor did I authorize anybody to write it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe it was paid shortly afterwards? A.  Yes, the prisoner called on the 4th of April, and paid it—he has been in the habit of getting things before on three or four occasions, and on one occasion he paid for some goods which he had had when he gave orders for others.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 3 7.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury.— Confined
   Two Years.
