Cross-examined. When I first heard of a claim being made on us I
wrote to Mr. Chilloott-Bobbett told me he had instructed Mr. Chillcott
in the matter-he denied it-he did not tell me he was guaranteeing Mr.
Chillcott's out-of-pocket expenses in the matter-Bobbett is in the same
line of business as myself-there was a case of Cox v. Bobbett-I have
had no litigation or law expenses with Bobbett-the prisoner said first
"Make it 301.”-I met Bobbett and the prisoner together on Friday, the
18th–I did not suggest to the prisoner at that time that if he got Spong
to accept 201. I would make him a present-I said on the Monday “If
you can settle the matter I will give you a trifle"—the sum at which it
was to be settled was not mentioned as 201. - the trifle was not men-
tioned as 51.- I did not say I would if my brother consented-I said I
would consult him-257. was to cover all costs, including the trifle to the
prisoner-I think Bobbett heard that arrangement-the prisoner did not
call my attention to the fact that I had includod his trifle in the com-

croes the cheque-I should not have given him a cheque in his own
name—he asked me to make it out in Mr. Chillcott's name-51. was not
mentioned between the prisoner and me as a trifle-I did not say "If
you can settle this matter for 201.”—201. was only mentioned when I
offered him 201., and he said “Make it 307." —it was not then I said I
would give him a trifle-I think he said "I hope you won't forget the
little present you promised me " -I said "I will give you a cheque for
257. to cover everything "-201. was to be given to Spong, and the other 51.
was to pay the law expenses-he told me Mr. Chillcott would have three
guineas - as I understood 251. was to pay all the costs and the prisoner
-I have seen Bobbett here to-day-I don't know if he heard all the
conversation; he was present.
Ro-examined. When I gave the cheque on the Saturday nothing was
mentioned about a trifle to Jewell—he accepted the 25l, and gave the
receipt, and I gave him the 251.then he asked me to give him the
little present which I had promised him, and I said No; I think you
have done pretty well"-he then said he was going to give Spong 201.
and 5l. was for the expenses—if I had known Spong had not put his
mark on this receipt, and knew nothing about it, I would not have given
my cheque for 251.
