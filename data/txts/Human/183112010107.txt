   ROBERT FORD. I am the prosecutor's son. I saw the prisoner and another man come into my father's room, one Monday night; I was looking out at my father's window, and said, "Is that you father?" the prisoner said, "I am not your father, but I want to speak to you;" I went as far as the end of the street, and then I said, "I must not go any farther;" we came back, and they both went up stairs; the other man pretended to be looking at my cap, and he went behind my father's shelf, and filled his pockets with shute; the prisoner then came up - they whispered together, and this prisoner put the bobbins in his hat, with silk on them - then he took a handkerchief out of his pocket, put shute into it, and tied them up; I said, "Oh, don't take my father's shute, I shall get murdered" - he said, "The more you halloo, the more I'll take;" I said, "Murder! thieves! thieves! murder! they have got my father shute;" they then run away - when the prisoner had got two or three doors off, I saw the officer take him; the bobbins were found at the foot of the stairs.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you lose sight of me? A. No; when you ran down stairs I looked out of window and saw you - I am sure he is the man that took my father's shute.
   GEORGE DEVEREUX FORD. I am the witness' father. I went out on the 24th of November, but I left my work safe, and when I came home I missed nine bobbins of shute; I found three of them at the foot of the stairs, at the bottom of the passage - the other six are lost; they are worth 24s. or 25s. - if the thief had not been found I should have lost my character for ever.
 COURT. Q. Did you know the prisoner before? A. I never knew him before.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had a pint or two of beer at the corner of the said street, and as I was going past I heard the two boys call out; I went into the entry to hear what was the matter, and a man rushed out in his shirt sleeves, he took me, and said, "You have been at the work;" I said, "You are mistaken;" he went to get his clothes on, and the patrol took me.
   BENJAMIN CRISPIN. I took hold of him at the foot of the stairs, and he dropped the bundle where it was found.
     GUILTY. Aged 42. - Transported for Seven Years.
 240.  JAMES YOUNG was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 5s. the goods of Charles Fentum , from his person.
   CHARLES FENTUM. On the 9th of November I was on Westminster-bridge- I did not feel any thing taken, but the officer spoke to me; I missed my handkerchief, which the officer had.
   JAMES WILD. I am a Police-officer. I had watched the prisoner for nearly half an hour - there were one or two more with him; they were trying several gentlemen's pockets - I saw the prisoner take this handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket; I seized him, and took it from him, the other got away.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me take it from the prosecutor's pocket? A. Yes, and I took it from your waistcoat.(Property produced and Sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 241.  ROBERT BUTLER was indicted for bigamy.
   MARY ANN ELDRIDGE. I know the prisoner - he married my sister Elizabeth Eldridge; she is alive, and is here; I was present at their marriage, at St. George's church in the Borough, on the 17th of April, 1820.
   SAMUEL PRENDERGAST. I am an officer of Lambeth-street. I produce the certificate of the first marriage - I examined it with the book, and found it correct - (read); I took the prisoner in Gravel-lane.
   JOHN PAUL. I am clerk of All Souls', St. Marylebone, and have the register of marriages. On the 28th of September, 1825. Robert Butler, batchelor, and Elizabeth Swinford, widow, were married in that church - I believe the prisoner to be the man.
   MARIA CORNISH. I know Elizabeth Swinford; I was in the church, and saw them married - the prisoner is the man; I did not see them again till I saw them over the water - I had known Elizabeth Swinford for some time - her first husband's name was Joseph Swinford.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Had you known the prisoner before? A. Yes, between eleven and twelve years.
 COURT TO MARY ANN ELDRIDGE. Q. Did you know any thing of the second wife? A.Sir, I did not.
 Cross-examined. Q.Had you been in the daily habit of seeing the prisoner after his marriage with your sister? A. Yes; my sister had a child in about eleven months; they did not live together above two years - a man named Cain has been acquainted with my sister; I have seen them together - I have not visited them: they have visited me; I do not know that my sister ran away to live with Cain, and that Cain came and thrashed the prisoner to make him keep the child - nor that my sister was taken before a Magistrate by the prisoner for ill-treatring him - or that the prisoner was turned out of his house, and all his working-tools taken from him by Cain, at the instigation of my sister - I have seen children at Cain's; I do not know that they were my sister's but they passed as such. GUILTY. Aged 32.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. - Confined 1 Month.
