331. JOHN CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of Dec., 1
handkerchief, value 58., the goods of Edward" Lennox Boyd, from his
person,
Robert Ellis. I live with Messrs. Relph and Co., Cornhill. On the
afternoon of the 28th of Dec. I was in Cheapside, and say the prisoner
draw a handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket-he then crossed the
road, and passed it to another person-I told the prosecutor.
EDWARD Lennox Boyd. I live in Waterloo-place, Pallomal]. This is
my handkerchief-I had it in my pocket on the 28th of Dec.—I did not
perceive anything, but Ellis spoke to me in Cheapside-I followed him,
and he pointed out the prisoner-I saw my handkerchief in another man's
hat-I seized the hat, and took it-that man said it was not him-I let
him go, and ran after the prisoner, and caught him-I had had my hand-
kerchief in my pocket about a minute before,
Prisoner's Defence. This gentleman ran after another man, and topk
the handkerchief from his hat; he then ran, and took me; that is all
know.
Guilty. Aged 20.-- Recommended to mercy by the Jury, Confined

332. EDWARD MORGAN and GEORGE CLARK were indieted for
stealing, on the 18th of Dec., 1 boot, value 58, the goods of John Balli
and 1 pair of boots, value 10s., the goods of William Stocker i and that
· Morgan had been before convicted of felony,
ROBERT Page, I liye at Mr. Hodgkinson's, a schoolmaster. On Wed-
nesday afternoon, the 18th of Dec,, I was going to Kilburn to take some
clothes—I had one boot belonging to Mr. Bull, which had been given me
to take to Mr. Turner's to be repaired, and I had two boots of Mr. Stocker's
to carry to the shoemaker's at Kilburn -I met the prisoners, and three other
persons with them-Morgan took my cap off my head-I turned round,
and looked back, and then I saw Clark taking one other boot from my
barrow-he pretended to throw it away, and then put it under his arm
they ran off about twenty yards, then one of them came back-he took
another boot, and they all ran off together-I lost all the three boots-I
found my cap lying on the ground jast behind-these are the boots.
FRANCIS DENNIS. I live at Kilburn, and am a shoemaker. On the
Wednesday when these boots were lost I saw Page in the evening, and the
next morning I saw the prisoners together passing slowly along-my sus.
picions were excited, and I watched them to Mr, Hancock's plantation
Clark entered the plantation, and Morgan stood outside person came
in the same direction, and disturbed them-Clark came out of the planta-
tion-he and Morgan went up the road together I went into the planta-
tion, and found these three boots exactly where Clark had been.
GEORGE Brown. I am gardener to Mr. Hancock. About nine o'clock
that morning I saw both the prisoners in the plantation I took Morgan
into custody, and Holloway took Clark I asked what they did there
they said they were looking for a blackbird.
