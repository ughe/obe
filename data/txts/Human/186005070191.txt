 486. ANN GROVES  (60) , Feloniously uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. POLAND conducted the Prosecution. HENRY ANDREWS( Police-sergeant, 30 B ). I produce a certificate of a conviction—( Read: "Central Criminal Court, January, 1859. Ann Groves, convicted of uttering counterfeit coin, and Confined nine months. ")—the prisoner is the person. WILLIAM INSELL. I keep the Sun and Dove public-house in Cold Har bour Lane—on Thursday evening, 29th March, the prisoner and Leary came to my house together—I served them half a quartern of rum, and Leary gave me a bad shilling—I said, "This is a bad one"—Leary said, "Perhaps you will give it me back again"—I said, "No; I never give bad money back, the best thing you can do is to be off"—the prisoner then gave me a good sixpence to pay for the half-quartern of rum—she said she did not think her friend would bring her in there to treat her with half a quartern of rum, and give a bad shilling—they left in company, and I kept the shilling separate till I gave it to the constable. GEORGE WOOLLETT. I am a nurseryman, and live in Cold Harbour Lane—on the evening of 29th March, the prisoner and Leary came to my shop—they wanted to buy a couple of plants that were in the window—the price was 8 d .—the prisoner gave me a shilling—I put it in my pocket—I had no other there—my wife gave her 4 d . change, and they went out with the plants—soon afterwards the constable came in, and from what he said, I took the shilling out of my pocket—I thought it was a good one and I told the constable so, and he went away—the same night I gave it to my son to buy some corn—I had kept it separate from other money—my son brought it back to me and said I had given him a bad shilling—I examined it, and found it was bad—I wrapped it in a piece of paper and kept it by itself—I afterwards gave it to the constable.
   Prisoner.  I did not give you the shilling.
   Witness.  Yes; you took it from under your shawl and gave it me.
   Prisoner.  You did not say before that I gave it you.
