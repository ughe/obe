I am the wife of Jonathan Sherman Waller, a chand ler and shoemaker, in Thames-street, Greenwich. On the 10th of April, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, Mary Ann Bedwell came for a penny candle—she paid me a sixpence, and I gave her 5 d . change—I put the sixpence on the shelf—there was nothing with it—in two or three minutes afterwards, Home came for a penny egg—I said I had not got one—I gave him a three-farthing one—he paid for it with a sixpence—I gave him 5 1/4 d . change—I sent my little girl to fetch her father, and gave him both the sixpences together. JONATHAN SHERMAN WALLER. On the 10th of April, I received two sixpences from my wife, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening—I sent one double, and went up the street—I told the policeman, there were some men passing bad money—I went back to my shop with him, and marked the sixpences, and gave them to him. THOMAS HICKLINO( police-constable R  118.) I received two sixpences from Mr. Jonathan Sherman Waller—these are them—( producing them )—I received one sixpence from Mrs. Moore—this is it—( producing another )—I took the prisoner into custody—I found on him one farthing—that is all. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—these three sixpences are all counterfeit, and two of them were cast in the tame mould—the other is not.
   Prisoner's Defence . I was at home at the time the children swear I gave them the sixpences—I buy and tell fish about the town—when the policeman came to me, I went with him—the Inspector would not book the charge—he told me to come next morning, and the policeman came to me, and I went—it is not likely that I should go about the town, where I get my living, and pass bad money. GUILTY.*—Aged 21. Confined Two Years.

 1390. WILLIAM ELLIS and WILLIAM CALLY were indicted for stealing, on the   16th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  ., the goods of Charles Graham Marshall , from his person. GEORGE THORNTON( police-constable E  91.) On the 16th of April, I was on duty in Greenwich fair—I saw the prisoners, about six o'clock in the evening, trying several gentlemen's pockets—I was with another con stable—I saw Cally take from the prosecutor this silk handkerchief, and hand it to Ellis—he was about putting it into his breast, when Dowsing took him, and I took Cally—I spoke to the prosecutor, who identified it—he stated before the Magistrate that his name was Charles Graham Mar shall, and signed the charge as such—he goes by that name at his lodging—he signed his name on the sheet, "Charles Graham Marshall," and answered to that name—I searched Cally at the station-house, and found another handkerchief round his leg, and a pair of gloves in his pocket—I called at the prosecutor's lodgings, and find he is gone to France, and not expected to return for three years.
