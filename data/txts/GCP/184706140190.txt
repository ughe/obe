Philip KESTER. I am housekeeper at the Royal Hotel. There are two
houses, Nos. 6 and 2-I am housekeeper at No. 2--the prisoner staid there
some time, and then went into lodgings- I recollect a fire taking place near
her lodging-I went to her shortly afterwards-- I took some keys with me,
and tried to open a trunk--she had lost her own keys--I cannot recollect
when the fire took place-it was some days before the Saturday on which
this matter happened-I recollect sending my wife to the prisoner on the
Saturday evening with a chain--she had told me four days before, that she would
go to Bristol-she did not know what day she should go-she told me on the
Wednesday to send the chain, and she said something about it on the Saturday
morning between eleven and twelve o'clock.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Where did you take the chain from? 4. Out
of pawn between seven and nine o'clock in the evening paid the money
from my own pocket, 21. 168. 3d.-1 did not owe the prisoner any money-1
lent her the money because I read her letters she received from Germany,
and I took her for & respectable lady-I thought I should get my money
with interest - I did not take a receipt from her the pawnbroker's is in
Fleet-street-I do not know the No.- I took the chain to the hotel, and gave
it to my wife to take to the prisoner-I was afraid to take it to her myself
-I must not go out of the house without permission, because my wife
cannot speak English.
CONSTANCE KEYSER. I am proprietor of the Royal Hotel. There are two
houses, No. 2, and No. 6; one of them is kept by Philip Kester-I recollect
the prisoner coming there in March-she came recommended--I do not admit
customers generally without they are recommended-while she was there, she
was in the habit of diving at the table d'hôte almost every day, and she continued
to do so four or five times a week after she left-she conducted herself very
respectably indeed--these articles of jewellery are hers----she was always sit-
ting beside me, and I recollect them all very well-- I recollect Mr. Bloom of
Bristol-he is her uncle-she was preparing to leave my house on the 5th of
June to go Bristol,
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You would have lent her this money? 4. Yes;
of course, and a deal more--it was not very strange that she should apply to a
waiter--my waiters are in the habit of lending money sometimes, and I am
myself-the prisoner's name is Fredericka Asgé in my book I always called
her "Madam' -I considered her to be married.
MR. PARNELL. Q. You have kept the hotel some time? 4. About four
years- it is not unusual for persorts who keep foreign hotels to lend money
to their customers, if they want it--they come very often with foreign money
to the house -- the prisoner always paid.
