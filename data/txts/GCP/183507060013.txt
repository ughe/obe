Ninth Session, 1835.
WILLIAM MORTON. I am in the employ of the Provident Savings'
Bank, at St. Martin-in-the-fields. I produce the book of deposits-I
have an entry corresponding with the entry in this book (reads) “ 71,640,
Ann Smith, married, 6, Baltic-street, Old-street, 6 April, 1835, £30;" it is
signed by Ann Smith-I have another entry signed by George Smith,
giving authority to his wife to pay monies, or to draw them I do not know
either of the prisoners.
Carson
GEORGE LLOYD. I live in Britannia-street, City-road. I know both
the prisoners--I lived with them for four years I have seen them write
frequently, this entry of Ann Smith resembles the handwriting of Ann
Smith, and I believe it to be hers; and this signature, “George Smith," is
very like the man's writing; it exactly resembles what I have seen-I
think it is his.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Are you the gentleman who
went away in arrears? 4. I had missed several things out of my room,
and in consequence of that I rejected paying my rent at that moment, but
I am willing to pay my rent-I know Mrs. Mully, but I do not know her
handwriting.

John Lovick. I am assistant to a pawnbroker, in Bath-street.. I have

ROBERT ROBERTS. I live at Manchester, and am a sawyer. The de-
ceased, Henry Roberts, was my brother-I saw him alive twelve months
ago last May--I had not seen him before for fifty-four years- I came to
town on Saturday, the 4th of last April-I went to Mr. Hyde, who had
sent me two letters, and he told me to go to Baltie-street-I found & will
and a bit of paper besides I have since taken out letters of administration
-my brother had told me he was worth a good bit of brass--I saw the
officers find these scissors and things.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. You wanted your brother to
come and live with you? A. Yes; but he said he was very well taken
care of by Mrs. Smith-I wanted some brass of him, and he said he would
go and see if he could get me some, and he went up stairs and brought me
some down.

SARAH ROBERTS. I came up with my husband in consequence of the
death of his brother-I was at Worship-street office, and as I was going
down the stairs, the prisoner, Mrs. Smith, said to me, “Mrs. Roberts, I
beg you a hundred pardons, I hope you will be merciful to me; I am
willing to give my share up, if Mrs. Mully will give hers up" when she
said that, the officer came up and said, "Go on."

Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you examined as
witness ? A. Yes; the examination was over then-I did not go back and
tell the Magistrate this, as I thought it was not my duty-I had not gone
out of the office, I was going out I was not in the same room with the
Magistrate : we were going down stairs--the officer came up I suppose he
beard something of this I cannot tell how near he was; whether he was
within one yard, or five yards, or a hundred yards.
Mr. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you go before the Justice again that day?
4. No; I was examined to tell what I knew after this conversation, but
I did not tell it-the officer was coming along when Mrs. Smith said this,
and he said, “ Get on."
