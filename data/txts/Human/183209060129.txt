 2034.  RICHARD COLLINS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , at St. Martin in the Fields, 3 coats, value 4l.; 2 1/4 yards of cloth, value 1l.; 4 pairs of trousers, value 3l., and 1 bag, value 1s., the goods of John Walter , in his dwelling house.
 THOMAS MOORE. I am in the employ of John Walter, a  tailor, at No. 415, Strand, in the parish of St. Martin in the Fields. On Wednesday, the 25th of July, about ten minutes to eight o'clock, I was opening his shop, and as I took the shutters round into the court - I did not see the prisoner go into our shop, but he came out with four pairs of trousers, three coats, and two yards and a quarter of blue cloth under his right arm; he walked out; but when he saw me running after him he ran up New Exchange-court, and threw them down - I still followed him, laid hold of his coat, and kept him till a gentleman came; he lifted his hand, and attempted to strike me, but never got away - the things are worth more than 8l.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I throw the things down before you came up to me? A. Yes; he dropped them, and ran up the court - I caught him in Heathcock-court, about a dozen yards from the Strand - it is a throughfare.
   FRANCIS LITTLE. I am a Policeman. I was called in when the prisoner was taken - I took him into custody in New Exchange-court, at a public-house door, and found the clothes.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming down the court, and met the young man; he charged me with the robbery - I said he could go and fetch a Policeman; I stopped there five minutes while he went, and if guilty of a robbery I should not have stopped.
   THOMAS MOORE. A man held him while I went.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Life.
 2035.  CHARLES AITKEN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , 36 knives, value 2l. 18s. 6d.; 3 pencil-cases, value 24s.; 1 opera-glass and case, value 27s., and 1 ivory tablet, value 4s. 6d., the goods of Charles Essex , his master, in his dwelling-house; and WILLIAM DAYTON was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   CHARLES ESSEX. I am a dealer in cutlery, and live in Well-street, Gray's Inn-lane, in the parish of St. Pancras- the prisoner Aitkin was my errand-boy. On Saturday, the 11th of August, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I told him to put up the shutters of the warehouse and fasten all the doors; I had a Policeman up stairs, and when the prisoner got on the step of my door I gave him in charge; he was brought back and searched - there were found on him nine two bladed horn handled knives, two with buck-born handles, and one with an ivory handle; they were mine, and worth 18s. 7 1/2d.; no threat or promise was made to him - when he was searched, he said, "Oh, Sir! I hope you will have mercy on me;" I said, "Policeman, do your duty;" he cried a great deal and begged for mercy - the Policeman said,"You young rascal, is this the way you rob your master, what were you going to do with the property?" he said he was going to take it to John Neale and Lipman, who were to be in the first box in the Union gardens, on the right-hand side out of the Bagnigge-wells-road.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. The property must have been taken at different times? A. Certainly; I have every reason to believe he had been robbing me for eighteen months; he told me what he had done with my property - I had information from a Policeman named Baylis, letter D 63.
 Q. Have you not reason to believe the prisoner has been led away by other persons? A. No, I think he is naturally depraved.
 COURT. Q. Has he given you information by which you have found your property? A. A small portion of it.
   NATHANIEL BROWN. I am a pawnbroker. I have a tablet and pencil-case, which were pawned by Dayton - he told me he pawned them for his father.
   LUIS WHITE. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pencilcase - I do not know who pawned it - it was a boy.
   RICHARD HOWARD. I have a silver pencil-case, pawned by a boy - I cannot say who.
   JOHN EDWARD NEALE. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have an opera-glass pawned for 7s. 6d. in the name of Lewis - I have no recollection who it was.
 MR. ESSEX. The prisoner sent me a letter giving information, by which I recovered about 4l. 10s. 2d. worth of property - my loss is upwards of 80l.
   ROBERT BAILEY. I had known th prisoner about three weeks previous to this.
 Cross-examined. Q. You gave the officer information, I believe? A. Yes, I have not had a great deal of property from him; I was never tried - I am errand-boy to Mr. Wilton, of Pearle-street, Bagnigge-wells; I became acquainted with the prisoner through  Joseph Neale, who was taken up - I was in custody about four years ago, at Hatton-garden, for pulling down some haycocks at Highbury; that is the only time I was ever in custody.
 COURT. Q. Did you receive any thing from the prisoner? A. Yes, a dozen knives, which I gave up to Baylis, and gave information.
   ROBERT BAYLIS. I am a Policeman. I received a dozen knives from Bailey, who gave me information - I took Aitken into custody, and found this property on him.
 AITKEN - GUILTY of stealing to the value of 99s. only. Aged 16. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 DAYTON -    NOT GUILTY.
