NEW COURT .— Wednesday, Oct.  27 th , 1847.
   PRESENT  —Mr. Alderman GIBBS ; Mr. Alderman HUGHES ; and Mr. COM  MON SERJEANT.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 2312. JOHN ARMSTRONG was indicted for stealing 11 walking-sticks, value 2 s  . 4 d  .; the goods of William Arpthorp ; to which he pleaded GUILTY . Aged 14.— Confined Three Months .
 2313. GEORGE LANGFORD was indicted for stealing 1 box, value 2 s  ., and 2 compass-springs, 1 l  .; the goods of Henry Barrow , his master; to which he pleaded GUILTY . Aged 19.— Confined Three Months
 2314. HENRY OWEN was indicted for stealing 1 patent log, value 4 l  .; the goods of the General Screw Steam-shipping Company , in a port of entry and discharge; to which he pleaded GUILTY . Aged 21.— Confined Three Months .
 2315. CHARLES HENRY GAZLEY was indicted for stealing 300 yards of velvet, value 18 l  .; the goods of Charles Edward Dawson and another, his masters. HENNRY JOHN COLLETT. I carry on business at No. 39, Friday-street, and am agent to Charles Edward Dawson and another—the prisoner was my assistant. I missed fourteen ends of velvet—I have seen some of them since—here is one with a mark and number on it—it has been sold from Man chester, but was returned to me—I had not sold it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q.  To whom had it been sold? A.  To Messrs. Crocker of Watling-street—I do not carry on business myself—I am agent to Messrs. Dawson, and to others—I have missed these pieces at different times—I take stock from month to month—here is one piece I can positively swear to, No. 18,980—the others I have no doubt are mine—there are nine ends produced altogether—the prisoner purchased one piece of claret velvet himself, and two pieces of black—these others are in papers with our marks on—this one had been in a paper like this, and had the mark erased—this is the piece I can swear to—I would not swear to the others where there is the slightest possibility of a mistake. GEORGE KING. I am a pawnbroker. I took in three of these ends of velvets—they were not pawned by the prisoner. GEORGE KILLINGHAM. I am a pawnbroker. I produce six pieces of these velvets—one of them is the piece that is sworn to.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who pawned them? A.  A man about forty-five years old, not the prisoner. JOSEPH THOMPSON( policeman, F  62.) I took the prisoner—I went to his father's and received from his father a duplicate of one of these pieces of velvet—I have also a duplicate which I received form Frederick Price. FREDERICK PRICE. I know the prisoner—he gave me two duplicate to sell for him—this produced is one of them.
