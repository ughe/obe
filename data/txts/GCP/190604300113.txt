SULLIVAN, John (21, porter), and COLMY, William.
Robbery with violence on John Sennett, and stealing from him
15s. 9d. in money.
Mr. Cundy prosecuted; Mr. Huntly Jenkins defended.
JOHN SENNETT, Greenwich Road, Deptford. I am a traveller
in timber. On April 20 I met Colmy, who was a kind of
casual acquaintance; I had often been employed' on the same
ship with him. He knew that I had been in trouble and had
been three years in prison. I had money in my pocket that
I had received that day from solicitors for making private in-
quiries. Just before seven I alighted from a "bus at Dock-
head; I saw Colmy standing outside the "Feathers"; "he said,
"Hullo, old man, how are you?" I had been suffering from
a severe cold, and I had had a drop of rum. Colmy invited
me to have a drink. I went in and had one, and shortly after-
wards lost all consciousness: there was something done to the
drink. At the Feathers "I saw Grant, a man I had a slight
acquaintance with, and a lady he described as his wife. Sullivan
came in later. From the "Feathers” I went to the "Marguig
