WILLIAM COUSINS (Sergeant B). At 9.30 on the evening of 3rd Sept.
I was in Beulah Hill, Norwood -I met Bastow and said "I believe your
name is Bastow "-he said “Yes”-I said "I am a sergeant of police,
I shall take you in custody for being concerned with Regan in stealing
six cows on 24th October, from Mr. Arnold, of Jersey”-he replied “Oh
dear, I had nothing to do with it; Regan brought two of the cows to my
place, and he paid me 108. a week each for their keep, which I can show in
black and white, so that I can wash my hands clean of having any trans-
action; you do surprise me "-going to the Lower Norwood railway.
station, he said "I will tell you this, I don't care who knows it, the night
the cows arrived at Waterloo Station Rogan came home ill, and asked me
if I would go to the station and fetch them for him, which I did, and had
them conveyed to Pimlico; I wished I had never had the two at my
plaee; Regan lodged with me, and paid me two quid a week”-on 1st
December I went with Marshall to a cowshed on Bastow's premises, The
Poplars, and took possession of two rugs, which Arnold identified as
having been his and sent with the cows.
WILLIAM MORLEY FRENCH. I am clerk to Messrs. Wontner and Sons,
solicitors, and agents to the Public Prosecutor in this matter--Marshall
handed me a large bundle of papers, books and documents I have found
no leases of land in America-Mr. Barnes's (the solicitor for both prisoners)
clerk has had an inspection of the documents I found a diary there.
(The witness read several pages of this diary, in which were entries as to God.
frey's and Arnolds cattle, the entries being to the same effect as the evidence
given by witnesees. MR. MBAD read two letters from Colonel Dennis concerning
the ranches.)
