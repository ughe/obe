MCCARTHY, Thomas (29, baskes maker), pleaded guilty of steal.
ing one gold chain and one gold coin, the goods of George Finch, from
h's person.
This occurred in Bishopsgate Street, at the corner of Catherine
Wheel Alley, and was a case of wateh-snatching. Prosecutor followed
prisoner, who was stopped by a railway constable. On the way to
the station he was observed to throw something away, but a £2 piece
which had been attached to the chain was found on him. Previous
convictions were proved, and on one occasion he was ordered 20
lashes.
Prisoner by way of extenuation, said he was one of those unfortu-
Date men who were foroed to steal, having no friends, and being un-
able to obtain employment. Of the last 13 years he had spent 12 in
prison.
Sentence: Five years' penal servitude.
CARPENTER, Thomas (23, labourer), pleaded guilty of feloniously
marrying Sarah Margaret Andrews, his wife being then alive.
Prisoner's wife, it appeared, had left him, and he took to live with
him Margaret Andrews, with whom he went through the form of
marriage on April 8 of this year, she knowing that he was married.
Prisoner was taken into custody for assault, having and a quarrel on
Easter Monday with some relatives, who stated at the station that he
had committed bigamy, and this on inquiry turned out to be true.
The Recorder, in sentencing prisoner to two days' imprisonment,
entitling him to be discharged, said that this case illustrated what he
had often had occasion to remark, that few crimes differed so much
as bigamy. In the case of Lavender (p. 22) there was every circum-
stance of aggravation. But for the disturbance on Easter Monday,
probably nothing would have been heard of this case.
GONVILLE, Charles (19, clerk), pleaded guilty of maliciously
damaging one plate-glass window, value £9 10s., the property of
the Aerated Bread Company, Limited.
Sentence, 12 months' imprisonment, with recommendation for treat
ment under the Borstal system.
