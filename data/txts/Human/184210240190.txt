 3029. JOHN WILSON was indicted for a misdemeanor. JOHN TANNER. I keep an outfitter's shop in the Minories. The pri soner came and represented himself to keep a tailor's and outfitter's shop—he said he had to get a chest of drawers and a washing-stand for Captain Clark—that he lived at No. 6, Lincoln's Inn-fields, and we were to make the drawers—he said he was making clothes for Captain Clark—I said I should like to see Captain Clark, in case he would like any alteration—he came again in a day or two, with a drawing of the drawers and a signa ture—I got on with them, and he ordered me to send them a week after wards—believing that he had an order from Captain Clark, I sent these things—I have inquired, and found the prisoner keeps a cabinet-maker's shop in Lincoln's Inn-fields, not a tailor's and outfitter's—I have not found Captain Clark. WILLIAM WORFORD. I am a blacksmith, living at Croydon. I was employed by the prosecutor to carry the drawers on the 14th of Septem ber to No. 6, Lincoln's Inn-fields—it was a lawyer's chambers—I made Inquiries, and could find no such person—I returned with the drawers, and on the 16th I went there, and the prosecutor said, "I have sent two boys with the drawers; I want you to go and see them delivered safe"—I went, and saw one of the boys with a truck—the prisoner was pushing behind—he said, "How is it you did not bring them up last Wednesday?"—I said, "I was up here, but could not find you"—he said, "It is up here, and it was No. 6, Bear-yard—there was no shop at No. 6, Lincoln's Inn-fields. WILLIAM PRIEST. I live in the Strand. The prisoner came and asked if I purchased any articles—he said he was a cabinet-maker—I and I did, but did not want any—he called again, and said he had an article at the house in Bear-yard, and I bought these drawers of him for 5 l .
   Prisoner's Defence.  I had orders from Captain Clark for a chest of drawers; Mr. Tanner sent them home; they were not made to order, and the gen tleman would not have them. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Nine Months.
