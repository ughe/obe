A,  lam—I gave the same testimony—Shakleford was the only person then indicted. ( See Eleventh Session, page  1105.) JOHN WELLS. I am counting-house clerk at Mr. Milbourn's—Cameron introduced Shackleford to me by the name of Beaumont, and gave me this card—there were some references given to me—I remarked they were not trade references, and Shackleford remarked he was sorry he could not give trade references, as he was only now commencing business—among other parties referred to was one of the name of Bachelard—I went to him—I found him in an office at the back of the Mansion House, an office up one pair of stairs, with several clerks in it—it was called the "Cork Stuffing Company"—when I inquired of Bachelard, he said he had known him seve ral years, he was a highly respectable young man, he knew he had 700 l . of his own, and he had lately married a young woman with considerable pro perty, which could be made available if necessary—that he had houses in Sloane-street, and if he were us he would trust him to any amount—I went from there to Mr. Ingram—he told me he had had the same refer ences that I had, and he had trusted him to the amount of 150 l ., and he did not think we should do wrong in trusting him likewise—I then went to Mr. Earp, in Fleet-street—I got a good character of him there, though I did not see Mr. Earp, and on that I permitted the prisoner to have goods—on the evening of the 16th of May I went to the Swan-with-two-Necks with the first parcel of goods, which consisted principally of silks—I asked for Beaumont, and Shackleford appeared—he was in the coffee-room—he paid me 30 l . in cash, as agreed when he bought the goods—I went the next evening to the Swan-with-two-Necks again—I saw him again, and he paid me 25 l . on account of another parcel of goods left the same evening—I then saw him as Mr. Beaumont—on the 1st of June Shackleford called at the warehouse again, and he then paid me 35 l ., which was for some goods sold the day before—on the 6th of July I received 14 l . 8 s . from Shackelford, and he wanted more goods, but Mr. Milbourn refused to let him have them unless he paid for them—there was a bill drawn on him for 122 l . 7 s ., which has been dishonoured—the balance now due to us, is 174 l . 16 s . 7 d .—Freeborn took the goods at all times, and I went with him.
