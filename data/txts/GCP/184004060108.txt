1153. CATHERINE M'GINN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th
of March, 1 cash-box, value 38. ; 3 sovereigns, 4 half-crowns, 7 shillings,
4 sixpences, 1 bill of exchange, value 401. 98. 3d.; 1 other bill of exchange,
value 611. 8s. 2d.; and 1 other bill of exchange, value 191. 12s.; the pro-
perty of Ebenezer Fox, her master, in his dwelling-house.
EBENEZER Fox. I am a colourman, and live at No. 50, Old Compton.
street. At the time in question I lived in Frith-street, in the parish of St. Ann
Westminster-the prisoner was my servant of all work for between two and
three months on Sunday the 15th of March I went out about half-past
six o'clock, leaving my cash-box in a drawer in my bed-room-she had
access to the room, and had seen me every evening take my cash-box up
stairs—it contained 3 sovereigns, 19 shillings, in silver, a bill for 611. 86,
one for 401. 98., one for 191. 108., a great number of papers, and a 11. forged
note-I saw them safe on Saturday night about twelve o'clock the drawer
was not locked, but the box was- I went out at half-past six o'clock-came
back about half-past eight, and met Hawkins at my door-in consequence of
what be said I went up stairs and called the prisoner down-she was up in
her bed-room-I asked her what she had been doing she said, “Oh, Mr.
Fox, I have broke open your tin box" I asked what she had done with
the papers and contents-she said, “Come up stairs, I will show you; the
devil tempted me at the moment, and you can do what you like with me"
-I went up and found the cash-box replaced, but broken open, and all the
contents, except 1 half-crown in it—the forged note was not there.
RICHARD HAWKINS. I am a porter, and live in Phenix-street, SL
Giles's—I have known the prisoner about two months. On Sundas
night she came to my lodging at a quarter before seven o'clock,
and wished me to go to the London and Birmingham railway with her,
as she expected to meet her cousin there, I went with her, as I was to
carry her box, but we did not meet him there—while we were waiting for
the train coming in she shook some silver from her bosom I saw her pick
up two or three shillings--when we were coming away she showed mes
Bank-note, with “forged" printed on it in four different places---
afterwards went into a coffee-shop-she had a good many papers there
one was respecting two houses near Bridport-street, Marylebone we left the
coffee-shop-she made a full stop, and said the devil a foot further would
she go towards her master's house-I asked her for what reason, and she
told me and my wife that she had broken open her master's tin-box-I
walked on, and she walked with my wife-I went right up to Mr. Fox's
house-he was coming in with his family from church, and I told him this.
Henky Dopp. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and have the
cash-box-I did not search her--we could not find the forged note.
