COOPER PLEADED GUILTY.-Confined Two Years each.
MESSRS. POLAND and MR. J. O'CONNELL conducted the Prosecution, and
MR. LILLEY defended BURKE.
DOROAS BUCKINGHAM. I am the wife of John Buckingham, a butcher, of
Surrey-place, Wandsworth-road-on 23d June, Thomas came for 1 lb. of
steak, and gave me a bad half-crown I looked at it, and gave it to my
husband, who chopped it; but before that, Hewlett came in and said some-
thing to me.
ALFRED HEWLETT. I am a furniture-packer, of 13, Southampton-street,
Nine Elms-on 230 June, about 7 o'clock, I was at the corner of the Nine
Elms with a young man, and saw the prisoners together-Burke had a
handful of money in his left hand-he passed one piece to Cooper, and
another piece to Thomas--I went to the station, and spoke to the sergeant-
before that I saw Thomas go into Buckingham's, the butcher's—I was on the
other side of the way- I went over, and stood at the door till he put the
money down, and then I went in, and saw the half-crown chopped—I went
out, and found Sergeant Coltman, and then saw Burke and Cooper by the
toll-gate—when they saw me, they walked on towards & new road where
some building was going ou— I followed them, put my hand on Burke, and said,
“You have got some bad money; I have been watching you this last hour"
- he said, "Me! that I have not "-Cooper said, "I will smash your nose
in for you ;" and then put his hand in his pocket, took out the money
wrapped in paper, and threw it over the wall-I saw some of it fall out, and
scatter- constable came up, and I pointed out the place to him-Hayes,
a constable, lived there I saw him over the wall.
