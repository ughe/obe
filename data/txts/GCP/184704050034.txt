Feb. the prisoner came into my shop, about nine o'clock in the evening-he
asked for some calico- cut him off three yards and a half-it came to Is. 3d.
- he put down a shilling and three penny pieces- I saw the shilling was bad
- he then asked the price of some handkerchiefs, and said he should want
some in a day or two, and he would come in again-I said, "You know this
is a bad shilling which you gave me"-he said, "God bless me, is it?"- he took
out three good shillings, and gave me one-I broke the bad one in two-one
piece dropped on the counter, and he took it up-I kept the other piece-he
went out, and I followed him-he joined another man on the opposite side of
the way, and they went down Jamaica-street-I followed, and passed them-
I crossed the street again, and they went along on the other side-the prisoner
went into the Rose and Crown, and I went in after him-he threw down a
shilling on the bar, and said something, but I could not hear what--the land-
lady was there, who is exceedingly deaf-she was reaching down a bottle-
picked up the shilling - I put it between my teeth, and found it was bad-1
said, “Halloo, my lad, this is another one-you are my prisoner"-I laid
hold of him, and a scuffle ensued-I collared him-he struck me several blows
on the head and face-I still held him-we got into the street-he slipped out
of his coat, his hat fell off, and he ran up Charles-street, and left his coat with
me-several persons followed him, and he was brought back-I am sure he
is the man- i marked the shilling, and gave that, and the half of the other
shilling to the policeman.
Prisoner. I had no calico wben I was taken; is it likely I should bare
parted with that without your seeing me, when you followed me all the way!
Witness. You had plenty of opportunity of passing it to the other man- I was
some distance from you-you bad no calico when you got to the public-house.
Court. Q. Are you sure he is the same man? A. Yes-he came into my
shop with a cigar in his mouth, and assumed a great deal of consequence; that
made me notice him.
MR. DOANE. Q. He bad an opportunity of parting with the calico? A.
Yes, plenty-he stood against some palings-he might have thrown it over,
or have parted with it in many ways.
WILLIAM GAREY (police-constable K 395.) I was on duty about half-past
nine o'clock that night- caught the prisoner-his coat and hat were off-
took him to the Rose and Crown, and he put them on-I found on him two
good shillings and a farthing--this is the shilling, and the half-shilling, which
I got from Mr. Stephenson.
MR. JOHN FIELD. This shilling and part of a shilling are both counterfeit
- I believe they have been cast in the same mould.
Prisoner. I was never in the shop in my life; I was with my mother
going along the Commercial-road.
Guilty. Aged 29.- Confined Six Months.
