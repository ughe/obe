WILLIAM JOHN CHURCH, horse keeper. I live at 52. Cirencester
Street. I was out hawking with my wife on August Bank Holiday,
selling Japanese hats; I go buwking when I have no work. We sold
all out, and got home about six o'clock. I then had tea and laid
down. I got up about a quarter to nine and took the "missus" for
s ride on the tram. We got back to Lock Bridge åt a quarter to 11,
and went to the Princess of Wales" publie-house, staying there!
until half past 12. After that we then went home to supper. My
mother was with us about half an hour, and I should think she left
about a quarter to one. There was also my step-father with us, but
he had no supper with us. I said at the police court that I saw him.
before we went down the steps, but I made a mistake, it was just
afterwards. It would be about 10 minutes to one when I saw him at
my house. My mother left us about a quarter past one. When we
walked up the steps to see her off I heard someone speaking to me
from above that would be on the ground, because we live in the
basement. I looked up and saw a policeman-Jenkin-who asked
me for my name and address, and also a mun in plain clothes, who I
Bow know is named "Heard." I asked the constable what he wanted
my name and address for. He said he was going to summon me for
using abusing language. I told him there must be some mistake, or
else he was drunk. He did not say anything about obstruction or
charge me with anything but obscene language. When I said he
was drunk he said nothing. I told him my name and address. I
firs: said " John L. Sullivan," as I thought he w036 joking. I then
gave him my right name and address. He wrote it down on a piece
