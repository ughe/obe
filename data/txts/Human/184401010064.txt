 438. SUSAN HOOPER was indicted for stealing 1 printed book, value 1 l  .; 11 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Charles Hastings Collette , and another. EDWARD EDLE. I am a bookseller, and live in Chancery-lane. On the 6th of Dec. the prisoner brought this book and another to my shop to sell—it is "Collier, on Partnership"—I stopped her. CHARLES HASTINGS COLLETTE. I am a solicitor—the prisoner was in the service of the laundress where I was—she had the opportunity of getting my books—this book is the property of my partner, Mr. Pritchard, and myself—I did not miss it till the policeman brought it.
   Prisoner.  I took it to sell for the housekeeper's daughter. GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined Four Months .
 439. ELLEN WELCH was indicted for stealing 1 umbrella, value 10 s  .; the goods of Louisa Barnjum , her mistress. LOUISA BARNJUM. I am single—the prisoner was in my service—I missed my umbrella on the 19th of Dec, and spoke to her about it—she denied all knowledge of it—she afterwards said she had lent it—this is it. JAMES GOLDER. I am a pawnbroker—this umbrella was pawned with me by the prisoner, on the 18th of December, for 2 s .
   Prisoner.  I did not intend to steal it. GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined Two Months .
 440. GEORGE SIMMONS was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief, value 4 s  .; the goods of Joseph Smith , from his person. JOSEPH SMITH. I live in Union-place, and am a shoemaker. On the I afternoon of the 10th of Dec. I was at the corner of Finch-lane, Cornhill, and felt a pull at my pocket—I turned, and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand—this is it.
   Prisoner.  It was not in my hand, it was on the ground. Witness.  I saw it in his hand, and he threw it down.
   Prisoner.  I was passing, it laid on the pavement, he turned and collared me. GUILTY.* Aged 16.— Transported for Ten Years .
 441. EMILY HEWITT was indicted for stealing 1 1/2 yard of satin, value 2 s  .; 1 pair of stockings, 9 d  .; 1 pair of shoes, 2 s  .; and 1 necklace, 1 s  .; the goods of Edward Maitland Stapley , her master. ANN STAPLEY. I am the wife of Edward Maitland Stapley, of Goswell-road, tobacconist—the prisoner was in my service, and having reason to sus pect her, I searched her pocket, and found this satin, this pair of stockings, and the shoes, which are all mine.
   Prisoner.  My mistress found the shoes on me, and beat me severely with them. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined One Month .
