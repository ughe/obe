   GEORGE BOLLOND MOORE. I am a carpenter and joiner. In October last I opened a beer shop at Fulham, on a Sunday in October; six or seven weeks, ago Akers came there, about eleven o'clock in the morning; I had not heard of this robbery till he came - he staid till about nine o'clock in the evening; he and I had a pint or two of beer -I am not allowed to draw in the time of service, and he drank by himself afterwards - I have known him three or four years, and have worked with him: he said to me, "I have made a good thing of it," and said he had been at work at our old shop, Mrs. Dyott's; he said, "We were d - d fools not to take the one away that we left behind some ivy;" he called it a box, and said it was left behind some ivy against the wall - I am sure he said, We; he said Price and he were there together at night - that he went out and fired a pistol in the air, and then ran back and told Mrs. Dyott that some robbers out there had fired at him - he had been talking of himself and Price; I am sure he said, "We were d - d fools not to take away the box left behind the ivy, as well as the rest" - and said it was worth 60l.
 MR. HURCOMB. Q. You have left Fulham now? A. No, my wife is there now - I have lived at Sheen, in Berks; I did not go away from there, leaving my wife and family chargeable to the parish, for I had no wife then, nor have I done so since - I have not been there for these five years.
 MR. PHILLIPS, Q. You left your wife at Fulham? A. Yes, to-day; I have not been to Newbury these three years - I have worked at Fulham these five years, but have been home once.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
 336.  WILLIAM SANDERS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 pair of boots, value 8s., goods of Benjamin Ward.
   JOHN EMERY. I am in the employ of Benjamin Ward, of Islington. On the 26th of November, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, I was inquiring about these boots, and missed them; I had seen them safe five minutes before in the shop, and in about half an hour I found the prisoner at the station-house with them - I am sure they are my master's boots.
   THOMAS HOBBS KING. I am a Policeman. Between six and seven o'clock in the morning I saw the prisoner in conversation with another young man, coming down White Lion-street, Pentonville, about a quarter of a mile from Ward's shop, Smith crossed knocked at a street-door, and asked if they wanted buy lemons; the other kept the same side of the way - the prisoner was not admitted to the house; I went up and asked what he had got under his great coat - he said a pair of shoes; they were a sort of half-boots - I asked him how they came in his possession; he said he brought them of a man at Hornsey, that the man carried a bag, and he gave 5s. 8d. for them - I took him to the station-house: no lemons were found on him - seeing the other man had another pair of boots under the breast of his coat, I went back for him, but he got away - the prosecutor lost two pairs; I found out Emery, who claimed them.
 Prisoner. Q. There was nobody with me? A. I saw them both talking together, each having a pair of boots.
   JOHN EMERY. I know these boots by the figures on them - Mr. Ward made them: they sell at 9s. 6d. - we lost two pairs that morning, but found only these.
 Prisoner's Defence. There was a young man before me, but not with me; they gave 8s. as the full value at Hatton-garden.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Six Months& Whipped.
 237.  JOSHUA SPURLING and SAMUEL JACOBS were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of December , 50 lbs. weight of iron, value 5s., the goods of the   Independent Gas-light and Coke Company.
   THOMAS FULLWOOD. I am engineer at the Independent Gas-light and Coke Company. They act under that name, and are authorised to prosecute and defend in that name; I have seen the Act - this iron was laying on the ground in their yard at Haggerstone- I had not particularly noticed it for a week before it was taken; it was five bars -I found it at the Police-station on the 7th or 8th of December, about eleven o'clock in the morning - it had been made to a pattern of my own invention: I found about a hundred weight and three quarters of it there - I believe the prisoner had been employed on the works by the commissioners of the road, to wheel ballast away which they had purchased.
 Spurling. Q. Had you seen me on the works with a barrow? A. I think I have; but I am not certain of Jacobs.
   WILLIAM SMITH. On the 7th of December, about a quarter before seven o'clock, I was on duty as a private patrol of St. Leonard, Shoreditch - at the corner of Union-street, leading towards Kingsland, I saw Spurling wheeling a barrow - Jacobs was walking alongside of him; I crossed over, and asked what they had got there - they said, "Some iron:" I asked where they brought it from - they said,"From Kingsland, from Mr. Perry's marine-store shop;" I said they must go with me to the watch-house, and while I was talking to them, Jacobs slipped two shovels from behind his back - I put them into the barrow, and with the assistance of the Policeman, took them to the station: the shovels have not been claimed - I was present when Fullwood saw the iron at the office, and claimed it; I was sure that Perry did not keep an iron shop at Kingsland.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Spurling's Defence. I went down to my father's to do some work; I was coming up and happened to see the barrow in the road - Jacobs said, "Will you give me a help along with it?" I did not know but it was his property, and wheeled it for him - Smith said, "What have you here?" I said, "Iron;" he said, "I can't let you go;" I said, "Well, I may as well go, it is not my property."
 WILLIAM SMITH. Jacobs said he brought it from an old iron shop - Spurling did not contradict him; he said he had wheeled the barrow for him.
 SPURLING -    GUILTY. Aged 21.
 JACOBS -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Two Months, and Whipped.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
