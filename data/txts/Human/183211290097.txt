206. WILLIAM SMITH and WILLIAM JENKINS were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 4 live tame pigeons, price 3s. the property of William Deeley.
   CHARLES JONES. I deal in birds and rabbits, and live in Pancras-place, Pancras. On the 26th of November, at a quarter before six o'clock in the evening, the prisoner Smith and another boy brought me two pigeons, and asked a shilling for them; and seeing me not anxious to take them they said they were dissolving partnership, and had more to sell - I then bought them for 2s., and in a few minutes a Policeman brought the two prisoners in: I said Smith was one of them that had sold me the pigeons - Deeley selected the two I had bought.
 CHARLES STEWART. I am an officer. I stopped the prisoners with each a pigeon; Jenkins' brother was with them - I asked where they got them; Jenkins said he had bought them at Fletcher's, a pigeon-fancier - I asked them where they got the money to buy them; he said he had held a horse for a gentleman - I was taking them to Fletcher's, and Smith said, "We did not buy them there, we bought them here," pointing to Jones' - I took them there, and from what he said, took them to the station.
 WILLIAM DEELEY. I  deal in pigeons- I live in Brill-row, Somers-town; I do not know the prisoners; I lost these pigeons on Tuesday the 27th of November, out of my shop window - a lath had been broken out of my shop window, and the pigeons taken - I know these to be them; I saw them before the Magistrate.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 13.
 JENKINS -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharded.
 207.  JOHN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 2 carriage lamps, value 10s., the goods of Sarah Clarke.
   CHARLES JENNINGS. I am a Policeman. On the 26th of November, about eight o'clock, I saw the prisoner and two others, at the corner of Judd-street and Speldhurst-street, Brunswick-square; seeing me they immediately separated - I saw the prisoner had something under his arm; I followed him to Coram-street; he turned into a dark corner, and threw something down; I immediately laid hold of him, and asked what he had there - he said nothing; I told him to pick up what he had put down; he said I might be d - d; I gave him to another officer -I found two carriage lamps there, which I found belonged to the prosecutrix.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see any thing under my arm? A. I did.
   JOHN HERRING. I drive a fly for Sarah Clarke, who is a stable-keeper; I came in about seven o'clock; a carriage stood in front of the gateway in Speldhurst-street; the lamps were all secure then; I did not miss them till the officer brought them to me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I stopped to speak to a young man; I left him, and the Policeman followed me and said, "What have you got?" I said, "I have nothing" - he said, "Pick up what you have there" - I said, "I don't know that any thing is there."
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 208.  ANN TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of November , 1 shawl, value 7s., the goods of George Ravenors.
   HENRY NEWSTEAD. I am a shopman to George Raveoor, a pawnbroker, in Tothill-street, Westminster. On the 30th of November this shawl hung about a foot within the doorway - I was behind the counter, and saw it drop; I went to pick it up, and found it was gone; I went out, and saw the prisoner turn round the end of the window, down a passage; I ran after her, and asked what she had wrapped up in a piece of cotton, which she had; she said,"What she had was her own;" I took it from her, and it turned out to be a shawl - it had been pulled down from the door, having been fastened with a string.(Property produced and sworn to).
 Prisoner's Defence. A woman had the shawl in her hand, and said, "I have been to fetch this out of pawn; hold it for me till I come out again, I am going to fetch some more;" she went in once more, and walked on before me; I was following to give it her; my husband was a quarter-master in the 1st regiment of the Guards; he is dead.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. Confined for Three Weeks.
 Before Mr. Recorder.
 209.  RICHARD VANDOME was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of November , 1 coat, value 3l., the goods of George Palmer.
   GEORGE PALMER. I keep a beer-shop in Brick-lane. On Sunday, the 18th of November, about ten minutes after nine o'clock at night, I put my coat behind the counter on a barrel of ale, folded up - I have seen the prisoner at the shop several times; he was sitting on the opposite side of the counter at this time - I missed it in about ten minutes, he was then gone; somebody said he had seen the prisoner go out with it - I took a person who had sat by his side to the station-house, and in consequence of what he said I sent the Policeman to No. 24, John-street, Brick-lane - he found the coat, and brought it to the station.
   JOHN POND. I was at the beer-shop, and saw the prisoner there; and a few minutes after nine o'clock I saw him walk out with the coat under his arm - it struck me afterwards that it did not belong to him, and I gave information, as I missed it from the barrel; he was taken in an hour.
   JAMES MILTON. I am a cabinet-maker, and live in Lamb's-fields. I went to Palmer's house for a pot of porter - I saw the prisoner come out of the beer-shop with the coat over his arm; I went in, and heard Palmer had lost one, and told him what I had seen - I am certain of him.
