PERCY HEDLEY HALL Examined by Mr. Bodkin. I am a solicitor,
a member of the firm of Cooper, Walker, and Hall, 7, Birchin Lane.
Towards the end of April, 1906, I was consulted by Mrs. Butcher in
reference to the “Financial Guardian," and on her instructions wrote
a letter to Ablett (Exhibit 73). The next day I got Exhibit 74 in
reply. I wrote another letter, stating that the cover had not run off
as they had said. Before writing that letter I had carefully looked
through the official list with regard to Canadian Pacific stock. In
answer to that I got Exhibit 76. I wrote another letter offering to
take up the stock less the amounts paid or take £150 in settlement,
I got no reply and commenced an action against Ablett, claiming
the return of £150, or, in the alternative, £150 damages for breach
of contract. I was not able to effect service, and an order was made
for substituted service, and I sent a writ with a copy of the order by
post to Ablett. In reply I got a letter signed "George Martin,"
initialled "J. R.," stating Ablett had nothing to do with the business
and had gone abroad without leaving his address. (Exhibit 81.) The
documents were returned. After that I advised my client not to
waste any more money over it, and the action was abandoned alto-
gether. I have not received any part of the £150.
Cross-examined by Mr. Wellesley Orr. When Mrs. Butcher con-
sulted me she brought me a circular, I saw it was the usual cover
transaction. I think my information that the cover had not run off
came from the daily paper. I know the daily paper only gives the
closing quotations. A different price might have ruled in the middle
of the day. I did not take in the Financial News" and I did
not have a tape, and consequently I looked at the Daily Telegraph
to verify what Mrs. Butcher told me. I called at Ablett's office twice
and found the typewriter girl there. I could get nothing out of her.
and, therefore, I wrote. The second time I called she said, "It will
be all right; Mr. Ablett will attend to it." She did not seem to
understand much about it, and treated me in an offhand way. The
claim for "breach of contract " was a formal thing. In issuing
