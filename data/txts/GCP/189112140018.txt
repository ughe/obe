ALBERT TIMS (N361). On 20th November my attention was attracted to
a crowd in Shepperton Street - I went, and saw the prisoner held by the
prosecutor, who said the prisoner and another man had stolen his watch
and chain, and he gave the prisoner in charge for assaulting him; he
said he had struck him several times-he was very much upset and
excited; he appeared to be in pain, and was unable to attend the Police-
court for a week afterwards--the prisoner said, "I have not stolen his
watch ; I have only been in the public-house to have a glass of beer;
you may search me"- he was taken to the station; he made no state-
ment there-the watch has not been recovered.
Cross-examined. The prosecutor was holding you in the Shepperton
Road, against the public-house-Baring Street is close by, on the opposite
side of the road.
PATRICK SHANNAHAN (G 211). On the night of 10th November I was
called to the Rosemary Branch public-house, where I saw the prosecutor
and Tims holding the prisoner-the prosecutor said the prisoner had
stolen his watch and struck him in the chest; the prisoner said, “Search
me, search me, I have not got a watch "-we took him to the station.
Cross-examined. Several men, respectable as far as I know, spoke in
your favour, and said you did not do it; that was as to stealing the watch
---on the next morning the prosecutor did not appear against you; I
went to his house, and he was too ill to attend; I saw him sitting in &
chair by the fire.
The prisoner said he came out of a public-house and asked the prosecutor, who

97. HENRY PERKINS (17) and HENRY PARKER (17), Break-
ing and entering the dwelling-house of Benjamin Sparks, with intent to
commit felony therein.
MR. MOMORRAN Prosecuted.
BENJAMIN SPARKS. I am the landlord of the Grapes public house,
Mill Street-on the night of 4th December I was the last up; I locked
up my premises—there is an outside gate fastened by a padlock; and a
door, partly glass and partly wood, locked--about three o'clock in the
morning I was called up by a constable-I found the lock of the front
gate broken, and the fanlight orer the door pushed back-anyone by
getting through the gate and fanlight could get in the house.
HENRY HASTED (C 152). Early on 5th December I passed these pre-
mises several times - about half an hour after I had seen them safe I
found the padlock wrenched off and broken.
