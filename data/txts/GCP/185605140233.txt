THOMAS JOHN GOUGH. I am a tailor, of No, 5, Lauderdale-buildings,
Aldersgate-street. Beckley lodged with me till some short time after Nov.
last, and the prisoner used to come to see him, sometimes once a week, and
sometimes twice he came invariably on Saturday evenings, and occasionally
on Tuesday evenings, and he invariably carried a basket; and on one occa-
sion I saw in it something in a brown paper parcelone Saturday night
about 6 o'clock in the evening, it might be three weeks prior to Beckley
being taken, the prisoner came ; I opened the door, and he asked for Beckley,
I told him he was not within; he seemed vexed, and said that he had come
long journey, from Stratford, and was tired-I told him if he liked to
leave his basket, which seemed to be heavy, I would take care of it till
Beckley came-he declined leaving it, and said that he would go over to the
public house opposite, and await Beckley's return, if I would let one of my
children let him know when Beckley came in, and invited me to partake of a
little porter at the public house I told my children, and then went oyer
with him to the public house where he put a brown paper paroel on a form,
and I saw a brown powder which seemed to ooze from it, as it does from
these paroels produced—I did not remain two minutes with him, I had no
drink I did not see him afterwards at my house, but my children told me
something-subsequent to Beckley's apprehension, I saw him at my house
and opened the door to him, and I met him in Barbican after Beckley's
apprehension, and noticed that his whiskers were shaved off.
