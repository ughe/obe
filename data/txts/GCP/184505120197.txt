CHARLOTTE MUNGO. I am the wife of Airth Mungo, who keeps the
George. I saw Smith close the house about twelve o'clock-I went into
the tap-room, and put the gas out-it was then, as near as possible, twenty
minutes or a quarter to twelve-there were no persons in the tap-room
then, to my knowledge-I looked under the places-I did not look up the
chimney-I observed no soot there-I saw it afterwards-there was not
much it might have been so when I was there, and I not observe it--
there was no cap or shoes lying on the floor, and the tills were in their
proper places-when I came down, after being disturbed, I found one of
the tills on the counter, and a pair of shoes, a cap, and some soot in the
tap-room,
WILLIAM HENRY CUNNINGHAM. I am a water-man or kind of ostler
at the George. I was in the tap-room on Sunday night, the 4th of May,
and saw the prisoner and two others there--they first came into the tap-
room about half-past nine o'clock-I left at twenty minutes before eleven
-the prisoner and the other two men were then there, by themselves.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you quite sure of that? A. Quite certain
they had one pot of beer-one of them brought it into the tap-room-
nobody in particular waits on the customers--I do sometimes when I am
there I did not take any beer in to them-I was sitting against the door,
and they were by the fire-I sat facing them—they sat there all the time
--the man brought the beer in when he came in at first--they did not go
out afterwards in my presence-they talked very little--the man with the
beer came in behind the others—he was a tall man-I do not know his
name-I had seen him before-he was there three Sunday evenings before
that-he was a dark-complexioned young man-I was at the police office
I saw some of the prisoner's witnesses there--neither of them were the
tall man that came in-I did not speak to the prisoner or either of the
others- I was sleepy.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Were there other persons in the tap-room besides those
three men ? A. No, nobody else, all the time, and I left them there.
ALEXANDER HOPPER (police-constable P 183.) On Monday morning,
the 5th of May, at one o'clock, I was on duty in Camberwell-road-1
tried the door of the George public-house, and thought it rather gave in
I pressed against it again, and hallooed out to my brother constable that I
did not consider it was all right-1 heard a noise inside like a dog, or
something shoved against the door-a bar, which was laid against it,
fell, and I got in- I observed the till on the counter, with some coppers in
it-the back door was unsecured, the bolts out-anybody could have got
out there wbile I was coming in at the front-I found a pair of shoes, one
on the stove, and the other lying below, with soot, and a cap with soot on
it-I found nobody there-Wells came in about twenty-five minutes after I
had first gone in, and three policemen-from what Wells said to me I went
in search of the prisoner, and about eleven o'clock on Monday evening I
found him in the stable-loft in Waterloo-street, Camberwell, apparently asleep
-I shook him, and asked if he slept there last night-he said yes, he did -
a gentleman there said, “Bill, you know you did not"-he then said he did
not-I asked him where he had been on the Sunday night-he said he had
been at the Boyton Arms (which is a beer-shop on the banks of the canal)
with two others; that the landlord closed up the house, (he did not
mention the time :) from there he came down Southampton-street, and
