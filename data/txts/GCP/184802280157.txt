THOMAS JAMES. I am a watchmaker, at Woolwich, and a goldsmith of the
City of London. I have known Barlton five years he has borne a most
honourable and upright character, to the best of my belief-I recollect the
witness, Duffeld, calling with Burlton at my shop some time in Feb.-I took
them through the shop into the parlour-Burlton called on me to give him
the address of Dr. Penny, who lived in Billiter-street, and who had run into
his debt 41. or 51. - I gave it him-I did not leave him and Duffield alone
together from the time they came in till they left-I heard all that passed
between them-I did not go from one shop to the other, nor go up stairs.
NR. PAYNE. Q. How long were they there! 4. About five minutes
I had five watches on my parlour table-I only turned my back to go to a
book-case, to get the book in which Dr. Penny's address was written-I
wrote the address in my parlour—I did not go into the shop-Burlton owes
me 10l, or 121, and has done so for about two years and a half-I know Mr.
Chatham-I never said to him or to any one that I should not come here at
all only Burlton owed me money, and I thought if he was convicted I should
not get it-Burlton has been in my house many times, and I would trust
him with untold gold – I remember when he was waiter at the Pier Tavera
and Hotel-there was a robbery talked of at that tine-he was never sus-
pected I saw his late employer, Mr. Tomlins, last Wednesday-he is
very poorly, or he would have been here, and he said he had never robbed
biur of a shilling.

WILLIAM BARLOW. I am a carpenter and builder, at Plamstead-common
I have known the witness, Duffield, nine years he is not a man that I would
believe on his oath.
Court. Q. Have you ever known him examined on oath ? 4. Never.
Mr. PAYNE. Q. How long have you lived in your present place? 4.
Seven years--I had men in my employ last summer, but business being dull
during the winter I have not now-I never had any quarrel with Duffield
I never heard him give evidence, but I know his character-I know Mr.
Thorpe.
