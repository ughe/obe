CHARLOTTE REYNOLDS. I am the mother of the prisoner Thomas-we
have our house-carts up this little roadway leading out of the public
road-I heard of the assault on the prosecutor my two sons came home
together a few minutes before the five minutes to nine train came in-
they never went out again that night; they never went out of my van-
I first heard of the assault on the Monday morning-William Pateman
was at home when my sons came home he is married to my daughter
--Mary Reynolds was there, the wife of my son George, and Mary Pate-
man, who is married to the prisoner's brother.
WILLIAM PATEMAN. I am a donkey driver, and am brother to the
prisoner, and married one of the sisters of Reynolds, and live with the
mother up this road-I remember hearing of the assault on the engine-
driver my brother Thomas and Reynolds came home with the donkeys,
and I see them unsaddle them just as the five minutes to nine train came
in-I followed them up to the van and stood on the steps, and some con-
versation took place I am sure they never left again that night-I am
sure the train was in time that night I have a clock in my van, and
I take that train two or three times a week to go shopping.
MARY REYNOLDS. I am the wife of George Reynolds I heard of the
assault the next day, Sunday my husband and his brother came home &
few minutes before the nine o'clock train came in—I am in the habit of
hearing that train, and regulate my time by it—they went out to tie up
their donkeys-my husband never went away all night, and I saw his
brother go into the next van to me.
MARY PATEMAN. I heard of the assault on the Monday-on the
night of the occurrence the prisoner Reynolds came home with his
brother George a few minutes before the five minutes to nine train came
in-he never went out again-William Pateman is my husband-Rey.
nolds went into the next van to me.
ROBERT PATEMAN. I heard of the assault on the Monday after-I see
my son, the prisoner, in Mr. Prescott's beershop, The Alma, at about ten
minutes past nine--I heard him come to my house before he went there,
but I never see him, and he went away directly to the public house, and
I soon followed him-I saw the donkeys tied up, his boy always does
that there was a lot of fruiting people working round in the neighbour-
hood—I did not see any other donkey drivers there-I went into the tap-
room--they might come forty times into the bar without my seeing them
-I was with them until ten o'clock, when they shut up-he went to Birch-
wood Corner after that because they shut up at eleven.
THOMAS PRESCOTT. I kept the Alma at the time in question on the
night of the assault I saw the prisoner Pateman at my house at five
minutes to a quarter past nine, I think-there were two more donkey
men in the house together drinking-Thomas Reynolds was not
in my house at all that night I have known the two prisoners for
twelve years, I should think, and I have never heard anything against
them—they are hard-working respectable men in their line of life, I was
asked by the police to give evidence on the Tuesday, I think, as they
were taken on the Monday-they sent then to tell me not to come—they
thought they could do without me, I didn't want to go at all.
By the Court. The men had their donkeys with them I cannot answer
for their having sticks—the donkeys were there till I shut up my house
