On the 25th of May I was Playing at cards with him and another man, who had come up from Maidstone, and who used to lodge on some other floor—I had brought home a gold watch, at five o'clock—I showed that and some other things to the prisoner—the other man came in while we were playing—they saw me put the things into a box—I locked it, and put it into a blue bag—it was a square box—I used to carry it in the bag—the other man did not see the contents of the box—me all three went out—when we were standing in front of the street door, they asked me which way I was going—I said to Bishopsgate—they then went the con  trary way—I returned about nine o'clock—I had left my box locked—I had not left my door locked, because there is no key—I missed the box and con tents, and have never seen them since—I gave information to the landlady—the prisoner came home at eleven o'clock, and I gave him in charge—he had no right to go into my room when I was out—I had lived there about six months—the room door goes rather hard, and makes a great deal of noise.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALF . Q.  Have not you been occasionally in the prison's room, and he in yours? A.  I was never in his room—he used not to come into my room till within a few days before this, but he had come nearly very day for the last eight days—I always opened the box, and put in the goods, when I came home—we had only played at cards two or three times, and not more then twice in the last eight days, and he used to come to my room perhaps twice a day during the last eight days—I made no objection—I did not like to—I thought it very curious—the other man's name is Cohen—he is a foreigner—I have known him ten years—I know nothing about him—he has not returned since—he had come up from Maidstone the previous day—I gave the prisoner in charge, about eleven o'clock, in the parlour down stair—there are two or three lodgers, most of them in the same trade as myself—I never saw the prisoner with jewellery—I have never been up to his room—I have no other name but Kirsch Lands berg—when I saw the prisoner I did not speak to him, but gave him in charge.
   MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Had you previously had some conversation with Mr. Parish? A.  Yes—that led me to give him in charge—Cohen came home before him—I do not know how long before, as I was not at home. EMMA CHARLOTTE PARISH. My mother is a window, and keeps the house No. 5, New-street, Bishopsgate, in the parish of St. Botolph Without, Bishops gate; Landsberg lodged in the first floor front room, Cohen at the back, and the prisoner on the third floor. On the 25th of May, about six o'clock, the prosecutor, the prisoner, and Cohen went out together—after they had been gone about five minutes the prisoner returned—I opened the door—he went into the parlour—I went in, then left him there, and went down stairs—he then went up stairs and opened Mr. Landsberg's room—the door makes a noise when it is opened; it bursts—when I heard the door I spoke to my mother, and she went up stairs—there was no other person in the house but a lady on the second floor—my mother was up stairs three or four minutes—the prisoner then went out—he turned to the left, and I could not see him as I looked to the right—I heard somebody go out—the lady was not out all that day—there was one gentleman lived in the house—he came in twice, and went out twice—I let him in once, and let him out once, and my mother let him in once, and out once—he had got nothing with him—another gentleman came once, and let himself in—he had nothing with him, and I let him out—neither of them could have had a bag or box without my seeing it.
