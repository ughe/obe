Q. This would prevent your carrying on business in the Strand, but not
in Coventry-street? A. Yes, because we lived in the Strand.
Q. Did you give any directions to any body to give up the gold ? 1. I
told my solicitor I would make every reparation in my power--I gave
directions to my solicitor to cause the gold to be given up, and that I was
ready to make every reparation in my power-I gave up all the gold which
remained after this unfortunate transaction- I did not give up any notes
-there was 501. taken out of my cash-box, in Coventry-street, by Roe-
I have not undertaken to give over any money of any kind, nothing but
the gold-I paid for the gold 3301., 4001., 7001., and 131., besides--the
greater part of the gold I think I have not paid for-I cannot tell exactly
what money I have received for the gold I got from Mrs. Abrahams, be-
cause I received money for silver bars, and Scotch and Irish notes and
gold coin at the same time I can swear I received 12001. from Messrs.
Bult for the gold, and there is a balance in their hands, and 4001. from
Cock and Johnson's- I do not recollect what the balance is in Messrs. Bult's
hands-he was to pay about 601. balance for the gold, to the best of my re-
collection, but I do not recollect-there was no arrangement for my handing
over any more money of any kind-I do not know a man of the name of
Davis I have heard of his name I should not know him if he stood be-
fore me I do not know him, not the man who is indicted-I never saw
him to my recollection in my life I might have seen him passing the
street, and not know who he was my sons were not off before I was
taken up-I left them at home when I was taken-I was at liberty above
a week after I was first examined-it was above a week after I was sworn
and examined at Lambeth-street that I was taken up-it was on a Tues-
day I know, and I think it must have been on the 16th of April I was
taken up.
Q. You had the gold in your house at that time? A. I gave it to my
servant Dean to take care of I gave it him the morning after we melted
it, when I received the message from Messrs. Bult-I gave it to him to
take care of-to put away-I either gave it him, or told him to take it
out of the iron chest-I was agitated and alarmed about it-I gave to
him to put away-to put it out of the way I did not use the word con-
ceal-I gave it him to take it out of my sight-I did not see where he
took it-my house was not searched before I was taken up-I do not
know whether it was after I was taken up-I did not ask Dean where the
gold was before I went to gaol-I only gave it him to take care of to put it
out of the way I believe he is my servant still the gold was in my iron
chest-I kept the key of it at the time-my son Isaac had nothing at all
to do with taking care of any part of the property, only Dean-I only sent
Isaac with a bar to Cock and Johnson's-he attended in the front shop.
Q. Was he trusted in respect of your property generally? 1. He is
my son--he did all things I wished him to do-Dean only had the care
of this property-before that I locked it in my iron chest myself-I kept
my papers and deeds and coin in that iron chest-I put bowls of
coin there of a night-there was no other melted gold there, to the best
of my recollection-I am a general dealer in gold and silver, a bullion
dealer generally-my son Selim had nothing to do with the shop in the
Strand at that time I sent for him on the Wednesday morning-he had
