Cross-examined. 2. The lame man was not Adams A. No.
GEORGE BALL (re-examined). I saw the prosecutrix on the night of the
17th-I live at the station, and was there when she was brought in-she
was in a filthy state; she smelt as if she had been made water over, and she
was one mass of mess from top to bottom, just as if she had been drugged-
she was in a regular silly state; there was not the slightest sense about her
then, nor was there next morning ; indeed, she did not recover her senses
for two days afterwards—there is no doubt she had been dreadfully drugged
-she is rather short-she has not exactly got her right senses about her.
The prisoner Adams put in a written defence, stating that he met the
prosecutrix at a public-house and remained in her company for some time, but
left her at the Wellington, and knew nothing of the robbery.
