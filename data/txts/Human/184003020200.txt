   Prisoner's Defence.  I was coming over Waterloo-bridge, I turned down Belvedere-road, and saw a lad go into this yard—I went after him—I saw the window open; he saw me come, and came out again I suppose—I went to look in to see if I could see him, and my hat fell into the room—I stepped in after it and the gentleman seized me, and said I wanted to rob his place. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Twelve Months.
 996. WILLIAM TANNER and WILLIAM BROWN were indicted for feloniously assaulting William Usher , on the   25th of February , with intent to rob him. WILLIAM USHER. I am servant to the Honourable Society of the Inner Temple. On the 24th of February, about a quarter to twelve o'clock, I was in St. George's-road, leading from the Elephant and Castle to New Bedlam—I had come from a public-house, where I had been to join a bene fit society, and was going home—I was alone—I had been drinking a little, but was quite capable of taking care of myself, and knowing what I was about—I was walking on the edge of the foot-path—I did not observe or hear any person in the road near me, but I received a violent blow on the cheek—I turned round momentarily and perceived Tanner—I had scarcely time to make a slight grasp of him, when my heels were tripped up, and I was knocked down nearly on my back—my breath was almost knocked out of my body—the blow did not knock me down—it cut my cheek—I should suppose the blow was from a fist—I had a bundle in my hand, and the moment my heels were tripped up, a snatch was made at it, but I had it tied round my wrist, and they could not get it off—it was all done momentarily—what passed after I was down I cannot say—I know Tanner was one of the men, for I turned round instantly on receiving the blow, and saw he had very black eyes, as if from blows he had received—I never saw him before, to my knowledge—there was a light from a gas lamp—I cannot say how many there were, it was done in such a sudden manner—the policeman and Lee came to my assistance momentarily, or I should have been robbed—I did not miss any thing—I had some fifteen or sixteen shillings loose in my trowsers' pocket. HENRY LEE. I am a plumber. I was in St. George's road, going home on the 24th of February—I saw Usher crossing the road near the foot-path in a slanting direction—there were five persons five or six paces from him close together—three of them darted from the five, tripped up his heels, and knocked him down—I instantly ran to his assistance, and collared Brown, who was on the prosecutor, and had his hands on him—the prosecu tor laid on his side between the road and the path—I secured Brown, and he bit my hand to try to get away—the policeman took Tanner instantly, in my sight, and had great difficulty in keeping him—Tanner was stooping over the prosecutor in the same position as Brown—there was plenty of light from the gas lamp—they could not be off seeing me—they could not see the policeman—he was in a dark corner of the street.
