   COURT . Q.  When you drove the "Albion" that day, what conduct was pursued by them or you? A.  Ipursued the course that he had done to me before, retaliating—I waited at the Crown to see whether Mr. Bray's omnibus came to oppose Mr. Trevett's—It did come, and I went with it, and followed it—It he started and I started—he went first—Thomas Lee, his driver, gave me a challenge, and said, if he was game  to Woolwich, was I game  to follow him—I said, "I am game  to follow you wherever you like to go"—he did not go to Woolwhich, but he ran up and down the streets, and I followed him about, out of the regular track—there was no obstruction against me for three days—they never ran their pole against me, so that passengers could not get in—I never saw any thing of the sort.
   JURY . Q.  By whose orders did you do that? A.  Mr. Jhon Trevett told me I was to drive that omnibus that day, and that was all. AARON MARTIN. In June last, I drove the "Triumph" omnibus, be longing to Mr. Robert Trevett, from the Wheatsheaf to the Bank and back—I remember Mr. Bray's omnibuses starting on Whit-Monday—on that morning I saw them standing at the corner of New Church-street—we stand at the corner of Old Church-street, the Edgeware-road separates them—I pulled up at the corner of Old Church-street, and John Lee and Henry Maun, the drivers of Mr. Bray's buses,  said, "We have come to melt the old cobbler's wax"  —I started twenty-four minutes after nine o'clock, and Henry Mann started with me—we were in a line, he at the corner of New Church-street, and I at the corner of Old Church-street—when I stopped at the corner of Oxford-terrace, he stopped with me—when I stopped at the corner of Uxbridge-road, near the New Inn, (Where we stop,) he stopped exactly with me—my omnibus stood close to the curb, and he stopped right against me—when I stopped at the corner of Regent-street, he stopped with me—when Itook up a passenger, he passed me—when he took up one, I passed him—we went on to Day and Martin's—I pulled up on the near side of the road to the White Horse, No. 100, and he pulled up on the opposite side—he stopped near Bray's stables in coming up, he did not in going down—I went on to the Bank, and took my three minutes—he did not take any time, but went into Cornhill, and plied for passengers, and when I came off, he started and pulled against me down the Poultry—I went back as far as Day and Martin's—he there changed horses, and I lost him, and did mot have him any more—the second journey he went down with Mr. Cowderoy's bus.
