JOSEPH Budson. On 21st October, I saw the prisoner hanging about
outside Mr. Harmer's shop-he took something out of his pocket-book, or
purse, wrapped it up in a bit of paper, and put it in the yard wall adjoining
the shop-he then went into Mr. Harmer's, and also into Mr. Oekenden's
I then went to the wall, called a witness before I touched it, and then found
a bad half-crown wrapped up in a piece of paper-I gave it to the constable
in the shop, and told him in the prisoner's presence that I had found it on
the wall, and saw the prisoner put it there--the prisoner said nothing to that.
(Policeman, K 493). I found the prisoner and Mr. Harmer in Mr. Ocken-
den's shopthe prisoner was charged with passing a bad half-crown-I asked
him for it-be remained silent, and I searched him, and found the bad half-
crown in his coat sleeve-I asked him his address, and he said that it was not
requisite-Mr. Hudson gave me a second half-crown-I found on the prisoner
11. ls. 4d., as he had the change which the boot-maker had given him.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These two half-crowns are bad, and from the same
mould.

EDWARD WILTON. I am a butcher, of Russel-place, New North-road,
near to my brother-in-law, Mr. Clark-the prisoner bought a pound and a
half of pieces at my shop, which came to 10 d., and gave me a five-shilling
piece-I passed it through a little window to my brother-in-law, who was
in the parlour-he gave her the change, and she left the shop-he afterwards
showed it me, and it was bad-we ran after the prisoner three or four
minutes after she left, but she had gone - I gave the crown to the constable
on the 25th.
