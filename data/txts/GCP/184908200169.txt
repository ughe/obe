MARIA GOBBETT. I lived with the prisoner. In the early part of 1846 I
recollect his coming home early in the morning, and bringing a clock and a
looking-glass-I asked him where he got them from-he said that was his
business-he went out and I saw no more of bim till the evening, when he
dame and took the clock away, and came back without it-this clock is the
same one-I asked him where he had been all day-he said what was that
to do with me, and I went to bed -he desired me to sell the looking-glass,
and I did so, to Mr. Gorbell.

Tuomas GARDNER (policeman, M 79). I went to Manning's house, and
found the clock-I afterwards apprehended the prisoner, and told him what
it was for-he said there were plenty of charges against him, he should be
able to clear himself of that.
SAMUEL GORBELL. I live at Hickman's Folly, Dockhead, and deal in
furniture. I purchased a glass of Gobbett, very much like this one I believe
it is it.
Prisoner's Defence (written). “Maria Gobbett has been living with me
several times for two years and ten months; she left me in May, 1848, went
to Gravesend, and was committed for a month ; her brother gave me this
clock to sell, as he had a distress in his house, and I sold it for him to Mrs.
Manning; I was taken into custody in May twelvemonth, and had twelve
months imprisonment; during my imprisonment, Maria Gobbett got aquainted
with a policeman, who made her drunk, and gave her a sum of money to tell
what she knew about certain robberies committed by me; so I was taken into
custody for this clock; the policeman has been in the habit of sleeping with
her at a house of ill-fame; she has done this because I would not live withi
her any longer."
MARIA GOBBETT re-examined. It is all false.
