   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  I believe when Johnson came home the policeman Wood was there? A.  Yes—when I told Johnson, he said, "Bless my soul! is that so?"—he is a writer, or an author—I knew nothing of his circumstances till he was given into custody—Mrs. Allport cooked for him, and she has said he was poorly—he complained of ill health when he took the lodging, and said Mrs. Allport had been his nurse—she did every thing for him—I washed the table-cloth and sheets—Mrs. Allport asked me when I would have them, and she gave them to me—they have been at all times forthcoming—I had the rent by instalments through the winter, but lately more regularly—when I have missed any trifling thing I have said to Allport or to Johnson, "As far as you are concerned, all I re quire is, that on the day before you leave, or on that day, you will permit me to see that all my things are there"—I had no wish to prosecute—I believe I mentioned to Johnson's father, that if the things had been all safe, I should have thought nothing about it—I wished to get them out of the house quietly. JAMES WOOD( police-constable N  277) deposed as in the former case. (See page  366.) THOMAS MAYNARD. I am foreman to Mr. Edwards, a pawnbroker—I have two pillows, a bolster, a blanket, and a table-cloth, all pawned by All port.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe they have constantly pawned and taken them out? A.  Yes, all but one spoon, which was pawned on the 3rd of May—those things have been pawned and redeemed once a week—I think this sheet is worth 7 s .—4 s . was advanced on it, which was all that was asked—it is not worth more than 5 s . in trade. JAMES WOOD re-examined.  I searched the room, but Johnson was not there—I took him to the station, and found on him fifty-four duplicates, twenty-two of which relate to this property. HENRY LIEBRICHT. I am assistant to Mr. Sowerby, a pawnbroker—I produce a copper tea-kettle, pawned by Allport.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe she has pawned articles at your shop almost every day? A.  This very kettle was pawned in the morning, and redeemed in the evening, for about a fortnight previous to the 2nd of April. The record of the conviction of Francis Beard was put in and read. NOT GUILTY.
