I was too deeply interested in the success of the bank to, have sanctioned such a thing—I purchased five more shares in August, 1861, at 16 l ., per share—at the time the business was passed over to the London and Westminster, I had some conversation with Mr. Burch; it was a few days before it was actually transferred; I can't give the date—I asked Mr. Burch to supply me with a paper specifying all the assets which constituted the value of the bank—he furnished me with a long slip of paper—this it it, there is some of my writing upon it ( read ) "City, 105,000 l ., and 42,000 l .; West, 22,000 l ., and 5,000 l .; loans, 6,000 l ., and 3,000 l .; total, 183,000 l .," at the bottom of the page, "City, 190,000 l .; loans and discount, 33,000 l .; cash, 8,000 l .; Brighton, 15,000 l .,"—on the other side, against that amount, is put, "Good-will and furniture West, 20,000 l .; loans and discounts, 3,000 l .; cash, 1, 500 l .; total, 270, 500 l .; deductions, 183,000 l ., leaving 87, 800 l .,"—at the time I received that I made a memorandum on the back in Burch's pre sence—he saw it—the memorandum is, "Confident of 8,000 l ., clear"—that was my note—the object of it was to ascertain how much per share was likely to be paid to the shareholders of the Unity Bank, and I reckoned it at 30 l ., per share—I remember Mr. Eyre making certain suggestions as to the management of the bank—I was present at the time the board adopted those suggestions; I was always present if I was able, and I seldom passed within the neighbourhood without looking in to see if I could be of any use—I had nothing to do with the preparation of the balance-sheet in question—I knew that it was printed and circulated amongst the shareholders—I had no reason to doubt the correctness of that balance-sheet—I saw the detailed balance-sheet on the table; it was handed round—we all looked at it—I have no doubt I saw it—I was present—I think we had two meetings; at one it was in a rough state, and at the other it was in a mere perfect state—that is according to my recollection of it—I presume this is it ( looking at it) , I should not like to swear it—I think it is most likely that both balance-sheets were on the table—I know I saw the one that was published to the shareholders.
