 1001.  JOHN DEVINE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of March , 1 door, value 16s., the goods of Elizabeth Hall.
 ELIZABETH HALL. I am a  widow. On the 12th of March I lost this door from an unfinished house, which my husband was building before he died - I know it by some marks on it.
   THOMAS TUCHIN. I am a grocer, and live in Whiteconduit-fields. On the 12th of March, the prisoner passed my shop-door towards the prosecutrix's house, and in about three minutes he came back with the door on his shoulder; two other men were with him; I overtook him with it - he said a gentleman had employed him to carry it to the Angel; the other two men went away when they saw me pursuing him - he said he did not know the gentleman.
   THOMAS HUNT. I went with Tuchin; I saw the door was gone from the house - we pursued and took the prisoner with it - he said, a man, whom he never saw before, was to give him sixpence to carry it to the Angel.
   EDWARD PALMER. I am a Bow-street patrol. I came up and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was out of employ, and accepted of this job; I did not know the door was stolen.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Confined Three Months.
 FOURTH DAY. MONDAY, APRIL 14.
 First Middlesex Jury. - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1002.  GEORGE CULLEY and THOMAS FAGAN were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of February , 1 cask, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Harris.
 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT DUKE. I am an officer of Hatton-garden. On the 28th of February I was in company with Bassett and Lloyd, near the house of Linton, at Islington-green; he is a retailer of table-beer, what is called a pinman; I saw the two prisoners about six o'clock in the evening, come there, and Culley had a firkin on his shoulder; they were walking in company together; I should think they were a mile and a half, or two miles from Mr. Harris'; he lives in the Hampstead-road; they went up the gateway to Linton's brewery, and set the cask down at Linton's door - they then went to a workshop in the same yard, next door to Linton's, made some inquiry, and then returned up the yard; I went to them, and asked what they had left the cask there for - they appeared confused, and I said, "What business had you to leave that cask there?" they said they had found it in Smithfield, and were going to take it home- I think it was Fagan who said he picked it up, and Culley said he was assisting him to carry it; it was a small cask, and did not require two men to carry it; I took them in charge, and fetched the cask from Linton's door.
   ANDREW LLOYD. I am an officer, and was with Duke- his evidence is correct; here is the cask - it has "T. Harris, Hampstead-road," on it, in large letters.
 THOMAS BASSETT. I am employed by brewers to watch their property. I was with the officers when this cask was taken.
 THOMAS HARRIS. I am a  brewer, and live in Hampstead-road. Fagan has lived in my service, and knew my premises well. This is my cask - it is worth 8s. 6d. I have lost a considerable number.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Fagan had been in your employ? A. Occasionally; Culley was not in my service - we loose casks in a variety of ways.
 MR. ALLEY. Q. Do you ever allow a cask to be sold? A. No; that is a fixed rule in the trade.
   THOMAS BISHOP. I live near Linton. The prisoners came to me on the evening in question, and asked if Linton was within - I said I believed he was; they went into the yard, returned, and said he was not; Fagan asked if I knew what public-house he used; I said I believed he was at the Fox, at the corner.
 CULLEY'S Defence. This man asked me to help him.
 FAGAN'S Defence. I picked up the cask.
 FAGAN -    GUILTY. Aged 37.
 CULLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 44. Transported for Seven Years.
 1003.  THOMAS FAGAN was again indicted for stealing, on the 28th of February , 3 casks, value 24s., the goods of Thomas Harris; and SAMUEL LINTON was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to have been stolen.
   THOMAS PERRY. I am drayman to the prisoner Linton, who carries on business at Islington-green; he sells small casks of table-beer- I have seen Fagan there several times, selling casks. One day about the 30th of November, he brought a porter cask, marked "T. Harris, Hampstead-road;" he saw Linton, and said, "I have brought you one of your casks:" they walked out together, as if they were going to have a glass; Linton used to pay 2s. for them; I saw no money pass that day. About December Fagan came and sat a cask down - Linton came and asked if I had any money - I gave him 6s.; he gave Fagan 2s. for it - it was marked with Mr. Harris' name; Fagan came to me when I was washing casks in the yard, and said, "Is it not a shame, the old man does not allow more than 2s. for the casks - he sells them for 4s. 6d."
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long have you lived with Linton? A. Four or five months; I left last Saturday week: before that I lived with Mr. Mitchell, of Hackney.
 Q. Were you discharged for dishonesty? A. I am not compelled to read my life to you; I was discharged once: I had been about three weeks with Linton when Fagan brought the first cask - I did not inform Mr. Harris of it.
