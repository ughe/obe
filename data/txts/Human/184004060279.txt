ROBERT SAMUEL PEARSON. I am a rafter in the Commercial Dock, and live with my mother, in Russell-street, Rotherhithe. The prisoner came to lodge there on the evening of the 2nd of March—he slept with me that night—I got up at half-past six o'clock next morning, and went to work, leaving him in bed—I did not miss my clothes till that evening, when I went to put them on—they had been hanging up in the room where we slept—these are my jacket, trowsers, and shoes—( looking at them. ) CHARLES HENRY FALCONER. I am an officer. The prisoner was given into custody to me—I found on him two duplicates, which led to where these articles were. JAMES WALTER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Thornton-street, Rotherhithe. This jacket and trowsers I took in from the prisoner on the 3rd of March. THOMAS BAKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Free-school-street. I took in a pair of shoes of the prisoner, on the 5th of March.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I got up at seven o'clock and missed my money out of my pocket; I did not know what I should do, and took his things to the pawn shop (not knowing the English law) to get my money back again. GUILTY. Aged 36.— Confined Four Months.

MARY SEARSTON was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of March , 1 coat, value 3 l  .; 1 pair of breeches, value 1 l  .; 1 waistcoat, value 9 s  .; 2 frocks, value 6 s  .; 4 yards of linen, value 6 s  .; and 1 shawl, value 2 s  .; the goods of Eli Webb , her master; and that she had been before convicted of felony. ELI WEBB. I keep a beer-house at Clapham. The prisoner was in my service for six months—she left on the 10th of March without notice—I went up stain, and missed a coat, waistcoat, and the other articles which are here, and are my property. HENRY DENMAN. I am in the service of Mr. Folkard, a pawnbroker at Vauxhall. These articles were pledged by the prisoner at different times previous to the 19th of March. CHARLES BURGESS GOFF( police-constable L  31.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Lawson's office, the clerk of the peace for Surrey—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY.
