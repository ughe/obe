   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Does not that consist of your good-will obtained from running your omnibuses at certain hours from Pad dington down to the Bamk? A.  Certainly.
   Re-examined. Q.  There were certain times allotted to you to run; what times werethey, three minutes? A.  Yes; I had six times allocated to me in the day—the other parties were not allowed to run at the same minutes—If any strangers have interfered, I was to do the best I could with them—I believe strangers have interfered, I do not recollect—there were arrangements made by the committee if a stranger interfered, but I can hardly tell what, as I so seldom attended—I do not recollect being there when any stranger had interfered—It is some time since I was there—I cannot tell whether, when I was there last, anything was stated by the committee of any arrange ment having been made before, of what was to be done when any stranger interfered—there was none that I recollect. BENJAMIN BALL I am a perfumer, and live in Oxford-street. I have observed Mr. Bray's omnibuses, and the conduct of the other omnibuses towards them—I know the "Emperor" and the "Albion"—we have been very much annoyed by these omnibuses running one against another—about six weeks back I wanted to get into Bray's omnibus, and the "Em peror" came right against the steps, so as to prevent my getting in—I have seen such conduct on several occasions—the "Emperor" and the "Al bion" appeared to obstruct Mr. Bray's omnibuses by running—when any person wanted to get into Mr. Bray's, the "Emperor" would run across the steps, sometimes their pole came almost on the pavement.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Has Mr. Bray's gone at a quick pace along the street? A.  Yes, Sir; I have seen it go at a quick pace—I have seen it run to get out of the way of the others—I have not seen it racing parallel with them, because they have gone before him or behind his—he has gone about six miles an hour—I will not swear he has not gone eight—I never knew of their doing anything improper—I should not be surprised if they had been summoned for furious driving; that is what oc curs every day.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Do you mean that the "Emperor" and the "Albion" drew up? A.  It was the "Emperor" that drew up—the "Albion" impedes by getting before the omnibus—I have seen them both with it—I have generally seen the "Emperor" be hind, and the "Albion" before—I think so—I think there are several om nibuses called by those names.
