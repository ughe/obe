   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You were all very sober that night? A.  Yes—I am a labourer, and work for my father at jobbing, or any thing—he is a pork-butcher, and keeps a shop at Romford—I serve in the shop but very little—I work in the field sometimes, and sometimes in the shop—I do not get my living at fairs and races—we had only been in one booth that night—I he Crown and Anchor—I know Cutts from living close by—he is a gardener—I did not hear Cutts say, "I want to fight, but the b—r won't let me"—my brother and I had had a bottle of porter together—my sisters were with us—we had three bottles between us all—I here were six or seven of us—my brother, my two sisters, Carter, a horse-dealer, and myself—I cannot tell who paid for the drink—I did not—I believe Carter did—he stood treat—we found the fight going on as we were returning from the fair—I went up to them as, I saw Cutts lying down, and the prisoner holding him by the throat—I did not say, "Pitch into him, the b—," nor did I hear it said—my brother did not say so—he never interfered at all—I did not hear Cutts say, "I want to fight, but the b—won't let me"—he was standing up when I went by, and he called to me—he was afraid of getting killed, I pose—I was taking a walk at the fair—I had no pea and thimble—the prisoner's wife was stand ing alongside when Cutts called out to me—I went up to take the prisoner off Cutts—I did not pull his wife's petticoats over her head—I did not pull her petticoats up at all, nor meddle with her, only by shoving  her away, as she was kicking Cutts—I did not take hold of her by her shoul ders—that I swear—as I went to take the prisoner away she hit me, and I pushed her away—my arm came about her shoulder—I did not strike her with my fist—she fell—I did not take her by the shoulders before she fell—her petticoats did not go up at all.
   MR. RYLAND . Q.  Did you do anything indecent to her clothes or any thing? A.  I did not—I did not go up to interfere till Cutts was on the ground. JAMES BAILEY. I work as a gardener, at Romford. I was present and saw the best part of the fight between Cutts and the prisoner, and staid till it was over—after it was over I saw the prisoner go and strike Joseph Axon in the side two or three times, with a knife I pose, but I did not know it was a knife when he struck him—my brother John was there—I went up to the prisoner and asked if he had got a knife in his hand—he said, "Yes, and I mean to use it too"—I hat was after he had used it to the prosecutor—he had it in his hand—I did not see him put it any where—I saw the blade of a knife in his hand—that was about half a minute after I saw him strike Axon in the side—I afterwards saw Axon bleeding.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How far does Axon live from Fairlop-fair? A.  Between three and four miles—he walked home—I had not been along with Axon in the fair.
   COURT . Q.  Did you notice when the prisoner's wife was thrown down? A.  No, I did not—I saw her pulled away, and it was almost immediately after that I saw the prisoner strike Axon. GUILTY  . Aged 24.— Strongly recommended to mercy on account of the provocation. — Confined Six Months.
