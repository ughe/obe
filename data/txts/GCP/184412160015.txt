203. JOHN GRAHAM, alias Jones, and JAMES BARRETT were
indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John
Williams, on the 28th of Nov., at St. James's, Clerkenwell, and stealing
therein 1 waistcoat, value 6d., bis property.
MARY ANN WILLIAMS. I live with my father John Williams, in
Kine-street, Clerkenwell; he is a tailor. On the 28th of Nov., a little
after eight o'clock in the evening, I was in the shop, and noticed both the
prisoners at the window-while they were there, I heard a pane of glass
broken, and then saw the arm of the prisoner Barrett through the window
- he took a waistcoat and ran away-I did not see what became of
Graham-Barrett had worked for my father.
Crass-examined by Mr. DOANE. Q. Were you at first in the shop, or
in the parlour behind the shop? 4. In the shop-I was not doing any-
thing I am quite sure I could see the persons outside I swear positively
to them I cried "Stop thief!" when I saw the waistcoat taken. al
JOHN WILLIAMS. I live in King-street, in the parish of St. James,
Clerkenwell-it is my dwelling-house. The waistcoat was worth 6d.
Barrett had worked for me about nine days before.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Is your window common glass?
A. Yes.

CHARLES SHEPHERD SWEETMAN. I live in Ratcliff-row, Bath-street. I
heard the cry of “Stop thief !” and saw the prisoner Graham running along
-- he threw the waistcoat away-I saw it picked up-it was the one pro-
duced-I secured Graham till the policeman camehe struck me several
times, and threw me down twice to get away--when he was stopped he
said, "I did not break the window, nor throw away the waistcoat."

I do not believe he was.
JAMES EDWARD SARGEANT (police-constable G 122.). I received the

times to separate me from him, and pulled him with considerable violence.
MR. WILLIAMS. This is my waistcoat.
