On Sunday morning, the 25th of June, I was on duty at the Abbey-walk, West Ham-lane, near Print ers'-buildings—I then saw Craske and Mascall—in consequence of informa tion I received from them I went round to the back of Printers'-buildings through the passage that divides them—when I got to the back—I saw a man run through the passage opposite to me, in the direction where I had left Craske and Mascall—I went back and found them where I left them—I then sprang my rattle, and Mr. Tucker's watchman came up—I gave Craske and Mascall into his custody—I then went to Mr. Vooght's premises—I sprang my rattle—Mr. Vooght came down—I examined the cellar-door and the tiles, and then went with him to the wall—I there found some bottles of wine in a basket—I then returned and searched the cellar—I afterwards searched the orchard, and between the corner of the wall and the back of Mrs. Thornbill's premises there is a boarded fence—I saw a broken bottle on the grass near a hole in the fence which runs across the garden—I also found this centre-bit near to Mr. Vooght's back door, which was open.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did the man you saw run in the direction towards Printers'-buildings? A.  Yes, in the direction of Mascall and Craske.

I know the Cholera-houses—I do not know whether Wilks lives there—I found a bottle in a ditch opposite the Cholera-bouses—there are only two houses—I tasted it, but do not know what was in it—it was a stone-bottle—it made me rather giddy—I put it into the stable of Mr. Wagstaff's yard—the bottle is here now. BENJAMIN HOLLOWAY( a prisoner. ) I am now in Newgate on a charge of stealing two duplicates—I know Brockwell and Barker by sight—I went of my own accord to see Barker's sister at a lodging-house in Keate's-court, Keste-street, Spitalfields—I do not know the day of the month—I did not then know of this robbery—I saw his sister—she told me her two brothers were taken—it was on a Tuesday, about eight weeks ago, as near as I can recollect—I first saw Barker about five o'clock the same evening to Kente's-court, with Brockwell—I went there the next day—I found them there then—they were out when I first went—I went to see his sister, as I was a friend of hers—I was there about an hour and a half—they were out at the time—I went, altogether, three or four times to Keate's-court, and saw Barker and Brockwell each time—they asked me if I would go down to their houses for some bottles which contained some ketchup, and whether I would bring them away—I said, Yes—both of them asked me to do it—they did not tell me where I should find the bottles—they said a person down there would give them to me when I got there—they said they were in the habit of making a lot of ketchup—I did not know their houses-Barker said his home was No. 37, Tucker's-buildings—I do not know where Brockwell's house is—I did not go down about the bottles—while I was there a person came in and said there was a robbery at Plaistow on the Sunday—we laid, "Was there indeed?"—Brockwell said, "We knowwho it is"—I do not know that person—a good many persons lodged at that house—the person said a good many people were taken, and Barker said, "Perhaps, if I had been down there, I should have been taken innocently"—Brockwell said the same, and said he should not like to be dragged through the streets for a thing that he knew no thing
