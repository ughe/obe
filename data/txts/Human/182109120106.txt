 1268.  THOMAS RULE was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , the sum of 3 s., in monies numbered, the property of John M'Pherson.
 JOHN M'PHERSON. I  keep a public-house in Globe-lane, Mile-end. The prisoner was an occasional customer. On the 26th of August, he came for a pipe of tobacco, I looked through a crevice in the kitchen door and heard the lock of the till go. I ran out, pulled him over the counter, with half-a-crown and sixpence in his hand; he said it was the first time he served me so; I said I thought he had robbed me several times - he acknowledged robbing me six times.
 Prisoner's Defence. I pulled out 3 s. to see what money I had, the gentleman came and said, "You rascal, you have been robbing the till."
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.

 1270.  GEORGE ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , one watch, value 14 s.; one seal, value 1 s., and two keys, value 1 d., the property of Anthony Carvile , from his person.
   ANTHONY CARVILE. I am a seaman. The prisoner came out of the London-docks, with a pipe in his hand - he passed me, came back, threw his pipe down, pushed me, and took my watch out of my pocket - he got away with it.
   JOSEPH KING. I was in the Red Lion, public-house, Nightingale-lane, I saw the prisoner there, he followed me and the prosecutor out, and by the London-docks he turned back; I saw him push the prosecutor down and snatch his watch - I followed and secured him, and with assistance, took the watch from him. Two or three came up and said, "Knock him down." I was obliged to let him go - he was taken next day. I am sure he is the man, he was quite sober.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN CLIFTON. I am a patrol. About half-past ten o'clock, I was informed of the robbery, and had a description of the man, I took him in half-an-hour; he said the man had got his watch again and it was nothing to me - he was sober.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much in liquor. The man said at the office that somebody else took it.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1271.  JAMES BULL was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of May , four sacks, value 10 s., and sixteen bushels of beans, value 3 l., the goods of Isaac Ennos.
   ISAAC ENNOS. I am a miller and corn-dealer, and live at Wilsden. On the 19th of May, 1820, a man who I believe to be the prisoner (but had rather not swear to him) came and asked how I sold beans, I said, 34 s. a quarter - he offered 33 s., and told me to send them to-morrow, or it would not do - I sent my man with them to No. 98, Chapel-street, Marylebone- I did not see the prisoner afterwards, he was to send the money back by the man, on delivery - I did no mean to part with them without the money; I gave the carter a receipt - he said he lived at No. 40, there is no such number. He bought them for himself, and said his name was Wilson, Chapel-street.
   JOHN BARNES. I am carman to Mr. Ennos. The prisoner met me at the corner of Bell-street, and asked if I had any corn for him, I said, I had some for somebody, and shewed him the bill - he said, "It is for me, we will take them to the shoemaker's shop below, and pitch one quarter at the door, and take the other up stairs" - I pitched both quarters at the door; he asked if I had any bran, I said No; he said he would get the money and sacks ready by when I returned - I went down the wharf, returned and he was gone, sacks and all - I did not intend to leave them without the money - I am sure he is the man.
 Prisoner. Q. When I met you, did you not say they were for me - A. No; I shewed you the bill, you said, "That is right."
   ANN DAVIS. The man brought the beans and pitched them at my door, and said he would fetch the cart and horse to take them away; he brought a strange cart and horse and took them away - he did not live at my house - he came three weeks before to take a room which I had to let; another man took them away.
   JOHN TUNKS. I carted the beans from Davis's house to Tottenham-court-road for the prisoner, he is the man; he never paid me, but said he had another job - I cannot positively swear that he is the man.
   JOHN LIMBRIC. I had information of this robbery, twelve months ago; I have been looking after the prisoner ever since.
   WILLIAM MASON. I live at Camden-town. About eighteen months ago, I bought two quarters of beans of the prisoner, not knowing whose they where, he brought them between ten and eleven o'clock in the day, I took them to Salisbury's, a neighbour, weighed them, and gave him 2 l. 6 s., and a pot of porter for them; he took the sacks back - the market price was 2 l. 8 s.
   ISAAC ENNOS. They were worth 3 l. 18 s.
