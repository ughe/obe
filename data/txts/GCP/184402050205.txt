Josiał VINCENT (police-constable R 196.) On Monday morning the 8th
Jan., about half-past two o'clock, I was on duty in Church-street, Deptford,
and saw the prisoner crossing Church-street, on the footpath of the railway,
towards town, with a large bundle at his back--being an unseasonable hour
I went after him and asked what he had in the bundle--he said that he
was a servant, that he had just left his situation, and had got his dirty
clothes; that he had met with a friend and had taken a glass-I asked if
they were his own-he said, “ Yes"_I took him to the station, and found
the bundle contained two live pheasants, four live pigeons, two dead ones,
seven dead fowls, and two dead ducks, and no dirty clothes--I showed the
things to Waterhouse.

JOSEPH WATERHOUSE. I am gardener to Col. Thomas Perronet Thompson
-he had some pheasants, pigeons, fowls, and ducks, safe on the evening of the
7th Jan.--I missed them between eight and nine o'clock on the 9th-I have
since seen them in Vincent's hands, and know them to be the same--they
were kept in the stable and loft-the stable was not locked. B O UT
Prisoner. Q. When I lived with Col. Thompson, were these things kept in
the stable ? A. In the summer they run loose, but in winter they were closed
-I forget whether Col. Thompson had pheasants while you were in his sex
vice-the fowls used to be locked in the hen-house, and the key taken to a
drawer kept by young Mr. Thompson I can identify all the poultry as my
master's-I know the pheasants by a ring round their necks, and the cock
pheasant had lost its tail-they are English ring pheasants-I am quite sure of
the fowls and ducks--the yard-gate was open till ten o'clock-I do not know
whether it was locked after that.
