JAMES COLDMAN (police-constable M 159.) I received charge of Margaret
Flanagan on the 31st of May, at her master's ; and I saw Johannah Flanagan
Dear Mr. Russell's shop-I got from her a duplicate of a shawl-she said that
Margaret Flanagan had told her her mistress would be in want of the green

1546. JAMES LOFTUS BROCK was indicted for feloniously assaulting
Ann Brock, and cutting and wounding her on the left arm, with intent to do
her some grievous bodily harm.
ANN BROCK. I am the prisoner's wife-he is a lieutenant in the army on
hall-pay-we were married in 1820, and have three children-he is addicted
to drink, and when under the influence of drink he is generally very violent
- he has told me that he was not right in his head at times, and to be
cautious what I said to him when he was under the influence of drink-he
said he did not know what would be the end of it, as he knew his head was
bad-we live at No. 105, East-street-on the 3rd of June he came home
about half past ten in the morning-he bad been out all night, I think--he
was very much agitated indeed-be had been drinking-be went into Mr.
Crossland, the landlord's place I could not hear what was said, but I could
hear the tone of his voice-I do not know that he spoke to me-he kept
talking to himself for two or three hours- I went down to the yard-he
followed me round the yard- I went round because I was afraid he was
going to strike me-he had not said anything to make me think so, but
I saw he was in a violent passion-he did not say what he was in a passion
about-he went up stairs, I remained down till nearly twelve o'clock-he re-
mained quite still, and then I went up stairs-when I got up he came behind
me with a knife I did not see it, but I felt bim stab me in my arm-he
made no observation at the time, that I heard-I ran out of the room, went
down stairs, and got away-I did not hear him speak-my recollection is not
more faint now than it was before the Magistrate-he said nothing after he
stabbed me -- there was nothing said in the house at the time about my life
I do not know what was said out of the house, for the prisoner and the
policeman walked on together-my son was in the room, and struck the
prisoner's arm-I cannot say in what position the prisoner's band was then,
