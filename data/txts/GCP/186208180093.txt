MARY ANN SHEEN. I am a single woman, and live in Wells-street, Poplar!
I knew the deceased_I was at the Wellington on the day this occurred
I saw the deceased come up to the Wellington--the prisoner was then
in her mother's house, five doors from the Wellington-I afterwards saw her
running down the street towards the Wellington, with one side of her hair
hanging down-she was in a great passion-I saw her run up to the
deceased who was then standing behind the Wellington barshe catched her
by the side of the head ; this is the cap she catched her by (producing it);
and she drawed her over the bar--I tried to take her away from her as well
as I could the deceased's head came on the stairs, and the crack that her
head got on the stairs you could hear out of the door-I saw her fall with
the right side of her head on the stairs, she fell heavily-the prisoner's sister
aught hold of the prisoner by her two hands, not to let her do any more
to the deceased, but she put up her foot and kioked her twice with her heel
in the side of the head--the deceased was quite sober--the prisoner was
not rightly sober, she had taken a little, but nothing out of the way.

WILLIAM Bain. I am a surgeon at Poplar on 29th July, I was called
to see the deceased- I found her in bed and almost insensible she com-
plained of pain in her head, and put her hand up to it- she gradually got
worse, and died on 6th August- afterwards opened the body-I found s
considerable quantity of clotted blood on the right side of the head, between
the membranes of the brain--that had caused insensibility and death-she
was paralysed in consequence of the compression---those appearances were
quite consistent with the fact of her having received an injury on 14th July
-a violent fall with her head against the stairs on 14th July, might have
produced the condition in which she was-ber general health was good
had known her for a long time, it is consistent with that state of things,
that she might have walked about after receiving the injury; that is very
usual.
COURT. 0. Do you mean that you cannot account for her derth upon any
other supposition than that she must have had a blow or falli 4. It is
quite possible that great excitement might cause a rupture of a vessel, as we
find in apoplexy, without a blow or fall; at the same time I think that a
blow or fall was the cause of death; that appeared to me to be more proba-
ble—if she had fallen with her head against the stairs on 14th July, that
would account for the condition in which I found her head.
