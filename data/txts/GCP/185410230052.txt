MR. DUNCAN. Q. Is this a bill? A. Yes; from his schoolmaster-he
has been in the hospital since he left school; he was there five or six weeks
-blood came from him-he is very bad.
COURT. Q. You were working in Thames-street! A. Yes-I live near
Kent-street-I suppose that is very nigh a mile from Thames-street-the
boy did not leave home with me; he came over about 10 o'clock-I went
to have a pint of beer at Fresh Wharf Tavern—my wages are not due
soon as I finish my job, I then make out the bill-I finished this job on
Friday night-sometimes I do not get it for a week; I might get it in an
hour, perhaps I might be a fortnight before I got a ship, and might have
three or four ships—it is piece work.
MR. RUSSELL. Q. Do you get 3d. an hour ? A. 18. or 28. an hour;
I have got to pay men, some 38. a day and a pint of beer out of that
-two men and myself had been employed in that ship-I have not
paid them.
MR. DUNCAN. Q. You say you have worked thirty years? A. Thirty
years-my character is well known; that policeman knows me (The polico-
man referred to stated that he did not).
TIMOTHY MOLLONY. I live in High-street, Borough. I am a labouring
man--the prisoner, John Mollony, is my son—I remember last Saturday
week, 14th Oct.; I gave him 138. 6d., between 8 and 9 o'clock, to go to a
pawnbroker's (handing in two duplicates)— I cannot read these duplicates-
the mother gave him the money to get something ont of pawn—that 138. 6L
was made up of two half crowns, a 28. piece, and 6s. 6d. in silver-my wife
is here.
Cross-examined Q. How came you to give him this money this parti-
cular morning? A. I will tell you—we are paid on Friday night, I had
not pledged these particular things myself; the mother, I suppose, has
pledged them-all I know is, I recollect I gave my son these particular
coins-the mother sent him-I last saw him between 8 or 9 o'clock in the
morning-that was at my own house in the Borough, close to the church,
in Bullings-court.
MR. DUNCAN. Q. Where do you work? A. At Mr. Cohen's; they are
of the Jewish persuasion; they do not work on Saturday, nor me-I am
paid on Friday night-it was part of my wages that I gave to the boy; 1
gave the remainder, all but sixpence--I have worked at Mr. Cohen's for
about five years.
ELLEN MOLLONY. I am the wife of the last witness, and the mother of
the boy. On last Saturday morning week, I wanted the boy to get three
things out of pawn-there was one a 9d. ticket-these two tickets are a 9d.
ticket and a 108. ticket-there was another ticket for 28., for a shawl-all
the things were pawned at the same place, Mr. Blizures-I pawned them
myself-I told him to make haste with the shawl : I wanted that the first
thing—that was the 28. pawn-I know he had the 138. 6d., his father gave
it him ) said, “ Johnny, bring me the shawl "-the money being taken
from hin, I have not got the other things.
Cross-examined. Q. He brought the shawl back? A. Yes.
LEONARA LYONS. I am the mother of the prisoner Lyons. He is
thirteen years of age; he jobbed about the Borough market, carrying luggage,
when he could get a job--he had one shilling, that is all.
