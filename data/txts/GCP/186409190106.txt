LAWRENCE, Mayor.
Before Mr. Recorder.
918. JOSEPH BAYLIS (49), and JOSEPH HEMPSTED (55), Stealine
shawls, the property of Edwin Ewin, the master of Baylis.
MR. BESLEY conducted the Prosecution, MR. GIFFARD defended Baylis, and
MR. ORRIDGE defended Hempsted.
EDWIN Ewin. I am a draper, in High-street, Borough-Baylis was in
my service as shawl-buyer and salesman-he served behind the counter
previous to the day I gave him into custody I gave some directions to a
police-constable on Friday, 20 September, about 11 in the morning, I saw
Hempsted in custody--I was fetehed from the City-I had seen him many
times before in my shop, but I did not know his name or his business-
Baylis has been in the shop at the same time, and Hempsted has waited till
he was disengaged, I fancy-I never saw bim buy any goods in the place
I went with the officer after Hempsted was taken, and fetched two shawls
which had been left at a public-bouse close by-I brought them into the
shop, and said, “Who sold these shawls 9"-Baylis immediately replied, "I
did "-I said, “What did you sell them for "-he said, “ Seven shillings."
-I went and looked on the check-file, and found that 78. had been paid to
the cashier-I then said to Baylis, “This is a very strange business,
your selling such goods as these for 78.," and I then inquired of the met
generally what was the cost of the shawls--one dost 68. and the other
98. 6d. or 98. 9d.--I knew from the tickets what they had cost, i marked
them myself—I have not got the tickets; they were not on the shawls when
I got them from the Black Bull publie-house-one ticket was produced by
a young man-I saw it, but I imagine it was destroyed—there are no tickets
now on the shawls—there are tickets on all goods that go out of my house,
with the selling price marked in characters, not figures-I know the value
of the goods; the value, to buy them, is 188. 6d. the two—the price, to a
wholesale buyer, would be 178.--I said to Baylis," It is a very strange thing
that you sell goods like this," and he said, “I know I have done wrong; I
will make it up if you will forgive me"-Baylis had directions as to price-
if it was a wholesale customer he was to sell at the wholesale price, and if a
retail customer at retail price; no deviation whatever I gave those diree-
tions to Baylis, and to every other salesman-I took this piece of paper
(produced) off the cashier's file; it is Baylis's handwriting-it is not signed
by any one--it ought to have been signed by some one-every bill ought to
be examined by another person, and bis initials put on it, to show that it is
correct; and in case the bill is wrong, he is fined for examining it wrong-
these shawls are my property—they bave been in my shop
