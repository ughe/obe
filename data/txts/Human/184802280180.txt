JOHN WEATHERHEAD. I am station-master at the Bricklayers' Arms station of the South Eastern Railway—between 300 and 400 person are employed there—some of them have access to the stores—japan and turpentine are kept there in large quantities—one of these cans contains japan, and the other turpentine—the company had property of this sort in their store in such cans—I am positive of this japan-can—here is writing on a former label which has been partly torn off this other can—I would not swear positively to it, but it corresponds in all respects with those we have; and so does the japan and turpentine—I have brought some from the station—this is worth about 2 l . 4 s . with the cans—I never saw the prisoner till he was in custody—people could get to the fence by going across a field.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You do not know anything of the qualities of japan or turps? A.  I do, by seeing the men use it constantly—I do not think there are 100 two-gallon cans similar to these. JAMES BISSICKS. I am in the employ of Charles Turner, a varnish-maker—he supplies japan and turps to the South Eastern Railway Company—I have tested this japan—it corresponds in all respects with the japan supplied to the company by Mr. Turner—the part of a label on one can has my writing on it—I believe both these cans are part of the stores supplied to the company—both bear a "T," the initial of Mr. Turner's name.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you gave the can with that writing on, you are not aware? A.  No—part of it is broken off—whether that japan was all used I cannot tell. PHILIP FROWED. I am inspector of the M division of police. I knew Thomas Marshall, the policeman in this case—he died since last Sessions—this is his writing to this deposition—( read—"Thomas Marshall, policeman, M  254, says ):—On Monday last, 17th instant, about twenty minutes to six o'clock, I saw the prisoner carrying the two cans produced; Brightman gave me information; I stopped the prisoner, and asked what he had there; he said, 'Some turps;' I asked where he was going; he said, 'Home;' I asked him where; he said, 'Come with me and you shall see;' I asked him where he had been, and where he had brought it from; he said from home, and he was going to take it back again; Brightman said he would show where the prisoner had brought it from, and we all went together to the Williow-walk; Brightman pointed out the passage from which he had seen him bring the cans; the prisoner said he had been there to case himself; I asked how he came to be there; he said he had been to Camberwell, and afterwards to the Swan in the Old Kent-road; both of those places are out of the line of road; the prisoner tole me the cans contained about two quarts of turps; I found one contained about seven quarts of turps, and the other two quarts of japan.")
