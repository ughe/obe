   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You any Webber was intoxicated? A.  He was at eleven o'clock in the morning—Tite said he knew nothing about it in the first instance, and afterwards he acknowledged to being along with Webber when he carried it.
   Q.  Did you ever say here to-day that he was along with Webber when he carried it? A.  I said that he said to Jewell. "It was him that carried the lead, and sold it to your brother Sam, was not it?"—I have told you his exact words—he did not say at that time that he was along with him.
   JURY . Q.  Was it from his words you drew the inference? A.  Yes.
   Frost. Q.  When did you see me first? Witness A.  About eleven o'clock that morning, at Shadwell—you were drunk—I did not drink with you to make you drunker, to my knowledge—I did not go into a public-house, and call for a not of beer—I did not know Tite till after I had been to Jewell's I took Webber with me—he had then got sober—he was with me from eleven o'clock in the morning till three in the afternoon—he had 9 1/2 d ., and I allowed him to have some beer—he begged very hard.
   COURT . Q.  When was the public-house he had it at? A.  I rather think it was at the corner of Grove-street—upon my oath I did not drink with either of the prisoners after taking them into custody—they offered me the pot, but I did not drink.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  What time did you go to the public-house? A.  It was about three o'clock, after I had taken Tite—Webber was sober then; as far as a man could be that had been tipsy—I suppose 4 d . was paid for the liquor—I did not see it paid—9 1/2 d . was taken from him at the King David-land station—I cannot tell the sign of the public-house—it was the first time I ever was in it—I was not on duty at the time—the man begged hard to have a drop of beer, and I allowed him to have it—there were three of them—there was a friend of Tite's.
