ANN SANSUM. I am the wife of James Sansum, who keeps the Cheshire Cheese, Dean-street, Holborn. On the 13th of Feb., the prisoner came to me—I let her have a lodging, and did not charge her anything for it—these sheets and blankets were in the room—next morning she went out—I went into the room, and missed them—I locked the door, and went down stairs—I then went up to the room again, and the prisoner was sitting on the I spoke to her about these things—she said she had not seen them—she walked into the room, and I locked her in while I went to her husband. ELEANOR DUNN. I am the wife of Martin Dunn. I keep a clothes' shop—the prisoner came to my shop, and brought this blanket to dispose of and about half-past eleven she brought one sheet, and between two and three the other sheet—these are them—I am quite sure she is the person.
   Prisoner.  I was distressed—I took the first articles to get some breakfast, and the second to get them some dinner—I told the landlady I would get them out again—I told Mrs. Dunn I did not sell them—I left one for 7 d ., the other for 6 d ., and the other for 9 d .—I said I must have them at night. ELEANOR DUNN re-examined.  When she brought them I said, "Are they yours?"—she said, "Yes, my dear, I want to purchase bread for my children." ANN SANSUM re-examined.  When we were at the police-office next morning, the prisoner said, "I meant to have told you where I had taken them to." GUILTY. Aged 45.— Confined Two Months .

PATRICK COCKLING was indicted for Stealing, on the   25th of Nov ., 3 1/2 yards of rope, value 1 s  . 4 d  .; 1 paint pot, 2 d  .; 1 brush, 4 d  .; 1 quart of plaister of Paris, 6 d  .; 1 yard of chain, 6 d  .; 1 chisel, 1 d  .; 1 awl, 1 d  .; and 1 gimlet, 1 d  .; the goods of John Charles Felgate , his master. JOHN CHARLES FELGATE. I am a builder, and live at Stepney. The pri soner was in my service—I knew where he lived—I had occasion to go to his house on the 25th of Feb.—I there found this plaister of Paris, this scaffold cord, and other articles—they all belong to me—the prisoner was my carman—he had not the use of these things, and had no occasion to have any of them at his house—I was using this brush just before Christmas.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILDE . Q.  Have you many persons in your em ploy? A.  Yes, bricklayers and labourers—I used this brush for priming over the wood—it is a very particular brush—I have used it many times—the handle got splintered and hurt my hand, so I cut it, and rounded it off—I did not miss this rope till I found it at the prisoner's—I do not sell these things—I had not seen this rope on my premises for the last seven or eight weeks—I know it by the binding of it—all my ropes are bound at the end—most ropes are dipped, but mine are not—some are bound with fine twine, and some with coarse twine—I cannot state when I had this gimlet, but I swear to it as I have used it so many hundred times—my tools are kept in a box in my shop—if my workmen required an instrument they might use my tools—the last time I used this chisel might have been before Christmas—I bad a lot of this chain hanging on hooks in the shop—this piece was the shortest amongst it—I had four pieces be sides this—this one has been often used for chaining the gate—I recollect buying five pieces of chain—I was particular in what I brought away from the prisoner's—I left several pieces of rope there, because I would not bring anything that I was not sure of—I know this brad-awl—I have used it so many times.
   COURT . Q.  Had he any authority to take them? A.  No—I never sold him any.
