   Q.  As you believed he was the man who did this wicked deed, and did not disclose it because he desired you not, why do it on Monday? A.  I thought it was right to speak what I knew—I did not know the surgeon had been examined when I made my statement—I knew a doctor had been called in—I cannot say what day he went before the Coroner—Monday was the last day of the Inquest—I was in a room by myself during all the proceedings on Monday—Mrs. Haggerstone bid me "Good night," after the till-treatment, as if nothing had happened.
   Q.  Now, did you not expect, by making the declaration you did, against the prisoner, on Monday, that you might save yourself by it? A.  No; I did not—I knew myself innocent of it—I did not make the statement on Thursday because he wanted me no to do it.
   COURT . Q.  Are you a married man? A.  Yes; I lived at Saunders's beer-shop, at this time—I live at Paddington when I am at home—I did not call for assistance when the outrage was committed on the woman—she never called out at all—I do not know whether I am taller than the prisoner—I did not consider I had any business between man and wife—the woman never called out, and I did not interfere—I never said a word to the Coroner, about his saying, "D—n you, if you don't go your ways, I will murder you"—I cannot tell whether I am taller and stouter than the prisoner, or not—he was in front of the woman when he gave her the blow. WILLIAM DEKINS. I was neat Mr. Wyatt's lane on the Saturday night, on which Mrs. Haggerstone was found in the well, about half-past twelve o'clock—Wyatt's lane is not exactly in a direction between Saunders' beer-shop and Haggerstone's—it is close at the side—it lies to the right in going from Saunder's to Winding-shot-field—it is close by the field—I heard two persons talking, over in the field as I came round by Mr. Wyatt's—I heard two voices, whether male or female, I cannot tell—one of them either said, "D—n my eyes I have been to the Crown," or "I will go to the Crown"—I cannot tell which—the well is about 100 yards from where I heard the voices—they were talking loud—they appeared to me to be fall ing out—I heard nothing more in the lane; but after I went home I heard a dreadful scream once. SARAH STEEL.
