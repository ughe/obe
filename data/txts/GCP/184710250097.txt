killing and slaying Matilda Clifton.
MR. BALDWIN conducted the Prosecution.
MARGARET Goodwin. I live at No. 6, Beauchamp-street, Leather-lane-
the prisoner and his wife resided in that house-ber name was Matilda. I
was called on Sunday morning, the 3rd of Oet., about two o'clock, by the
prisoner's child- I went to the front attic, the room they lived in, and found
the deceased lying on the floor insensible--she was supported by her hus-
band-be said it was a very unfortunate job, and asked me what he should
do I told him he had better get a medical gentleman, and he went—while he
was gone, I laid the deceased on the floor, and applied some vinegar-Mr.
Caldcleugh came immediately and examined her person-she had come to
her senses before he came- I was with her she told me that her husband
had kicked her--she made use of a coarse expression-she was not sober-
I did not see any outward appearance of injury-there was a quantity of blood
on the floor-cold water was applied to stop the discharge-I put a large shawl
mund the lower part of her person-she was not a sober woman-she had
complained of floodings several times during the last few months--I have
reason to believe that complaint was well founded- I had seen her on the
Saturday night, the night before, about ten o'clock-she was then quite
well, and in her usual spirits-she had said pothing to me on the subject of
flooding that day I afterwards examined the clothes she had on at the time
I found her-I did not find them rent or torn.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. The flooding soon ceased? 4.
Yes, immediately I laid her down--that was before Mr. Caldcleugh came-she
was in the habit of complaining, and in ill-health latterly-she was of decidedly
intemperate habits.
