Mr. BALLANTINE objected to the reception of evidence of this nalure, it

judice against the prisoner in the minds of the Jury, by bringing to felonies
before them at the same time. MR. BODKIN urged its admissibilily as showing
the intention of the prisoner in the act which he did, in the same tay as more
than one uttering was proved in cases of forgery, or utlering counterfeit coin.
Mr. Justice CRESSWELL was of opinion that the question being whether the
act done, tcas done with a view to produce a particular result, it tas material
to show out of the prisoner's on mouth, that he tas aware that the course he
was pursuing tas likely to bring about that result, and that upon that ground
the evidence was relevant, and admissible.
