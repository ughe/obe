   Q.  Pray have you ever been in any difficulties? A.  Why, I certainly have been in difficulties—I have compounded with my creditors—the last time, I suppose, was about four months ago, about three months ago—I am not certain to the date—I was not a dealer in bills—my father-in-law was security—the whole of my debts was between 3000 l . and 4000 l .—I have only paid my trade creditors—the chief creditor was my father-in-law, for money which I received from him—he guaranteed the whole of the creditors 20 s . in the pound, with interest, which every creditor was paid in full—I think my trade debts might amount to 1100 l .—I will swear they amounted to 700 l .
   Q.  And that your father-in-law has paid 20 s . in the pound? A.  Not on that account—not on the last trading account—I mean the first.
   COURT . Q.  You were asked what your debts were three months ago, you said, between 3000 l . and 4000 l . and that your trade creditors amounted to 1100 l . A.  No—I wish to correct that.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How much have you paid in the pound to your cre ditors, with whom you last compounded? A.  About 5 s .—my father-in-law has secured it—I have not paid it—it is not due for about a fortnight or three weeks—he has given bills for it—he is a woollen-draper in Lawrence-lane—the whole of my debts then were between 3000 l . and 4000 l .—Messrs. Costers was not among my debtors—they were persons from whom I had goods in my line of trade—I was never insolvent before that to my knowledge, except when I compromised with my creditors, on account of advancing money on these deeds—that is nine or ten months ago—I advanced 1250 l .—I know Mr. Holding—I do not know that I owe him any thing—my father-in-law has Undertaken to pay the whole of my debts.
