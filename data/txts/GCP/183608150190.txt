MARY ANN FORD. I live in Philip-lane, my husband works in Al-
dersgate-street. I was in Silver-street on the 12th of August, and as I
came across from Silver-street, there was a young man with a bundle of
umbrellas under his left arm--they were covered with green baize-the
young man came across the road, and his right arm touched my left-an
umbrella-maker living in our court, made me take notice, and I followed
him down opposite the locksmith's—the people came running down the street
- he turned his head, saw them running, and threw down the umbrellas in
the gutter, a few yards from me- I did not go to the watch-house when the
prisoner was taken-I do not know him--the man had dirty looking white
trowsers on-the prisoner is not the young man I saw throw down the
umbrellas-I cannot say it was not him-1 saw no features because I was
bebind him, but I am sure he had dirty looking white trowsers when they
were thrown down there was a cry of "Stop thief" -- there were several
persons standing who might have taken him, but I could not see bis face.
JAMES Bouton. I am an appraiser, and live in Addle-street. On the
12th of August, I was returning home from Aldermanbury-I had arrived
near the end of Phitip-lane, when I heard the cry of "Stop thief," and
immediately afterwards saw two men run past me, one in white trowsers,
and the other in dark-the one in white trowsers was on the right hand side,
and the other on the left- I turned after them, and went down to the end
of the street-they then parted, the man in white trowsers to the right, and
the other to the left-two girls were following them-I returned towards
my own home, and when I got to Philip-lane, I found somebody in
custody-I then went to the station-house, and was refused admission
- I really cannot say whether the prisoner was either of those men.
