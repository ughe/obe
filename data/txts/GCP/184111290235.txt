Mr. RYLAND. Q. Had you any money by you at the time you re-
ceived the money from Glynn's? A. Not any-I paid away the whole of
that money when we took the Axe, except one 51. note-on the 28th of
October we let the house, and were paid 1701. in Bank of England notes
- I did not take the numbers and dates of those notes-there was one 201.,
three 101., ten 5l., and 161. in cash; therefore I had then eleven 5l. notes
altogether, including the one in question--when I counted the money in
the bag on the 29th of October, there was 1601. in it, consisting of one
201., three 101., eleven 5l, notes, and about 501. in gold; and I had about
51. in my pocket-I have lost about 301.; a 5l. note, a 101. note, and five
sovereigns.
Court. Q. How can you tell that the note in question was not one
of those you paid away? A. This was the only 51. pote I had in my
possession of those I received from Glynn's- I paid all the others away
-I am certain this was the note I retained-after I had paid away the
other 5l. notes, I looked at the one remaining, so as to know the number
of it-I swear that-I took the number of this note the day before I left
the Axe-it was the only note I then had in my possession, and it was in
my purse-here is the memorandum I made the note is dated July 13th,
1841, and No. 64319- I wrote that on the 27th of October, the day
before I left the Axe.
MR. RYLAND. Q. What was your reason for making that memoran-
dum, when you had only one 51. note left? A. Because I should know
it was my note--my husband took the other notes from Mr. Brown, who
took the Axe of us-after we went to the prisoner's mother's house on the
28th of October, we put the money altogether my husband had not ac-
cess to the bag-he never saw the money after he gave it to me-I never
told any body the number of this 5l. note, till it was shown to me--I
stopped it at the Bank-I went with my husband to do so, on the 18th or
19th, I think I do not know whether or not my husband took the paper
with him-I told them the number and date, and they stopped it-I do
not think I produced the paper-I think I stated the number and date
from memory-I believe the prisoner's mother takes in washing, and does
what she can for her livelihood - I believe it is a respectable house-I do
not know, I knew the prisoner a year and a half ago-she used to do
needlework for me-she goes out to needlework and dress-making-she
made me two dresses at home once, and also came to my house to work ;
and in September they went to live at this house, and she wrote me a note,
saying they had apartments to let, and would be happy to accommodate
is-she did no millinery at home, while I lodged there, nor did she go out
-she did nothing at all while I was there-her mother took in a little
washing, and there were other lodgers in the house-there was a lady, her
daughter, and son, in the first floor; a Mrs. Holt, her daughter, and niece,
and a little girl.
Q. Now, there being all those persons in the house, do you mean to
assert that you and your husband have not quarrelled about his getting
possession wrongfully of this money? A. No, not about the money-I
do not know whether there was a looking-glass smashed one day-I be-
lieve there was--my husband smashed it-we were quarrelling then-it
was not because I charged him with attempting to break open the drawer
where the money was, to get possession of it-(looking at a chisel)-1
