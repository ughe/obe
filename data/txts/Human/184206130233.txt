   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  You never saw the prisoner before? A.  No—it was about eight o'clock in the morning—I was about ten minutes in her company—I did not meet any body else—she went into a little shop and bought a farthing's worth of gooseberries—I know the shop, but I did not go in—I stood at the door—I saw her again about half-past ten the same night, at her own house—I was taken there by my mother and the policeman—I described her to my mother, and told her how she was dressed, when she was at the pawnbroker's.
   COURT . Q.  Are you sure she is the woman? A.  Yes—she was not dressed the same in the evening, but I knew her. MARTHA PARROT. I go to school with Elizabeth Cummings—I was near the pawnbroker's that day, and saw the prisoner speak to her—I am sure she is the woman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How was she dressed? A.  She had a dark lilac gown, and a white straw bonnet with black ribbon—she was not above a minute in my view—I saw her go to Mr. Roberts—she was in custody when I saw her again, and was dressed differently. ELIZABETH ANN SCALES. I was at the pawnbroker's, and saw Eliza beth Cummings there—I saw a woman touch her on the shoulder when she was at the steps—the woman had a white straw bonnet on trimmed with black ribbons—she had this dress on—( looking at one. ) WILLIAM HENRY FISH. I am a shoemaker. I know the prisoner—I saw her go out from her own house that day, about nine o'clock—she then had this dark dress on—not the one the witness has seen. ISABELLA CUMMINGS. I am the mother of Elizabeth Cummings—I gave her a sovereign—she came home and gave me information—I made inquiries, and went to the prisoner's house—I asked her if she knew my little girl—she said she never saw her before—I said, "You saw her this morning, between eight and nine o'clock, and took a sovereign from her" she exclaimed, "Good God! I have never seen the child." CHARLES STEER( police-constable R  198.) I took the prisoner—I said she must go to the station-house—she said she would not, but I took her—she said, "Good God! how did they find me out? how did they know I lived here?"—the prisoner's husband gave me these two dresses.
   MR. DOANE called SARAH PERRY. I live in High-street, Deptford. I know the prisoner—I saw her a little after nine o'clock that morning—she had a dark flower ed gown on, with a light flower, and a Tuscan bonnet trimmed with black. (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Two Months.
