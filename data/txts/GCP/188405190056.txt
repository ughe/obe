MR. A. METCALPE Prosecuted; MR. GEOGHEGAN Defended.
MICHAEL JACKSON HARRIS. I carry on business ia partnership with
another person as M. S. Poole and Co., cigar importers-on 12th Novem-
ber, 1883 we employed the prisoner as a traveller and entered into this
agreement produced) with him-he entered into our service under that
agreement-he had to account to us once a week, on a Saturday generally,
paying us the sums of money as paid to him without deducting anything
-in March I had an order from Mr. Allen to the amount of 31. 138. 6d.
I was never subsequent to 27th March paid three guineas by the prisoner
as coming from Mr. Allen-I was not paid 31. 138. 6d. paid to him on 5th
February-Miss Adams had an account with us—we were not paid
21. 168. 9d. subsequent to 19th April-these sums and several others
have never been accounted for by the prisoner.
Cross-examined. By the agreement the prisoner was to have 5 per
cent. on moneys received on his orders, to be settled every three months
he applied to us at the end of February for his commission-I did not pay
him, he was indebted 11. 198. to us-I told him 80-he did not ask to
look at the books I don't know that since then he has repeatedly called
at the office, I discharged him before April 8th–I have a doubt whether
I received this letter of April 8th-I did not give him three months
notice--this produced) is in the handwriting of one of the partners, he
might have given him notice in it in March - the partner is entitled to
Act on behalf of the firm-he is not here; he is Mr. Poole, and is very
il at Hastings—the prisoner got about 1621. worth of orders—the total
amount of commission paid him was 31. 88. 10d., we paid him in money
and goods--we had no receipt-I don't know that he claims 111. now, he
never said anything to me I have not seen him sinco 7th April–I 19-
ceived this letter dated 22nd March. (The prisoner in this stated that
had called on Saturday at 2.10 and trusted that he should be able to get in
different accounts.) He knew our business closed at 2 o'clock, and he
came after that so that we should not see him-he had 21. or 31. commis-
sion, I cannot swear to a pound, I have not got a copy of the receipt-I
did not get him to sign a statement of account between us-We entered the
amounts in our cash-book, it is not here I had notice at the Mansion
House to produce my books, I don't know that my solicitor had notice,
I did not think they would be required I have been served with a writ
for 3401. from wholesale houses--I have entered an appearance and
paid 1001. into Court-we gave the prisoner as & reason for giving him
notice to leave that we were going to close the business-we say in the
letter to him “Your system of not coming here on Saturday does not
suit us," but we gave him notice on account of giving up the business-
I think that was written, we did not say in that we were going to give
up the business—Mr. Poole manages the business-he has been ill some
time-he went away in March-he keeps the books when he is here-
the amount of commission due to the prisoner is not 111., but 58. 40.-
we have only received 531. 78. 10d. of the 1621.-after I had discharged
him he complained that he had lost business in consequence of written
circulars—he asked me one day whether I was going to send orders-
I said no, I had made inquiries about the people and should not execute
them-I said "The man has absconded”-I found out the defalcations
at the latter end of March, after I gave him notice to go I went to his
house and found he had gone three weeks since to New Cross-about
