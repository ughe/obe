We all went for a drive and I returned to London that night. That is the only time I have ever been in Southend. I went to Herne Bay at the invitation of the Mayor of Stepney, Alderman Hirst, to inspect the children of the Scattered Homes. I dined with Hirst. Cal  cutt was already down there, I believe ill; I did not set him. I returned that same evening. I frequently called at Calcutt's office about the repairs he was doing on my houses. Heward sometimes sent me round to the house to see Calcutt. I frequently had drinks with Calcutt, I paying as frequently as he. I had one turkey from Calcutt, at Christmas, 1905. As we Jews do not eat meat or poultry not killed according to Jewish methods, I cut it up and gave it to two poor Gentiles I was assisting. There is not a word of truth in Calcutt's statement that he gave me £10. I had been financing as a sleeping partner a builder named Nash. I lost about £960 on the transaction. I gave him money to pay all debts, in cluding one of £23 to Smith and Hill which he did not pay. Calcutt, being a customer of Smith and Hill, I asked him to try and settle it for £10; he did not succeed. I afterwards got old George Barker, who was also a customer of Smith and Hill, to try and settle it, which he did for £15, which I paid him. I had a few small houses. When I got on the Board Calcutt had been asking me to give him some work, and in 1905 I got him to do repairs. As I am a man of small means, especially after my building losses, I told him to send in his accounts quickly—not to run long bills. I continually asked for the account. I was applying for a renewal of the lease and had to make good dilapidations. I went with Calcutt to his solicitor, Forbes, and asked him to raise money for the purpose of paying Calcutt. Mr. Forbes said he could not do it. After that Calcutt visited me by appointment, and I offered to sell him the six houses and one in Bridge Street for £800; he offered £650, which I subsequently agreed to take. Of course, his bill was to come off that. He afterwards refused to go on with it. This was November 17, 1907. I ceased to be a Guardian in 1907. Calcutt has worked for me up to January, 1908, until he closed his yard. Calcutt was to collect the rents (about £2 a week) and pay himself for the repairs. Kemp, Loftus, and Stammers were on the opposite party to me, and knocked me out in 1907. When Calcutt was arrested Clancy was already collecting my rents as agent. I was dissatisfied with his management, and wrote him a warm letter which I asked him to read to Calcutt, who was then residing at Southend. When Calcutt was out on bail I got his address from Heward and went to see him—at Poplar. He was out, but we met in the street. Mr. and Mrs. Smith and Mrs. Calcutt and her baby were with him. We went in to have a drink.
