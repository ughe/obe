 Prisoner. That is not the person who gave me the money, it was his wife - he never gave the cheque into my hand. Witness. I gave it to him myself.
 COURT. Q. You put the cheque on the file of the society, where was that kept? A. It hung up in the bar; the club meet on the 3d and 4th Monday in every month.
   JOHN JAMES BESWICK. I am secretary to the St. Ann's Union Benefit Society, that is the title. Beswick" is not the hand-writing of any of my family.
   JAMES BESWICK. I am the son of John James Beswick, and was fourteen years old last January. I never wrote this order, and know nothing about it; it is not my writing.
   WILLIAM IRELAND. I am a member of the St. Ann's Union, and was so on the 21st of February - there are other members besides me; I subscribe to the funds - if I am taken ill, or disabled, I receive support from the funds of the Society.
   GEORGE SALTER. (Looking at the order.) I know the prisoner; I am a carpenter, and live at No. 6, Osborne-street, St. James' - I have seen the prisoner write a great many letters; I believe this order to be his hand-writing, but cannot swear that it is.
 COURT. Q. How long have you known him? A. Nineteen years - he has been in my employ; he kept my books for the last twelve months - I had frequent occasion to see him write; I suppose the whole of the order to be his hand-writing - it is a good deal like his writing - I have trusted the prisoner to receive and pay money, 40l. or 50l. at a time; he always did it faithfully - he behaved honestly; he had been very much reduced till he came to my house - I used to employ him as well as I could.
   JOHN THOMAS re-examined. This is the order I received from the prisoner, and put on the file of the Society, on the evening of Saturday, the 21st of February; I gave it to Beswick on the Monday following, in the evening - it had been on the file till then; nobody but my own family had access to the bar - I know it to be the same paper, because I put my hand-writing on it.
   THOMAS GOOK. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner on the 4th of March, in Osborne-street, St. James', at Mr. Salter's house; I searched him after I got to the office, and found this paper, among a number of others; this induced me to go to Mr. Beswick's - I told him, both before I found the paper, and after, that he was charged with forging certain orders; he wished me to allow him to put that paper into the fire; I told him he had had plenty of time to destroy any thing he chose before I took him - I then placed him in the back office, among a number of other prisoners: I observed him watching me very closely - I had occasion to go into the Magistrates' room, and after returning from the Magistrates' room, I found he was gone from where I had placed him: the place he had been in was not locked; I immediately ran out of the office door, and observed him turning the corner into Blenheim-street, about fifty yards from the office - I followed, and overtook him; I have known him twenty years - he bore an excellent character.
 The order was here read, as set out in the indictment; the paper found on the prisoner was verbatim the same, except not being headed "St. Ann's Society."
 Prisoner's Defence. I am unprepared with Counsel, having had so little time; I never knew a word of this charge till I had a note from the solicitor on Saturday - the keeper of Clerkenwell prison did not know I was to be tried here; I understood it was for a fraud.
 MR. PHILLIPS. The landlady is here, if the prisoner wishes to examine her.
 Prisoner. I have nothing to ask her.
