ALICE SMITH. I am nursemaid at the King's Arms-on 7th July,
about 12.45, I went into the barmaid's room and saw her watch and
chain and some silver bracelets safe-just after two o'clock I went in
again and they were gone.
FREDERICK EADE. I keep the King's Arms, St. George's Road-on
7th July, about 2.15, I missed a pair of opera glasses from my bed-
room, which was disturbed, I had not seen them for several days.
ALFRED KAMBER (Detective L). On 19th July, about 11 a.m., I was in
the Waterloo Road, and saw Clark and Kelly on the opposite side of the
road-I crossed over-they apparently caught sight of me, and as I got
on the kerb they ran in different directions -I ran after Clark, and after
& chase caught him in Gray Street, and told him he would be
charged on suspicion of answering the description of a man wanted for
stealing jewellery at the King's Arms, Westminster Road-he said, "I
know nothing about it, I was in Charing Cross Hospital at the
time"-Kelly is the other man-the witness pointed Clark out at the
station.
Cross-examined by MR. TURRELL. The barman picked Clark out from
six others, who came from the street-they all answered his description-
I went to Charing Cross Hospital, and found Clark had been there as an
out-patient at 12.10 a.m. to have a scalp wound dressed, and it was
dressed by Mr. Powell -I told him the robbery was committed on
7th July- I did not say Monday-he attended the hospital on the 12th
only.
ALFRED NICHOLLS (Detective L). On 24th July, about noon, I saw Kelly
in Coral Street, Lambeth-Kamber was with me-he entered No. 84-I
knocked at the door, and was admitted—I went to the first floor front
room, and found him there with a woman I told him we were police
officers, and should take him into custody for being concerned with Paddy
Shea in committing larcenies at public-houses--I mentioned Mr. Shee's
case and Mr. Eade's-he said, “ All right, governor; I suppose I
shall have to stand to it”-he was placed with others at the station for
identification I searched his room, and found twenty-three pawn-
tickets relating to clothing and jewellery, none of which has been
identified.
GUILTY. The prisoners then PLEADED GUILTY to previous convictions,
Kelly** at Clerkenwell on 18th June, 1888, in the name of Walter Munroe,
and Clark at this Court on 18th May, 1885, in the name of Joseph Shears.-
Eighteen Months' Hard Labour each. GABIN**_Seven Years' Penal
Servitude. PARKS. VAUGHAN, and REYNOLDS-Eightoon Months'
Hard Labour each.
The Jury commended the officers' conduct.
