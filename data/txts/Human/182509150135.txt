1581. JOHN ADEY and SAMUEL RICHARDSON were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , three clothes baskets, value 12 s., the goods of John Rider.
 JOHN RIDER. I am a  basket-maker. On the 27th of July, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning, I sent Jonathan Primatt to Bethlem Hospital, with three clothes baskets.
   JONATHAN PRIMATT. I was eleven years of age last March. I am in Rider's employ. I was sent by him on the 27th of July, from Crispin-street, Spital-fields, to take three baskets to Bethlem Hospital; when I was near London-bridge I saw the prisoner Richardson, who asked me to go on an errand for him, and he would give me 2 d. - I said Yes; Adey was on the other side of the road, and Richardson gave him a penny to mind the baskets; I went on the errand, to carry a note to a gentleman - the gentleman said "It is not mine." I went back in about five minutes and the baskets were gone - I looked about and saw the officer with two men.
 Prisoner RICHARDSON. I saw the witness, and asked him to go for a pennyworth of biscuits, and he picked up the note and took it to the house. Witness. No; he gave me the note.
   DANIEL FORRESTER. On the 27th of July I saw the prisoners together in Cheapside; I saw Richardson go to a lad who had a bag on his head, and speak to him for some time; I then followed the prisoners toward London-bridge, and when they got near to Eastcheap, Adey sat down on the end of the curb - Richardson went up to the witness who put the baskets down; in a few minutes Adey took them up - he and Richardson went away together - Adey pitched them down in George-street, and I took them into custody. I came back the same way, and found the witness, who was crying - he had two halfpence and this note in his hand.
 ADEY'S Defence. I never saw this prisoner before, but he asked me to carry the baskets for him.
 ADEY -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 RICHARDSON -    GUILTY. Aged 12. Transported for Seven Years.
 1582.  ELIZABETH CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , a shift, value 6 s., the goods of Mary Ann Roper.
   GEORGE THOMAS HANNINGTON. I am a calenderer, and live in Sea-coal-lane. On Saturday, the 27th of August, between four and five o'clock; the prisoner came to my house and inquired if a person was there who washed for Mr. Roper; she said she was sent for a shift for her, as Mrs. Roper was afraid to come out as it was wet; I gave it to her myself; the duplicate was sent to me afterwards, - I went to the pawnbroker's and got it.
 MARY ANN ROPER. This is my property; I never gave the prisoner any authority to fetch it.
   GEORGE GODFREY. I took up the prisoner - the shift was given me by Mr. Hannington.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to live in the house where the prosecutrix lives, and because I owed them a little money they turned me out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1583.  WILLIAM KING was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , a handkerchief, value 5 s., the goods of Samuel Oviatt Goldney , from his person.
 SAMUEL OVIATT GOLDNEY. I was on  Ludgate-hill on the 13th of September, about eight o'clock in the evening - a person asked if I had lost any thing, and I missed my handkerchief; it was safe two minutes before - I saw the prisoner about one hundred yards off.
   JAMES LEA. I am an officer. I was on Ludgate-hill on the 13th of September - I saw the prosecutor, and the prisoner and three others closed upon him, and followed him - I saw the prisoner, whom I knew before, lift up the gentleman's right-hand pocket, and take something out, but I could not see what - I crossed over and asked if he had lost any thing; he said Yes, his handkerchief; the prisoner had got on as far as the Belle Sauvage inn, where I took him - his companions had turned towards St. Paul's - one of the other boys were taken, but he was discharged by the Alderman.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going down Ludgate-hill, towards Bridge-street - I stopped for a minute by the Belle Sauvage, as it was wet, and the officer took me.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1584.  JOHN STOCKS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , two sheets, value 8 s., and a blanket, value 7 s., the goods of William Onslow , in a lodging-room.
 WILLIAM ONSLOW. I live in  Lower Whitecross-street. The prisoner lodged in my house for about three weeks before the property was missed. Here are two sheets and a blanket, which were part of the furniture of his room.
   MARY ONSLOW. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the prisoner with a bundle a short time before I went to the Magistrate - I think on Tuesday, the 13th of July. I followed him out, and saw the officer take the bundle from him. I saw it opened up stairs, and a blanket taken from it.
   GEORGE LOCK. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - he had been stopped, and the bundle taken from him. - I found upon him three duplicates, a of blanket, a sheet, and another sheet.
   JOHN KILLINGWORTH. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned two sheets and a blanket at my house.
 The prisoner pleaded urgent distress in mitigation of his offence.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. Confined Six Months.
 1585.  ABRAHAM SUMMERFIELD was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of June , fifteen pairs of boots, value 3 l. 15 s., the goods of George Miller , his master.
 GEORGE MILLER. I  keep a shoe shop in Skinner-street. These boots are my property; they have my initials on them, but are not made by myself; I had missed three or four dozen pairs at different times previous to the detection of the prisoner.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was not he in your employ? A. Yes; he lived twice with me - the first time for about five years; the last time he came to me in great distress, being out of work, and I said I would give him 10 s. a week, and the moment he could suit himself better he could leave. He is not a good workman - he was to go on errends; the second time he worked as a clicker - I give my other clickers 24 s. or 25 s, a week.
