I am certain the prisoner is the man I saw in the public-house. I went down to the office, and met Foster, the officer. He went with me to the Red Lion, but he was gone then. I found him by the turnpike-gate, and pointed him out to Foster, who went up to him. He was taken to the Black Dog public-house, and my handkerchief taken off his neck by Foster. The prisoner Gale came into the public-house, and he said to her, "I believe all is right." Foster told Lea to follow her home. I followed her, and went up to her room. Lea let down the bed, and I found some of my flannel - it was a petticoat, partly made, and some calico - a child's tippet, and my purse, was found in her pocket. I had seen no woman about the house. Gale solemnly declared she knew nothing of the robbery, and said her husband gave her the purse.
   MARY BARNS. I lodge in the room over Mrs. Short. On the 1st of August, about twelve o'clock at night, I opened my window, and heard the church clock strike twelve. After that, not finding myself very well, I struck a light, put it out again, and tried to go to sleep, but could not rest. I got up and opened my window again, and thought it was break of day, it was such a very moonlight night. The watchman called half-past one when I opened the window. I saw a man in the yard, and saw him go to climb over the wall - I gave an alarm of Thieves. He had on a dark coat, which seemed to hang low, and to be full behind, but it was not a great coat. He had a high crowned hat, which was narrower down at the brim.
   WILLIAM FOSTER. I am an officer. On Tuesday, the 2d of August, in consequence of information, I went to look for Clark, and found him by Mile-end turnpike; I asked where he was on Monday night - he said at home - I asked what time he was at home; he said he could not say to an hour; I asked where he got the handkerchief which was round his neck - he said he bought it yesterday morning in Petticoat-lane for 1 s. 6 d. - I said he must go into the public-house, for I wished to search him; we went backward, into the parlour - I saw both his hands up to his neck, and when we got to the parlour door, he threw the handkerchief off his neck into the parlour, and said, "Go in there, and I will be with you directly - I want to go backward." I said "No, you must go in now;" he had the door half open - I called him: he said, "Let me go; I am taken very bad;" I took him into the parlour, and told Mrs. Short to look at the handkerchief; she exclaimed "That is my handkerchief, and what my husband wore when he died?" she nearly went into a fit; he said "Be quiet, my good woman, I dare say all things will be right;" I searched him and found a key, which, he said, was the key of his door; I asked where he lived; he said at No. 23, Easterfield-street, Stepney; I then went back into the yard with him, returned to the parlour, gave the key to Lea, and told him to go and search there. I took Clark to the office, and as I took him through the passages of the public-house, Gale stood there; he said something to her, and then said "It is all right;" I took him to the office; Lea brought Gale and some property there - the prosecutrix's house is in the parish of St. Dunstan's, Stepney.
   JAMES LEA. On the 2d of August, between ten and eleven o'clock, in the morning, I saw Clark; Gale came running into the passage of the public-house, and said, "Where is my husband?" Foster took Clark away, but before he went they whispered together; Gale crossed over the road; Short and I followed her to No. 23, Easter-field-street, Stepney; she went up, opened the door, and Short claimed some calico which laid on a chair there, and between the mattress and bed I found some flannel, which she claimed, and, between the bed and sacking, some more flannel; and a tippet in the room; and in Gale's pocket, I found a purse - she said Clark gave it to her.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 ELIZABETH GREEN re-examined. It happened at a quarter-past one o'clock, for the watchman looked at his watch afterwards, and said it was half-past one - there was no day-light.
 CLARK's Defence. I bought them in Petticoat-lane. Foster says he collared me; he never put his hand on me - I went quietly with him.
 One witness gave Clark a good character.
 CLARK -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 46.
 GALE -    NOT GUILTY.
 1571.  WILLIAM DUNCKLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of August , a clock-case, value 2 l.; six yards of cloth, value 6 s., and a shift, value 2 s., the goods of Joseph Grove , in his dwelling-house.
 JOSEPH GROVE. I live in  Richmond-street, St. Luke's. The prisoner is my son-in-law. I married his mother fifteen years ago - he is twenty years old, and is married himself - he was in the habit of coming to see his mother. On the 19th of August I went out, and on returning missed a clock-case, six yards of linen, and a shift; the linen was locked up in a trunk in the bed-room, and the clock-case was in the cupboard. I know nothing about the linen - I might not have seen that for a month. I found the clock-case at Worship-street on the Tuesday following - they were stolen on Friday.
   JOHN TWEEDY. I am a constable. I was sent for on the 23d of August, and took the prisoner in charge. I found three duplicates in a tea-caddy in his room - one for the clock-case, the others for the linen and shift. He said his father gave him the clock-case to pawn. I took him to the office, and he was bailed for an assault. Grove was examined.
   ELIZABETH CHOPPING. I was at my door in Richmond-street, and saw the prisoner coming out of Grove's house, with a bundle in a yellow handkerchief, on a Thursday or Friday in August, and when he returned he shewed me seventeen shillings - he said he had been to the pawnbroker's for his mother.
   WILLIAM SHARP. I am servant to Messrs. Reeve and Co., pawnbrokers, Red-cross-street. This linen and shift were pawned by a woman in the name of Mary Groves- I am sure it was a woman.
     NOT GUILTY.
