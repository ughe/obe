1682. MARY SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of June , 1 pair of studs, value 12s.; 1 ring, value 8s.; 1 ear-ring, value 8s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s., and 1 snuff-box, value 1s., the goods of Robert Brock , from his person.
   ROBERT BROCK. I am a jeweller. On the 23rd of June I went from my house in Spencer-street, early in the morning, with the property stated in my pocket; I met the prisoner in Shadwell- she was alone and asked me to give her something to drink; I went into a public-house, and gave her some gin - she then forced me up a court, into the house of another girl, and took the property from me; I tried to extricate myself, but could not: she put her hand into my trousers pocket, and took out the studs and rings - she took the snuff-box from my waistcoat pocket, and the handkerchief from my coat pocket; as soon as I could see a Policeman, she ran off, and threw the handkerchief to the other girl, who ran off, but the prisoner was taken - I was drunk, but knew what I did.
   WILLIAM CRICK(Police-constable K 255). I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner together in Shadwell, High-street for some time; the prosecutor told me he had been robbed of all his property - I asked if he wished me to take the girls; he said, "Yes, I have asked them to give up the property, and they will not:" they were walking till they saw me, and then they ran off - I saw the prisoner throw a handkerchief to another girl, who has got off.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1683.  SARAH WICKS and ANN HARDING were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of June , 1 purse, value 1s. 6d., and 17 sovereigns, the property of John Purnell , from his person; and that Wicks had been before convicted of felony.
   JOHN PURNELL. I am a mariner. On the 20th of June, I had been up all night, but was sober; I was looking after a sister of mine, and met the prisoners about half-past three o'clock in the morning: I do not know in what street I first saw Harding - she asked me to have something to drink; I said it was too soon: there was another girl with her, but Wicks was not there then - they then asked me to have some coffee and bread and butter, which we did; I was coming away, but they followed, and took me into a private room - I staid there an hour and a half, but had nothing to do with any of them; Harding then went out, and brought in Wicks: Harding said she was her sister, and asked if I did not fancy her - I said No, I did not fancy either of them; I got up and went out - the prisoner's followed me to a public-house, where Wicks took my purse out of my pocket - Harding was close to her at the time; I had seventeen sovereigns in my purse: Wicks ran off with it - I followed, and Harding stopped me, and said she would come back, that she was sky-larking - I have never got the purse since; the officer took Harding; Wicks was taken three days after.
   GEORGE HUSTWAYTE. I saw Wicks take the purse out of the prosecutor's pocket, while he was drinking a glass of ale; she went out of the public-house instantly - he followed her, and Harding followed him.
 Wicks. When the prosecutor was leaving the house, he was asked if he had all his money right, and he said Yes.
   JOHN PURNELL. Hustwayte said, to me, "You will be done," and I said, "I am wide awake."
   THOMAS PRENDIVILLE(Police-constable 140 G). The prosecutor told me he was robbed, and I took Harding at the time.
   GEORGE DOSSON. (Police-constable G 123). I took Wicks from the prosecutor's description.
   MICHAEL GLADWELL(Police-constable G 162.) I have a certificate (which I got from Mr. Clark's office) of the conviction of Wicks; she had three months imprisonment in the House of Correction; I took her, and know she is the person.
 Wicks' Defence. I met Harding, who asked me to go to the room with her; I saw the prosecutor on the bed, with his clothes undone - he said, "Come, and I will give you all something to drink;" we went and had something to drink at the public-house - I then came away directly; I was not near him - there were two more girls in company.
 Harding put in a written Defence, stating that she had left the prosecutor for some time, and after she returned he complained of being robbed, but being innocent, she did not attempt to escape.
 WICKS -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 HARDING -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1684.  SUSETTA SOLOMONS was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 25th of June , 11 books of sheet gold, value 16s., the goods of John Davison , well knowing them to have been stolen.
   JOHN DAVISON. I am a bookbinder. I have an apprentice named Thomas Tonge; I never authorised him to sell any of my property - I never saw the prisoner till I was at the office; my gold was going quicker than it ought to go, and I was about to make a complaint to the man who used it - I cannot swear to this gold, but I had property of this description.
   THOMAS GEORGE TONGE.
