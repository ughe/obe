2037. JOHN LAWRENCE, and HENRY CHAPPEL, stealing 100
sovereigns, 80 half-crowns, 500 shillings, 200 sixpences, and 2 bags, value
2d.; the goods of Edward Smith, (since deceased) in his dwelling-house.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
EDWARD SMITH. I live with my mother, at the Bricklayers' Arms,
Becleston-street, Pimlico. My father died last Dec.-- he was alive at the
time of this robbery, but it hastened his death-on 18th Sept. last, about a
quarter-past four o'clock in the afternoon, I was in Eccleston-street, near our
house, and the two prisoners came up to me, and asked if I could tell them
where the Bricklayers' Arms was- I do not think they mentioned the land-
lord's name-I directed them-I went home half an hour after, and they
were then standing at the bar, drinking gin and water-on 21st Sept., about
seven in the evening, I saw both the prisoners again-I saw Lawrence first
in company with another man, not Chappel--they were standing at the
counter, taking half-and-half-Chappel came in shortly afterwards, and called
for a glass of half-and-half-he stood talking to me about a quarter of an
hour-after that Lawrence and his companion asked for two glasses of gin-
Chappel was not with them I had to turn my back to serve the gin, and
when I turned round again Chappel was gone I did not see him go-Law-
rence and his companion had some gin and cigars-they then began to talk
about their friend not having come; that they should not stop any longer,
and they left shortly after-800n after that, my sister Mary, who was sitting
sewing, jumped up, and ran upstairs, as though something had suddenly
attracted her attention-she then called out to me that the house had been
robbed.
