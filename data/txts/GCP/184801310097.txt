• MARY JOHNson. I am the wife of James Johnson, of 8, Mason's-place.
From the front of our house we can see the back of Mr. Hanaford's on this
Sunday evening, about a quarter past six o'clock, or later, I saw a flame of
fire in the second-floor back room at Mr. Hapaford's--I opened the window,
and called out that there was a fire in Rahere-street, and Mr. Watkins was the
first person I saw-I told him there was a fire there I was not exactly
looking out of window before I saw the light; I was suckling my baby, and
happened to look up, and saw the fire.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Your window is immediately oppo.
site the back of the premises? 4. Very nearly-I started when I saw the
fame, and threw up the window-I was frightened, and called, "Fire!" -
Watkins went away directly I spoke to him.
ELIZABETH CALLOW. I live at 86, Rahere-street. About half-past six
o'clock, on the night of the fire, I saw the prisoner at the door of the house-
it was almost momentarily after the alarm of fire--he had a dark japanned
hand oil-lamp in his hand-it was alight-he said the fire was there I asked
how it occurred-be said he supposed it was by his mistress taking a lamp
up stairs, to dress for chapel; that the wick had fallen or blown off the top of
the lamp, and set fire to the bed-curtains--I left him at the door, and went
back to my own house, as I had left my door open-I saw several persons
going in to the house at the time--I left him standing at the door.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Did not he tell you that the fire
was put out? 1. No-I did not enter the house I was first alarmed when
I was in my yard, by hearing a breaking of glass it was that brought me to
the street door.
COURT. Q. Where did the sound of the breaking of glass come from?
4. A few doors up-it was at the moment of the fire--a person came from the
back of the house and said there was a fire, and I ran up the street with the
person I thought the breaking of glass was in the street, but it was in a
yard five or six doors up, next door but one to the prosecutor's house--it fell
on some tin, and that made a noise it was from a person throwing up &
window-the line broke, and the window falling broke the glass I did not
see the window,
Mr. BODKIN. Q. Had you heard an alarm of fire before you heard the
breaking of glass? 4. No, I heard it almost momentarily afterwards-I
only went through the passage to the door, and then I found a person coming
round from a court at the back of the house, calling out that there was a fire.
