Q. Look at this paper (producing one), and then tell me whether you did not
occasionally delay to settle his accounts for some time? A. This refers to the
accounts he had out with him on his journey-I could not furnish him with
every item when he went, but I sent part of them after him-when he came
back, it was his duty to account to me for everything he had received-I never
delayed to settle with him from the return of one journey till the commence-
ment of another-his salary was 11. a week, and a commission of 5 per cent.
on all sales which he effected above 601. a-week, and 158. a-day for travel.
ling expenses--I should have been very happy to have accepted those terms
myself, I did not tell him that the 5 per cent. on the sum above 601. per
week would produce him 21. or 31. a-week- I told him, if he exerted him-
self, he might get a very respectable living-when he found he could not
collect 601. a week, I altered his salary to 30s. a-week, and dropped the com-
mission altogether-I believe he began in 1844- I cannot undertake to say
that in one week he did not do business to the amount of 1001., but we
should take the average of the journey--the weeks were to be averaged while
his salary remained 11. a-week-I wrote to him in June, about his making
some bad debts, and he wrote me word that he would give me notice to quit
my employ-after that he wished to stay again, and urged it very much on
me; I did not urge it on him-I had been out of town, and on my return I
looked into the books and found the confidence I had placed in him had
been betrayed - I would not see him-I referred him to my solicitor, and
sent Manley my foreman to him, to go into the accounts, I should not have
refused to receive the amounts that were alleged to be due, I could not
afford to lose them--the prisoner did not come to me in pursuance of an ap-
pointment I made with him-his brother called on me-I am not aware that I
desired the prisoner to come to me the next day to settle these deficiencies-
I have found out the deficiencies I have indicted him for since then, through
going a journey-I am afraid I have much more to find out-I have been
very lax with him, and have allowed errors which I ought not to have done.
Not GUILTY.
(There were two other indictments against the prisoner, upon which no
evidence was offered.)
