FANNY JANE OBORNE. I live with my aunt in Leader-place, Westminster
On the 23rd of March the prisoner came there at a quarter to eight o'clock i
the morning--she said she had come from Windsor by the steam carriage, and
wanted my aunt to recommend a person to go and fetch her boxes, which
we did-the shawl was lying there, and she asked me to let her put it on her
shoulders, as she was very cold travelling all night-she then borrowed a
shilling and got some rum, and had some luncheon-then she got me to
write a note to her aunt, at the Marquis of Lansdowne's--we went out, and
she asked me to lend her another shilling, which I did she went away with
the man who was to fetch the boxes, and when I got home I missed
flannel petticoat, handkerchief, and gloves, which had been on the mangle
where the shawl was.
ELIZABETH HARMAN. I am a searcher at the station-I produce this
flannel petticoat which I found on the prisoner.
FANNY JANE OBORNE. This is my petticoat-there is no mark on in
but I have another like it, and it is my own making.
JOHN BARKER (City police-constable, No. 466.) I produce a certificate of
the prisoner's conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office--(read)-the pri-
soner is the person who was then convicted.
Guilty. Aged 23.-Transported for Ten Years.
