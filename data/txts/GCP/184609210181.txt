EDWARD PERRONET SELLS, Jux. I am carrying on business with my
father and Mr. William Jones, at Bankside, in the parish of St. Saviour.
Southwark. We have a stable at White Lion-wharf-we keep fourteen or
Efteen horses-Jacobs was horse-keeper in our service, and resided on the
premises--I know Simpsou by sight, and I know his stable the distande
between that and the back door of our stable is thirty yards-it was measured
by me and my partner---that back door is to allow the horses to escape in
case of fire--it ought never to be opened on any other occasion, it was not
intended for any purpose of business on the 29th of Aug. my attention was
called to Jacobs being in custody saw him in the yard in the presence of
the policeman, and asked how he came in that position-be said he was very
sorry indeed, it was the first time he had ever done anything of the kind; he
hoped. I would not lock him up; he would be sure to remain on the premises
till be was required-I told him he was in custody of the police I went to
Simpson's premises, and found a bin with six or seven bushels of corn ini
three sacks, if full, would contain twelve bushels--I compared the corn in the
bin with that on our premises-it was exactly the same in my opinion--there
was chaff and corn lying on the floor besides the corn in the bin I noticed
the oats that were mixed with the chaff-I think they were part of the same
parcel--I heard that there had been some marks of Oats seen outside my
stable--I looked in the course of the day, and saw a few oats I did not
trace them. I

ROBERT BUNSPORD (police-constable M 101.) I was with Ross during
part of that morning-I took Simpson-I told bim what he was taken for
he said he had just heard of it in going along Bankside-he said he borrowed
the sacks of Jacobs for the purpose of carrying pollard and oats to Richmond,
where he had a large job, and the oats he bought of Mr. Meek, yesterday-he
said he was in the habit of buying oats of him almost every day.
John MEEK. I am a corn-chandler, and live in Holland-street, Blackfriars-
road. Simpson deals with me-my attention has been called to Saturday
morning, the 29th of Aug.--I sold Simpson bran and beans on the Friday
evening-I had not sold him any corn myself-I had not been at home in the
daytime I have ascertained that corn bad been sold to him almost every day
in the week, a sack or two-I am not able to say whether I sold him such
corn as this I had about twenty quarters of such oats as these in my posses-
sion, with some black oats mixed with them.
COURT. Q. Had you any such corn as this? A. I think these are Dutch
oats--I had about twenty quarters- I think there are more black oats in
these than in the Dutch oats I had I think these are not my Dutch oats, but
I had Polish oats-these are not my Polish oats.
