Evidence for the Defence.
THOMAS HOPE. I am a gunner in the Royal Field Artillery, and am
the prisoner's brother- I was with Bill Clark when my brother showed me
£13 10s., and Dearman was with my brother on August 2nd-I saw
my brother and James Dearman on the early morning of August 9th
--my brother produced 138. ld. I had ld. to get cigarettes.
AGNES HOPE. I am the prisoner's mother-I live at Clare Field Cottage,
Hounslow-on August 5th, about 4.30, the prisoner came home for
£13 10s.- he had other money.
LEONARD HOPE. I am the prisoner's father on August 5th he asked
his mother for £13 10s. which she was minding for him-he had been
trying to be good.
Scott FRANCE. I am the court missionary-since his last conviction
the prisoner has been given work, and he tells me he has saved money.
The prisoner in his defence stated that though he had disgraced himself
by getting drunk he was not guilty of the offence.-GUILTY. He then
PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony at Guildhall, Westminster, on
April 8th, 1899. Other convictions were proved against him, including
seven for felony. Twelve months' hard labour.
