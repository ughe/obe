952. WILLIAM JOSEPH BROOKSON (28), ALLEN YOUNG (60)
and WILLIAM DEVOIL (41), Stealing two tons of coals the goods of
the Great Northern Railway Company, the masters of Brockson.
MESSRS. Grain and Gill Prosecuted ; MR. PURCELL appeared for Brookson,
Mr. D. METOALPE for Young, and MR. WILLES for Devoil.
DAVID. LINOOLN. I am clerk to Mr. Brown, colliery agent, of King's
Cross, I know the three prisoners--Young is carman to Mr. Bull, a
coal merchant having a siding at the Great Northern Railway Station,
King's Cross-Devoil is one of an association of coal porters who have the
privilege of the yard and load for the merchants from the trucks--I have
frequently seen him working for Mr. Brown-on 6th September I was
passing along Copenhagen Street, Islington, about 1.48 p.m., and saw
one of Bull and Co.'s vans, containing about a ton of coals, at the door of
the Mitre public-house, and Devoil carrying & sack of coals into the house
-I went back to the yard, and about an hour after I Saw Young in the
yard with the same van empty, and he said "Can you give me a load 1"-I
said "I thought you were busy, seeing your ven outside the gates un-
loading coals" —he said "I have been doing a load for Bull; I can't do.
any more, 'as he has no coal in "--my suspicions being aroused I went to the
weighbridge and sew the clerk, Brookson, in the office after examining
the book I said “Who has Young's van gone out for 1"-he said "For
Bull"--on 8th September I saw Devoil at work in the yard—I said
“Hallo! I thought you had turned carman, seeing you with & yan on
Saturday "—he replied “I was only there getting the beer for dinner, and I
assisted the man to unload."
Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE. I said at the police-court on 6th
September that Young said "I have done a load or two for Mr. Bull"--I may
have remembered what was said better then than now.
