WILLIAM MUNNS. I was in Furzefield-wood on the 4th of November with Gibbs—I saw a person, and in consequence of what Gibbs said, I ran after the person—he got out of the wood, but I did not lose sight of him—I over took him—it was the prisoner—he had a stick in his hand—he struck at me—I put my arm up, and he knocked my hat off—I ran up to him, and got the stick out of his hand—I got behind him—he got a knife out of his pocket, and said, if I did not let go of him, he would run it into me—he struck at me six or seven times—I kept behind him, and got hold of his arm—Gibbs came up, and said, "Is it you, Philip?"—he said, "Yes," and then I let him go—he attempted to stab him, and I knocked him off—he said he would not be taken by anybody—he got out of the field over the hedge, took a brick and threw it at Gibbs—I saw Gibbs hit him on the arm in the field, and try to knock the knife out of his hand—after he got over the hedge, and threw the brick, I said to Gibbs, "It is not worth while to run the risk of our lives; you know him."
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time was this? A.  About half-past five, or from that to six o'clock—the prisoner was about forty yards off when we first saw him—I could see at that distance what he was about—after I went up to him, Gibbs came up, and addressed him by his name—I let him go—he walked away, and we pursued him again—it was after we came up to him again that Gibbs struck him on the arm—he had attempted to stab me and Gibbs—he walked away a little, and Gibbs said he should go to Mimms cage—he said he would not be taken by anybody—Gibbs did not strike him before he had attempted to stab him—Gibbs held his stick up before him, he then made a stab at him, and then Gibbs hit him with the stick.
   MR. HOWORTH . Q.  Did he still keep the knife in his hand? A.  Yes, and kept threatening us, as I have stated. CARTER FAREBROTHER. I am land-steward to the Marquis of Salisbury—Furzefield-wood belongs to the Marquis. Gibbs is employed as game keeper of that manor—I gave him directions to look to the underwood, and see that no damage was done, that no person cut any or took it away, and to detect a person who was doing so—I authorised him to detect any person whom he might find there—I had authority from his lordship to do so—that manor is under my control.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This wood is in the occupation of the Marquis of Salisbury? A.  It is—Mr. White is the tenant of the farm, but not of the wood—his land is on one side—a person coming from Mr. White's might come through the wood by trespass—there is no footpath—I gave Gibbs authority to watch the wood, in a verbal manner and in writing. STEPHEN GIBBS re-examined. Q.  Did you receive an authority in writ ing from Mr. Farebrother? A.  Yes—I really do not know what has become of it—I searched for it, and could not find it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long is it since you received that written authority? A.  I think it must be twelve months ago—I should think it has been lost these six weeks—I had an authority when this took place—it was lying on the mantel-piece then, but it got knocked down by the children, and got burnt—I did not see it burnt—the woman where I lodge said so—I did not expect it would be asked for—I searched for it a day or two after the case was before the Magistrate.
