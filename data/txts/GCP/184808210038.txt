JEREMIAH FULLER. I am chief assayer of the Goldsmiths' Company. I
received this purse on 2nd Aug., for the purpose of being assayed-I ask ved
it--it is not silver--the ingredients of standard silver are 11oz. 2dwts of fine
silver to 18dwts, of copper in the 12oz. Ib. - this is 2oz. 18dwts. worse than
standard--the Goldsmiths' Company would not permit the mark to have been
put on such an article as this, as silver-it would be base metal-it is the
practice if anything of this sort is brought there, which is not up to standard,
to break it to pieces.
Cross-examined. Q. It would be one-eighth or one-ninth part less valu.
able than if it was standard silver? A. Considerably less than standard
-standard silver would be 58. to the ounce, and this is about 3s. 8d.—it is
only those persons that have their punches entered at the company who can
get the mark put on-I am not aware that the prisoner is one, nor Johnson
there are a good many working silversmiths not entered at the company, who
get a friend who has his name entered, to get things marked for them the
company first had information about this on 1st Aug., and I went to Dr.
Halley-I got my information from the solicitor of the company-I do not
know where be got his information from I have not seen Johnson at the
hall.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. When an article is manufactured, may it be sent
into the world without bearing the mark of the company? 4. No--those
who have not punches frequently submit their goods to those wbo have them,
to get them marked-I have no doubt of this being a proper mark of the
company's.
Court. Q. How long have you been assayer to the company! A.
About eight years, I have become acquainted with the marks I should call
this a mark let in, transposed (pointing it out)- this is not the mark for an
article of this description, it would be the mark for a spoon-according to the
quantity of copper used in the metal, it would be more or less yellow,
Josiah SHARPE, Esq. lam deputy-warden of the Goldsmiths' Company.
I have examined the rim of the mounting of this purse--it is not such a mark
as is used for this species of article, but for tea-spoons and other small spoons
--the letter "T,” in old English, is on it-that letter is used to denote the
year in which the article is stamped-that is not the proper letter for the pre
sent year-it was used last on 29th May, 1845- different letter is used
every year--the proper mark for such an article as this would have been
altogether different-part of the marks would have been put on the front of
the clasp and part on the back, to prevent the possibility of a part of this
being made use of and attached to a piece not ball marked, and there would
also have been a small mark on each of the mountings of the tassels and all
the small movable pieces--the mark on the front would have been the bon
passant and the leopard's head- I believe this mark has been let in.
Cross-eramined. Q. What mark is on it now? A. The lion passant,
the letter for the year, the leopard's head, and the Queen's head-
