we all three walked together towards St. Giles' church, where we parted with the girl Wilkins; I did not hear Williams say that he would meet her again that night - this was about a quarter to one o'clock; I wanted to part with Williams, but he said, "You shall not go until we have a parting glass;" we went up Plumtree-street, thinking to find a public-house open in St. Giles' - when we got there they were closed; Williams then said he knew of a watering-house, at the corner of Red Lion-street, which was open all night - we went up Phoenix-street, and turned into Charlotte-street; Williams fell over something, and, getting up, picked up a basket - a little further on I saw a dark bundle lay, which I picked up; Williams put the basket on his arm, and said we had better call a cab to take them somewhere until the morning, and see if there would be a reward offered; we had not opened the bundles, and did not know what they contained - we went to the top of Plumtree-street; Williams then said, "Give me the bundle, and go and call a cab;" when I got into Broad-street I saw a cab on the stand, and called him off - I got into the cab; Williams put the bundle and basket in - he then got into the cab himself, when the girl Wilkins came up at the time, and asked Williams to give her a ride - he asked me whether I knew of a place to leave them until the morning, to see whether they were advertised; I told the cab man to drive to Eagle-street, to an acquaintance of mine, but I found they had left - Williams said, "Never mind," and told me to get in, as he knew of a friend of his, where in a day or two he was going to lodge; he told the cab man to drive to Little Saffron-hill - I told him I should not get in, as there was not room for three; I walked by the side of the cab all the way - Williams got out, and knocked at the door, which one of the Cohens opened; Williams asked me to bring the things out of the cab - Cohens invited the girl to come in; I asked Wilkins if every thing was out of the cab; she said Yes all but a blue handkerchief which she found at the bottom of the cab, which she put into my hat -Williams then asked me how much money I had got; I told him about 7d. - he then said, "We have not got enough to pay the cab;" Cohen said, "Never mind, I can pay, then you can pay me to-morrow," as they had known Williams for five years; it was the first time that I had ever been in Cohen's house - after the cab man was paid and went, Williams told me the things were all safe at Cohens'; it was past two o'clock - the elder Cohen said, "Perhaps your friends would take a cup of tea, as you have been drinking;" we all sat down and had some breakfast; I then remarked to Cohen it was getting late, and that I gave up all hopes of getting into my lodging - Cohens then said,"Never mind, as you are friends of Williams, you are welcome to stop here until the morning;" while we were having some tea, some person opened the door, and knocked at the parlour door, which Cohen opened, and in came several Policemen, and asked for some things that had been stolen from a station-house- it struck us, for the first time, what the basket and bundle contained; the elder Cohen said, "It must he the bundle or basket that you have found;" the Policemen then said, "These are the things that we have lost;" they then took us all prisoners.
   Abraham Cohen's Defence. Isaac says the goods were concealed - I do not understand what he means; they were only put down by the side of the beds - they were not brought to me for sale; I knew Williams when I was in business, and entrusted him to sleep in my house - if the goods were stolen, I could have put them out at my back door.
 Issac Cohen's Defence. I have nothing to do with my father's business, I was there by accident.
 JURY to  GEORGE POWELL. Q. Did you find the door or window of the station-house open? A. The area gate was open, and a person by going down there could put their hand into a broken pane of glass, and put the bolt of the door back - I had left the door shut.

 2320.  WILLIAM SPALL was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 3rd of October , 2 bushels of barley, value 10s., the goods of Skidmore Ashby and another, well knowing it to have been stolen.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
 SKIDMORE ASHBY. I am a  corn-merchant, and live at Staines, in partnership with my brother John. I had, on the 23rd of September, a quantity of barley in sacks in a granary- there were eighteen sacks; the sacks were marked William Ashby- it had a particular seed in it, cliver seed, which could not be separated from it; I saw it safe on Saturday afternoon, the 22nd of September, about four o'clock - it was locked with two locks; on the Tuesday morning I went and found the binges were broken; we went in, and missed four sacks of barley - I went into the field, and about one hundred yards from the granary, we found a sack of barley and a sack of beans, covered over with rushes; there is a shed twenty yards from the place; I and some other persons agreed to watch this sack for several nights - on Wednesday evening, when I, James Shears, and two other persons were watching, the prisoner came; when he came to the door, he said, "Blue Dick, are you there?" we did not answer - he then went to the heap of rushes, and took about half the barley out of the sack, put it on his shoulder, and went away; he left the other tied up, and had not covered it up - we had put the lock on the staple of the door, and some person had locked us in - the prisoner had come close to the door, and pushed it, but there was something against it, and he could not open it; we broke through the boards, and got out - he had got about one hundred and fifty yards off, and Shears took him with the barley on his back; he was taken into custody - we examined the barley, and it answers to the sample and bulk.
   JAMES SHEARS. I am in the employ of the prosecutors, and watched with them. I saw the prisoner come and take some of the barley away - I followed, and took him into custody; I have compared the barley found on him, and that in the bulk, and they correspond; this sack is Robert Holland's, that I took from him - this one is what he shot the barley out of, and has my masters' names on it
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
