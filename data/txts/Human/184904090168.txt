   Cross-examined. Q.  What does he owe you? A.  About 180 l .—I could not take these shares without advancing him money, because he would not give them up—he asked me for a loan on these, and agreed to allow 5 l . when the shares were sold, to go off the old account—he gave me permission to sell the shares if in three weeks he did not redeem them—I am willing to give up the shares now, if the advance is paid me—Mr. Walker brought, an action against me in Oct.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  I believe you offered to give up your shares without the money? A.  I did at the Mansion-house.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Are you willing to do so now? A.  No—it was offered by the defendant to give me a Judge's order for the amount—he was willing to do it at that time—that was on 12th March. JOSEPH MANN. I am a clerk. I became acquainted with the prisoner in June, 1847—I was in his service a short time—I remember his insolvency in 1848—I was a creditor of his to the amount of about 100 l .—he was cleared by the Insolvent Court in March—there was no dividend paid—I have seen this circular about these wines—there was the same thing when I was with him—he was then in Pancrass-lane—he had no stock at that time—I occasionally went to the docks to taste wines—I was with him five months, and left him on 23d Nov., 1847.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you clerk to Mr. Stannard, or master, which? A.  I was clerk—I saw an advertisement in the paper, which I suppose was his—I paid 100 l . for integrity when I went in his service in June, 1847—I did not sketch this circular, not a line of it—I recommended a printer to him to print them—I knew it was a circular—I had seen it on his table, and knew the purport of it—I knew it was quite false—I did not take it to be printed—I procured him a printer.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  What salary were you to have? A.  To commence at 70 l . a year, and to be raised to 100 l . SAMUEL JARMAN. I am clerk to Mr. Humphreys—I produce these docu ments from the Insolvent Court—they are certified copies under the seal of the Court in the matter of Edward Stannard—one is the balance-sheet—the amount of debt is 1510 l ., and the other is the adjudication—on 17th March an order was made to discharge him from prison.
