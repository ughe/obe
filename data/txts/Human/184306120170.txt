   Q.  Had you seen her in company with the man that she described herself to have gone through this ceremony of the ring with? A.  Never, but on one occasion, when I visited her at No. 17, Manor-street—before we were married a person came in and described himself to be her husband—but she met me the same evening and declared to me that the ceremony that was gone through was what I have described, and on the faith of that I kept com pany with her—she declared that there had only been the ceremony of the ring and that had taken place at Henley-upon-Thames—she absconded from me the day week after our marriage—I had been living in the same house with her up to that time—I produce a certificate of the marriage—I have not examined it with the register—I was present when it was drawn in the vestry, and I have not the smallest doubt of its being faithfully copied—I am acquainted with the parish—it is the parish church where people go on Sundays—I have heard it called by the name of St. Mary Abbot's, Kensington.
   Prisoner.  I never told him about going to Henley at all. Witness.  I am quite positive the transaction she spoke of was at Henley—it was spoken of twenty times, and more than that, during a lapse of more than three months.
   Prisoner.  He knew I was married before he married me, for Mr. Cox and Caroline Cox told him so, and I told him so myself. Witness.  Caroline Cox was in the room when the prisoner's husband made his appearance, and it was said in his presence—she was quite willing to marry me—we were all four in the room, the prisoner, and Caroline Cox, Richard Cox, and myself—I went there without any appointment—I called promiscuously as I passed, and Ri chard Cox told me she was his wife—he did not tell me under what circum stances the marriage took place—I met her that evening, and she told me what I have stated, that it was a ceremony which she supposed only amounted to a joke, at Henley-on-Thames—she said she knew the ring was borrowed on the occasion from her aunt, who lived there, and she threw it into the fire on ascertaining what it had been for.
   Prisoner.  He wanted me to be married, and I said I could not, but I met him one day, and he would not let me go till we were married. Witness.  We got into an omnibus, and went to the church and were married—the clerk, the pew-opener, and her son, a person of the name of Parsons, were present—none of them are here.
