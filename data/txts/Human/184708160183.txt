1918. SAMUEL CHARLES PRINCE was indicted for stealing 1 half-eagle, value 28 s  .; the monies of Eric Orr ; in a vessel in a port of entry and discharge. ERIC ORR. I am second mate of the James Edwards , which is in the St. Katharine's Docks; the prisoner was steward. On the 26th of July I lost a half-eagle, which is worth about 1 l . 8 s .—the prisoner had wanted to borrow money of me the day before, and I told him I had not got any—he had no business in my berth that I know of—I missed this piece of money—I took the prisoner to my room and spoke to him—he stated at first that he had not taken it—at last he said he had taken the half-eagle piece, and told me to say nothing about it, that he might not have discharge—I told him there was no necessity for it, he had been bad enough without—he had put the money in a house, and spent a cent of it.
   Prisoner.  I never took it all; I went to speak to the mate, and this witness called me into the state-room, and had a rope's-end; he said I had taken the piece; I said I had not, but sooner than have a piece of work I would pay more than the value of it. ROBERT GIFFORD( policeman. ) I took the prisoner—he said he knew nothing about it; but sooner than have any piece of work he would pay five times the amount—the next morning he asked if he should plead guilty—I told him I should give him no advice—he then said, "I took the money, but it was through drink and the persuasion of another party." ANN WILSON. I live at No.4, Neptune-court. On Sunday night the prisoner came to sleep in that house, and he left his coat for the price of the room, he had no money—on Monday night, the 26th, he came again with a gold piece.
   Prisoner.  I had twenty dollars which the captain paid me; it was my own money.
   COURT to ERIC ORR. Q.  Did he received twenty dollars? A.  The cap tain would not give him a farthing—I swear he never had a farthing—his pay was twenty dollars a month; but he acted in such a way that he was not worth a farthing—he wasted more victuals, and spoiled more than he was worth.
   Prisoner.  This witness told me the captain was a very bad captain, and he said he would take his gold watch and throw it overboard; I am as innocent as you are; I did not take it. I assure; I had my trunk full of clothes, and 5 l . owing to me; the Magistrate said I might have them, but the ship was gone to sea and I could not get them. GUILTY . Aged 18.— Confined Nine Months .
 1919. GEORGE WAKEFIELD was indicted for staling 3 bushels of beans, value 16 s  .; and 4 sacks 4 s  .; the goods of Walter Powell , his master: and CHARLES GOBBY for feloniously receiving the same, well-knowing them to have been stolen. HENRY LOADES. I am in the employ of Mr. Walter Powell—I know the prisoner Wakefield—he was in Mr. Powell's employ. On the 1st of July he was ordered to take three sacks of oats and some straw to Mr. Fordham—I helped him load—he told me to take a sack of oats into Gobby's—I took it—I hallooed out, no one answered, and I left it on the stairs—I saw Wake-field again that night—he owed me a shilling, and I asked him if he could pay me, and he gave me 3 s .—the sack of oats came from my master's loft, where the other sacks came from.
