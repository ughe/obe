ROBERT Cox. I am shopman to Mr. Tarrant, a pawnbroker, Great
Charlotte-street, Blackfriars-road. On the 25th of January, the female
prisoner brought this pin, which I produce, to the shop, and offered it in
pawn for 31. I think-I was deceived in it, and lent her 50s.-having
some doubt of it being a diamond, I said, “This is very handsome, no
doubt, it cost a great deal of money"-she made no answer-I said, “I
suppose it cost as much as 51 !” she said, "Oh dear me, yes, it orst me
considerably more than that"-I asked her name, she said, “Mary Ann
Williams, housekeeper, No. 92, New Cut," and that it was her own pro-
perty- I have since found it is spurious, and not worth above 88.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. Supposing it to be a diamond,
what would be the value ! 1. About 81. in the trade-I dare say it would
cost you fourteen or fifteen guineas--I had a great doubt of it, but was
deceived by the gas-light-had I been quite certain it was a diamond, I
should have lent considerably more-I took it as a real diamond, but had a
doubt of it-had I believed it was any thing else, I should not have lent
above 108.--I described the prisoner's dress when I was applied to, but it
was not by that I recognized her-I should have known her in any
dress.

North, pawnbroker. On the 30th or 31st of January, the female prisoner
offered a claw pin in pledge, precisely such a one as the last witness has
produced-she asked 31. on it-I tried it on a stone, and found the stem a
different quality of gold-the stem was worth about 358. an 0%., and the
claw, 458.-the workmanship was very defective-I asked, was it a
brilliant? she said “ Yes" I said, "How do you know it?" she said, “I
believe it to be so; it was given to me by a gentleman, who was lodging
at our house for about a week; he was short of money, and gave me this
pin, assuring me if I wanted money at any time I could pledge it for 46.
or 51. I told her not to go elsewhere with it, that it was not genuine,
and she might get herself into troubleshe gave me the name of Williams,
