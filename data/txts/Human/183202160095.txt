 737.  THOMAS DUTTON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of December , 1 counterpane, value 5s.; 1 bolster, value 3s.; 2 pillows, value 3s.; 2 blankets, value 6s.; 2 sheets, value 5s., and 2 flat-irons, value 1s., the goods of John Joice.
 SARAH JOICE. I am the wife of  John Joice- we live in Little Windmill-street, Golden-square. The prisoner occupied a back room in my husband's house - he was taken into custody on the 8th of February, on another charge; I then went into his room, and missed the articles stated.
   WILLIAM GOULD. I am apprentice to a pawnbroker. I have two blankets, pawned on the 6th of December, and two irons on the 17th of December, in the name of Thompson; I cannot say by whom, but these are the duplicates.
   WILLIAM ORGER. I am an officer. I found these duplicates on the prisoner's wife; the prisoner said he took the things, and pawned them, but he was in great distress, and meant to redeem them when he got employment.
   THOMAS SHIRLEY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, pawned by the prisoner on the 9th of November, in the name of Thompson - this is the duplicate I gave him.
 SARAH JOICE. These are my articles. The prisoner is a dyer - they had nothing of any value of their own; they might he in distress - he owes me a good deal for rent, besides what I have lent him; his wife could earn 12s. a week, but they did not make the best use of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am a native of Liverpool, where I was in partnership in business, but through unforeseen circumstances have been brought to distress; a gentleman at Islington has promised to employ me.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Ten Days.
 774.  JOHN DRANE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 1 box, value 3d., and 2s. 5d. in copper monies, numbered, the property of William Morrish.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to be the property of some persons unknown.
   ISAAC STEVENSON. I am clerk of the boatmen's chapel, at Paddington. On the 12th of February I saw the prisoner sitting by the fire, at the lower end of the chapel, when I went in; and while we were singing the first hymn he removed, and went near the door - he then went out; I thought something was not right - I followed, and asked how he came to leave the chapel; he said, "For God's sake, forgive me, I did it for want," and he gave me this charity-box, which had stood on the mantel-piece in the chapel - Mr. Morrish is the sub-treasurer, and any money missing he would be required to account for, but we did not know what was in it till it was opened, and 2s. 5d. was then found in it; it was not fixed.
     NOT GUILTY.
 775.  JOHN GATEHOUSE was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of January , 1 wheelbarrow, value 10s., the goods of John Chapman.
   HANNAH CHAPMAN. I am the wife of John Chapman. On the 25th of January my husband sent the prisoner with this wheelbarrow, to fetch a tub from a gentleman's house - he went away, and did not return; he had been a week in our service.
   JOHN ROLSTON. I apprehended the prisoner as I was returning from Clerkenwell, on Monday week; I took him by the arm, and said, "Do you hear what Mrs. Chapman charges you with?" he said, "I don't deny taking it;" he said he had sold it to a man for 5s. or 6s. - that he could not tell the man's name, nor where he lived, but it was in the straight road from Hammersmith to Brentford; I went with him, and got it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined One Month.
 776.  FREDERICK GODDING was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of February , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of William Holdsworth , from his person.
 WILLIAM HOLDSWOTH. I live at Kingsland-green, and am a  butcher. On the 19th of February I was in Shoreditch; I met four boy s - I rather suspected them, felt my pocket, and missed my handkerchief; I saw the prisoner crossing the road - I ran, and took him; he took my handkerchief from his pocket, and threw it down in the road; I took him to the station - this is my handkerchief.
 TIMOTHY RYAN. I am a Police-officer. I received the prisoner in charge.
 GUILTY. Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 777.  WILLIAM CORNSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of February , 1 cap, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Cambridge Smith , from his person.
   THOMAS CAMBRIDGE SMITH. I am the son of Ann Smith- she lives in North-street, Spitalfields. On Saturday, the 4th of February, I was in the Tenter-ground, playing; the prisoner came behind me, and took my cap; I saw him run away with it - I ran to my mother, and spoke to the Policeman, who took him.
   JOHN SMITH. I was with my brother in the Tenterground - I saw the prisoner snatch the cap off his head, and run away; I saw him afterwards with the Policeman.
   JOHN REYNOLDS. I saw the prisoner take the cap off Smith's head.
   HENRY BOLTON. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner in Spitalfields-market on the 15th, four or five hundred yards from the Tenter-ground; he was pointed out to me by Thomas Cambridge Smith - I did not find the cap.
     NOT GUILTY.
