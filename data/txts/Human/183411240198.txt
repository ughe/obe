JOSIAH STONE. I am a gentleman's servant. On the 22nd of August I was in the service of Mr. Halden, at New Cross, near the turnpike—on that night I was coming out of my master's gate, about ten minutes before ten o'clock—I saw two men up against a high wall on the opposite side of the road—I think the road is about twenty yards wide—they were both close together, and did not move on—they were standing up—during the time they were standing against the wall a cart or waggon went by—I did not take particular notice which—after that went by, the young man's hat fell off—one was an old man, and the other a young man—the young man had his back towards me, and he had the old gentleman against the wall for about two minutes—they were standing still, only the young man had his arms round him somewhere about his middle, as I thought—after they had been there about two minutes, or two minutes and a half, the young man's hat went off, and he left go of the old gentleman, and walked away—the footpath is feather-edged  off, and the old gentleman walked away about fifteen yards into the road—the young man then felt on the footpath, as if he was feeling for something—he also grappled in the grass on each side the footpath, both by the wall and the other side—he then picked up his hat, and ran after the old gentleman, who had got about fifteen yards down in the middle of the road—he laid hold of him, and flung him down on his back—he had him there about a minute and a half in the middle of the high road—the old gentleman's hat fell off about five yards from him—I was standing at the end of the lane—I had not gone on—I stood my ground—I did not stir a foot—it was fifteen yards from where I stood to where the young man threw the old man down—the young man left the old man down in the road—the old man had gone
