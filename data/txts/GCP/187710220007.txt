757. JOSEPH OWEN (26), Feloniously cutting and wounding George
Turner, with intent to disfigure him, also to do him some grievous bodily
harm.
MR. AUSTIN METCALFE conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE TURNER. I am a dustman, of 18, Providence Place, Lisson
Grove—the prisoner formerly worked for the same firm as a boatman-
on Saturday, 29th September, I was returning from work about 8.15 p.m.,
when I saw the prisoner quarrelling with another man near the Running
Horse, in the Harrow Road-I advised the prisoner to go home-he
seized my nose and nearly had a piece off-I asked him to let me go-I
went to the hospital, and the prisoner was locked up-I have never given
him any offence-he was drunk; I don't think he could know what he
was doing.
JOSEPH HIGGINS (Policeman H 75). I found the prisoner drunk in the
Harrow Road on 29th September, and fighting with a man named Whit-
mill-I took him to the station and charged him with being drunk and
disorderly
JAMES WHITMILL. I am a dustman, of 40, North Road Wharf, Pad-
dington—on 29th September the prisoner quarrelled with me at the
Running Horse-he was drunk-the prosecutor came up, but did nothing
to the prisoner-the prisoner struggled with him and got him on the
ground-he went to the hospital and a constable took the prisoner.
RALEIGH OWEN, M.R.O.S. I am house surgeon at St. Mary's Hospital
-Turner came in on Saturday evening, September 29th-he was sober
and quite sensible, but suffering from a lacerated wound on the right side
of the nose, extending to the side of the cheek and to the cartilage of the
110se; such a wound would be caused by teeth a great deal of violence
must have been used.
Prisoner's Defence. I did not know what I was doing.
GUILTY-Five Years' Penal Servitude.
