   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . I believe, when I first saw you at Lambeth-street, Mrs. Moss and Mrs. Levy were in custody. A.  Yes—I do not recollect whether Mr. Moss was in custody or not then—Mrs. Moss and Mrs. Levy were not in custody the first time I was there—I had been examined two or three times then—I cannot say How long the last ex amination was after the first—it might have been a fortnight—I think my deposition was read over to me at the last examination—I do not know when it was taken down—Lea did not tell me there was a mistake—he never named it.
   Q.  How came you to go to Mr. Humphreys. A.  Why, it was so long, and I was afraid I should forget it—the time being so long, I wished to have it read over again—it was only read over to me once at the office—I dare say that was a month before I went to Mr. Humphreys—I was afraid something might escape my memory—I really do not know whether Mr. Moss was in custody when I was examined—I saw him in the dock once—I saw him at Lambeth-street—I did not see him anywhere else—I have seen him, since I was examined, at the Flying Horse—I really do not know whether Lea was with him at the time or not—I do not know whe ther it was once or twice I saw Mr. Moss at the Flying Horse—I do not know whether Lea was in his company on any of those occasions—I think he was by—I cannot say he was there, hut I do not know whether he was in the room with Lea—the Flying Horse is the public-house close to the police-office, where the policemen go.
   Q.  Where are you when you are there, in a private room, or with the guests? A.  Very generally with the two nieces, helping them to do about the place—I am in the kitchen when I am not with the nieces about the house—I am never in the tap-room—I see Lea and Roe sometimes go into the parlour—I am never talking to them, nor hearing what they say.
   Q.  I suppose, from the time you first went there, you have had no conversation with Lea or Roe about your evidence. A.  Lea came in one morning, and I said, "Mr. Lea, I am so frightened I shan't keep it in my memory," and asked him if he would take me to have it read over—I was not at the office when my master gave his evidence—I have never been in the office only when I had to give my evidence—my evidence was given as against my mistress and Mrs. Levy.
   Cross-examined by MR. LORD . Q.  You have spoken to Mr. Moss once or twice, you think, at the Flying Horse? A.  Yes; he asked me How I was—I am certain it was after I was examined that I saw him—Mr. Caspar was frequently in the habit of coming to my master's house—on Sunday, the 24th of March, I am quite sure I saw him there.
