SUSAN OLDER was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of Octo ber , 2 knives, value 1 s  ., 2 forks, value 8 d  .; 1 pair snuffers, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 4 spoons, value 10 d  .; 1 shaving-box, value 6 d  .; 1 brush, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 1 ounce of sewing silk, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 3 pieces of tape, value 3 d  .; 4 reels of cotton, value 4 d  . 1 ounce of pins, value 3 d  .; 25 needles, value 3 d .;
 1 handkerchief, value 2  s . 1 pocket, value 6 d .; the goods of James Lindsey, her master. REBECCA LINDSEY. I am the wife of James Lindsey, who keeps a general shop at Lewisham. The prisoner was in our service for eight months—she used to dress in the top room—I had occasion to go up there, and found an article which ought not to be there—I called her up, and pointed out a large tooth comb, a small tooth comb, and hair brush—they are things which we sell—I asked her whose they were—she said they were her own, and she had bought them at London—I pointed out our shop mark on the brush—I sent for Mr. Barham the constable, and all the articles stated were found in a box which I had lent her—she had the key, but it was not locked.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Was the mark on the brush a pri vate mark of your own? A.  Yes—I made it myself—our shop had been repaired, and there were workman about—a number of things had been taken up to the prisoner's room in a basket—I found the shaving box, knives, and snuffers, at the bottom of her box—the pins and needles were in her dress, and the combs inside the dress—the large comb was on the table under the looking-glass, and the brush and small tooth comb in the dress ing-drawer of the table—I could have seen them at any time if I had looked into the drawer.
   COURT . Q.  Was it necessary to move the things out of the box to see them? A.  Yes—they were all at the bottom, except the pins and needles, which were in the dress, and I think the pockets fell out of the dress—the combs and brushes were very nearly new—she had used them.
