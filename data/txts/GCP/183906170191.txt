THOMAS HARLAND. On the 25th of March, I was on the stand in Corn-
hill, with my cab—I remember about twelve o'clock, Moss got into the
cab-(Moss was here produced to the witness-that is the man-he desired
me to go to the Dublin Steam Wharf, Irongate-he told me to drive
fast- I went there-Moss sat in the cab, and showed a piece of paper to
one of the porters (Kingston)-the porter introduced him to the clerk, I
believe-he fetched the clerk to him - I have since seen the clerk who was
fetched to him--he asked Moss to step in, which he did I pulled the cab
over to the other side of the street, and waited-he then came out, and
one of the porters with him, carrying two boxes--I do not recollect whe-
ther the clerk came out again—the boxes were about that length and
height--they were deal boxes- I noticed red sealing-wax on them, and I
think a pink tape, but I am not quite certain I think Moss had a blue
bag with him when he went to the wharf, but I am not certain the boxes
were put into the bag, and Moss got in I gave one of the porters a four-
penny piece by Moss's direction, and Moss gave one of the porters a shil-
ling-he told me to go to the Cross Keys, Wood-street, Cheapside, and to
drive as hard as I could-when I got there the boxes were taken in, and
Moss got out of the cab--a lad, I think, took the boxes out-I believe both
the boxes were taken to the Cross Keys office-be kept me waiting a
little while, while he went with a lad to purchase a blue bag, but he could
not get one, he returned and discharged me.
Cross-examined by Mr. LORD. 0. By what means do you know the
man who came with you in the cab that day? A. I know him by sight-
we had a little bit of a dispute about the fare-I know him as I should
know any one else-I remarked at the time that he was dressed in black-
he had a broad-brimmed hat, and rather largish whiskers--not such as he
bas to-day, they were larger than he has to-day rather--they were black.
Mr. BODKIN. Q. Look at Moss, have you any doubt of that being the
man? A. Yes, that is the man.
