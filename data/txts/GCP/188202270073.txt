GUSTAVE SALING. I am a leather dresser--in December, 1880, I owed
the defendant for some goods, and took this acceptance from him (For
941., dated December 20, 1880)—he called on me & week before it was due
and said that he had bought some more goods and given me as a
reference-I asked him about my bill-he said "It is all right; it will
be met”-it was dishonoured on presentation, and I then went to his
warehouse and saw him-I noticed 301. or 401. worth of manufactured
stock on the racks, and a good many lying on the table, some of which
I had sold him, and there was quite 401. worth unmanufactured I
said "You have dishonoured my bill"-he said that he could not meet
it-I said "There is a lot of goods of mine ; you should let me have them
back if you could not meet it"-he said that he had filed his petition-I
did not know that, and said "Then I cannot bave them back” I went
again next day, and quite half of the manufactured goods were gone,
and about one-third of those which I had sold him, but a good many
manufactured goods were left-I went again next day, and there were
only goods here and there, hardly anything of value, only a few odds
and ends-I only saw him the first day I called; he promised to see
me afterwards, and I called several times, but he did not keep his
promise-I filed a petition against him in the Bankruptcy Court-I am
the petitioning creditor.
Cross-examined. The bill was due on the 23rd, and I called on him
on the 28th about 6 p.m.-I saw several men there; I don't know
their names.
