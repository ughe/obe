19th of Sept., I wrote a let ter to Mr. Bridgman, and received an answer—in consequence of further in formation, on the 21st, I applied for a search warrant, and proceeded with it and Pritchard to the chapel-yard in question—I observed a spot which denoted that the earth had been recently removed—by my direction the earth was re moved from that spot, and within five or six inches of the surface, a wooden coffin cased with lead was discovered—that was fifteen yards from the defen dant's back premises—there was no lid to it—there appeared to have been one—the nail holes were perceptible at the side where the lid had been made fast—it was filled with loose earth, and some bones—the officers emptied it, and took it out, and it was left for some days in the chapel-yard in the custody of the police, day and night—after some days, the fragments of wood that I had seen in the tomb, on the 11th, were applied to the coffin, and it formed the entire wooden lid—it had been broken to pieces, and a hole knocked in the middle at the widest part, and when the whole was put together it fitted exactly, might have been nailed on through the old nail holes—I think the coffin was found on Wednesday the 21st, and it occurred to me on Saturday the 24th to do this—I made an appointment with the police on the Monday, to have the tomb fresh opened, and was obliged to have the bricklayers again to re move the stones—every possible search was made to find the lead belonging to the lid, but we could not find it—there was a little fir tree which appeared to have been recently planted a few feet from where the coffin was found—we searched there, and everywhere else where we saw any earth recently removed, but did not find the lead—we found bones, and skulls in different parts—close to the head of
