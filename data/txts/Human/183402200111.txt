 NEW COURT, Wednesday, February 26th, 1834.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
   JOHN FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 purse, value 1d.; 1 sovereign, and 3 sixpences, the property of Charles Joseph Egleton , from his person.
   DANIEL CLARKE. I was at a sale shop in Piccadilly about three o'clock in the afternoon, on the 13th of February; the prosecutor was standing before me, and I was looking over his catalogue - the prisoner pressed between us, and pressed against the prosecutor - I looked round and saw another person pressing more than there was any occasion for - I drew back, and saw the prisoner draw his hand away from the prosecutor's right side, and he handed the contents of his hand to a person on his left - he appeared to have something in his hand - the person walked away immediately out of the shop, and the prisoner followed him - I told the prosecutor, and the prisoner was followed.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was not the room very much crowded? A. It was - the prisoner was close to me - I saw his arm - I did not see his hand - I saw him take his hand from the prosecutor's pocket.
 Q. Did he take his hand from his pocket? A. He took it from his side - he is one of the swell mob, and has been so for years - I have had threats held out to me.
 Q. Did you not tell my Lord in the first instance that you saw him take his hand from his side, and now you have sworn it was from the pocket? A. From towards his pocket.
 Q. Which of the three is truth? was it from his side, from his pocket, or from towards his pocket? A. It is all the same thing, he drew his arm from his pocket - I am a paper hanger - I went to the sale on purpose to buy- the prisoner pressed against me - there was no one between me and him - there were twenty-five or thirty people in the shop.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure that the person who pressed against you was the prisoner? A. Yes; his right arm pressed against me, and he pressed against the prosecutor, and when he was taking something from him I felt his elbow rub against my side, and I saw him pass the contents of his hand to a person; he appeared to have something in his hand, but I did not see any thing in his hand - I saw the motion of his arm.
 Q. What do you mean by saying he appeared to pass the contents of his hand to his neighbour? A. I saw him draw his arm up from the prosecutor's right side, and pass it over to another person, but there was something in his hand, or the other would not have left immediately.
   CHARLES JOSEPH EGLETON. I had a purse in my pocket with two sovereigns and three sixpences in it at the sale shop - I took one sovereign out of my purse to pay in a deposit, in case I could not get at my purse when the place was crowded - I put my purse into my pocket again with one sovereign and three sixpences in it - when Mr. Clarke spoke to me I missed my purse, and went out and took the prisoner - I had not seen him in the shop.
   THOMAS CHAPMAN(police-constable C 69). I was on duty at the watchhouse in Little Wild-street. I received information and went to Derby-court, where I saw the prosecutor with the prisoner - he gave him in charge for robbing him - the prisoner said, "You may search me, I have not got his purse."
 MR. EGLETON. When I went and collared the prisoner, I told him he had robbed me of my purse.
 Witness for the Defence.
   JOHN AVANN. I am a builder, and live at No. 1, Meredith-street, Clerkenwell - I have known the prisoner five years - he lodged with me a year and a half ago - he certainly is not one of the swell mob to my knowledge - on the day of the sale I met him in Piccadilly, and went with him to the sale room - I went to buy some articles, but I found the lots I wanted had been sold - I think the prisoner and I stayed in the room about twenty minutes - I came out with him - he certainly did not hand me a purse or anything else - he has been a respectable man.
 COURT. Q. Do you swear he did not hand any one else anything? A. That I cannot tell, the room was very crowded.(  John Russell, watchmaker and working jeweller, William-street, Clerkenwell; John Bicknell, commercial agent, No. 8, Durham-street, Vauxhall; and Thomas Kimber, of No. 275, Strand, gave the prisoner a good character.)
     NOT GUILTY.
