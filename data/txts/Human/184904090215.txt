MR. BODKIN . Q.  If Crawley came, and saw the flour, could he know that Mr. Empson's signature would obtain it? A.  No, I should think not. THOMAS JOSEPH EMPSON. I am agent to Mr. Feltham; he consigns flour to me to supply his customers—these orders are not my handwriting, they are forgeries, they were not written by my authority—I had not sold any flour to a person of the name of Wilson about that time.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is the way in which this flour is sold in the market, by samples or by description? A.  That all depends on the person who buys it—most persons know the character of flour, and buy it without a sample, and an order is given by me to deliver it—if a person were to get this order, it would not act in the same way as a bill of parcels; it is a delivery-order—I am known as a dealer—it would be known in the trade to what description of flour my order pointed—if my order were brought to a person who knew what the flour trade was, he would know what he was buying, on my order—the flour which is consigned to me is of a particular sort—I have a certain connection that I go amongst, and sell it to—this order is like a dock-warrant—I should buy flour on a note of this kind, if I knew the man whose flour it was, but I should like to know the quality of the flour—I issue perhaps four or five of these orders in the course of a week—I had one cus tomer named Wilson—I did not sell to Wilson about that time; he never bought this quantity.
   COURT . Q.  Supposing I came to you, would you, on my statement, and this order being delivered to you, buy these goods? A.  Yes, I should.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  You go to your particular customers and show them samples? A.  I do not; they know the quality—if I went to a stranger I should very likely have to show a sample, unless they made inquiry about the quality of it; I then give the delivery-order—I have never known that when I have given a customer a delivery-order, that that customer has gone into the market and sold it.
   JURY . Q.  Could any person go and sell these tickets, or sell upon these tickets; suppose he were to go to a Mr. Thompson, and say, "I have got these sacks, they are not delivered," could that person buy them? A.  I never knew such a thing—it might be done, the one having faith in the other—I write the orders on the railway.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Supposing a person to be in possession of orders of this kind, but it. being inconvenient for him to take the flour, and supposing he were acquainted with another person, would it be possible for him to sell that order?
