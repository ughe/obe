 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where do you live now? A. In Swan-street, Minories; my brother is a carver and gilder - I live with him; the lead in the house looked very good lead; nobody could call it bad lead - Gray never told me to keep anything secret; every thing was done in the day time; I did not know the landlord. - The lead was taken off before we went there, but he said he was going to replace it directly; he put a new wooden spout up.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You heard him order the lead to be ripped off? A. Yes, and he said there should be new lead; it would have been better for us if the lead was left on - there was nothing in its place but paper pitched.
   THOMAS YOLLAND. I live in Somers-town-terrace. I am a house-agent and appraiser; I went to this house, and put a man in possession, by Mrs. Campbell's direction, on the 22d of January - I went on the roof, and saw all the lead taken away, and the fencing gone, so that any person might have fallen off; I then went down stairs - the water was running down the walls - the ceiling dropping down, and the whole house in such a state as would strike a person with horror - it was occasioned by the lead being removed from the top; the cistern and leaden pipe were gone; a wooden trunk was put up, but that was not to be compared to the value of the lead; Gray called on me on the Friday following - I asked why he took the lead off the house; he said there were several holes in it, and that the cistern fell down - I had put in a distress for 44l. 10s.; the effects were valued and sold for 13l. 5s.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was the rent due from Gray or Early? A. From both, I suppose; I did not see the lead before it was taken away, and cannot say it was lead; there were no repairs done.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Had you not communication with Gray? A. Yes, he spoke of it as lead; I was not there after the 29th - no repairs were done then.
 The prisoner, in a written defence, stated that he had bought the fixtures, and given his acceptance of four months to Early; that he found the premises dilapidated, and got Mr. Cole to survey them; that he had not moved the lead for his own benefit, but that it was done in the course of the repairs.
   SAMUEL COLE. I am a carpenter and builder. I have known the prisoner many years; I surveyed the house, No. 4, Devonshire-street, in September last, by his desire - I found the back part in a most dilapidated state - it was weather boarded, but the boards were quite rotten - the lead-flat leaked in three or four places, and had bagged the ceiling under it; there was an old lead pipe down the angle of the weather-boards - it was in a very bad state, and had rotted the weather-boards; it was necessary to have them repaired - the pipe must be taken down to repair the boards; the sink had rotted the boards underneath it, and was stinking; nobody could have lived there comfortably; the floor was not rotten, but damp through it; I have been there within this fortnight; there was a water-closet, the pipes of that were bad - I suggested what repairs I thought necessary - I certainly considered the repairs necessary, considering he had four years and a half lease to run; the prisoner has commenced the repairs, and was going on with them - he bore a good character.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. He did not get you to look at them before he took them? A. No; he called the day after; I sent no notice to the landlord of what was to be done; I advised him to carry the boardwork higher, and make a lean-too roof; to remove the privy, and suggested that a woodden trunk, well-pitched, would carry the water off the top; I advised the removal of the sink, and a stone one has been put up - I saw that about a fortnight ago, since he has been custody - I do not know when it was done; the cistern was in the way, and I thought a wooden one would answer the purpose.
 Q. Might he not in two days have had fresh lead put on? A. Yes, but I advised him to have it done different; he lived Islington before that, and at Holloway, four or five years ago - he did what I advised him; the water appeared to have come through the ceiling recently - the stains were on it.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he do what he could within the time? A. Most assuredly; it would cost him a considerable sum to do all I advised - I think the lead would not have sold for what he has done.
   THOMAS SMITH. I am a bricklayer, and have been so twenty years; I was employed by the prisoner; I saw the lead-flat of the house - it was in a very bad state, cracked in ten or twelve places; I put my trowel under it, and tore it up very easy - it did not appear to me to have been repaired for eight or ten years; a crack or two might have been repaired; the ceiling was stained, bagged, and the wet had come in; the boards had given way, and the ceiling had sunk down; the lead was too heavy; I advised him to put on tiles - he ordered me to do so.
 Q. I believe you were attacked with the gout in your stomach? A. No; the prisoner was; that prevented his attending to it for four or five weeks, and he recovered it temporily during the time - I knew he was ill; I saw the cistern that evening in a good state; he said the sink stunk; I advised him to remove it, and have a stone one; I fixed a wooden cistern over the sink - I moved the privy, and made a new cesspool; the gutter was removed, as it was then unnecessary; and I repaved the yard; he repaired the place as much as I would have done for myself; my account was 12l. or 14l. - the lead was not worth more than 6l., according to my idea of it.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When was you called to view the premises? A. Two or three days after he went in; Early was not apprised of the alterations - I should think the lead weighed eight or nine cwt. I advised that the gutter should be taken up; the wooden cistern was to be leaded - the stone sink was put up the latter end of September - it was in the yard, visible to all; he has paid me partly for what I did - I should think him 20l. out of pocket.
