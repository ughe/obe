the property of Francis Henry Layland.

MR. RIBTON conducted the Prosecution, and MR. WILLIAMS the Defence
FRANCIS HENRY LAYLAND. I am a commission-agent and merchant, of
38, Basinghall-street-on 18th January, the prisoner came, and said, "I
have sold twenty-four pounds of cigars, the same as I had a sample of; will
you let me take them with me?"-I said, “No, not unless you pay for them
at the time you take them away"-my father was present the conversation
was with him, but we both said that the prisoner then asked to have a
6-1b. box to take with him-we said, “No, we will not trust you on any
account whatever"-he then asked to have them sent down to his house at
Deptford, which we agreed to do, on condition that he paid the money for
them, or returned the goods by the boy-he left, and twenty-four pounds of
cigars were sent by the boy about 5 o'clock to the prisoner's house, Douglas-
street, Deptford—the boy came to the office at 9 the next morning without
the cigars or the money.
Cross-examined. e. Were you in business for yourself at this time?
A. Yes, I cannot remember whether my name was up at that time-my
father's name was up-he has been in business some time--he bas. had

--- he did not come to our office from the prisoner the next day I have
never seen him with regard to the prisoner ; not as to this transaction;

once taken cigars from our place, and gone round with our boy from public-
house to public-house to sell them, but I refused to let him do it again I
did not refer Mr. Miller to Mr. Davis, the solicitor for the prosecution.fo

for cigars that was before he came for the twenty-four pounds.
