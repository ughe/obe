WILLIAM HARRIS. I am inspector of places for slaughtering horses-I
received information from Mr. Giddens last Friday, and I saw the prisoner
at his place-he said the gelding was Mr. Archer's, of Dorking, and he
had brought it there because it was glandered, which it was I asked if he
came through Kingston gate--he said yes, and that he started at two o'clock
in the morning-1 asked where the toll ticket was-he said he had not got it-
I then took him to the station-I made inquiries at Kingston gate- I came
back and told the prisoner what I had heard-he was locked up-he was
taken in the parish of St. George the Martyr, Southwark--Mr. Wheatley
identified the gelding in the prisoner's presence, and the prisoner then said
a man gave it him to take to the knacker's-Mr. Wheatley said that was false
-- he then put his hand to his hat and begged forgiveness-be said it was
through poverty, and his wife and family had only had a penny loaf to eat
for two days.

John GARDNER. On the 21st Feb., I was near Kennington church and
saw the prisoner-be asked if I would get him some beefsteaks and he would
give me a balfpenny-he gave me a shilling- I went to Mr. Holdsworth's,
near the church-I got the steaks and gave the shilling—I received eight-
pence balfpenny in change-I went and gave the steaks and change to the
prisoner-he gave me a halfpenny, and we then parted -a short time after I
saw him again at the same place--he said if I would go to Mr. Green's and
get a pennyworth of salts he would give me another halfpenny-he gave
me a shilling--I went, and Mr. Green cut the shilling in halves- I went back
to the prisoner and told him it was badhe said, “You got it from the
butchers"- I told him I did not (I had not brought any shilling from the
butchers)- I went to the butcher, and when I came back the prisoner was
gone-I did not see him again till the 7th of March, when he was in Horse-
monger-lane-I picked him out as the man, from a number of others.
Prisoner. Q. When the policeman came with you, did he tell you what
sort of a man I was ? A. No-he asked if I was sure it was you-he said
nothing to me before I came into the yard-I bave no doubt of him.
JAMES RUSSELL. I am servant to Mr. Holdsworth, a butcher. Gardner
came for half a pound of beefsteaks-he gave me a shilling-1 gave him a
sixpence and 24d. change-1 did not give him any shilling--the shilling he
gave me I put into the bowl-there were three sixpences in it, but no other
shilling-in a few minutes he brought me another shilling, and said " You
gave me a bad shilling"-I said, “I could not, you only gave me a shilling"
- I kept that shilling and the shilling he gave me first, and put them into a
box-1 afterwards gave them to the policeman.
