1997. WILLIAM INGRAM was indicted for stealing 1 ass, price 12 51.:
the property of William Edwards.
WILLIAM EDWARDS. I deal in coals, and live at Clapham. On Friday,
the 5th of Aug., I had a donkey turned out on Clapham-common--I saw it
safe at nine o'clock next morning, and missed it about half an hour after-
wards-about four o'clock the same afternoon, I saw it in Smithfield-it is
worth from 258. to 30s.
EDWARD LAURENCE. I live in Park-place, Walworth. On the 6th of
Aug., about twelve or half-past, I was in the Blackfriars-road, and saw the
prisoner riding a donkey, with a man walking by his side--they both spoke
to me the prisoner asked me to buy the donkey-I said I did not know-
he offered it for 128.-I offered all the money I had got, which was 88.-he
said he would not take it-he went about forty yards further, then ran back
to me, and said I should have it there was an old bridle with it I said, "I
had got nothing to hold it with"-he said, "I cannot give you this, because
my father said I was to take it home again," but at last I had it, to lead the
donkey home with-I paid him the money, and came away with it-I took
the donkey home, and put it in a little barrow to see if it would draw, but it
laid down and was no use to me, so I sent a man named Simpson with it to
Smithfield that afternoon,
Prisoner. Q. Did we halloo after you, or did you ask us whether it was
for sale ? A. You hallooed after me.
Jury. Q. Who did you pay the money to? A. To the big one-the prisoner
said it was his father's, and he was not to take less than 12s, for it-the other
was not likely to be his father, he was only seventeen or eighteen years old
I do not know whether 8s. was below the value I never bought one before.
WALTER CHARLES DODD. I live in Union-street, East-lane, Southwark.
I met Laurence in the act of buying the donkey-he paid the prisoner 86.
for it-after that I heard the prisoner say, “Shall we halve the money here"
-the other said, "No, go into the public-house" -- they went in, and I saw
no more of them
John MENNIE (policeman.) On the 7th of Aug., the prisoner was
brought to the station by Laurence, charged with stealing a donkey from Mr.
Edwards-he said a man named Blaxted told him he was going to sell a
donkey for Mr. Edwards, he afterwards said that he took the donkey and sold
it for 8s., and that all the money he received was a fourpenny-piece, two
penny pieces, and a halfpenny.
Prisoner's Defence. I was going after my master, Mr. Bluff's, donkey,
and met the man with a donkey; he asked me to go with him to sell it; I
went with him, we sold it; I knew nothing about it being stolen ; he gave
me a fourpenny-piece, and 2 d.; I said it was Mr. Edwards' money, and
heaved it on the common; I would not have it.
JURY to MR. EDWARDS. Q. Did you see the donkey in Smithfield the
same day you saw it at Clapham-common ? A. Yes I had no wish to sell
it, and did not authorize anybody to take it to Smithfield.
Guilty. Confined Four Months.
