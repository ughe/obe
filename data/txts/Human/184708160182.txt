 1917. JOHN HENRY MANN was indicted for stealing 1 box, value 1 d  .; and 2 watch movements, 6 s  .; the goods of George John Oram , and another, his masters. GEORGE JOHN ORAM. I am in partnership with my father, George Samuel Oram, in Whiskin-street—the prisoner was our apprentice. I went out on the evening of the 27th of July, and left him at work—I came home about eleven o'clock, and he was gone—I did not miss anything then, but on the following day the policeman came, and missed two watch movements—these produced are what I lost.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.  I believe he has behaved very well before? A.  I never found him dishonest before—he was rather apt to play a few tricks, and to indulge in strange peculiarities and fancies—he was rather self-willed and headstrong—I have had some trouble with him—he has always been with me at the business, and worked regular hours. EDWARD KELL( police-sergeant S  84.) About one o'clock in the morning on the 28th of July, I met the prisoner and another boy in Holloway—I stopped them, and asked them where they were going—the prisoner said, "To Barnet, and from there to Liverpool"—he said he was in no business, he was going to get what he could—I searched him, and found these movements on him—I asked whose they were—he said they were his own, that he bought them in Petticoat-lane for three shillings, and he was going to dispose of them to help him on the road.
   Cross-examined. Q.  He would tell you nothing about himself? A.  He would not—he was taken to the police-court, and remanded—we found from the police book where his master resided—the other boy was dis charged.
   MR. PRENDERGAST called
   MRS. SALISBURY.  I am the prisoner's sister—I saw several articles which looked like these, in his possession before this—he showed them to me—he wanted to make a watch for himself—he is a very honest boy, but very tricksy. NOT GUILTY .
