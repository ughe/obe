To the Court. I did not cut the horse's tail; it is very saldom I carry a knife. I have known Riley five or six months. HENRY RILEY(prisoner, on oath). I am a costermonger and live at 31, Nesbit Street, Homerton. On August 5 I was at the "Skip Aground," Lea Bridge Road, at about ten o'clock with a lad named Gardner. Friend came up and asked me if I knew where he could borrow a cart. I said, "Yes, I think so. My landlord has got use. and I have no doubt he will let you have that if you give him a bit of silver for the hiring of it." I went with Friend to Head's leaving the horse behind at "The Stag" in charge of another man Head was under the influence of drink, but I asked him if he would lend "Little Jack"—meaning Friend—his cart, and he said "You." Head said he would let him have the cart, and he took it away. I then parted company with Friend. Next morning I was again in company with Gardner in the Old Ford Road. We met Friend with a horse and cart. He pulled up and asked where we were bound for Being holiday time, we said we had nothing to do. Friend then said, "Would you like to go for a ride?" We thereupon got into she came and Friend drove us to Dorking. In Pixham Lane he asked us to get down while he was doing his business. We waited close upon an hour, and then Superintendent Boon arrested us and took us to the station, and charged us with stealing the pony. I did not steeling the pony, and know nothing about it or about the harness. Is in true that Mrs. Munday sold me some harness, and I dispossed of a to a friend named Smith, a greengrocer. I do not recognise the collar as the one which Mrs. Munday sold me. I think it was the second week in January last that I bought the harness.
 Cross-examined. We drove from Old Ford Road to Epsom, and stayed there an hour, and I suppose it would be three o'clock when we reached Dorking. We pulled up several times, and, of course had a drink at each place. I had 4s. or 5s. when we started. Friend was supposed to meet a man at Epsom, but did not do so. We talked about various things over our beer, but it never struck me to the Friend what he was going to Dorking for; it did not concern no at all. I merely went for the ride, it being holiday time. I never considered how I was to get back, but I think Friend said he was going to exchange the horse. I am a fairish judge of a horse. The was a very aged old cob and showed a lot of wear. There was on old collar in the cart. It did not strike me to ask what it was doing there. I heard Mrs. Munday say she sold me that very celler, but that is a lie. I do not know where it came from or where the other harness came from. I heard Mrs. Munday say that I had taken a fancy to her cob on more than one occasion. That is false. I had never seen her cob in my life until I went for this ride with Friend and never wanted to buy it. I know Clapton pretty well. I suppose the "Ship Aground" is about a quarter of an hour's walk trass Reader's Field, where Mrs. Munday's horse was stolen from. Harvey's stable is close by.
