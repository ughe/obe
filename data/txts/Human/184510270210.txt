   MR. BALDWIN . Q.  The prisoner asked you for the key of this out-house to put some things in? A.  Yes, for a lodger that he had got, and I saw two crates in it—they hid the furnace, and I could not see that it was gone—I cannot say whether the crates were placed one on the other or not—I ordered the box to go, and the things were secured in it—I had a great deal of conversation with Mr. Barnett about the things after I traced them—one was a great fly-wheel, seven feet in diameter—I had conversation with Mr. Barnett a fortnight after the house was left—I have seen him standing, here—I heard the prisoner had two executions in his house—at the time I gave him the key I did not give him any control over these things—I took it for granted he would not interfere with them—I do not remember precisely at what time he asked for the key—it might be a fort-night or three weeks after he had the premises—he put up some taps and vats for the Alton ale—he took everything away again—the house was gutted. JAMES BARNETT. I am a coppersmith, with Mr. Pontifex—I carry on business in Dover-road. I know the prisoner—I purchased these things of him about the 8th of May—he came to me, and I went to the premises where these things were—he said he had purchased the fixtures and tools for 60 l ., and he asked me twenty guineas for them—my foreman valued them, and I gave him 12 l . for them—I purchased them all at once.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you bad any conversation with Mr. Rice about this case? A.  I have—he told me the prisoner was to sell them, and have a profit of 3 l ., or something of that kind—I mentioned to the prosecutor that he had authorized the prisoner to sell them, and that he might get 2 l . or 3 l . by selling them.
