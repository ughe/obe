Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How many persons were there altogether? A.  About six or eight—I was passing from the fair with a friend—I was with the superintendent of the police and the high-constable, but they were not there then—I went in with a Mr. Tuck—he is not here—I had not been into any other public-house the whole day—I was engaged in my business till half-past ten o'clock—I had been at Mr. Tuck's, and drank six pennyworth of warm brandy and water—I do not say the pri soner was at the public-house—I never saw him till he was in custody—he might have been behind me. JOHN MARTIN MEWS. I am a pensioner in the Royal Hospital, and I sell coffee in the street. At a quarter past three o'clock, on the 26th of May, I was selling coffee about three yards from the door of the Bells—I saw the prisoner come out with a coat under his right arm—he went about two yards—there was no body with him—he took and shook it, and folded it up, and clapped it under his left arm—he went round by the church—it was quite light, I could see the dial—he is the man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you sure you saw the dial? A.  Yes—there are four to the church—it was a quarter after three o'clock—I had drank nothing but coffee—I arrived there at ten o'clock at night—I had had nothing but a glass of peppermint—I had been to no public-house that morning. JOHN SIZER. ( police-constable R  103.) On Thursday morning, at twenty minutes past three o'clock, Mr. Fagan told me he had lost his coat, and Mews told me something—I went after the prisoner, and found him near Greenwich church about twenty minutes past three—I asked if he had got a coat belonging to any one besides himself—he said, no, he had not—I did not search him—I asked him a second time, and he again denied it—I then said I should take him into custody, on suspicion of hav ing stolen one—after we had walked four or five paces, he said he had a coat in the boot of his coach—we turned back again towards the coach, and he pulled this coat out from the boot of an omnibus coach, and gave it to me—this is it—I do not know whether he was the driver of it.
   Cross-examined Q.  Did he appear sober? A.  He might have had some drink, but I did not see any effects of liquor.
   Prisoner's Defence  I picked the coat up on the step of the door. EDWARD SMITH. I know the prisoner—he is foreman of Mr. Wolfe's omnibuses—I was with him that morning, and we had a pint of half-and-half together—I saw a fight with the prosecutor and another person—the prisoner left the house first, and I followed him—there were about ten person in front of the bar—when the prisoner got out I saw him shake this coat—he said, "I have got a coat of Somebody's—I picked it up on the threshold of the landlord's door"—it was all over dust—it had been trodden on in the room—he said, "I am going to put it into the boot of the omnibus, and when the owner comes he shall have it." ALFRED WOLFE. I am the prisoner master. I was standing minding the omnibuses, and I saw the witness, Mews, at this time—I believe him to be drunk—the prisoner told me he had a cost placed in the boot of the omnibus. NOT GUILTY.
