 Q. How many floors are there? A. Four - the bin is on the fourth floor - it would be necessary for a person in the third floor, to look through a hole to see the wheat flow into the bin - there is no hole in a floor exactly under another hole: but within sight of it, three or four yards from it.
 Q. Have you been speaking to any of the witnesses in this case? A. No; not on any particular point - I did not speak to any one about what he was to say - I only told my son to tell the truth - I never knew him to tell a story - I spoke to my son to-day about it - I told him to tell the truth.
 Q. Did you go before the Grand Jury with him? A. No; I did not - I came from Uxbridge with him - I do not know whether I talked with him there - I have been foreman at the mill two years - I found two of these prisoners there when I went there - I have 100l. a-year - Hinchly has 27s. a-week - Hibbert 20s., and Smith 23s. - I have had no quarrel with these men in particular, only in the course of work - I have ordered them what to do - they have insulted me many times, but I never resented it nor cared for it - I never told my boy of it - he might have heard my wife speak of it - I have never spoken to Charles Burr, or my son about this, but only to tell the truth.
 WILLIAM BURR. I am the proprietor of the mill. I know the situation - if these boys were in the upper part of the mill, they could see the bin, and by coming down they could see the wheat running into the sacks, and by removing a little on one side they could see the door.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you often been in the mill? A. Yes; but I never knew of these holes till this transaction - I cannot tell whether they were made for this purpose - I should think none were so large as a foot - there are holes in many large buildings - three of these men have been many years in my employ, long before Clarke came.
     NOT GUILTY.

 721.  JOHN JOHNSON was indicted for bigamy.
 MARY ANN BOTELAR. I live at No. 68, Charlotte-street, Portland Place. I am single. I know the prisoner - I was present when he was married at St. Andrew's, Holborn, on the 14th of October, 1827 - he married  Mary Ann Harvey- I was her bridesmaid - I knew the prisoner before that time, and Mary Ann Harvey also - she is now alive; I saw her to-day - I am positive the prisoner and she were the persons who were then married.
   MARY ROBERTS. I am the mother of Mary Ann Roberts- I know the prisoner - he used to come to see my daughter, and he married her at All-Souls church, on the 15th of September last - I was not at the church, but I saw the certificate of the marriage - they came to my house and passed as man and wife from that time - they lived together.
 Prisoner. Q. How do you understand that I married your daughter? Witness. I was not present, but I heard them asked in the church.
   ROBERT BUGG. I am beadle of Clerkenwell. Mary Ann Roberts was before the magistrate, but she is under age, and her mother was bound for her appearance - I produce these two certificates - I have examined them with the books - they state that "John Johnson of this parish and Mary Ann Harvey of this parish were married in this church by banns, on this 14th day of October, 1827," and that "John Johnson, bachelor, and Mary Ann Roberts, of the district rectory of All-Souls, in this parish, spinster, were married in this church, by banns, on the 15th day of September, 1833" - I know the prisoner by his having relief from our parish - his wife pointed him out as her husband.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Confined for One Year.
 722.  JOHN JAMES FRASER was indicted for bigamy, and that he was afterwards apprehended in the county of Middlesex.
 MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
   CHARLES KNIGHT. I am a plumber and glazier. I produce these certificates, one from Queenborough in Kent, and the other from St. George the Martyr - they are true copies. (Read.)
 "Marriages solemnized in the parish of Queenborough, in the county of Kent, 1830 - John James Fraser, widower, of this parish, and  Sarah Dunn, widow, of this parish, were married in this church by banns, this 25th day of November, in the year 1830, &c."
 "Marriages solemnized in the parish of St. George the Martyr, Southwark, in the county of Surrey, in the year 1831. - James Fraser, of this parish, widower, and  Ann Bliss, of this parish, widow, were married in this church by banns, this 21st day of December, in the year 1831, &c."
   FRANCES GREEN. I am married, and live at Sheerness - in November, 1830, I was present at the marriage of the prisoner with Sarah Dunn - I witnessed the register - the prisoner is the man.
 Cross-examined by MR. STAMMERS. Q. You are sure it was the prisoner? A. Yes, I had never known him before then - he was married in the name of James Fraser.
 MR. DOANE. Q. When did you last see Sarah Dunn? A. A little while back - I have seen her several times since 1831.
   MR. STAMMERS . Q. But you had not seen her before that? A. No - I cannot tell whether her name was Sarah Johnson or what it was - Sarah Dunn was the name she went by on that occasion, and I heard several persons speak of the marriage afterwards.
 COURT. Q. But afterwards, when you heard the people talk of a marriage, did they talk of Fraser having married Sarah Dunn? A. Yes, and she was married by that name.
 MARY ANN BLISS. I lived in Lambeth in 1831, and carried on business there - on the 21st of December, 1831, I married the prisoner in the parish church of St. George the Martyr, Southwark, in the name of Ann Bliss - my name is Mary Ann Bliss - I gave my name so to Mr. Fraser, but on the day of our marriage he told me it was only a mistake made by the clerk.
