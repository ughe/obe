ANDREW HOGARTH( green-grocer, Charles-street, Stepney. ) On the afternoon of 7th of April I was passing by a booth in Stepney fair with my child—I felt something at my pocket, turned, and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand, gathering it up, and making his way into the crowd—I took him by the arm—he dropped it, I picked it up—I had it just before to wipe the child's face with—I took the prisoner nearly to the station, and met a policeman. HENRY STAMMER( police-constable K  315.) At the station, I said to the prisoner, "Have you got anything about you?"—he said, "No"—I felt be tween his shirt and his back, and pulled out this handkerchief—it is partly silk and partly cotton—he said he did not steal it, a boy gave it him outside—there was a mob of two or three hundred persons outside.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The boy told me to mind it for him, and he would give me 1 d . when I came back. (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 11.— Confined Fourteen Day .
 1293. SELINA SEARCH was indicted for stealing 1 pair of boots, value 1 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Joseph William Clark . EMMA SHEPLEY. I am sixteen years old, and live in Villiers-street, Strand. On the 9th of April I was in Mr. Clark's, the pawnbroker's shop, in Long-acre, in one of the tide boxes—the prisoner was behind me—there was a looking-glass opposite—I saw the prisoner get on a box, take a pair of boots, and place them under her shawl—I told Simons—she dropped the boots, I believe—I saw him take them—there was twenty minutes between her taking them down and her being taken into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Where were you standing? A.  in the box—the glass is on a chimney-piece—I went to pawn something—I did not see the things drop—I heard them—I went into the shop to call the young man—I did not know the prisoner before. JOHN FREDERICK SIMONS. I am in the service of Joseph William Clark. From information I received from Shepley, I called a constable—I told the prisoner she had stolen a pair of boots—she dropped them on the floor, from under her shawl—I picked them up, and gave them to the constable—when she was given into custody, she said, "You must be mistaken, young man"—they had hung on a line, about eight feet from the ground—the box on which it is represented she must have stepped is two feet high, and then they would be within her reach.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do yon know them? A.  By our private mark—( looking at them )—they were fastened to the line, swung over by a string—Shepley was about a yard and a half from the boots—a short person could see in the glass what passed—a tall person could not—there was no thing to prevent a person in the shop from seeing the woman on the box. WILLIAM BOYCE( police-constable F  71.) I went to the shop, and saw Simons stoop down and pick up a pair of boots—I took the prisoner into custody—she said Simons was quite mistaken.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you swear you saw the boots drop? A.  I am quite certain—they could not have dropped from the string, they would not have fallen on that spot, but a yard and a half from it. (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 32.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor.— Confined Three Weeks .
