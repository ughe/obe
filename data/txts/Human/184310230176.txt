JAMES WHITCOMBE. I live with my father, in Norris-street, Hoxton, I saw Copas go into the house—I then saw the prisoner take the truck away, with these things in it—there was a bag in it—he took it out, carried it through the street, rested at a public-house, and then took it away with him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  I work for a cabinet carver—I was sitting on some logs in Pump-row—was out for my tea half-hour—I had never seen the prisoner before—I am quite sure he is the person—I saw him for about half an hour—after I had told Copas, I looked for a policeman—I found two, and told them—they went after the prisoner, and caught him with the bag—I had not lost sight of him—he was on the other side of the way. JOHN JONES( policeman. ) Whitcombe told me that a man had stolen the bag off the truck, and said, "Yonder he goes"—he pointed the prisoner out to me and the other policeman—we took him with it—the prisoner appeared quite sober. DANIEL BLAY( City police-constable No.  159.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY.  3044. CLARA LEWIS and JAMES GLENDON were indicted for steal ing, on the   18th of Oct ., 1 watch, value 6 l  .; 1 watch chain, 15 s  .; and 1 watch key, 5 s  .; the goods of Samuel Irvine . SAMUEL IRVINE. I am butler to Samuel Duckworth, Esq. A few minutes before one o'clock, on the 18th of October, my watch, with a gold chain and seal, was on a nail over the fire-place in the pantry, which is about fire yards from the area door—I returned in half an hour, and they were gone—this is the watch-chain and seal.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What time had you seen it safe? A.  A few minutes before one—the bell rang, and the kitchen-maid went up to answer it—the watch must have been taken about that period—I do not believe that while the kitchen-maid went up, the party came and com mitted the robbery—there was another person taken up, but not in conse quence of what the kitchen-maid said—she could not identify that person. WILLIAM EDWARD VINRON. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned on the 18th of Oct., before four o'clock, by the prisoner Lewis.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know her? A.  Yes; she frequently pledges—I do not know that she keeps a bad house—I offered her 2 l . for the watch—she then said I knew whose it was, but did not mention the name of any person—I do not recollect her saying that a gentleman in her house asked her to pawn it. JOSEPH KENT( policeman. ) I took Lewis—I told her it was for pledging this watch—she said, when at the station-house, that she had pledged it. HENRY WYATT. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. This seal, chain, and key, were pawned by Glendon on the 18th of Oct., about three o'clock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know there was another person with him? A.  Yes; because when I asked him for a halfpenny for the ticket, he said he would go and get it—he stepped out and got a pennypiece, some person must have given it him.
