PETER ELISHA. I have the management of Mr. John Garnett's property; he has a house in Bennett's-place, Bethnal-green-road- three days after the robbery I went and fitted this lead to the house; it fitted exactly; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JAMES GRANT. I am a watchman. On the 28th of June I was in South-street Bethnal-green at twenty minutes past two o'clock in the morning; and saw two persons go down Hart's-lane towards Bennett's-place; I could not see anything with them, but just as they had left me I saw a piece of lead lying near Mr. Watson's gates; I took that up; I then saw another piece in the kennel - I took them both to the watch-house: I came back and saw the two prisoners walking and talking. At past three o'clock I went again to the place and found two more pieces of lead among some timbers; I sprung my rattle; another watchman came, and we took the lead to the watch-house - I came back again and saw the two prisoners walking near the same place; I then looked about and found another piece of lead in a stone-mason's yard; I took that, came back again, and just before five I saw the two prisoners come to the place walking and talking, and looking towards where the lead had been; I said to a man, "I don't like those two lads, I have some suspicion of them;" we took them, but found nothing on them but three knives, a button book, and a handle of a door; one of the knives was sworn to.
   VALENTINE STOKES. I am a beadle; I saw the knives, the book, and handle of the door, laid on the table at the watch-house; I am sure they came from one of the prisoners, but I cannot say which; on the Tuesday following Mr. Elisha came and I showed them to him.
   JAMES GRANT re-examined. I did not see the knives taken from them; I was laying the lead down at the time; I did not speak to the prisoners when I first saw them; I saw them go to where the lead had laid; when I saw them the second time it was getting light; it was about three hundred yards from Bennett's-place.
   PETER ELISHA. This knife I had put on the premises in one of the rooms of the house where the lead was taken from.
 ROBERT's Defence. We live within one hundred yards of the place, and had plenty of time to get away.
 M. PALMER. I am in the ginger-bread line. Roberts has worked for me for two years; he had leave to go to the play that night, and said he should sleep at his father's.
   SARAH TAYLOR. I am the sister of Taylor; he knocked at my door at five o'clock that morning and said he was going to his work.
 ROBERTS -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 TAYLOR -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Six Months.

   ELIZABETH HICKS. I am wife of John Hicks - we live in Noble-street, St. Luke's. I lost three frocks, six petticoats, a pelisse, a pinafore, and some other things out of my room where I live and sleep; they had been in a box; I wanted a night-gown for the child, and went to the box and missed them, on the 20th of August - I had seen them in the course of the day; the prisoner had lodged with me on the 2d of August, but I don't know where he lodged after that - these are the articles.
 Prisoner. Q. Will you swear they are your's? A. Yes; they belong to the child, who is my grandson.

 1902.  DANIEL BRIANT was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 3 blankets, value 4s.; 2 sheets, value 3s.; and 1 bed, value 3l.; the goods of Elizabeth Robinson, widow.
 ELIZABETH ROBINSON. I am a widow, and live in  Cartwright-square near the London-docks; the prisoner lodged at my house about four years - he left me on the 1st of July, but did not tell me he was going; he locked the door of his room. On the 4th of July, I got an officer and opened the door; these articles were all gone, and there was nothing worth mentioning left - they had been let as part of the furniture of the lodging; the prisoner appeared to be married - there was a woman with him; he came back on the 4th of July, and abused me for opening the door.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Had you not taken some spirits that day? A. No, there was no abuse on my side; he said, he had taken the bed, and I might do my worst.
   ELIZABETH ROBINSON. I am the prosecutrix's daughter. On the 4th of July, we sent for an officer, and got the door open; the prisoner sent his sister-in-law, and then he came himself - he told my mother he had taken her bed and she might do her worst.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you not find the key of his door hanging outside? A. No, the officer drew the staple out: the bed has not been found.
   JAMES ROBERTS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - he asked me what I wanted with him; I said for taking a feather bed and other articles from Mrs. Robinson - he denied it, but when he saw her, he said, he was willing to settle the matter; I questioned him about the bed - he said, it was made away with; he offered to pay Mrs Robinson 4s. a week.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Two Months.
