   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  King William-street is some distance from Bedfordbury? A.  About half a quarter of a mile—I was standing at the corner of Chandos-street, and Bedfordbury, when he drew the truck away from the door—he had an opportunity of seeing me—I have not had any conversation with anybody who has got up this prosecution, since I was before the Magistrate—I went to Mr. Humphreys the same evening—my evidence was written down before the Magistrate—I did not mention at Mr. Humphreys', for the first time, that I had been to the house, and ex amined the lead—it was before the Magistrate—I believe I said at Mr. Humphreys', for the first time, that I saw the prisoner move the truck into the back-street—I followed behind him when he was drawing the truck—he had left the truck for about a moment, when I examined it—I did not see where he went to—he did not tell me he was going to have the lead recast—it was at Mr. Humphreys' I first mentioned about the nail-holes—Mr. Elliott was not there at the time—I did not see him there at all—I went to the house afterward—I was in my police dress—my examination before the Magistrate was read over to me—I stated that it contained all I wished to say.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Mr. Humphreys is the solicitor for the prosecution? A.  Yes—I attended there, and had my examination taken down—I was desired by Mr. Humphreys to come to his office. WILLIAM HUBBARD. I am the husband of a lady whose name was Sophia Hayles—she was a widow, and I married her, fourteen or fifteen years ago—she had a son by her first marriage—he is not of age—I and my wife have re ceived rent for this house in Bedfordbury since our marriage—there was for merly a tenant named Spronton there—when he went out he let the prisoner in—the prisoner has paid me rent—I have had very great difficulty in getting it from him, and have been obliged to seize his goods several times—at last I was obliged to bring an action in my own name—I have not received any thing in consequence of that action.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner has been in the house twelve years, I believe? A.  About eleven—I hold the title deeds of this house—the title to it has not been in dispute, to my knowledge—I went to Mr. Humphreys, at the last examination—I was before the Magistrate the day the prisoner was committed. WILLIAM ELLIOTT. I am clerk to Mr. James Taylor, who is agent to this property—this house belongs to the son of Mrs. Hubbard before her marriage—he is heir-at-law—Mr. Hubbard and his wife have been in the receipt of the rents since their marriage—an action was brought for use and occupation in consequence of not getting the rent—I attended at the Master's office to tax the costs—the prisoner appeared in his own behalf—the costs were taxed at 100 l . and some odd shillings—the prisoner said he was not prepared to pay the amount, he should want time—I told him there was a large arrear of rent due, and he had better give up the premises—he said he was willing to do so—I told him Mr. Hubbard, I had no doubt, would make a great sacrifice if he would give up the possession of the property; he might give it up at Lady-day—he said he would.
