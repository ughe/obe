JOSEPH WELLER. I am a fly-driver, and live at Connor-road, Brixton-hill
--on the evening of 9th May I was at the Horns tavern-I saw Dixon
there; he had a saw in one hand and a piece of pork- I did not hear the
prisoner say anything to him, but I turned round and saw them souffling
together, and they fell—I stood in between them, and parted them the
prisoner said to me, “ Joe, will you stand in front of me when the man has
struck me with a saw t" - I said, " I don't know anything about that; I
have not seen it"-he said, "If you don't get out of the way I will hit you."
-I said, “Would you take advantage of a man when he has got his hands
full I"-with that I received a blow from him, and was knocked down by the
side of the deceased.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the blow struck with the saw 4. No;
I did not notice that the prisoner's hands were bloody after the souffle, I
was attending to the deceased; I remained with him till he died-he got a
blow after I was struck-it was my hands that were full.

JOHN CHAPMAN. I am a surgeon at Lower Norwood—I was called in to
make a post mortem examination of the deceased I found a fracture on the
right side of the skull, by which a large triangular piece of bone had been
displaced ; under that bone, a blood-vessel had been ruptured, and a large
quantity of blood was thrown out on the skull; on the opposite side of the
skull a blood-Vessel had also been ruptured, and a quantity of blood was
effused on the surface of the brain there, of which injuries he died—the
fracture extended to the base of the skull.
Cross-examined. Q. The fracture probably was caused by his head coming
in contact with some hard substance! 4. No doubt of it; it was not the
result of a blow with a fist ; that is quite impossible-it would require
some force—the force of a fall might have done it-his own weight in falling
would be quite sufficient to account for it
