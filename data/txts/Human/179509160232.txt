   HENRY AUSTIN sworn.
 I am president of the inland office.
 Q. Of course you know the prisoner at the bar? - Yes. On the 1st of September he came to me, very much agitated, and said, that he had been applied to by a gentleman of whom he had received a letter containing money, who had informed him that the letter had not got to hand. I asked him whether he had received charge with it at the time, and whether he had seen it entered at the time he delivered it in? He said he had forgot to do that; I told him as that was the case he must pay the man whatever it was; which he promised to do.
 Q. The prisoner was continued in the Post office as letter carrier, notwithstanding this thing had transpired, and he was the person that let you know that the fact had really happened? - He has been in the office about two years; I never had the least doubt of him.
 Q. Would he be taken into the service of the Post office again? - Yes, he would.
 Q. They, I understand, take up this defence? - They do.
 Q. It is at their desire that you attend? - It is.
 Court. When there is a money letter, and when there is not, do not they separate the letters? - They do.
 Q. And they always distinguish that to the person that takes them at the window? - Yes.
 Q. How many of these money letters do you think may be brought by a bellman at a time? - A man may bring three or four in the evening, and he may not bring one in the course of a week.
     Not GUILTY.

 454.  WILLIAM HENRY WILCOX was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 4th of July , twenty-three loaves of bread, value 18s. the goods of Henry Freame.(The case opened by Mr. Knowlys.)
   HENRY FREAME sworn.
 Q. I believe you are a  baker in Northumberland street, in the Strand? - Yes.
 Q. Was the prisoner, Wilcox, in your service at the time this matter took place? - He was a twelvemonth from the 16th of April last. On the 4th of July, in consequence of missing bread before, I counted my bread that was left in my kitchen, there were twenty-six, twelve, and seven quartern loaves; on that day, I being unwell, had requested Mr. Lashbrooke, a neighbour, to be at the top of the street, in case he should resist. About ten o'clock in the morning he had been down to Westminster, serving all there; he then came in and put two loaves in his pail, and out he went.
 Q. Did you see him? - I did not. He went out with the pail to fetch yeast; in consequence of which I looked into the kitchen and missed two loaves. I immediately followed him; I came up with him about the middle of the street, or hardly so high, it may be twenty or thirty yards from the door; when I came up to him, I took hold of him, and I believe I said, you scoundrel, where are you going with that? I perceived two loaves in the pail; I do not recollect that he made any reply whatever; but requested me to let him walk without being held. I told him he need not be ashamed of me if he was not of his action. Mr. Lashbrooke came up within three yards when I laid hold of him.
