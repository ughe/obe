DE KEYSER, Mayor.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. This half-crown and shilling are bad—ifs
coin is chipped with a knife it is undoubtedly bad.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. "On Wednesday a
gentleman gave me a half-crown, and that I passed at the prosecutor's
house. I did not know it was bad. That was the only one I passed.
On Saturday & gentleman gave me four shillings, and one of them I
passed at the prosecutor's. I did not know it was a bad one."
The prisoner repeated the same statement through the interproter, and said
that Mr. Handler gave her in custody because she would not pay him 38. 6d.
L. J. HANDLER (Re-examinod). I did not say that unless the prisoner
paid me 38. 6d. I would have her locked up on the day I found the
half-crown in the till my takings were 308., but there was only one half-
crown.
Not GUILTY.
OLD COURT.-Tuesday, February 28th, 1888.
Before Mr. Justice Hawkins.
331. CHARLES BONLETT (30) and FREDERICK BRENHAM
(22), Feloniously forging and uttering an order for 5l. with intent to
defraud.
MR. POLAND Prosecuted.
MARY BALFOUR. I keep & confectioner's shop at 77, Marchmont
Street, Tavistock Square-on 24th December Brenham, who is not quite
& stranger to me, came to my shop and handed me this letter produced)
containing this cheque for 5l.-I read this note and noticed that it was
headed "University Hall"-I asked him some questions about the hall,
and he seemed to know something about the place. (Letter read:
“University Hall, 24th December, 1887. Mrs. Balfour, - Please supply
the undermentioned, for which I enclose a 5l. cheque. Will you kindly
settle up the balance and send by bearer, and oblige, GEORGE PONSPORD,
care of E. Askew." Then followed a list of goods, and then it stated:
“Pease exouse cheque, as I have not got ready cash. Please send
balance and oblige." The cheque was for 51., and purported to be signed by
George Ponsford, and was payable to Mrs. Balfour, and was draron on the High
Wycombe Branch of the London and County Bank.) The price of the goods
was 88. 4d.-I believed that it was a genuine order and cheque, and
supplied him with the goods, and handed him the balance of 41. 116. 8d.
-my boy Pearce then left the shop with Brenham for the purpose of
carrying the goods-I paid the cheque into my bank, and it was returned
marked "Forged," as it is now-at the end of January I saw Brenham
at the station with six or seven other people, and picked him out-I am
quite sure he is the person who brought the letter and cheque-I do not
know Bonlett.
GEORGE EDWARD PEAROE. On 24th December I was outside Mrs.
Balfour's shop, and carried the basket of things towards University Hall
--I first saw Brenham a little way from the shop, and he said "I think I
can carry these things now"-another man then came up who I believe
is Bonlett, and Brenham said "Here's my brother; he will help me carry
the things"-I then handed him the goods out of the basket, and he
said "I would not hurry back if I were you; I would take my time"-
