   EDWARD CHURCH. I am an attorney. Mr. Wadden put this bill into my hands - I never saw the prisoner about it until he was taken - I went to Gutter-lane, to his warehouse, and found a boy who gave his name as Kirk, he said, he was Richmond's clerk - I did not find Richmond - I stated what I had come about; I found the warehouse quite empty, quite stripped of every parcel of goods, and furniture, of every description - I went to where the bill was made payable, and inquired for Mr. Clark, but could not find any such person there.
   WILLIAM ADAMSON. I am an inspector of Police - the prisoner did belong to the Police; he joined it in September, last year, and resigned in November - I know his hand-writing, (looking at the indorsement of the bill,) this is his hand-writing.
   JOHN JOSEPH MANNOCK. I am clerk to Mr. Wadden - I received the letter, which he found, on coming to his warehouse - I received it from a lad, about ten o'clock in the morning, before Mr. Wadden came to town, and gave it to him when he arrived at the warehouse; it was wafered - I delivered it to him in the same state in which I received it.
 The prisoner in an address to the Court, stated, that his affairs became involved in the year 1829, when he was introduced to a man named Clark, who held out flattering prospects to him, and persuaded him to go to France - that Clark and himself had accepted bills for each other; Clark was to remit the cash to a friend of his at the Commercial Chambers, where they were made payable.
   DANIEL LAY, JUN. I am a clerk in the office of Oliverson & Co., solicitors, Old Jewry, and was so in 1828 - I knew a person named George Delianson Clark, I do not know where he lived; I did not know him when I arrested him; I did not of my own knowledge know where he lived - I arrested him at the suit of Pulley, Pear, and Field - I did not go with the officer to arrest him - I saw him; he was arrested about the 9th of February, 1828, and remained in custody till after the 29th of May, but I cannot say how long - I never saw him at that time but once; I saw him two or three times, in 1831 - I have seen him write, I think this is his hand-writing, it is signed merely D. Clark - I have seen him write two or three times.
 COURT. Q. What did you see him write? A. An assignment which I prepared - I saw him sign it - he signed it, "George Delianson Clark," at the same time I saw him write some letters or memorandums; I read them - that was in 1831, when he was in custody.
   JOHN LARGE. I am a coachmaker. I knew Mr. Delianson Clark, in 1829 - I did not know him by the name of George - I have had transactions with him, in the way of business, and took an acceptance from him - I have seen him write several times - I doubt the acceptance to this bill being his hand-writing; it is not signed the same as mine; the signature is not certainly like the one I have - I have letters from him - this signature is not like his certainly, as far as I am able to judge.
   THOMAS PARK. I am a wine-merchant, and live in America-square, Minories - in 1829 I had apartments in Commercial Chambers - I knew George Delianson Clark, in 1828 - I had no transactions with him in the way of business - I saw him at my chambers; he called to induce me to accept for him while he was in Paris; he told me he was going to Paris, and I received letters from him at Paris - I have seen him write perhaps ten or twelve times - I should say the acceptance, of this bill, is not his hand-writing; he always signed his name in full, Delianson Clark; and his letters from Paris were always signed the same way - the hand-writing is in every respect is like his - I believe it to be his - I never had any dealings with him.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Had you ever a bill of his acceptance? A. Never; he drew on me - I believe the whole of this to be his writing, signature and all; it is very much like his writing - I should say it was his writing, and act on it.
   WILLIAM LAYWOOD. I am a solicitor at Furnival's-inn. I have been concerned for George Delianson Clark, he goes by the name Delianson, and George Delianson Clark - I have every reason to believe he is now at Brussels - I have seen him write several times within the last three months - I believe this bill of exchange to be the hand writing of Mr. Clark.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Had you seen him in 1829 write at all? A. I have not known him till about three months ago - I became acquainted with him the latter end of April - I believe he left London the 6th or 7th of last month, for Brussels - I have seen him write at least ten times - I never saw him accept a bill.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you acted on the letters you received from him? A. I have.
   WILLIAM POPE. I am an oilman, and live in Commercial-road, East - I know George Delianson Clark - I have seen him write three or four times, and perhaps more - the acceptance of this bill I have no doubt is in his hand writing.
