 How long did they stay out talking? - I saw no more of them; I don't know when the box was taken away, nor by which of them; I called the maid, and asked her if they were gone; she said she did not know; I asked the master, and he said they paid for the six-penny worth of brandy and water, and ran fast down the yard.
 You never saw the prisoner before; are you sure he is the man? - Yes; two or three days before he was taken, he came into the house where I was to take possession of some goods; I said I thought I knew him, and asked him if his name was not Blackett? he said it was; I had heard his name mentioned when they were together; the other man called him Blackett; and then I asked him where the other was? he said if I would give him a bottle of wine, or some money, he would tell me.
 Cross Examination.
 What are you? - I live upon what I have, I had 3000 l. left me.
 Did you say any thing to any body about this trial yesterday? - Not that I know.
 Did you say if this man would give you five guineas you would not appear against him? - I said no such thing; I said if the person would come and pay me half my money and half my expences, the bill might be thrown out; and he said he would put me in a way of doing it.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I was put in possession of the house where this lady was a lodger, I was there four days and nights, and we breakfasted, dined, and supped together, and nothing was said about it.
 He called four witnesses, who gave him a good character.
     GUILTY. Death.

   RICHARD BRYANT sworn.
 I am an officer at Sir  John Fielding's: I went with Mr. Clark, the first day of last sessions, to a house by Battle-bridge-wells, in Britania-street, I believe it is; Mr. Clark first sent a gentleman's servant with a letter in his hand for Mr Askew, to get the easier admittance into the house; the door was opened by Mrs. Horner, myself and Mr. Evans rushed into the house; we immediately ran up stairs, and upon the garret stairs, within three or four steps of the top, I met Askew; he had got on a cheque shirt, the sleeves of which were tucked up, and the collar was unbuttoned; he pressed very much to come down stairs; we had a little struggle with him; I insisted upon his going up stairs, and we forced him up: when we met him on the stairs, Bissell ran out of a garret that is on the left hand into a garret that fronts the stairs; we took Askew into the front garret, and kept him there till Mr. Clark came.
 How was Bissell dressed? - He had his coat off, a silk handkerchief tied round his head, his breeches knees were unbuttoned, he had no garters on, and was in a sweat; they both seemed to be in a working condition; their hands were all over mould and sand; they both wanted much to wash their hands, but I would not let them till Mr. Clark came up: in the front garret there was a forge fixed, a crucible upon the fire, with some metal in it, and a large quantity of charcoal lying by; there were the flasks; there was some sand, which was hot, that must have been just made use of; there was scouring-paper, and a bottle of aqua-fortis; these were all in the front garret, into which Bissell ran; there was a small earthen tea-cup, with some pickle in it, which is used in that business, and some water in a quart stone pan, of a greenish colour;
