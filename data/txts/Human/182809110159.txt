HENRY CHURCHILL. I am shopman to Mr. James Drew, a pawnbroker, of Clark's-place, Islington. On the 22d of August, about four o'clock, a person told me some things had been taken; I went out, and saw the prisoner about forty yards off, tying up the trousers - he saw me, turned into another street, and threw down the trousers; I pursued, took him, and took up the trousers - I have seen his master, who will employ him still.
   JAMES DAVIS. I was standing at the top of White Conduit-street, and saw the prisoner running with a number of persons after him; he threw down these trousers, ran about twenty yards further, and was taken by Whitmore.
   WILLIAM WHITMORE. I stopped the prisoner, and gave him to Churchill.
   THOMAS COPE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, who said he did it through distress.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1868.  SIMEON SLIGO was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 2 planes, value 5s., and three other carpenter's tools, value 9s., the goods of William Smith, and another.
   JOSEPH THOMAS TILLEY. I am apprentice to Mr. William Smith and his partner; they are cabinet-makers, and live in Charter-house-lane - the prisoner was in their employ for a few days; a small saw was missing: on the 5th of July I was going up Old-street, and saw it exposed for sale in Mr. Penny's shop.
   WILLIAM PENNY. I bought this saw, this plane, this square, and these other tools, of the prisoner.
   WILLIAM KIRBY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and produce the tools.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing about them.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined One Month.
 1869.  THOMAS WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , 5 loaves of bread, value 4s., and 1 basket, value 1s., the goods of William Duffield.
 WILLIAM DUFFIELD. I am a  baker. I lost five loaves and a basket from the corner of Portman-place, on the 5th of August, while I was gone to serve a customer; a footman told me it was stolen - I found it in possession of the officer.
   WILLIAM HINDS. I am an officer. I stopped the prisoner with the bread.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1870.  MARY ANN BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 3 blankets, value 6s.; 3 pillows, value 1s. 6d., and 2 sheets, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Johnson ; 1 coat, value 6d.; 1 waistcoat, value 6d., and 1 shirt, value 6d., the goods of Robert Rose ; 1 pair of trousers, value 6d., and 1 waistcoat, value 3d., the goods of Charlotte Barrett , and 1 sheet, value 1s. 6d., the goods of William Cuttle.
   THOMAS JOHNSON. I am a broker, and live in Rose-lane. I lost three blankets, three pillows, and two sheets, from the lower room of No. 11, which belongs to me; the prisoner lodged in the two-pair front room of that house - the blankets were part of the furniture of that room; a coat, waistcoat, and shirt, were also taken from there, which belonged to Robert Rose.
   MOSES SIMMONS. I am an officer, and took the prisoner; I found this one blanket under the bedstead, in her room, and these other things in the cupboard.
   SARAH JOHNSON. I am the wife of Thomas Johnson. The prisoner lodged in one of our houses - these articles were part of the furniture of another room, and were our property.
   LEONARD CLARE MATTHEWS. I have a blanket which was pawned with me on the 2d of July, and a pillow on the same day; I cannot say it was the prisoner who pawned them - it was a female.
 Prisoner. I pawned them, but they were my mother's property for years.
   DAVID JAMES. I am shopman to a pawnbroker, in Whitechapel. I have a pillow and a shirt, pawned at different times by the prisoner.
   WILLIAM PUTMAN. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pillow pawned on the 6th of August, in the name of Brown - I cannot say by whom.
   MATILDA CUTTLE. I am the wife of William Cuttle. This sheet is my husband's - it was lost from that house.
   SOPHIA ROSE. I am the wife of Robert Rose. This coat and shirt belonged to him, and were found in the prisoner's room.
   SAMUEL GREEN. I went with Simmons to take the prisoner, at No. 11, Rose-lane; I found this coat and shirt rolled up, and these duplicates were in the room.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating, that Johnson kept a fence and a brothel, and had two lodgers in prison- that the pillow and blanket were her mother's, and the shirt her brother's.
   MARY ANN BROWN. The prisoner is my daughter; this pillow and blanket, which were pawned at Mr. Matthews', are mine, and have been in my family for years.
 COURT to THOMAS JOHNSON. Q. What house is this you keep? A. A ready furnished lodging-house - there are no women lodge there.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1871.  JAMES HARRIS and WILLIAM COLTNEY were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 7lbs. weight of lead, value 10s., the goods of Charles Walker , and affixed to a certain building; against the Statute.
   EDWARD COCKERTON. I am agent to Mr. Charles Walker; he has a house in Phoenix-street, Bethnal-green; I was at that house on the night of the 28th of August, and all was right - this lead was afterwards found, and I fitted it to the roof, it matched exactly.
   JOHN RIGG. I am a watchman. On the morning of the 29th of August, about half-past two o'clock, I was sitting in my box and received information of some persons robbing a house in Phoenix-street; I went there but saw no one - there was a ladder against one of the houses, I went up it, and went down the middle house of the three; I there found a door fastened, and broke it open but found no one; I went up stairs again and found in one room a coat, a hat, and a pair of shoes, with a quantity of lead.
