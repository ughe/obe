I heard him threaten so often that I never thought he intended to do it—I believe he rents the house from some person; I do not know who—he told me that he paid 60 l . a year from it, but I do not know what profit he makes, or what the other people pay him—I live rent free for seeing to the house—the bed lies flat on the floor. SARAH TURNER. I occupy the back kitchen of this house in the base ment, there are only two rooms down there, mine and Lintott's—on 3rd October I heard the prisoner come down the kitchen stairs—I did not see him, but I heard him speak, and knew his voice—he knocked at Lintott's door two  or three times, and I heard him say "Open the b——door"—I heard him burst it open, and he was in the room a quarter of an hour; he then came out and shut the door very violently, and came upstairs—about five minutes after that a child came down to go into the kitchen, and screamed out "Mrs. Turner, our house is on fire"—I then went into lintott's kitchen and saw the bed on fire, near the fireplace, and the flames were blazing very high—the kitchen is rather dark, but it was a fine day, and a person going there would not require a light—I screamed to Mrs. Clark to bring some water, and ran upstairs and called Mr. Eady—after the flames were got down a little I went to the street door, and saw the prisoner walking up and down outside, while the fire was still burning—I said "You wicked old man, you have set the house alight"—he abused me, and said that I did not pay my way—I did not owe him anything—I then went back, and the fire was pretty well got out—I saw shavings and flock burning on the floor, which were from the mattress and bed—I have heard the prisoner say several times that he would burn the——house down, and all that were in it—he was sometimes drunk and sometimes sober when he said that—I do not know what the occasion was, but I believe he got into difficulties—he was not sober on 3rd October, but he was accountable for his actions—he had been drinking the day before.
