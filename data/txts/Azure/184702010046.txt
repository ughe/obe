520. MARY HOUSEMAN was indicted for feloniously stealing 24
bunches of artificial flowers, value 158. ; 3 caps, 58. ; and 3 boxes, 28. ;
William Alexander Peartree, her master.
SUSANNAH PEARTREE. I am the wife of William Alexander Peartree,a
jeweller in Arlington-street, Hoxton. I employed the prisoner by the wek,
to make artificial flowers—she went out about the 12th Nov. with thre
boxes, containing caps and artificial flowers, to sell for me—she did not
return—I saw no more of her or the flowers or tbe money—I believe she
it from distress.
Prisoner's Defence. I was to teach her daughter to make flowers, for which
she said she would give me more money than I had earned at the
before, I was greatly distressed, and had to ask her for a few pence to
food; she said she would give 78. a week, and could not give me mm;
I complained, and she then fixed a price to each bunch of flowers, which
more than any one in the trade would give; she then brought me thrée
and said I was to take them for sale; they were made with dirty materials;
the boxes I had were only paper, and not fit for such a wet afternoon, havig
no oilskin, consequently the goods were very much damaged and rendered
unsaleable; being driven to despair, and cold and hungry, I did not return,
but got employ elsewhere.
SUSANNAH PEARTREE, re-examined. I did not owe her any wages—de
had 28. 2d. of me before any was due—my daughter did not want to be ta%ht
the business—I took the prisoner in out of pure charity, as I
was badly off, and it was winter.
GUILTY. Aged 31 .—Recommended to mercy.—Conßned One Month.
