   WILLIAM PENNY. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on Sunday evening, about eleven o'clock, and found 19s. on him, but no notes; he was standing under a lamp, with a female - he said he was innocent; I said, "Why did you abscond from your house with the woman you have lived with twelve years?" he then began to pretend intoxication; he had a new hat on, and almost all his clothes were new - I knew that, for I was in the habit of seeing him daily.
 The prisoner, upon being called upon for his Defence, in a coarse and indelicate statement, represented that the prosecutor and Brookes had come to her house in a complete state of intoxication, and wished for a room, which he refused them, and turned them out of doors; and that he was robbed previous to coming to his house.
   JOSEPH GLASS. I am a chimney-sweeper. On Friday last, about half-past two o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prosecutor laying in the kennel, drunk; his face was bleeding - I picked him up, and a female that was with him; I wiped his face with my handkerchief, and took him to No. 9, Glasshouse-street - it must then have been near three o'clock, and between six and seven o'clock in the evening, I saw him sitting in the necessary, asleep- I and a female helped him out.
 COURT. Q. Where is the necessary? A. In the back apartment of the house, No. 9, next door to the Compasses - I was called there to assist him out; he was quite drunk, and could not stand.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you hear him say some wh - s had rooed him? A. He said so when I took him out of the kennel - I am sure he was intoxicated at half-past two o'clock.
   JOSEPH COCKNEY. I am a waiter at the Compasses. Last Friday, about half-past two or three o'clock, I saw the prosecutor in our house - he was neither drunk nor sober, half and half; I saw the sweep pick him up out of the kennel; the left side of his face was scratched; Glass took him to the next house - his hat was under our bar all the night; it was a straw hat - he had it on when laying in the kennel - he came into our house after he was picked up, about three o'clock, and had five or six glasses of brandy and water, and then the landlord refused to let him have more; he went out with another female, returned in ten minutes, and wanted more; he said he had been with a girl, and had been dished - and about halfpast four o'clock, when I took a pot of beer to the next house, he was asleep in the privy.
 COURT. Q.When did you hear about this business? A. Not till next day.

 1743.  WILLIAM WRIGHT and JOHN JONES were indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 13 handkerchiefs, value 3l., the goods of James William Brown.
   GEORGE GIBBS. I am in the employ of James William Brown, a hosier, in the Strand. On the 14th of July, about half-past three o'clock, I found the glass of the window broken and thirteen handkerchiefs taken out; I looked round, and the Policeman brought the two prisoners back to the shop - he had some of the handkerchiefs in his hand; I had not seen the prisoners at the window.
 JOSEPH MORTON. I am a Policeman. I was on duty, and saw the prisoners standing at Mr. Brown's shop window for about one minute; they then both ran away together; I followed, and took them; Wright took two handkerchiefs out of his bosom, and said, "I found these two, and will give them you - they are not mine;" Jones had nothing; on the way to the watch-house he abused me most scandalously, and struck me twice in the face; I did not see Wright drop any thing, but a gentleman picked up these six handkerchiefs in the Strand, and gave them to me, as I took them to the station-house; Jones got from me, but was secured again.
   WILLIAM TREWATHER. I searched Wright after he was taken, and found five handkerchiefs, between his shirt and skin.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Wright's Defence. I saw something in the window, and thought it was a bill; I looked at it, and a young man who stood there threw the handkerchiefs down - I picked them up, and gave them to the Policeman.
 Jones' Defence. I do not know this boy, and was not with him.
 WRIGHT -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 JONES -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Seven Years.
 1744.  JOHN MATHEWS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 1 box, value 1s.; 1 sovereign, 9 half-sovereigns, 120 half-crowns, and 90 shillings, the property of Sarah Cooper , in her dwelling-house.
 MR. DOWLING conducted the prosecution.
   SUSAN COOPER. I am the daughter of Sarah Cooper, who keeps  the Nag's Head, in Hackney-road. On the 18th of August, about seven o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came there with a person taller than himself, and had a pint of beer; they went away at eleven, came again about two, and had a glass of ale; I then went into the back room, leaving them alone in the front of the bar - I returned in about five minutes, and the prisoner was gone- his companion asked for a glass of gin and water; I went to call somebody to make it, then he said, "Never mind, I'll call again in five minutes," and went away; between four and five o'clock somebody came to borrow some money -I got the till out from under the counter, and there was only five or six new sixpences in it; the money-box was gone - I saw it there between one and two o'clock.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Did not the prisoner frequent your house? A. No, never; I have been there two or three years - I am not quite sure the clock had struck three when he left; I was backwards and forwards from the bar to the room behind.
