   Clark's Defence.  I was going down Princes-street; and met Mortishead; he asked me to give him a help with his truck down to George-street; he took it down to the store; he and I took it in; the policeman came and said, "What have you got here?" Mortishead said, "Some lead, and I can giv an account of it;" Hickman never came out of the shop at all; Mortishead said he would tell when he came back where he got it; and when I saw him go away, I ran away, because I thought perhaps it was not right; I went to work the next day, and my master sent me at half-past four o'clock for a piece of plank; I met the policeman at the Marsh-gate, in coloured clothes; he asked me if I remembered meeting him the evening before; I said, "No," because I was not quite certain of the man; he told me to go down to the station—if I was guilty of stealing the lead, would I have gone to work the next day within 200 yards of the place? RICHARD ARTHUR re-examined.  I found him at work by the Marsh-gate, in the Westminster-road—he was taking a plank to Mr. Chambers—that is about 150 yards from Hickman's shop.
   CLARKE  — GUILTY. Aged 27.
   MORTISHEAD  — GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Nine Months .
   HICKMAN  — NOT GUILTY.
 640. WILLIAM SPINKS and THOMAS GORE were indicted for steal ing 150 tails, called foreign docks, value 6 d  ., and 3lbs. weight of hair, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Cleeve Woodward Hooper , and another, their masters.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. CLEEVE WOODWARD HOOPER. I am in partnership with my brother—we are leather-factors, in Seething-lane—we formerly carried on business as tanners in Bermondsey—a quantity of tails and hair were removed from Ber mondsey to Leadenhall-street and from thence to Seething-lane—I let a warehouse where some of this hair and tails were on the 6th of Jan., and my foreman gave orders to have it cleared out—the prisoners have been in my service for fourteen years—none of these things were allowed to them as per quisites—I did not allow them, on the 8th of Jan., to take any of these tails—the hair produced corresponds with the rest that I have—these are the tails of hides, cut off in a dry state.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know that there is amongst tanners a custom that their servants should have these docks, as they are called, as perquisites? A.  No, it is a system that we have set our faces against—it is allowed in the country, but never in London.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  What is the value of those tails? A.  Six pence a pound—they are sold at 3 d . sometimes—it is according to the quality—I never heard of any being sold at less than 3 d .—there is no difference between tails and docks—they are more valuable in proportion to the quantity of hair on them—these are mostly long ones—I have not said that they were not worth more than 2 s .
