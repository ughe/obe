Mr. Bopkin, on behalf of the male prisoner, having obtained permission
withdraw his plea of not guilty, put in a demurrer to the indictment, and
issue being thereupon joined, he proceeded to argue that in order to support
charge of this nature, the letter must contain such a clear undoubted threat, a
to induce the prosecutors to part with their money from fear of personal injury:
and that the demand must import on the face of it, some real or assumed power
to enforce it. He contended that this letter contained no such demand; that i
only amounted to a request, or a suggestion; and that as it contained no menor
of a personal nature, it did not come within the statute.-See Rex v. Pickford
4 Car. & Payne, 227, and Rex v. Carruthers, Vol. 20 of Session Papers, p. 821
MR. BARON PLATT: The Court, as at present advised, considers this leh
ter 80 completely within the meaning of the Act of Parliament, that it would
be very slow indeed upon a technical objection to put an end to the prosecution
and therefore, for the purpose of having the matter argued in another place, the
point shall be reserved. The Court will give judgment in favour of the pros.
cution, but I am afraid the party must now bring a Writ of Error for the purpose
of raising it.
MR. JUSTICE WILLIAMS : Probably they would find it more convenient #
withdraw the demurrer.
MR. BALLANTINE: Then there must be a plea of Guilty. I contend that
the judgment upon a demurrer is final, and I shall ask from your Lordships
final judgment, leaving the prisoner to a Writ of Error.
MR. JUSTICE WILLIAMS': Surely it is competent to the Court to allow the
party to withdraw the demurrer.
MR. BALLANTINE: I submit not; it is now part of the record, and must be
disposed of. I contend that my friends by demurring admit the facts, and i
they wish more conveniently to bring the matter before a Court of Error, the
prisoner must plead Guilty, and then there must be a motion in arrest of jede
ment. I would not object to the demurrer being treated as a nullity; but if
having failed upon the demurrer, they now wish to try in the ordinary way,
I object; and I contend that the Court has no power to allow the demurrer to be
withdrawn. If a prisoner has pleaded Not Guilty, and is given in charge, i
what way would your Lordships dispose of that, except by the finding of the
Jury? I hardly know of a direct authority on the point, but would refer to the
Queen v. Bowen, 1 Car. & Kir. 501.
MR. BODKIN: It is a matter in the discretion of the Court. Your Lord
ships have already exercised a discretion in allowing the prisoner to withdrau
his plea.
MR. HUDDLESTON: The judgment would be "respondeat ouster."
MR. BARON PLATT: I own I have not the slightest doubt of the power of the
Court to allow the demurrer to be withdrawn at any time before judgment is actr
ally given. The exercise of that power is at the discretion of the Court. The
practice has always been to allow a prisoner indicted for felony, if he faile
upon a demurrer, to plead over; the original ground of that rule, was it
favorem vitæ; but it is not to be said, because a felon is not liable to be executed
that he is to lose that privilege. We therefore think the demurrer ought to be
allowed to be withdrawn.
The retractation of the plea of Not Guilty, and the demurrer, were ordered
to be expunged, and the JURY were charged upon the original plea.
