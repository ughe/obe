ALIGE GILES. I am the widow of the late George William Giles; he was
law writer, and was aged thirty-two years we lived before this occur
rence, in Feb., at No. 1, Princes-street, Commercial-road, Lambeth I have
one child. On Saturday night, 25th Feb., he came home about 12 o'clock
st night-in consequence of what he said he and I went out together,
little after 12 o'clock; we went to the end of the Cornwall-road together
I saw the prisoner, who was then a policeman, L 103_my husband said to
him, * 108 L, I will go and report you" the prisoner took hold of him byl
the collar, and said, “You are come out again, are you f now I will take
you "-I put my hand on the prisoner's shoulder, and said, "Don't take
him I don't take him ! he will not report you the prisoner said. It
you don't get away, I will take you both"__my husband struggled I had
hold of his right arm, and he had a roll of paper in his right hand he had
no stick, only a roll of paper-I believe he struck the prisoner with the
roll of paper, but it must have been very slightly, for I had hold of his right
arm--the prisoner had hold of him all the time when my husband struck
him, or was supposed to strike him, the prisoner said, “That will do, old
fellow; that will do, old fellow; come along, old fellow, come along, old
fellow," and he took his staff out of his pocket, and struck my husband
across the head twice-he struck him behind the left ear, towards the back
part of the head; the blows were very severe-when the prisoner struck
my husband he dropped the paper, and took hold of me with both hands
round the waist, and I and my husband both fell on the ground it was
the weight of my husband taking hold of me made me fall I heard some
one say, “You are exceeding your duty, policeman;" that was said at the
same time that the blows were given, or directly afterwards I did not see
the person come up who said that--when I and my husband went down, I
remember the prisoner kneeling on one knee by the side of us D am sure
no one but the prisoner struck my husband-the prisoner sprung his rattle,
and another officer, L 181, came up the prisoner took my husband to the
Tower-street station, and I went there too my husband was looked up
and in the morning I procured bail, and he was liberated brought him
home in a cab, about half-past 10 or 11 o'clock-he complained very much
of his head, and he went to bed directly after he got home he did not get!
up till tea time-I called him to get up, and he again complained _I felt!
his head, and found a lump on his head behind the ear, on the left side;
that was the position in which the blow was given, in which the staff came
down-the blows were delivered very near together from that time down
to the time my husband died, he very often made complaints respecting the
pain in his head-he complained at times, and at times he went to his work
- he did not go to his work continuously, as at other times he was
irregular, and one week he stayed away entirely; that was the middle
week-he complained of his sight failing; he said his sight was going away
-he often complained of being giddy, and of a weight in his head
requested him to go to a surgeon-on Sunday, 19th March, I went to the
station, in Tower-street, about 10 o'clock in the morning I found my
husband there; he did not know me, he was quite unconscious—from there
he was taken to the workhouse; they would not allow him to go to the
hospital-be never spoke to me again-he expired the next day. kog
