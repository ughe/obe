 1467. JOHN HOLBROOK was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and DOANE conducted the Prosecution, JOSEPH IZOD. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Blackman-street, in the Borough. On the 28th of March the prisoner came to my shop for half a pound of bacon—it came to 4 d .—he gave me a half-crown, which was bad—I told him so, I kept it, and gave it to the officer. THOMAS READY. On the 29th of March, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my shop and bought an article, which came to 6 d .—he gave me a half-crown—I suspected it was bad, and kept it by itself—I gave him two shillings change, and he left—the policeman came soon afterwards—I marked the half-crown, and gave it him.
   Prisoner.  I called for half a pound of boiled beef. Witness.  Yes, and I had none—then you had roast beef—you were in the shop about five minutes—another man came in while you were there, who I considered was one of your quality  —he tried to attract my attention—I could swear to you. JOHN BLAZER. I am an officer. I went to Mr. Izod's on the 30th of March—he gave me this half-crown, which I produce. GEORGB HAVILL( police-sergeant M  2.) Mr. Ready gave me this half-crown. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These half-crowns are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Six Months.
 1468. LAWRENCE LEE was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of March , 1 ass, price 1 l  ., the property of John Bradfield ; and 1 ass, price 1 l  .; the property of Samuel Smith . JOHN BRADFIELD. I live at Wimbledon. On the morning of the 23rd of March, my boy turned my donkey out, it was afterwards missing, and is worth 1 l . 1 s .—Samuel Smith, my son-in-law, also lost his donkey. I saw them both at Deptford.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  When had you seen it safe? A.  On the night before—I saw it again on the Tuesday week following. HENRY BRADFIELD. I am the prosecutor's son. I turned out a male donkey on Wimbledon-common, on the Saturday morning—it was missed that day—I saw it afterwards at the Fountain, at Deptford. THOMAS HORRICK. I am a chair-bottomer. On Saturday evening, the 30th of March, Christopher Jones came, and asked me to buy a donkey—he wanted 28 s . for it—I said it was too much—he said he had another in the yard—I said I would look at that—he brought in another, and the prisoner came in with him—I asked the price of that—the prisoner said, 26 s .—I said it was too much, and I offered two sovereigns for the two—they would not take it—I offered two guineas—they would not take it—they went away, and in ten minutes came back, and Jones said, if I would stand  beer-money I should have them—I said, "No"—they said I should have them—I counted the money on the table—Jones took it up, and he said to the prisoner, "You know what I have to give you for yours."
