JOHN GUNN( policeman G  224.) I went with the prosecutor to Gobby's—he came home with his van—the prosecutor asked him if he had any of his sacks on his premises—he hesitated, and said he did not know, or something to that effect—the gates of the stable were wide open, and we walked in—Gobby took two sacks off he place, and said, "I believe I have a sack or two of yours here"—the first he took, Mr. Powell said, "This is not mine"—he took down two more, and Mr. Powell said, "These are mine; how did you come by them?"—he said, "I do not know, they have been here some time"—Mr. Powell said, "Have you got any up stairs?"—he said, "Up stairs I have got no place for sacks"—Mr. Powell said, "I am informed you have; will you allow, me to look?"—he said, "I have no place up stairs"—I then said, "I am a constable; if you have no sacks, you can have no objection to our going up"—he said, "I have only got my room up stairs," and he looked round the stable, and said, "Here is all the place I have got for food for my horse"—at last he said, "We will all go up stairs"—there is a door from the stable to the bottom of the stair-case, and as soon as that was opened, on the first step, I tumbled against a sack—I showed it to Mr. Powell—he said, "This is one of my sacks; will you allow me to look at the corn?"—Gobby said, "Yes"—Mr. Powell put his hand in, and said, "This is very much like my corn"—Gobby said, "I know it is, I bought it of your George"—we went up stairs, and in the bedroom found two sacks—one con tained whole beans, and one split beans—Mr. Powell put his hand in the latter, and said, "These are my beans"—Gobby said, "Yes, I know they are; I bought them of your George the same time that 1 bought the oats"—Mr. Powell said, "How could you rob me in that manner?"—he said, "George told me there was an over plus of the vessel, and I paid him a sovereign, and he spent a shilling"—he talked about his looking over it, but I will not sat what—my attention was directed to the beans.
