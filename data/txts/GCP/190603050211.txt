Emma STARLING, recalled. I remember my daughter asking
me for a piece of brown paper and string, which I gave her. I
do not know what she did with them. The parcel she took
away was the same as she brought.
Mr. Curtis-Bennett submitted that there was no evidence that any
crime had been committed within the jurisdiction of the Court. The
evidence of Miss Rust was that at 4 o'clock on the evening of Janu-
ary 29th the child was certainly alive. If there had been evidence that
death occurred between Willesden and Cambridge, the case would un
doubtedly have been within the jurisdiction, but the death might have
occurred anywhere between Willesden and Glemsford. The evidence
of Mrs. Starling was that prisoner left her house with a brown paper
parcel at noon of January 30th, and that parcel was afterwarde found
to contain this dead body, but it was not to be presumed that because
the body was found in London death occurred within the jurisdiction.
Mt. Justice Grantham. Where do you suggest the indietment should
be laid? If the charge had been laid against her in Suffolk, what would
have been the position there?
Mr. Curtis-Bennett. As the law stands at present the position would
be precisely the same until the prosecution can show something.
Mr. Justice Grantham. According to that, she could not have been
convicted anywhere.
Mr. Curtis-Bennett. Until the prosecution can prove what they alloge
she cannot be convicted, though it seems rather a reductio ad absurdum.
It is for the prosecution to bring evidence that it is within the jurisdie-
tion of the Court. The presumption is that the onse ought to bave
been tried in Suffolk, because there we have the body for the first time
found inside a parcel.
Mr. Justice Grantham. This is the only jurisdiction in which the
child was alive and found dead. If you had been for the prisoner in
Suffolk you would have raised the same point.
Mr. Curtis Bennett. I might have considered the same point. Be
cause the point seems to be absurd it is not necessarily bad. I have
searched very carefully, and I cannot find that this particular point has
ever been raised, and it may be there is a flaw in our law. The prose-
cution should not be allowed to trust to luck as to whether they could
prove that the crime alleged was within their jurisdiction. Supposing :
man to be charged with murder within the jurisdiction of this Court, it
might be his defence that he was in Kent the whole day, but it would
not be upon him to show that he was in Kent until something definite
was laid against him.
Mr. Justice Grantham. The fact being that the body was found in the
custody of the prisoner in this jurisdiction with no explanation of it,
the inference fairly to be drawn is that the alleged murder was com-
mitted in this jurisdiction, there being so proof where it was committed.
