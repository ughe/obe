   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was he to have received from your firm, at any time, a sum of 200 dollars? A.  Not to my knowledge—the first I heard of that was when it was mentioned by a person a few days ago—the prisoner once informed me that Dr. Rungay had promised him a gratuity, but I do not recollect that be mentioned any sum—I do not think he mentioned the word "dollars"—he might have done so—it was not a remarkable circumstance to me—I had no interest in the matter. NOT GUILTY.
 778. FREDERICK SEILER was again  indicted for stealing, on the   8th of January , 11 s  ., the monies of Henry Blundell and others, his masters. WILLIAM ELLIS. I am in the employ of Messrs. Blundell. I sold a packet of a dozen pounds of wax candles to Mrs. Gibson—I do not know the day—I received 11 s . for them, and gave the money to the prisoner as he was standing against the office—they were all shillings. MARTHA GIBSON. I purchased these wax candles on the 8th of January, at the factory of Messrs. Blundells—I paid William Ellis 11 s . for them. GEORGE DIXON LONGSTAFF. The prisoner did not account to me for this 11 s . received on the 8th of January from Mrs. Gibson, and here is no entry of it in the book—the Saturday after the 8th was on the 11th of January, I received 11 s . of him that day for candles which he said were sold that day to Mr. Ripley, and I asked if he had any other money for candles that week—he said, "No"—I never said any thing to him about this sum of money as I had no suspicion of it—he was apprehended on the Thursday afterwards. NOT GUILTY.
