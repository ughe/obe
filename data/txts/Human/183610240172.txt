3472. JAMES BRITTEN and ELIZABETH BRITTEN were in dicted for stealing, on the   4th of October , 2 sheets, value 3 s  .; 2 blankets, value 5 s  .; 1 saucepan, value 6 d  .; 2 pillow-cases, value 6 d  .; and 1 pail, value 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Edwards . THOMAS EDWARDS. I live in East-square—I let lodgings. The prisoners came together, and took my first-floor furnished, at 4 s . per week—they lived there as man and wife—they gave their name as Baxter—they remained three weeks, and paid 7 s .—they left 1 s . back, and then stopped another week, and went away on the Tuesday as the rent was due on the Saturday, without giving notice—my wife went up and missed the articles out of the room.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you not making a mistake? A.  I have spoken nothing but the truth—they both came together to take the lodging—I never said that the female prisoner came and took the lodge ings, but she came and looked at them—I did not tell Sir Peter Laurie that the female came and took them—this is my writing ( looking at her deposition )—it was read to me before I signed it, but I am rather deaf—I can not hear every word—I told Sir Peter Laurie that both the prisoners came on Saturday, but it was late on the Friday. HENRY MOORE. I am a pawnbroker, in Great Suffolk-street. I have some articles which were pawned by the female prisoner. ANN EDWARDS. I went up stairs into the room with my husband, and missed the things—I met the female prisoner just coming out of Pavior's-alley and took her to the station-house. JAMES ATTFIELD. I am a policeman. I was at the station-house when the female prisoner was brought there—I searched and found four pawnbroker's duplicates on her—I took the man the next morning—he denied all knowledge of it.
   Elizabeth Britten.  I certainly did pledge the articles, not with the intent of keeping them, but of getting them out.
 E.  BRITTEN— GUILTY. Aged 36.— Confined Three Months.
 J.  BRITTEN— NOT GUILTY.
 3473. MICHAEL TOY was indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of September , 24lbs. weight of lead, value 10 s  ., the goods of George Webb and others, his masters.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES BUTLER. I am foreman to Mr. George Webb and others. On the 22nd of September, at about seven o'clock, I was in the Licensed Victuallers' School, at Kennington—there was work going on by my masters—the prisoner was in their employ, and was on the premises—I saw him out, and followed him some distances—I found a portion of lead hammered up, under his waistcoat, slung over his shoulders with a hand kerchief—I took it from him, and informed my master—I kept the lead, and let him go—he had gone off the premises he nearly half a mile—he did not come to work the next day, and was apprehended on the 11th of October.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did you not tell him to go away about his business? A.  No, I did not know how to act—I took him back because I had a suspicion—William Toakley was with me at the time—I insisted upon his coming back—I did not take hold of him he walked by my side—we have never had any quarrel.
