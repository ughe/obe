MARY BOYD. I was living at 11, Nassau Street, in July, while the

WILLIAM LOVELL. I live at 65, Bolsover Street-the prisoner lodged
there from September to January-I saw the little girl on several occasions
-the prisoner used to come home between 12 and 1 o'clock at night-I
told her if she ill-used the children so she would kill them, and if she did
I should be one against her--she turned them out at 12.30 at night on the
cold stones, naked, night after night, when she came home.
SOPHIA ORIBAR. I am the wife of William Oribar-I lived at 8,
Gloucester Street, Queen Square, about Whitsuntide, 1876-I have heard
the children screaming several times——one night at 11.30 I went up and
asked the prisoner to be quiet-she said if I did not go downstairs she
would wring my neck-I went down.
GUILTY- Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
* 415. GEORGE BALDWIN (45), Stealing, whilst employed in the Post-
office, a post-letter, the property of the Postmaster-General.
MR. METCALFE, Q.C., and MR. SLADs conducted the Prosecution ; and
MR. WILDEY WRIGHT the Defence.
CHARLES JAMES STEVENS. I am a travelling clerk in the missing letter
department of the General Post-office-in April last I was directed to
make an investigation as to the loss of certain letters--on the 25th April I
made up a test letter in which I enclosed 206 penny postage stamps-I
addressed it to Mrs. E. Thompson, 126, Pentonville Road, London-I
posted it at the northern district office, at 7.15, in the evening-I drew
the attention of Mr. Carman, the inspector, to it-at 9.30 I went to 126,
Pentonville Road and enquired of Mrs. Thompson, who answered the door,
and she brought me another letter which she had received-I afterwards
saw the prisoner in uniform going on with his delivery, and after that I
saw him go into a public-house in Henrietta Street, Brunswick Square,
where he remained about fifteen minutes-he then went into No. 19, and
in about a quarter of an hour came out in private clothes and went again
into the public-house-I went into another compartment of the bar and
saw him with another man--he then came out and walked down the street
and pareyed with another man in Compton Street for about twenty minutes
--he then re-crossed the street and went towards Cockburn Street, where
I stopped him and said I wished to ask him about a letter which I had
posted that evening to Mrs. Thompson-he said "I delivered a letter to
Mrs. Thompson to-night "-I said "I know you did, but not the one I
posted "-he said "I know nothing about it, I want to go "-I said “You
oannot go "- Rumbold, the officer, then came up, and Mr. Carman--I said
“Do you know this gentleman ?"-he looked at him and said "No"-I
repeated the question and he again replied “No”-I said “Look again"-
he said "Oh yes, I know who it is now; it is Mr. Carman"-we all then
went to a top front room of No. 19, where I again asked about the letter
