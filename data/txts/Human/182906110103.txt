 1239.  MARY HEWITT was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of May , 2 boxes, value 6s.; 3 pin-cushions, value 2s. 6d., and 1 memorandum-book, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Mary Goetze.
   MARY GOETZE. I am single, and have a counter at  the Royal Bazaar. I lost the articles stated, from there about half-past three o'clock, on the 29th of May, when the fire broke out; when my other articles were brought home, I missed these.
   MARY ANN WATKINS. When the fire broke out, I went to assist in saving some of the property - I took some to my father's house, and left them there in the care of the prisoner, who lodged there; my father's is No. 52, Castle-street, East-street, Oxford-market; when my father came home, she gave up all but these into his possession - these articles were found at the pawnbroker's.
   THOMAS DANDERS PERRY. I am a pawnbroker. These articles were brought by the prisoner the day after the fire, about eleven o'clock in the morning - I had some suspicion, and stopped her till I sent for an officer.
   GEORGE AVIS. I am an officer. I took up the prsoner; she said she had them put into her possession for a little while, and she had taken them to make a little money of, through distress.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. Confined One Month.
 1240.  MICHAEL CONNOLY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of April , 1 coat, value 20s., the goods of Lake Brunton.
   LUKE BRUNTON. I lost a great coat on Easter Monday; I went out with a friend, and did not miss it till the Tuesday morning, when I went to put my clothes in the box - I had left it on my bed; the prisoner had been a lodger - I went in quest of him.
 Prisoner. He took me out to drink with him, and we drank till one o'clock in the day. Witness. Yes, a friend took us out, and treated us both.
 WILLIAM SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. On the 20th of April, about one o'clock, this coat was pawned with me by the prisoner; I asked if it was his own; he said Yes, that his name was John Mooney, and he lived in Cow-cross- I asked him how he came to Islington to pawn it; he said he worked in the Pied Bull fields.
 WILLIAM CLARK. I took the prisoner. He was in liquor - he said he had pawned the coat.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined Seven Days.
 1241.  SARAH REYNOLDS was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of April , 12 dozen of buttons, value 2s. 6d., the goods of John Wyld.
   JOHN WYLD. I am a haberdasher, and live in Rathbone-place. The prisoner came to my shop on the 15th of April, to purchase some buttons - I showed her a drawer - there were four cards on the top - she bought four dozen, and gave me half a sovereign to change - I put away the drawer, and missed one card of buttons -I then said "You must come back, you have taken a gross of buttons" - I took her back - she denied it - I sent my boy for an officer - she then gave me these buttons and said she was very sorry.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 45.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1242.  WILLIAM PENDRICK was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of November , 1 male ass, price 10s., the property of John Herridge.
 JOHN HERRIDGE. had a male ass which I turned out in Mr. William Hammond's meadow - it was taken from there the latter end of November, or the beginning of December - I never saw it again till May, when I saw it tied to some palings at the prisoner's place, in Fulhamfields - I said it was mine, and asked him for it - he said it was not mine; I went and got a constable - I allowed the prisoner to keep it that Saturday, and then he was to give it up.
 WILLIAM HAMMOND. The donkey was in my field - I cannot say when it was missed; the prisoner lived in our neighbourhood.
 CHARLOTTE HAMMOND, I know the ass by its being into our meadow, and by its colour.
   WILLIAM GUMM. I saw the ass at Hamersmith, in November last, with its ears fresh cut, in the possession of John Harris, between seven and nine o'clock in the evening - it was offered for sale at 25s.
 JOHN HERRIDGE. Its ears were cut after it was stolen from me - the prisoner lives at Hillingdon - he said he bought it.
     NOT GUILTY.
