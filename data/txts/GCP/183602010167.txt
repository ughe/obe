Thomas Buxton, a warehouseman and agent, in Wood-street-I have been
with him two years and four months I know the prisoner, he lives in
Monmouth-street, I think at No. 76—it is a tailor's trimming shop-as I
was going up to Westminster with a load, about four months back, the
string of my boot broke-I went and delivered my goods and came back,
and bought a lace at the prisoner's shop-I saw Mrs. Leach-I had some
conversation with her-in consequence of that, I went afterwards to the
prisoner's house with goods--it was about four months ago - I took with
me a gross of reels--I saw Mr. and Mrs. Leach-I showed them to them
--they, asked what they were- I told them 98.-Mr. Leach said he coula
not think of that, but he offered me 38., which he gave me for the
gross-nothing passed about who I was, or where I came from-on the
22nd of January, I went there, and took with me ten gross of reel cottons
- I gave an invoice with them--Mr. Leach was there, sitting by the fire-
he opened a paper of cottons, there was the name of Geary on the
papers-he asked me to make out the invoice at the price of 58. 98. or
68. a gross-I made it out at 6s., which amounted to 31.-he gave me
11. 108.-I made out the invoiee in the name of Geary, as he asked me
I gave him the bill of parcels, and received the money-I know the price
