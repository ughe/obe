   Cross-examined. Q.  How often do you copy the account in this book! A.  About once a week—some weeks twice—it depended on whether Lord was there or not—when Lord was not there, I did it—this book was intended as a check for the whole—I copied the duplicate and counterfoil of 22nd Aug.—I have a distinct recollection of handing the sheet over to the prisoner—I never sent any, I always handed them to the prisoner—I know I handed it to him that day—he was on the platform—he lives in Tooley-street—he came down by the quarter past 9 o'clock train in the morning—it was in the evening I handed him this account.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  What you entered was from the small books into this? A.  Yes, and it has the prisoner's signature. DONALD MUNRO. I am a clerk in the employ of the Brighton Railway Company. This account ( Looking at it ) is my writing—it is for 2 l . 5 s . 6 d ., it is the account of Friday, 22nd Aug.—I wrote it out by the wish of Mr. Gower—he came into the office one morning with his right hand tied up—he said he had got his thumb smashed, and would we be kind enough to write a few lines for him—another clerk and I were in the office—I said, "Certainly," and I wrote this from a slip of paper which he produced—he took the slip of paper away with him—I copied this from the paper and from his dictation, both—he held the piece of paper in his hand and read it out to me—the paper was about the same size as one of these duplicates—I did not observe whether there was any printing on the paper he read it from—the account I wrote was written on a regular form, an excess form—the prisoner gave me that form to write it on—in the account I made out I do not see anything like 4 s . for tickets for Reigate—what I made out was 2 l . 5 s . 6 d .—his right hand was tied up with a handkerchief round it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner produced what you call an excess form, and you wrote on it? A.  Yes—he handed it to me—I did not see the paper from which I copied it—he held it in his hand—I might have looked at it—I cannot tell where the excess papers are deposited—I believed his thumb was injured. CHRISTOPHER EDWARD COCKERTON. I am station clerk at the London Bridge terminus. I should say the prisoner has been in the employ of the company from ten to twelve years—he has been head ticket collector for four or five years past—of late the up trains stop at Forest Hill, and the tickets are taken there—the prisoner had the superintendence of that station—it was his duty, at the close of the day, to fill up a form, and half was for warded to the London station—that ought to contain all the money received—this counterfoil of 18th Aug. did not come to me—this duplicate came to me as the duplicate of the excess book of 18th Aug.—the duplicates have come to me from Forest Hill by casual persons, in an envelope, generally—the prisoner did not bring them at all himself, they came by the train—by the paper that came on 18th Aug., the amount represented as received is 3 l . 0 s . 8 d .—in the counterfoil of that date, under the head of Margate, here appears 2 l . 17 s . 6 d . and 2 l . 7 s .—the account of them has not been rendered to me—in the counterfoil of 22nd Aug., the amount is 3 l . 4 s . 6 d ., and what was sent up to me was 2 l . 5 s . 6 d .—it reached me in the same way in course of business.
