With regard to the turkeys, the first he bought was at Christmas, 1902, 13 turkeys. I have sold turkeys at Christmas time for many years. Calcutt came in one or two days before Christmas Eve, saw the turkeys in the saloon, and asked me if I could let him have a few. I had no idea what he was going to do with them. They cost me about 11d. a pound, and I think I charged him 11 1/2 d. He never told me that he was buying the turkeys for bribes. In 1903 I sold him 12 turkeys at 11 1/2 d. a pound, and one weighing 25 pounds at 1s. 4d. a pound. At Christmas, 1904, I sold him three large turkeys, and at Christmas, 1906, I think, two large turkeys for three guineas. With regard to what has been said about his buying one from me, and send  ing it in my name to Knight, I was sending some turkeys out one night when Calcutt was there; Mr. Knight had been doing one or two little things for me, so I thought I would send him one, and Calcutt said, "I am going that way; I will deliver them for you if you like," and he took them. It went with my compliments to Knight, and it was my present. With reference to the journey to Woolwich in the governess car, I was leaving my Mile End house one afternoon to go to Woolwich and I met Calcutt in the lobby. He said, "I am just going to Woolwich; I have got my horse and trap outside; I will drive you there if you like." I said, "I shall be pleased for you to drive me there." He drove me there and back. On the way back he said, in the Blackwall Tunnel, "This is a nice lot, Mr. Hirst." Nothing more was said about it until we got near the Mile End Road, when he said, "I want to sell this lot." I said, "Do you?" He said, "Yes." When we got to Mile End he said, "Hirst, do you know anybody who would like to buy this?" I said, "No, Calcutt, I do not." Then he said, "Would not you like to buy it?" I said, "No, I have three or four traps of my own, and several horses and a pony, and I do not want any more." Then he said, "Did I know anybody who would buy it?" I said, "No, I do not think so. What did you buy it for?" He said, "I did not buy it; I had to take it for a debt." I told him if he liked to send it down to the "Eagle Hotel" I would see if I could get Mr. Hansell to find him a customer. Hansell is a tenant of mine at the "Eagle Hotel," Snaresbrook. I asked what he wanted for it, and he said £50; then he sent it down to Snaresbrook. After it had been there about a week, Hansell said someone had offered him £45 for it. I told Calcutt and he said he should not take less than £50. Then he tried to persuade me to buy it. He said, "That big horse that you had down at the "Eagle" is too big for you. It would suit me very well. Would you make a deal? You take my pony and trap and have a deal for the horse." I gave him a cheque for £38 and sold him the big horse for £12. I thought that was more than a fair price for the car. Calcutt's account of the transaction, as given in evidence here, is inaccurate.
