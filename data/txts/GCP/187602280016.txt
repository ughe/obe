MR. WILLIAMS Submitted that there was not sufficient evidence to go to the Jury
of conspiracy on the part of Cohen, whose position was evidently that of a ser
vant to Phillips, and who only acted under his master's directions. MR. SER-
JBANT BALLANTINE also urged that the counts for conspiracy could not be sup-
ported; and as to the false pretences, the prosecutor had never stated specifically
what induced him to part with the china, and therefore the counts charging
specific false pretences were not made out. THE COMMON SERJEANT considered
that as to the conspiracy, certain acts having been proved as to Cohen, it was
for the Jury to draw their own inferences from those facts; amd as to the false
pretences, it would be for the Jury to say whether the pretences made were false
in point of fact, and whether the goods were parted with on the faith of those
pretences.
