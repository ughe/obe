WILLIAM PARSLOW. I am the prosecutor's servant. On 16th Oct. I had
three horses in the stable, one was a bay mare-there was a set of harnes
there, and in the coach house there was a chaise-I locked the stable door
and placed the key in it's usual place on a ledge on the following mornine
at a little before 6 o'clock, I went, and missed the mare, chaise, and
harness--I have seen them since they are the same about a foot of the
mare's tail was then cut off-the staple of the stable had been drawn, and
all three gates were open.
CHARLES BENN. I live at a farm on Hounslow-heath. My man told me
of the mare being missed-- I gave instructions to the police, and afterwarde
saw ber at Reading, with about a foot cut off her tail ; she appeared to have
been hard driven, ber shoulder was wrung, and the hair was off her legs
she is worth 301., the gig 101., and the harness 31.--they are all mine
John SCARCE. I am a blacksmith, and live at the Greyhound, nes
Reading. On Friday morning, 17th Oct., about 10 minutes to 8 o'clock, the
prisoners came to my yard gate with a horse and gig-Riley asked me if I
had room for them to put the horse in-I said that I had no stable room
but there was a large shed, which they could put it in if they chose-they
did so, and said that they were not going to stop long-Hawkes drove it
into the yard-they afterwards said that they wanted to sell the mare and
gig for 171.— I told them I did not want it, but if I could get a customer
would-they offered me a sovereign for my trouble- I looked at the horse,
and thought it was not half the value, but it was sweating all over, and was
all over mud, and the chaise also- I gave information to the police, and gave
them the horse and chaise and the prisoners.
JOHN TOWNSEND. I am inspector of the Reading police. On 17th Oct
I received information, went to the Oxford Arms, Silver-street, Reading
which is about forty miles from Hounslow-heath, and found the prisoners in
the back yard- I asked them if the horse and chaise at the public house
above belonged to them--Hawkes said, “Yes I said that I should take
them into custody on suspicion of stealing them-Hawkes said, "It dos
not belong to me, it belongs to the other," pointing to Riley--I asked
Hawkes where he brought thein from-he said, "From Oxford" - I went to
the Hare and Hounds, which is kept by the last witness, and took postes
sion of the horse and gig; the horse appeared to have been driven very
hard—the prosecutor came and saw them the next day-the mare's tail had
been cut off - this is it produced), I found it at a public house about three
miles from Reading, on the Hounslow-road-I have matched it, and it
exactly corresponds.
Riley's Defence. On Thursday morning I came to London, without s
halfpenny in my pocket; I met this man, who asked me if I would have
half a pint of beer, which he gave me, and asked me to go on the road to
Hounslow with him, and he would pay my lodging; we had a good deal of
gin and water and beer on the road, and got intoxicated; I left him with
some horse dealers, bargaining about a horse, and travelled on: in abouts
quarter of an hour I met him with a horse and chaise; he told me that he
was going to Reading, his master had sent him, and he would give me
job; I jumped in, and he drove towards Reading-I know nothing about
the horse or gig.
