 Prisoner's Defence. I served two or three customers, and gave him the money.

 1199.  GEORGE LANE was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 28 lbs. of wool, value 28 s., the goods of John Claxon.
 MR. WALFORD conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE WAGER. I am servant to John Claxon, a farmer, of Clapper's-green, Edmonton. We were shearing from May till the end of June; the wool was put into a loft by the side of a barn. I missed some several times; one Wood had been employed by the bailiff; he lived with one Pinto - the prisoner lodged in the same house. I missed wool in July, and put some on a hurdle on Wednesday morning, the 16th, between ten and eleven o'clock, and missed it on the 17th about six in the morning - there was a fleece among it with particular marks on it - that was gone; and in consequence of information I went to Chaplin's house; he shewed me some wool, which was part of what I had laid on the hurdle; the loft was locked up; and I found somebody had pushed the board-up to get to it.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. The board required to be moved to get to it - Q. Yes; it was pushed in. The prisoner was not in my master's employ - but I knew him. Wood was taken, but has escaped.
   HENRY CHAPLIN. I live at Cheshunt, about seven miles from the prosecutor's, and am a dealer in wool. On the 17th of July last, about six o'clock in the morning, I bought about 141 lbs. of wool from Wood, for 5 l. 13 s. - the prisoner was with him - they brought it on a horse and cart with the name of Bushby on it. I paid Wood  the money.
 Cross-examined. Q. All you know is that the prisoner was with the person who sold it you - A. Yes; I never paid the prisoner for any - he helped to unload it - Wood made the bargain. Wood had often brought wool before; Lane only came twice with him, and appeared to be his servant.
   JOSEPH GIBSON. I am a constable of Edmonton. I apprehended Lane, on the Monday, at Pinto's, where Wood lodged - I found about a pound of wool, in a bag in the closet, in Lane's room, and found a bed tick doubled up, under the bed, and some little pieces of wool inside it, as if it had contained wool. Lane's wife and children lived in the same room. Wood is quite a little boy, about fourteen years old, and very little for his age.
   WILLIAM WAGER. I am bailiff to John Claxton. On the morning of the 17th, I saw Wood and Lane together, in a small pony cart, a little after ten o'clock in the morning, coming towards Edmonton, with empty sacks, and the bed tick in the cart.
   HENRY CHAPLIN. I do not know the bed tick - part of it was in a tick.
 COURT. Q. Why, Wood is only a boy - A. He appeared to me about eighteen years old, and a full grown man.
   GEORGE WAGER. Wood was quite a lad - nobody could take him for a man; he might appear sixteen or eighteen years old. I know the fleece by the tie up, and ochre. Wood had left us at the time it was stolen; he had left us for four months; but came backwards and forwards at times.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.

 1200.  GEORGE KENNING was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 21st of August , 50,000 wooden staves, value 100 l., the goods of James Wilkins , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 The prosecutor having delivered a regular invoice for the goods in question, the Court ruled that they could not be feloniously stolen.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1201.  JAMES STEWART was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , a handkerchief, value 2 s., the goods of Henry Hiatt , from his person.
   HENRY HIATT. On the 17th of August, about seven o'clock in the evening, I was in Old-street Road with my wife; two boys went before me, I felt a kick at my heels, and a tug at my pocket; I turned round and saw the prisoner drop my handkerchief; I saw it fall out of his hand.
 Q. You told the Magistrate you saw it laying on the ground - A. I saw it fall from his hand, nobody else was near me: he denied taking it.
   THOMAS EAGLES. I am a constable of Shoreditch. Hiatt brought the prisoner to me with the handkerchief, and said the boy took it from his pocket, he said nothing to it.
 Prisoner's Defence. Two boys who were behind the gentleman went from him. I went forward; he turned round, caught hold of me, and accused me of this - he declared to the people, at a barber's shop, that he could not say I did it.
   HENRY HIATT, I did not say so.
     NOT GUILTY.
