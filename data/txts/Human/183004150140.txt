 998.  SHADRACH MITCHELL and JOSEPH WILLIAMS were indicted for feloniously assaulting William Stevens , on the King's highway, on the 29th of March , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 handkerchief, value 9d., and 7 shillings, his property.
   WILLIAM STEVENS. I am a farmer's man, and live at Long-ford. On the 29th of March I came to Hounslow, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon, with a friend; we went to the White Bear, and had three pints of beer between us and another man, who lived in the yard - we then went to the Prince Regent, staid there about half an hour, and two of us drank three pints of beer - I walked about the town, and then went to the Cricketers, on the heath, alone, and had a pint of ale; I got there about halfpast six o'clock, and staid about an hour - the prisoners came in while I was there; I knew them before, and we had a pot of beer together - I got up to come home; they followed me out - Williams caught hold of my arm, and said, "Let us go up to the George the Fourth, and have a pot;" I refused - Mitchell caught hold of my arm, and told me to come along; I refused - they pulled me about one hundred yards: Mitchell then threw me down, and held me, and then they took my handkerchief off my neck - they took nothing else; they ran away, and I after them- I met Dinam, and went with him for Bennett, an officer, who took them in the town, and my handkerchief was found in Williams' pocket - I cannot say which of them took it; I felt Williams take 7s. out of my left-hand pocket - that was not found; I had no purse - I had taken all my money out at the Cricketers, to pay for the beer; they sat at the same table, and could see it - I was not at all intoxicated.
 Prisoner Williams. Q. When I came in did you not challenge me to toss for a pot? A. Yes, and I did; I said I had only a few halfpence in my pocket - I did that for a joke.
   CHARLES BENNETT. I am a constable. Stevens came to my house soon after eight o'clock, and complained of having been robbed of 7s. and his handkerchief; I went in search of the prisoners - he told me who they were, but did not go with me; I found Williams against the cage, and apprehended him - I found this handkerchief in his breast pocket, under his smock-frock; he told me Mitchell was one that was with him - I went in search of him; a boy came and told me he was talking to Williams in the cage - I went and found him there, but found nothing on him; Mitchell said the handkerchief was his own - I never heard the prosecutor say it was not his; he was a little elevated, but not drunk.
 Prisoner Mitchell. Q. When I was taken, did he not say he could not swear to me? A. I do not recollect it - he described your dress, and said he could swear to your dress.
   WILLIAM STEVENS. This is the handkerchief I had round my neck; I know it by these holes in the middle -I have had it half a year.
 JURY. Q. How came you to describe them by their dress? A. I said I could not swear to their names, but their dress; I have known them both nine or ten years, but had not seen them lately.
 Mitchell's Defence. I did not lay him down, or take the handkerchief; I followed Williams and him out of the Cricketers, and saw them all going towards the Wellington.
 Williams' Defence. I did not lay him down, nor put my hand into his pocket.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Justice Pask.
