   HANNAH JORDAN. I live at No. 6, Grafton-court, North-street, Manchester-square. Dennis Newberry lodges with me; if the postman brought any letters to my house I always received them while Newberry has been with me - in October last, I received no money-letter for Newberry, nor any letter at all for him - I received no money-letter in October; I know the prisoner; he is the postman who I received letters from when they came - he never delivered me a money-letter in October - he brought a letter for Newberry - I cannot say whether it was in October - I gave Newberry the letter I received.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know Grafton-street, in your neighbourhood? A. No; I never got but one letter from this woman since she has been at Bury - I never received a letter addressed to Grafton-street - nobody lodged in my room but Newberry - I do not know how many lodge in the house; there are several families lodge in the house - I only occupy one room, which is the parlour on the ground-floor - I do not know about the back room - the two rooms at the top are occupied - I received every letter for Mr. Newberry that ever came to the house, while he was with me - I am not much out of the house, because I have a little family - my husband sells things in the street; I do not go out with him - I have not been out a single day selling fruit within the last three months - I am not the owner of the house; I rent one room, that is all - I never received more than one letter for Mr. Newberry since his wife went into the country.
   RICHARD HUNTLEY. I am a letter-carrier. I recollect a short time ago seeing a receipt for a money-letter directed to Grafton-street, North, Manchester-square; I deliver in Grafton-street, Fitzroy-square; it therefore occurred to me that the letter did not belong to my division - I had it in my possession at my seat which is in the same division as the prisoner belongs to - I either gave the receipt to the prisoner, or put it before him; if it had been an unpaid letter I should have given it to the charge taker; but a paid letter or a frank, I should give to the prisoner or put it before him; that would be the course of my duty - when I read the direction, it struck me forcibly at the time it must be intended for Grafton-street, North-street, Manchester-square, and I shewed the receipt to the prisoner; I remember that, and I asked him if that would do for him; he said,"Yes" - I was aware that Grafton-court was in his delivery, I had been on that walk myself some years ago - I remarked to him that that laid wide from my Grafton-street - the prisoner's duty would be to take it into the inner office, and by shewing it to the clerk he would get me letter and sign a book for it; he would take the receipt with the letter to the party it was intended for, and get them to sign it, and he is expected to keep that receipt as his acknowledgement; I have some hundreds of these receipts by me - if a carrier was asked a question about the delivery of a money-letter, he would have the receipt to produce; I have had that to do myself; it has happened to me - the prisoner lives by Chadwells, Gray's-inn-road.
 Cross-examined. Q. The letter you saw, and of which you have been speaking, was addressed to Grafton-street, North, Manchester-square? A. I saw no letter; that was the address on the receipt, "Grafton-street, North;" I am pretty sure it was not North-street - I deliver at two Grafton-streets; my delivery is not near Grafton-court; it is the nearest Grafton-street to Grafton-court - the Grafton-street in my delivery, is the nearest Grafton-street to his delivery; his delivery is not next to mine - there is Grafton-street, Fitzroy-square, and Upper Grafton-street; and Pannel, my assistant, delivers in Grafton-street, East.
 Q. Suppose you had a letter directed to either of the Grafton-streets in your delivery, should you think of taking it to Grafton-court? A. No; nor should I have sent this, but seeing "North," and "Manchester-square," on it - I either put the receipt before the prisoner where he sat, or gave it into his hand - it is usual at times to put them into the delivery when the carriers are not there it is a frequent custom - I thought it was intended for Grafton-court from its having "North," and "Manchester-square" on it.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When persons are absent from their seats, receipts may be left there for them? A. Yes; the prisoner was at his seat - I will not swear he saw me lay it down; but I said to him, "Will this do for you?" he said, "Yes, it will" - I had his walk once; there is no Grafton-street in that walk; Grafton-court is about half a mile perhaps from the Grafton-street in my walk.
   JAMES AUSTIN. I am inspector of letter carriers.
