Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  Were you in the employ of Pear-man and Co., of London and Glasgow, at the time Loup was? A.  Yes; the London establishment was given up about last March, and I quitted one week and Loup another—Loup then set up for himself, and I went into his employ—he had the conduct of the whole of Pearman's business in London—he was there about fourteen months—I never heard anything discreditable or dishonest of him—he told me where the tools were, and said if I knew anybody likely to buy them I might take him to see them.
   COURT. Q.  Do you know a person named Cusell? A.  I do not know him, I have heard speak of him—I saw him in Loup's counting-house on the subject of the nails—I do not know what passed; I left the counting-house.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Were there two offices at Loup's? A.  No; I occupied the same office as Mr. Loup—his books were generally in the desk—I have not seen them about—I never took the liberty of reading them—I have no notion what was un them—I saw a gentleman who said he was a native of Spain—Buck went with me to see the tools.
   MR. HUDDLESTON. Q.  You were not clerk to Loup? A.  No, porter, GEORGE BUCK. I am a tool-maker, in the Waterloo-road—in Aug. last Proctor came to me, said he was in Loup's employ, and gave me a list of tools—I have not got it—I went to Johnson and Day's to see them—I did not buy any of them—I did not see Loup, or the Spanish gentleman—I heard of him.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  I believe you are a brother of Mr. Buck, of Newgate-street? A.  I am his son. FREDERICK JOSEPH RUTLEDGE. I am a glove importer, in Addle-street, Wood-street. On 6th Aug. Holliday came to my place alone, and pur chased gloves for shipping, which came to 32 l . 4 s .—I delivered them; and soon, afterwards, in consequence of something I heard, I had a commu nication with Mr. Barnet, the buyer for Leaf's house—I went to Pancrass-lane, and saw Gardiner in the outer-room, and Seager and Holliday in the other—I said I called for the money for the goods; they said they could not give it me just then, if I would call in a day or two they would see what they could do—I called several times afterwards—Seager used to be in the outer office—I did not see Gardiner and Holliday have any conversation with him on the subject of their dealings—I found my goods at Mr. Barnet's three days after I had sold them.
   COURT. Q.  Did not you say, "You told me the good were for shipping, and I knew they are at Mr. Barnet's?" A.  No. ALFREAD BARNET. In Aug. last I was buyer to the house of Leaf and Co.—Cusell offered me some gloves for sales—I bought them at twenty-five per cent. below the price; his son, who is a lad, was with him—I made a communication to Mr. Rutledge.
