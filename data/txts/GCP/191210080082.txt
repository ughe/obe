(Thursday, October 17.)
Sub-divisional Inspector WILLIAM SKILTON, H. Division. On July 24
I accompanied the procession to Free Trade Wharf. I have been on
duty in that neighbourhood during the whole of the strike. I saw the
van on fire, and as the engine came up I heard cries of, "Don't let it
go through." I saw several constables struck by pieces of wood and
stone; they were not seriously injured. I heard cries of “Get the
blacklegs out of the wharf.” I gave directions to the police to draw
their truncheons and to disperse the crowd vigorously. I saw Coffey
at the corner of Schoolhouse Lane, opposite the main gates of Free
Trade Wharf; he held up both hands, shouted "Rally again, boys,"
and headed rushes to the gate. I saw him arrested there; he shouted
out, " Are you going to let them take me?” Thirty or forty other men
rushed forward to rescue him; they did not succeed, and I ordered
him to be taken into Free Trade Wharf temporarily; he was taken
through the small wicket gate. I saw all the prisoners taking an active
part in rushing at gates; some of them tried to interfere with the
engine. The streets were cleared at about 3.30.
To Mr. Wild. I do not say all the crowd were strikers. There were
many men who were just as guilty as these men; we just happened to
catch these seven men.
