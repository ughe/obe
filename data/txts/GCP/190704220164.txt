the 14th, I saw Crump, who said there had been a big fight, but

me the whole of the work. He said I was lowest on Newton Chambers,
Sutton Main, and Alfreton best hard seam, but that Charrington,
Sells, Dale and Co. were 2s.4d. a ton below me for Welsh
washed nuts and Franklin and Co. ls. 10d. per ton below for that
same coal. Crump wanted to know when I was likely to be able
to pay them. I said I could pay them £10 on the contract being
senled. Two or three weeks later the contract was sealed and I
made the payments--£10 to Sansom, Tarrant, Watts, Skinner, F.
W. Hill, Bissel, and Crump. Watts and Crump came to my house
together; Skinner and F. W. Hill to my office at 469a, High Road,
Leyton, and Bissel called at my office on the bridge. These pay
ments were in gold. I sent Tarrant's by cheque. I think by post.
I borrowed £50 from my clerk, 8. J. Smith, by cheque on his
wife's account, which I cashed. This I used partly in payment
the sums named. After I got my last quarterly cheque for the 1904-05
contract I made further payments, Lewis Hill £25, Baird £10,
Riches £10, Spinks and Jackson £5. In October, I disbursed £70.
