Cross-examined by the Prisoner. When I was up at the police
court and in the police station to identify you I did not say different
from what I am saying now. (To the Judge.) On the Tuesday night
I was just coming home from work with my friend at the top of Pit
field Street. I saw three men at the corner of Wilkes Street, all of
them strangers to me. At the time of the robbery the man with the
watch ran down Wilkes Street and the prisoner and another man
stood still. The prosecutor went to the station. He did not know
them. I did not speak to either of them. At the station I spoke to
the inspector, and a paper came to my house to meet Detective Brog.
den the next night. I was to call at the station every night. I saw
him again the same night and the next might before I had been to 1
the station. He was standing at the corner by himself. I did not

Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I did not say I saw you every
night after the night of the robbery. I only saw you the following
week.
Detective BROGDEN, H Division. I was not present at the police
station on the night of the robbery, but I afterwards saw the two last
witnesses on the last day. I was given to understand that the pro-
secutor could not identify the men. I arranged with Weil to call each
night at the station and I afterwards patrolled the streets with a view
to finding the man he described. On the night of April 8, in consequence
of what they said I stopped the prisoner in Brick Lane, and told him
I should arrest him with being concerned with two other men not in
eustody with committing a robbery on March 31. He said, "You
have made a great mistake; I know nothing about it," and at the
station he said, “I can prove that I was in bed that night at eight
o'clock and never afterwards went out."
