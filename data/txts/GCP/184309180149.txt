2807. GEORGE TWINE was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of Aug.,
coat value 8., the goods of Alexander Harvey; 3 waistcoats, 10s., the
moods of George Fuller; 1 coat, 78., the goods of George Fuller, the
vounger; and 2 pairs of reins, 108., the goods of William Riches; and
that he had been before convicted of felony.
God
ALEXANDER HARVEY. I am in the service of Mr. William Riches, &
coach-master, at Clapham Common. On the 30th of Aug., I locked the stable,
and left it safe a little before one o'clock--my coat was then on my fellow-
servant's box-the reins were there-this is my coat, and these are my mas-
ter's reins.
GEORGE FULLER. I am in the service of Mr. Riches-I missed two
waistcoats and a jacket out of my box in the stable—these are them.
George FULLER, JUN. I had a frock coat there-I saw it safe a little
before eleven o'clock-this is it.
John DEMPSEY (police-constable V 268.) I found these things outside
Mr. Riches' stable door, at half-past one o'clock in the morning-the prisoner
was inside the stable-I took him—the door was locked inside-he had lifted
up a flap on the stable loft, and got in.
Prisoner. He did not find the coats at all—he did not see them till the
ostler called him back, and he took them. Witness. The ostler saw them
us we were going from the stable door.
JAMES SLATER (police-sergeant V 15.) The prisoner was brought to the
station-he said when he left Lambeth he did not intend to do anything,
but he thought he might as well take something, and if he got these he would
set up costermongering next day, and leave off thieving-he said he expected
to bave a ride over the water.
Prisoner. I don't think I did.
John Mennie (police-constable V 70.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office (read)--the
prisoner is the person. 10
GUILTY. Aged 25.-Transported for Seven Years.
2808. DAVID SIMS was indicted for bigamy,
JOHN ENGLAND. I am a tailor, and live in Philip-street-cottage, Philip-
street, Bath. I was present at St. James's church, Bath, on the 20th of
July, 1823, and was a witness to the prisoner's marriage with Jane Seawood
-I saw her this morning alive.
S
tro
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Had you known her previously?
4. Not till the marriage-I attended at the prisoner's request- she was mar-
ried as a widow-I have since heard, that the way she obtained her
widowhood was by her husband being transported.
JANE BAYMAN. I am in the service of Mr. Watts-I was present at
Lambeth church on the 25th of June, 1827, when my sister Elizabeth was
married to the prisoner-she was a spinster, and twenty-eight years old.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe she has not set this prosecution in motion ?
4. No-she found him a good husband, very sober, affectionate, and kind.
Guilty. Aged 46.-- Confined Ten Days.
