35. JAMES WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of November,
301bs. weight of nails, value 88., the goods of Alexander Cowan.—2nd
COUNT, stating them to be the goods of Ebenezer Golding; and that
he had been before convicted of felony.
ALEXANDER COWAN. I live in Aylesbury-street, Clerkenwell. The
prisoner was in my employ as porter occasionall—l weighed some nails
on Monday, the 2nd of November—they weighed 3cwt. and 10 or 121bs.
over—I am quite positive that was the weight—I sent them on Thursday
to Mr. Golding, Long-lane, Smithfield, shoemaker—I told the prisoner to
take them there, and tell him the weight—they were not exposed in the
shop.
Prisoner. Q. Did you buy the nails by the lb. or cwt. ? A. By the
cwt.—I purchased them at Mr. Southey's sale—the prisoner called on me
—I gave him the order to clear the nails—I did not think there Were
more than lcwt.
Prisoner. What motive had you for desiring me, to tell Mr. Golding
there were 3cwt. and 141bs. ? you know there was no such thing—I have
had information from Mr. Southey's clerk that there was barely 3cwt.—you
were very anxious, tbe next day, to know whether he weighed them—you
told my wife there was a mistake—I went to Mr. Golding, he said there
was only 2cwt. 3qrs. 71bs., and if you bought 3cwt., how can you have lost
331bs. had them in your shop close to your door. Witness. I told
