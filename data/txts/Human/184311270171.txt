 251. CAROLINE PEARCE was indicted for obtaining money by false pretences. MARIA SHILCOCK. I am the wife of John Shilcock, of Southampton-street, Bloomsbury. This paper was brought to me by the prisoner, signed Mary Ann Pearce—having read this letter I believed it came from Mr. Pearce, a widow, in Leather-lane—I received from my husband two sovereigns—I enclosed them in an envelope, and gave them to the prisoner for Mr. Pearce—the prisoner wrote on the letter" Received 2 l . "—( Letter read )—-"Dear Mr. Shilcock,—Will you have the kindness to lend me the sum of 2 l . till Wednesday morning, on which day I shall receive ray rents from Devonshire, and will repay it. Caroline is not at home, and Charlotte is not well, or I would have sent her. Please to send it by the bearer, &c., M. A. PEARCE. "—Believing it was from Mr. Pearce I sent the money—here is the seal on the wafer "God be with me"—it corresponds with a seal found in the pri soner's pocket.
   Cross-examined by MR. BEADON . Q.  What time was this? A.  About eleven o'clock in the morning—I had seen the prisoner once before at Mr. Pearce's—I said, "Do you live with Mr. Pearce?"—she said, "Yes, ma'am, I do"—I should have given her the money if she had brought the letter, and nothing had been said. MARY ANN PEARCE. This letter is not in my handwriting—I did not authorize the prisoner to take it, nor do I know any thing about it—she lived with me, and was going away at twelve o'clock to an infant-school in the country—she went out at eleven with a view to go to the Clerical office, to go to the school—I did not authorise her to get the money.
   Cross-examined. Q.  At what time did she leave your house for good? A.  About twelve o'clock. GUILTY. Aged 30.— Confined Four Months .
