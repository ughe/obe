HYMAN BEANSTOCK, 25, Neweastle Place, Whitechapel, coster.
monger. I have known Barrett for four months and Harris about
six years. One day last month, I cannot remember which, I Was
talking to Harris in Neweastle Street. Barrett came up and said he
had got some coin. I said I would have nothing to do with it. I
then introduced Barrett to Harris, and went off to my stall in Petti-
coat Lane. I heard Harris say to Barrett, If you have got a eouple
oD you I shall change them at my sister's public-house; she will not
lock me up." I next saw them together at the Derby race, Harris
told me he was changing a few coins. I saw Harris again on Friday
at the Oaks. I also saw Barrett by himself. On the Tuesday follow-
ing I was talking to Barrett at the corner of Castle Street when
Harris came up and said he had got locked up over a five-shilling
piece which he had left from Epsom races. Barrett said, Why
didn't you give it me back 1" Harris said he forgot. He said that
he had been taken out of bed.
Cross-examined by prisoner. I did not go to the Derby with you
and Harris. On Oaks day I went with you to the gambling club. I
did not come home with you from Epaom that day; I came home
with my brother. You were in the same earriage but we were not
together. I as not with you on the Saturday. You did go to my
mother one night and ask her to mind E2 10s. for you. I do not
know if it was on this Saturday. I went to prison before Easter for
passing bad coin. I was discharged by the magistnste.

Bs, and gave Freedland five-shilling piece, telling him to be back
