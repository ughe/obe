ROBERT SHEPHERD. I keep the Ferry-house, at Poplar--the prisoner
brought me this check, and I casbed it for him afterwards paid
it away.
Cross-examined Q. How long have you known the prisoner? 4.
Personally about sixteen months--I never heard anything against his
character.
Henry Rowe. I am in the employ of Spalding and Hodge, Drury-
lane-they bought goods of the prosecutor, to the amount of 161, 48. 11d.:
the discount off it made it 151. 168. 90.-I was present when that was
paid to the prisoner by our cashier on 4th Nov-the goods were had on
23rd Oct.
John Day. I am in the service of Mr. Simmons. The prisoner
obtained an order for twenty reams of paper from our house-I recolleet
their being delivered - I was present when a check was delivered to the
prisoner--this is it-it was given him in payment for the goods on 7th
Nov.--the invoice was 261. 3s. 5d.; with the discount off it came to
251. 10s. 4d.--I saw the prisoner sign his name in this book.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you dealt much with this firm? 2. Ever
since he has been there-he used to solicit orders -I knew nothing of him
before, and know nothing against him—the amount of my dealings with
the firm has been just under 1001, in fourteen months.
RICHARD GEORGE STATBAN. I gave the money for this check,
251. 10s. 4d., to the prisoner; I paid the check to Mr. Joyce.
Cross-examined Q. Were you a customer of the firm? A. No; the
prisoner was a customer of mine-he used to come to town on Mondays
and dine with me.
Thomas Joyce. I received this check from Mr. Statham, and paid it
into the Bank of England.
JAMES GAMER GAIN. I am son-in-law of the prosecutor, and manage
the business for him. The prisoner was in his employ to overlook the
men, and then afterwards to go round on Mondays, show samples of
paper, and try to get orders, which he ought to enter in an order-book, and
the price at which the articles were sold, and then at the end of the month
I would give him invoices sealed up to deliver to the customers-it was
his duty to receive money and hand it over to me, and checks also-here
is no entry in the order-book of these twenty reams of paper to Mr.
Simmons--this invoice to Mr. Simmons is not mine; I believe it to be the
prisoner's-here is his name on the back of the check, in his writing—when
the prisoner was first employed bis wages were 25s, a week, and after he
began to sell 368. a week and his expenses to London--that was what he
was receiving at this time I had a conversation with him about the
amount due from Spalding and Hodge; I asked him whether they had
paid it, or when they would pay, on Friday, 6th Dec.--I used to go
down to the paper-mill every Friday to make up the time-book-he said,
"On next Monday"-he has never paid me 251. 10s. 4d., on account of
