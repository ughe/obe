WILLIAM ROBERTS JAMES. I am clerk to the Board of Guardians of the Holborn Union, and vestry-clerk of the liberty of Saffron-hill, which is part of the Holborn Union—the prisoner was a collector of the poors' rates—he was appointed on the 1st of June, 1836.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Was that appointment in writing? A.  No—it was not an appointment in writing under the hand and seal of the Poor Law Commissioners—it is according t practice that the collector is appointed by order of the Poor Law Commissioners—that is a printed order—there was a printed order in this case.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Not appointing the collector? A.  No, it is an order to the Guardians to appoint, which is done at a meeting of the Board—he is appointed verbally—a minute of his appointment is afterwards made in the minute-book.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Is the appointment made by virtue of the order of the Poor Law Commissioners? A.  Certainly, and that is a printed order, describing his duties and the payment he is to receive—the entry of his appointment in the minute-book is made the next day.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Have you got the rate-book here? A.  I have—the rate to which the amounts in question refer was made on the 13th of Aug., 1846—I know the handwriting of the gentlemen whose names appear in the book—I have seen them write—it was the prisoner's duty to collect that rate, and when collected to pay it into the bankers to the account of the Overseers—I know that the Overseers have an account at the bankers—the audit up to Lady-day, 1847, commenced on the 4th of May—Mr. James Hales Mitchener is the auditor—about the 6th of May the prisoner was sent for by the auditor to bring his receipt check-books—he did not bring them—they were brought or sent some days after, but I was not present—the prisoner was present when the accounts were audited—the auditor expressed an opinion that ex planations were necessary and requested him to produce certain books—no amount of defalcation was then stated to him—it was not correctly ascertained at that time what the amount was—he was sent to get his receipt check-books, and was told to return immediately—I did not see him for several days afterwards—I produce the check-books, and also a return that was made by the prisoner to the auditor on the 6th of May—there are two accounts; one form Lady-day to Midsummer, 1846, and the other from Midsummer to Michaelmas, 1846—the greater part of this is in the prisoner's handwriting—he produced it, and that which is not his handwriting are some obser vations of the auditor's—the result of the account from Michaelmas to Christmas is, that there was a balance in his hand of 24 l . 14 s . 6 d .—the amount that was uncollected was 446 l . 15 s . 1 d .—that was the deficiency—this is only an aggregate account—I have no detailed account of pri soner's of the particular sums paid to him, and not accounted for by him—the auditor has a detailed account, made out by the prisoner and himself, going through the book, and putting down the sums—the prisoner was paid by a poundage of 2 l . 10 s . per cent. on the receipt of rates—he was paid by the Overseers—that has continued since his appointment—I have the Over seers' account book.
