EDMUND BURTON (police-sergeant D 25.) In consequence of informa-
tion I received I went to Knowl-green cottage, and saw the prisoner there
-I asked him if any lead was missing from the cottage, and if he had
sold any-be said he had not-I took him to Mr. Spencer's at Staines,
who said he was the man who had sold a quantity of lead to him in the
name of Edward Williams-I took some lead from Mr. Spencer's, to the
cottage, and found lead gone from an outhouse--it fitted there--the prisoner
afterwards said he had stolen the lead, but was driven to it by distress.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you have not the least doubt of that
being true? A. His family were in the most abject state of distress-I
assisted them myself.
John SPENCER. On the 14th of April I purchased a piece of lead of the
prisoner-I was to give him 1s. Ofa., as it was 10lbs. at 14d. a pound-
he hastened away, and only took the shilling.
Thomas Henry HUTTON re-examined. He had nothing for taking

pay them, but to give them 11 or 21. when it is let--they live in it rent
free.
