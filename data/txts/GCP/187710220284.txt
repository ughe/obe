JOHN HUTTON. I am the landlord of the Bull at Birchwood Corner
on Saturday evening, the 14th July, the last witness came to my house
and had some refreshment, and left my house a little before nine o'clock
- he took some bread and veal and ham wrapped up in a paper-he
returned about 9.30, very much excited-he had been knocked about and
complained very much I had him attended to, and drove him to the
station he was quite sober-I know both the prisoners—they are
donkey-drivers.
Cro88-examined by MR. RIBTON. I have known them between seven and
eight years—their father had my pleasure grounds-I have known
nothing against them—they have been hard-working and industrious.
(The witness explained the bearings of the locality with reference to the scene of
attack on a plan.) The Reynoldses keep their vans or house-carts here
during the summer-the spot where the scuffle took place has been pointed
out to me, and I have measured the distance from there to the road lead-
ing up to the house-carts—it is 100 yards from the mile-stone, about 150
yards from that place this roadway is a private road leading to Heath-
field Farm-on the day in question there was a party of excursionists
down, and there were a great many at my place, 500 or 600-there was 8
quarrel between some of them and the donkey-drivers, not my party-
there might be six, eight, or ten donkey-drivers at my place, and there
might be a dozen at the Lullington Hotel-this squabble was about four
o'clock--the prosecutor recovered, and I took him back that night, and
he went up with his train to London-he came to the station the next
morning, and I drove him over the Heath-we met a man named Gor-
don going over the Heath I have known him as a donkey-driver for &
long time—I said to Schofield “Is that the man ?" and he said "I will
swear he is one of the men"-I went with him to the Superintendent that
Sunday morning, and he told the Superintendent that was one of the men,
and he made a statement to him of how he had been knocked about-I
cannot swear whether I told the Superintendent the man's name-Gordon
was not taken into custody to my knowledge.
By the COURT. Gordon was half way across Dartford Heath when we
saw him-I should say three miles from my house, and one mile from the
police-station at Dartford-he got out of the roadway on to the footpath to
allow my horse and trap to go by—we met him I would not stop him
without a constable, because I knew where he could be found—Schofield
had a fair opportunity of seeing his face and figure I know now that he
identifies two other men—there is a likeness between Gordon and
Reynolds, they are the same stamp of man.
