   NEW COURT .— Monday, October  30 th , 1843.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 3029. WILLIAM WARDELL was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of Sept ., 1 chest of drawers, value 2 l  . 10 s  .; and 1 mat, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John Deal , his master: and EDWARD WARDELL , for feloniously receiv ing the same, well knowing them to be stolen; against the Statute, &c., to which
   WILLIAM WARDELL  pleaded GUILTY.
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM WAINE. I am shopman to Mr. John Dent, an upholsterer, in Crawford-street, Marylebone. On the 27th of Sept. a chest of drawers were removed from Montague-street to our warehouse—I missed them in the even ing—I dismissed William Wardell, who was in the service—on the day after I searched William's premises, and saw a chest of drawers—they were the same that had been taken from my master—the mat produced is also his, and worth 1 s . 6 d . JAMES COULTHARD. I am in the prosecutor's service—on the 27th of Sept. I assisted William Wardell to remove a chest of drawers—I have seen them since. MORRIS ELLICOTT. I am a shoemaker, and live in Highworth-street, Lis son-grove. William Wardell lodged at my house—on the 27th Sept. he brought home a chest of drawers—I assisted him to put them in the kitchen. JOHN PHELPS( police-constable D  13.) About five in the afternoon of the 29th of Sept, I went to the house of Edward Wardell, in Upper Boston-place, and saw a mat there—at nine the same evening I took him into custody for reoeiving stolen property—he made no answer—I said I had found a door-mat at his house—he said he had bought it of his brother for 4 s . 8 d .—I had not then found the chest of drawers—I went to Stafford-street, Lisson-grove, and found a chest of drawers—I have shown them to Waine. RICHARD STANFORD. I am pot-boy at the Hope and Anchor, in Hereford-street, Lisson-grove. On Friday evening, the 29th, about eight o'clock, Ed ward Wardell came and asked me to assist him with a chest of drawers a few doors off—I went with him to No. 4, Highworth-street, and assisted him to take a pair of drawers to Stafford-street, and left them in the front parlour—he gave me 4 d . and some beer.
   EDWARD WARDELL  — NOT GUILTY.
