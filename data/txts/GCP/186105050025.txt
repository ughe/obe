ERNEST SPIER (through an interpreter). I carry on business as an instru-
ment maker, in the Rue Chalot, at Paris, with my brother_we make opera
glasses, and things of that description-in January I engaged the prisoner
to travel for us in England he was to have 12. francs a day, besides
his travelling expenses—the 12 francs was to go for his support, and
his carriage was paid extra-when he left Paris we gave him three boxes of
samples among them were opera-glasses, of different patterns, fourteen
microscopes, eighteen eye-glasses, eighteen eye-glass frames, twelve cases of
mathematical instruments, some compasses, and aebromatic glasses, besides
many other things-he was to show them, and to get orders upon them
he had no authority to sell or part with the samples—1161. was the value of
them-I gave him 201. at starting, and sent him 501. afterwards after he
went we, for some time, received letters, and a few orders ; rather regularly
at first that regularity ceased from 24th March -he ought to have written
twice a week-he continued to write twice a week, nearly, until 24th
March, with the exception of once, when it was a fortnight when I found
he was getting irregular I wrote to him I wrote this letter of 24th March
(This was addressed to the prisoner in London, requesting to hear from him, and
directing him to send back the samples) -I received this letter in answer (This
was dated Black Bull, London, 30th March, 1861, acknowledging the receipt
