ANTON AHRRIOK. I am a passenger agent-on 3rd February the
prisoner brought me up at the police-court for fraudulently obtaining
181. 10.-I saw him sworn-ho gave his name Abraham Levy, and
swore that he never told me he was Solomon Jacobs-I understand
German-he was asked if he had seen Mr. Pasck at my office and said
that he never had, and had never told him that he did not want to
stay in London, but to send his tieke Southampton-as a fact, the
prisoner first met me on the 21st abouu 1 p.m., and asked me if he
could go next week to Cape Town, and what the price was-I said
161.--I saw him again on the 28th about 10 a.m., and he asked me
when the boat would go to Cape Town-I said, "Next Thursday from
Southampton"-he said, " At 2 o'clock I shall be here with the money,"
and then he was gone I did not ask his name then-he came back
about 2.30, and said that he had got the money-I took out my business
card and said, “What is your name?”_he said, “Solomon Jacobs "-I
said, "What is your age?"-he said, " 19 or 20, put down 20”-I put
it on the card and he gave me 151. and 31. to buy him an outfit-I said,
"Will you wait here, or will you go with me to get your ticket?”_he
said, "I will stop a little ;” he sat in a chair-I went to Mr. Pasek and
gave him the money and asked for a tiöket, and he went to the shipping
company for it-I returned about 4.15 and found my daughter in the
room with the prisoner, and he told me in her presence that Mr. Pasok
said that he could not go in that boat, he must go in the next-he.
went to Southampton for that night, and I telegraphed to him on Wed-
nesday as he had told me he did not know where I was to send the
ticket; I said that I would send it to the Union Steam Ship Company,
and gave him the address, and went with him and my wife to Waterloo
station, and said, "To-morrow you shall have the ticket for the boat if
possible, if not, for the next boat”-Mr. Pasck told him that next week
he could go by the Caroline-he went that night because he wanted to
go to Southampton at once-I sent the ticket in a registered letter to the
office, but it came back afterwards on the Thursday, about 7 p.m., an
old man and a young man came and had a long conversation with me
about him, and the next thing was I was taken into custody on Saturday
night—Mr. Pasck gave evidence, and I was discharged on the Monday.
