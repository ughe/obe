of felony
HENRY DUNSTER. I am servant to Robert Alexander Gray, who lives
at Camberwell. On the 25th of March, I fed his fowls between four and
five o'clock in the afternoon-I fastened the door afterwards- I went to
the hen-house the next morning with the policeman, between seven and
eight o'clock, and found the hen-house door broken open-I missed seven
hens and a cock-I had seen them the day before, between four and five
o'clock-I saw them again at the station-house at Camberwell, and knew
them again—they were then dead.
JAMES Mann. I am a policeman--I know the prisoners--I saw them
at six o'clock on Thursday morning, the 25th of March, on the high road
between Camberwell and Peckham, within two or three hundred yards of
Mr. Gray's house-I went a few yards up my beat, and missed them out
of the road-I went to the next policeman and spoke to him-I borrowed
his light, returned, and went on Mr. Pope's premises, adjoining Mr. Gray
-I got upon the fence, and saw the two prisoners coming from Mr. Gray's
hen-house I had the light full on their faces I saw Sherwin with some
fowls in each hand- I called to them to stop, but they ran away from me
Sherwin dropped the fowls as he ran-I sprang my rattle, and gave infor
mation of what had happened-I picked up the fowls and took them to the
station-house-I went back to Mr. Gray's and examined the heu-house-
