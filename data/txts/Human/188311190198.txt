   Re-examined.  I never saw her give it the breast. MARY BURKE. I am the wife of Patrick Burke, and live on the floor above the prisoner—I was in the house when the baby was born—I first saw it about a month afterwards—it then appeared to be healthy; it was thin—I saw the sister in the house: I can't tell when she left—shortly before the child died I met the prisoner on the stairs, and said "You are neglecting your baby;" she said "Mind your own business"—I think she had the child with her at that time—I never went into her room—I have met her in the passage—sometimes I have seen her going into a public-house or coming from it—I have seen her under the influence of drink since this child was born. MARTHA PEPPER. I live in the same house with my children—I know the prisoner—I only saw the baby once; that was a week before it died—it was in her arms rolled in a shawl in the street, going away from home—I did not say anything to her—I never spoke to her till the night after the bady died—she called a little girl I had the care of a b—bitch, and she called me everything—she said she had a man to keep her at 1 l . 15 s . a week; I said "Moreshame for you"—I had heard the baby crying the whole of the day—I never heard it crying before—I have seen the prisoner a number of times, sometimes sober and some times not.
   Cross-examined.  I occupied the top floor—the prisoner lived two floors below—it was in passing downstairs that I heard the child crying. MARY PERKINS. I saw the baby a few times before it died—it was a very small baby born—I did not see it again until the day of its death—I have seen the prisoner once or twice under the influence of drink—I have heard the children crying for food on one or two occasions, and have given them food.
   Cross-examined.  I saw the baby about an hour after it was born—it was plump, but very small—I never saw any blueness about it—I did not notice it particularly. JAMES INGLIS MASON. I am a surgeon at 15, The paragon, New Kent Road—on 17th October a child named Hudson was brought to me for advice by two women, I believe the prisoner to be one of them—I observed its smallness, thinness, and its shrivelled condition, and a slightly blue or veinous look about the extremities, the arms and legs—I gave a prescription, a slight stimulant—I don't remember the directions I gave about its food—on 24th October, in the morning, two women came again—I sent them away and asked them to send the mother, and in the evening the prisoner came with another woman—she told me about the child being dead, and wanted to know if I could certify as to the cause of death—I gave a certificate that it died of cyanosis, that is simply a defect in the circulation—I told them on the 17th that I thought the child would die—improper nourishment or want of sufficient nourishment would cause much the same appearance that I saw on the 17th—if the heart was in a healthy condition I should ascribe the appearance I saw to defective nutrition.
