 830. CHARLES NEW was indicted for a misdemeanor. WILLIAM HARWOOD I live at Chelsea-market, and am a tobacconist. On the 31st of January, about ten o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to me for a halfpenny print—he gave me a shilling—I noticed the shilling directly he was out of the shop, and found it was bad—I had not missed it with any other—I broke it and put it on one side—on the next Saturday he came again and asked for another halfpenny print—know ing him, I asked him for the money before I gave him the print—he put down a shilling—I saw it was bad, and told him so—he went away, leav ing the shilling and the print—I described him to Thatcher, and after wards saw the prisoner in his custody—I had broken both the shillings. GEORGE THATCHER( police-sergeant B  17.) I received a description from Harwood of somebody who had passed bad money—I took the prisoner, and Harwood identified him as he was lying in bed—I found 4 d .
 on him—he gave no account of these shillings—these are them. JOHN FIELD These are parts of two counterfeit shillings.

 831. WILLIAM CARRINGTON was indicted for a misdemeanor. JOSEPH MAY I am shopman to Mr. Moginie. On the 18th of Fe bruary, about ten o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came and asked for change for sixpence—I saw it was bad—I put it to my teeth, and bent it nearly double—I said, "If you wish six pennyworth of halfpence, You must bring me a good sixpence"—he took it and went away—I saw an officer and told him of it—he followed the prisoner. JOHN SCOTT( City-policeman C  18.) I saw May; and in consequence of what he said I went after the prisoner, And overtook him—when I came to him he was bending the sixpence straight upon the sheep peas, and he said, "I'll be d—d if I won't pass it yet"—I took him to the station—I searched him, and found one sixpence in his hand and another in his right hand trowsers pocket—the one in his hand was the one he was straightening.
