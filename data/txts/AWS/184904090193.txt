as he had struck her-I did not see her raise the knife at him, or show any

Mrs. Davies, and a little girl-the prisoner was eating dinner during the

bayonet, and put it in the case-when he struck her she was standing leaning
against the fireplace-the quarrelling had ceased for a little while, and then
she began again calling him names, and calling his mother very offensive
names-he went on packing, and appearing not to think that he had done any
harm-when she fell down, he said she would not fall to hurt herself, and he
took no notice-there was no blood on the bayonet-it was done in the heat
of passion.
Mr. HUDDLESTON. Q. Was the prisoner drunk A. No--the quarrel
began before dinner, and he struck her in the face-after that they sat down
to dinner-while they were at dinner they began quarrelling again, and he
struck her again then she got up with the knife in her hand, and I took it
out of her hand-after that we were all sitting down to dinner-he gave her
a slap of the face, she fell off the chair, and then she got up and stood by the
fireplace, and then he struck her with the bayonet.
ELLEN DAVIBs. I am the prisoner's wife. I was in liquor on that day-
I was very angry-I do not recollect what took place.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe your husband had been obliged to separate
from you about two months before ? A. Yes-I recollect going to his captain
when he had been beating me-] was in the habit of getting drunk sometimes
-I wish to throw my husband on your protection--I do not wish to give any
evidence to hurt him.
GEoRGe SHIPMAN. I am a surgeon at Woolwich. I was called to attend
Ellen Davies-I found a wound between the third and fourth bones of the
back-I probed it, it was from an inch and a half to two inches deep-it had
just escaped the kidneys-it was a part from which I should not expect much
blood-the locality of it prevented its bleeding, there are no large vessels
there-it was a triangular wound, which is a wound of a dangerous character
-this bayonet would produce such a wound-i had entered the flesh-it was
perfectly straight-it had gone by the side of the kidney, where it is soft and
fat-it might penetrate some distance without making a wound that would be
dangerous to life-if it had been a little lower down it might have been dan-
gerous- visited her three times a day till the Friday following, and twice
afterwards-I attended her from the 12th to the 21st of March-if it had
been a round wound, I should not have had half the trouble I had-a
triangular wound never heals kindly, when two lips of the wound are closed
the other remains open-] was afraid of an inflammation-it was between the
spine and the kidney.
