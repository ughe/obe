1606. CAROLINE SEYMOUR was indicted for stealing. on the 3rd
of June, 1 petticoat, value 1a., the goods of Emma White; 1 papkin,
value 6d. the goods of Sarah Rowbottom 2 gowna, value 3s. ; 1 apron,
value 4d. and 1 pinafore, value 6d., the goods of William Wyatt, her
master.
SARAH WYATT. The prisoner was in my service for about a fortnight
-I missed these articles (looking at them)--I had seen them on the second
day of her coming into the house, which was on the 12th of MayI missed
them a few days afterwards

Prisoner. Q. Can you show me the two dresses you have brought
against me A. These are them-I had them made for myselfthe
were dresses; but they are torn apart-I left them safe.
Prisoner. I wrote to master, begging him to take it into his considera-
tion to come forward, and say that part of it was his sister's, Sarah Row-
bottoms-that napkin is one belonging to my children.
COURT to S. WYATT. Q. Have you any mark on that piece of gown A.
Yes--the making of it, and the apron too-I know it by this band--I am
sure it is my husband's, William Wyatt's, property-they are not worth
more than 6d. altogether,
Prisoner. Did you not, (on your oath) when I washed up all my
aprons, say, 44 Ann, tie that apron before you, that you don't take cold
you know you are insane ; you are not fit to take an oath-while I was in
custody, you went to my lodgings, and took things of mine out of my box
to bring against me-you even took balls of tape and worsted.
BENJAMIN SKELTON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce this gown and a
napkin, pawned by the prisoner-the gown, I believe, is her own property.
EMMA WHITE. This petticoat is mine-it is marked-it was in my
box, which was looked.
Prisoner. I did pawn them, but White was the bar-maid, and she asked
me to go and bring a wreath of flowers for her, which I did, and some
white-satin ribbon, which is on her bonnet now-she gave me 2. for the
wreath, and they cost 2s. 6d. -she said, 44 Never mind, I will pay you"-
but she never did. Witness. Yes, I did.
Prisoner. When I went to bed at night, you said, 44 There is a white
petticoat you may have." Witness. No; I never did.
Prisoner. She did so, on my oath, if I might take my oath and this
apron Mrs. Wyatt lent me-White gave me the petticoat, with a pair of
stockings. (The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that the prosecutrix had
given her the apron.)
