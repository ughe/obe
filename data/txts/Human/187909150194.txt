She seemed very fond of it—I know nothing of the bodies of several children being found in the neighbourhood lately—nothing was said to me about her nursing other children in the workhouse. JOHN HARRIS. I live at No. 2, Richmond Villas, Kingston-on-Thames—I am registrar of births and deaths for the Kingston sub-district—on 2nd July I registered the birth of a male child, by a person who gave the name of Alice Ward—the name was put down as "Frederick," the date of the birth 4th June, and the address "Oatlands Park, "Weybridge"—my place is about 100 yards from the White Horse. JAMES LANGRIDGB. I am a pork butcher, of 23, Garden Row, Mort lake—on Friday afternoon, 18th July, about 2.30,1 was walking along the Lower Mortlake Road, and saw the body of a child in a ditch; I was on the pathway, and I crossed the road to look at some mowers in a field, and on looking in the ditch saw the body of the child; there was water in the ditch, and the back part of the head was partly out of the water—I called the attention of the mowers to the child, and then went and fetched a policeman—I saw him take the child out of the ditch—there were some articles of clothing on it—I saw them taken off afterwards; there was a flannel band and a diaper; that was all I saw. JAMES WARNE( Policeman V  261). On Friday, 18th July, in conse quence of information from Mr. Langridge, I went with him to the Lower Mortlake Road, and there saw the dead body of a male child in a ditch—I conveyed it to the mortuary at Mortlake, and a doctor was afterwards called and the child was undressed—this belly-band and diaper are what it was wealing—there were no other articles upon the child.
   Cross-examined.  I did not search the house of the prisoner's mother. SAMUEL SAMPSON( Police Sergeant, Scotland Yard ). On 19th July I received instructions to make inquiries about this child, and on the evening of the 24th I saw the prisoner at her father's house, at West End, Mortlake—that is between 300 and 400 yards from the ditch where the child was found—I told her I was a detective sergeant, and from information I had received I believed she had recently been confined of a child—she said she had—I asked her where the child was—she said "My child is all right"—I said "But where is your child?"—she again said "My child is all right"—I asked her the third time, and she repeated again "My child is all right and at Weybridge"—I said "Who has charge of your child at Weybridge?" she made no answer—I said "You refuse to give me any further account of your child any more than it is all right at Weybridge?" she said "Yes"—I asked where she was confined; she said at the Kingston Union—I asked in what name; she said "Alice Ward"—I then left to make further inquiries—I did not search the house—I went to Weybridge and endeavoured to trace the child, but failed to do so—on 4th August I went to the house again with Inspector Shaw and saw the prisoner at the door
