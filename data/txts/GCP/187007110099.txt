Mr. Harris at the Marlborough Street Police Court, and she was discharged
I afterwards went to her, and she told me she had got some Russian

fetched them from Mr. Dobson, a solicitor, of London Bridge--I introduced
the prisoner to him for the purpose of selling 6002. worth of bonds he
sold them for her--we went froin London Bridge to the European that
was about 23rd July, 1869–the prisoner, myself, and several of her friends
went to the European while Mr. Dobson took the bonds over to a broker-
Mr. Dobson came back and told her what they were fetching, and that if
she would wait till later in the day there would no doubt be a rise in them
she said she could not; she had an appointment and she must have the
money because she had to pay the deposit for a public-house-I went then
with Mr. Dobson to the broker, and he received notes to the amount of
5151-I changed a note at the Bank of England, and we met the prisoner
-I banded her the money in Mr. Dobson's presence-I did not see Mrs.
McKeon at all in that transaction-the bonds were payable to bearer the
prisoner gave 308. to Dobson and 11. to me---I owed her some money and
that made it up-previous to that I went with her to a man named Reinhardt,
s money-changer in Coventry Street, and she gave him 100L in Russian
bonds--I saw the prisoner again in August, after the bonds had been sold
--she told me then that the bonds belonged to aunty; that is the name
the prosecutrix went by, that she had stolen them, and she had got into a
mess about it-after some conversation she said " Aunty's at Ports
mouth, I will give you 100% to go to America; go to Stokes Bay, and get
her over, and then you can easily topple her into the water" she gave me
2. to pay my expenses to Portsmouth-I took the 21.-I did not go to
Portsmouth-I saw the prisoner sign this receipt at Reinhardt's as “ Honora
Austin" (This was a receipt for 411. 78. 6d., the proceeds of a 501. bond, signed
Honora Austin, and witnessed by J. M. Dobson)It is written by Mr
Reinhardt or his clerk-I drew up a paper, and gave it to the prisonerit
was to protect myself and Mr. Dobson-Mr. Dobson is an attorney on the
rolls still.

Cross-examined by Mr. Davin, Q. Did you suggest that the receipt should
be drawn out in the name of Honora Austin A. I did-Mr. Scarth had
nothing to do with the transaction-I did not represent myself as Mr.
Searth's clerk during the whole time-it would be false if I swore that I
had done so-I never saw Mrs. McKeon in reference to the bonds--the
body of the document and the attestation are in my writing- I believe the
signature is Mrs. McKeon's—I did not see her write it-I have led rather
sa irregular life I have drank a little-I mean to tell the jury that the
conversation about my going down to Portsmouth to topple the old lady
into the sea did take place between myself and the prisoner-I listened to
her suggestion, but I told plenty of people about it-I took the 21.the
conversation was in August last year-I drew up the brief for the prisoner's
defence at the Police Court; that was about two or three weeks ago I
instructed Mr. Bretton, Mr. Edward Lewis's partner-I tried three or four,
and they would not take the defence up- I did not hear Margaret Morrisy
speak to the prisoner as they left the Court.
