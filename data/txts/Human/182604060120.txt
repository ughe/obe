894. MICHAEL LARNER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of April , 1 printed bound book, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Bumpus.
 THOMAS BUMPUS. I live at  Holborn-bars, in the City. I am a bookseller- this book is mine; I had seen it safe about twelve o'clock on the 1st of April.
   JOSEPH NICHOLSON. I am porter at the Blue Posts public-house. On the 1st of April Mrs. Bumpus called to me, about ten minutes past two o'clock, to go after the prisoner - she gave me his description; I found him in Middle-row, looking into a bookseller's shop; I brought him back, and found this book in his right hand coat pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I cannot say I am not guilty, but the officer can tell you I was in a desperate state of madness, occasioned by a kick of a horse over my eye; I had drank some liquor that day; I was tied down in a straight waistcoat for thirty hours in convulsive fits in consequence of what I had done.
 GUILTY. Aged 23. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 895.  THOMAS PERKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of April , 1 wooden chest, value 2s. 6d., and 50lbs. weight of tea, value 13l. 15s. the goods of Thomas Conway and others, his partners.
   CHARLES TOWNSEND. I am warehouseman to Thomas Conway and others - they are wholesale grocers and tea dealers, and live in Maiden-lane, Queen-street, City. On Thursday last, the 6th of April, I loaded the cart with one chest of tea, one bag of tea, and some sugars; I gave it in charge of Blunt, the carman, about half-past eleven o'clock.
   JOSEPH BLUNT. I am the carman. I received this chest of tea in my cart on Thursday last; I drove it along Bishopsgate-street, and there I stopped; I was about forty yards behind the cart when I saw the prisoner go and move the chest of tea from the side to the tail - he then turned round and lifted it over the chain, took it on his shoulder, and walked away; I ran and caught him about half-way across the road.
   THOMAS WHITFIELD. I was sent down to Liverpool-street; I met the carman, who called and asked me to have something to drink - we were going to have it, when he turned round and said "I have a chest of tea in my cart, and I must have a sharp eye on it;" he then saw the prisoner taken it and ran after him; I saw him drop the chest, which Blunt gave me charge of, and he took the prisoner - the chest was close at his heels.
   WILLIAM RILEY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner and the tea.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along Bishopsgate-street, and a man asked me if I wanted a job; I said Yes; he said "If you will take this chest of tea to the Catherine Wheel, I will pay for it."
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 896.  WILLIAM PRYER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 8lbs. of beef, value 1s., the goods of George Lee.
 GEORGE LEE. I am a  salesman at Newgate-market. I lost a piece of beef from a book in my shop; I had seen it about ten minutes before it was taken; I saw it with a carcass of lamb which the prisoner had purchased in the morning - he paid 2s. on it; he came in the afternoon to pay the remaing 17s. 6d.; I told my man to have an eye on him - he told me he had taken a shin of beef; I ran out and saw the prisoner with the carcass of lamb, and a cloth wrapped over it - the beef was in the carcass.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you see how it got into the carcass? A. No; it is a small piece, but it could not have fallen into it, because the beef hung under the lamb - it required some force to get it in.
   WILLIAM PRENTICE. I am Lee's clerk. On the 7th of April I saw the prisoner take a shin of beef off a book, and put it inside the carcass of lamb; I ran in and told Mr. Lee- the prisoner had a cloth which wrapped round the lamb and the beef in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it not usual to have a cloth? A. Yes; I told Mr. Lee the instant I saw him do it.
   HENRY KELLY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, who was very outrageous; he said he would not go to the Compter - he clung round a post, and I was forced to use a little force with him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I understood the scale-man in the morning that he would hang the lamb in the back-yard, and I went there for it; the young man said it was on the right side, and he came and took the note off it; I took it on my shoulder, and some of the shins of beef fell down, one fell into the lamb, and one fell down - the man then caught the lamb and said I wanted to rob him; I said I had no idea of it; I thought it was only a joke; I then went into the market, and came back to Mr. Lee, who was talking with some gentleman; they said "Poh, Poh, nonsense!"
   WILLIAM PARSONS. I am a cheesemonger and grocer, and live in Catherine-street, Limehouse-fields; I have employed the prisoner two months, and he has always given me satisfaction, and laid out my money honestly; I was with him the day in question, when he paid the 2s. off the carcass, which he bought for me; when I missed him I called on Mr. Lee, and asked if he knew any thing of a man who had paid him 2s. off a carcass of lamb - he said he had been there, and attempted to steal a shin of beef, and he had got him safe enough, and if I would stand 2l. he would not appear against him; I would take him into my employ immediately.
   GEORGE LEE. He said the man had a wife and four children - I said I would not mention any thing but what related to his occurrence, though he has done several bad things.
 GUILTY. Aged 44. Recommended to Mercy, on account of his character. Confined One Month.
