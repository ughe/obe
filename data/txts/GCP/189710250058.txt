MR. MORGAN Prosecuteu, MR. SYMONDS Defended.
CHARLES WILLIAM Francis WILLIAMS. I carry on business as Bryden
and Co., corn dealers, 203, High Road, Kilburn--at the end of 1896 I
arranged with the prisoner to sell goods on commission-1 agreed to pay
him 5 per cent. commission on the returns, the customers were to be his
connection, and he was to collect and hand the money to me, the cheques
to be paid into my bank when received there were transactions first on
and then subsequent to December 19th, on which I paid him coin niissions
on the orders he had obtained ---he introduced Mr. Ling, corn dealer of
the Borough Road, Southwark, as a customer-I paid him three com-
missions on Mr. Ling's orders when he brought the orders--there was no
account between us--subsequently I gave him an additional } per cent.
commission on certain goods--he had no authority to put my name to
cheques, nor receive the proceeds—this cheque for £5 I received from
Mr. Ling after it was paid through his bank—it is endorsed by the
prisoner-I never received the proceeds—I know the prisoner's writing
(This was on the London and County Bank, and endorsed “Bryden & Co.")

no salary-I expected him to settle within a reasonable time, say once &
week-he never drew a cheque to me-I gave him a cheque for £5 on
February 10th-it might have been post-dated-I did not then owe him
£7 10s.-the cheque was not due for extra commission--- he had received
£15 or £16 in cash and not accounted for it-the endorsement is the
prisoner's ordinary writing, not disguised -if I made a bad debt it was
my loss, not his I took the customers to be sound when the prisoner
