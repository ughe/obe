ARTHUR H. J. WALKER. I was cashier at the Economic from the
beginning of 1904 to the failure, and had the keys of the safes. I
never saw a cheque fer £35,000 or knew of its existence. In the
A. cashbook, under date February 4, there is an entry, “Capital ac-
count, Financial, £35,000," and on the other side the cross entry,
"Deposit account, £35,000." In the waste book for that date there
is a credit entry of £35,000; it appears to have gone through Wil-
liams Deacon's, but it might have been presented specially. Mr. Gros-
venor came to the bank in 1905 about three times.
CHARLES INGLIS THORNTON, merchant in the City, said he had known
prisoner for 33 years; prisoner had always borne the character of a
thoroughly respectable honest man.
WILLIAM HENRY Fox, of Fox, Sissons and Co., chartered account-
ants, Austin Friars, gave similar evidence. He had himself lost
£1,100 in the Economic, but he absolutely believed in prisoner's
integrity.

SAMUEL GURNEY MASSEY (prisoner, on oath) repeated the statement
he had made (also on oath) before the magistrate. The following are
the material portions of the statement: I have been connected with
banking in the City of London since 1868. I was the first manager
of the Economic Bank when it was formed in 1893, and continued
managing trustee to the end, subject to a break of about two years, in
1902 and 1903, owing to illness. In the autumn of 1903 I received
# communication from Mr. Grosvenor, & stockbroker, of Walbrook,
and called and saw him with reference to the same. He wished to
know if I were prepared to sell my Economie shares at a discount, as
he was willing to purchase any shares of the company. He subse-
quently told me that he had by purchase of shares acquired the con
trol of the Economie Bank. He asked me if I would consent to be
manager. I told him I must decline unless further capital was taken
up, and this he thought could be arranged. I saw Mr. Roscoe, a
customer of the Economio Bank, who was a holder of over 1,100
shares, and introduced him to Grosvenor. Roscoe mentioned the
name of the Financial and Commercial Bank, Limited, and told me
that it was a strong institution and had £300,000 paid-up cash capi-
tal. Roscoe's firm were good customers of ours, and frequently had
£4,000 or £6,000 on deposit with the Economic Bank. I made in-
quiries as to the position of the Financial and Commercial Bank.
The directors were Lord Charles Pratt, who I was informed was a per:
