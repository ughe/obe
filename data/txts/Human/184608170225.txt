GEORGE WILLIAM MOODY. I am assistant to Mr. Corke. On the 7th of July I saw a parcel sent away—I thought there was something wrong, and I directly called Mr. Corke's attention to it—I asked if he thought he had got all honest people about him—I saw the prisoner give the parcel to Cheeseman—I bad seen her come into the shop, and take this parcel of satinett from the shop into the work-room—I went to the Parcel Delivery Company, and found a parcel—I opened it, and called Mr. Cork's attention to it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you remember having a conversation with Mr. Corke about an oath that he took at Weymoutb? A.  I have heard him speak of an oath he bad taken at Weymouth, that he would prosecute to the utmost everybody that was robbing him—he said something of the sort, that that was his reason why he prosecuted the prisoner—I never had any quarrel with the prisoner—she never, to my knowledge, charged me with beating the apprentices when I was drunk—she might have charged me with being drunk—I have beaten Sharpe—she might have spoken about that—I cannot remember it. GEORGE JOHNSON( police-constable P  142.) I was called into the pro secutor's house—I went up stairs with him and the prisoner—these article were found in the prisoner's box, in her bed-room—while Mr. Corke was looking over another box, I saw the prisoner take this parcel and pnt it into her pocket—she took these boots and shoes and placed them under the foot of the bed—after Mr. Corke had done looking at other boxes, I called his attention to the prisoner's pocket, and then to the foot of the bed. MATTHEW CORKE. The prisoner was in my service—these articles are my property—I can swear these boots and shoes, and crape, and silk, and blond quillings have not been entered.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How do you know these boots and shoes? A.  They bear my private mark—they are new—there are shop-tickets on these other things—I never missed any of these things—I never put any question to the prisoner till I had been told by Moody that he bad seen the parcel go out—the prisoner was a person of great trust.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 21.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury .— Judgment Respited.
