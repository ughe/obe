ALEXANDER HENNESSY (Policeman, E 84). I know the prosecutor's hone
-on the evening of 5th July, I first saw William Thompson, about to
minutes past 10 o'clock-I did not speak to him then I saw him near
passage that runs from Newman-street to Upper Rathbone-place-1
afterwards saw him in Newman-street at the other end of the passare
about twenty minutes past 10-I stood and looked at him for a second, and
said, “You have a waiting job on to night"-he sa’d, “I don't know what
you mean, I don't understand you"- I said, " I spoke plain enough for ye
to understand ; you have a waiting job on to-night"-he said, “Oh! its
not a late hour "-I said, " Whether it is a late hour or not I shall not have
you about here, I shall see you away "-I followed him through two or thre
streets-he had been pointed out to me before I am sure he is the man-
I saw him no more that night I followed him till I saw another constable
and then I left him.
JOSEPH LAMBERT (Policeman, E 68). I searched William Thompson on
the 24th, and found on him this coat (produced)—he was in custody at
High-street Marylebone, on another charge-I know him under the named
John Shepherd that is his right name-I received from Mr. Byre the
duplicate that has been produced-it is for a brooch-the prisoners were
takeu in custody on the 10th.
William Thompson. Shepherd is not my name; William Thompson is
my proper naine. Witness. I know that his name is Shepherd-I have
known him and his mother for several years.
THOMAS TYRRELL. I am a pawnbroker in Munster-square-I produces
gold watch and chain pledged with me on Saturday, 6th July, for 21. in the
name of John Shepherd; I do not know by whom.
THOMAS ALLWORTHY. I am assistant to Mr. Lee, pawnbroker, St. Mary's
street, Hampstead-road-I produce a cameo brooch, pledged on 6th July, by
a young man in the name of John Shepherd-I believe the prisoner Willian
Thompson to be the person, but I could not swear positively-I gaves
ticket to the person by whom it was pledged--this is the duplicate.
THOMAS DRY. I am assistant to Mr. Harrison of Wardour-street, pawo.
broker-I produce a coat pledged in the name of John Thompson, on 6th
July, for 158.-I gave a ticket-I do not know the person.
JOSEPH JOSEPHS. I live at 37, Crown-street, Soho, and carry on the
business of a pawnbroker-I purchased this opera-glass (produced) on 6th
July, from the prisoner John Thompson for 38.
MYRTHYL BRUNSWICK (re-examined). This watch, opera-glass, and cameo
brooch belong to me-they were safe on the night of 5th July when I went
to bed, and I missed the next morning.
GEORGE BRUNSWICK. This coat and comb are mine-I had a comb like
that, and I missed it-I lost two coats.
William Thompson. Q. What mark have you got on the comb that you
can swear to it by? A. There is no particular mark-there is a little
mark on one of the teeth.
Court. Q. Do you know it by that? A. Yes; it is not a natural mark;
it has broken a little piece off one of the teeth in using it - I have had 3
two years, and used it myself-it is my pocket comb I have no doubt about
it being my property-I was not asked the question before the Magistrate.
William Thompson's Defence. All I have got to say is, that we are not
brothers.
GUILTY.
William Thompson was further charged with having been before convicted.
