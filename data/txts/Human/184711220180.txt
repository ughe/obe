   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  I believe you had known Holliday before, and had dealings with him? A.  I had known him before, and had one transaction with him—I had not written to Holliday to come and see these goods—I sent a circular round, on opening the shop, on 16th Oct., to persons that I had previously had transactions with, stating that I had com menced business on Ludgate-hill—perhaps I might have sent one to Holli day, I am not confident of it—I had never seen Gardiner before—he was there perhaps a quarter of an hour—Holliday took the principal part in the matter—I did not, on making inquiries at Pinner's-hall, find that Mr. Schulkhauser had lived there with Holliday—I never heard the name till I saw it on the card—mine is a double shop, and moderately deep—they were standing close to me, only on the other side of the counter, some feet within the shop—there was no one else in the shop—they stood in various parts—they stood together in one part conferring for three or four minutes, but not out of my sight—we have glass show-cases round the shop, and I walked round with them, showing them the things—I will not swear there were more than twenty prisoners at Giltspur-street, when I selected the prisoners, but I think there were—I think Gardiner was standing next to Holliday.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.  I believe you had been shop man to Mr. Jones, of Cheapside? A.  Yes, Holliday purchased some tri fling articles there, to the amount of 3 l . or 4 l .—the gold watch that he wanted might have been worth 20 l .—I did not tell him, while at Mr. Jones's, that I knew he was a person fond of a bargain, and I should like to have his address—I had his address, because I perhaps might have sent some articles to him—I do not know that I sent him a circular—I might have done so, perhaps, to all my master's customers—I have never seen Mr. Schulkhauser that I know of—I cannot say whether I have seen Holliday with any other person than Gardiner—I do not think I have, but it is so long ago I cannot charge my memory—Holliday gave me his card while I was at Mr. Jones's, and said he did a good deal in the export trade, and he could do a good deal of business if it was made worth his while; and he said the same to me at Ludgate-hill—he did not tell me that he had a ship of his own—I never heard that he had—I cannot say how long it was before he came to me at Ludgate-hill that I had this conversation with him at Mr. Jones's—I think the address he then gave me was in Broad-street, not Pinner's-hall—it did not strike me as odd that he, having a place of business at Pinner's-hall, should order these things to be sent to Theberton-street to be packed—I did not know where Theberton-street was—he said he had to enclose them in a case with other goods.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  Have you any doubt whatever of Gardiner and Hol liday being the two persons that came to you? A.  No—if I had any doubt I would give the prisoners the benefit of it.
