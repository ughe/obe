Saturday afternoon. I do not know what McQueen came for. I
thought he had come about my accident. I told him that Redgrave
had been arrested, and that I had just had a visit from a detective,
and was expecting another one, and with that he took up his hat and
ran away. He stayed about five or six minutes. I do not know

Cross-examined by Mr. Slater. I am still in the service of Mr.
Davenport. I was not in his employment in 1901-02 when his offices
were in Piccadilly. I went there on one occasion on behalf of my
then principal. I did not at that period know Preston. Before I
saw him in Davenport's office I only met him on one occasion, when
I was sent by my principal over to Brixton Prison to get instruc-
tions. Preston and I were pretty friendly. We used to sit in Daven-
port's office and chat. On one occasion we went out to lunch together,
He then appeared to be badly off, and led me to believe that he had
no money to spare. He said, "We will go to a very cheap place,"
and we went to Snow's. He paid for the luncheon. I believe
Davenport was very hard up at the time. I was not working on
salary but on commission, and I am afraid that is in arrear. Busi-
ness was really being done by Davenport at that time. It consisted
mainly in letting furnished fats, and just about May and June I
think there were several going. I cannot tell what were the
average earnings of the office in July, Mr. Davenport took the
money himself and kept no books, and I had no control of the finan-
cial part of it. The earnings amounted to more than 5s, or 10s..
week. I know one week we let four flats, and if you let a flat there
is two guineas commission. That would be in May or June. In
July little or no business was done. I know that Mr. Davenport was
very much in arrear with his rent. I think he used to tell everyone
that. In July he was £20 in arrear, which was a considerable sum,
