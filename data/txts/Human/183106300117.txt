 1438.  THOMAS BURNS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of June , 1 candlestick, value 18d., the goods of Joseph Brock. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1439.  THOMAS BURNS was again indicted for stealing, on the 16th of June , 2 books, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Cotter.
 JANE COTTER. I am the wife of  Thomas Cotter- we live in Vere-street, Clare-market. On the 13th of June, Brock, the prosecutor in the former case, stopped the prisoner with these books, and I owned them.
   STEPHEN TWOHIG. I am an officer. Brock gave the prisoner in my charge, and said, in his presence, that he had offered to sell these books to him - the prisoner did not deny it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met two men in Clare-market, who had been to sea with me; they took me to a gin-shop, gave me something to drink, and in the market I bought these books of a man standing at the corner of Houghton-street - I went home for two hours, and on coming out again offered them for sale.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Three Months.
 1440.  MARY ANN CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of June , 1 shawl, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Richard Toomes.
   ELIZABETH TOOMES. I am the wife of Richard Toomes- he is a journeyman baker, and serves the prisoner with bread, on his master's account. She came to our room about six o'clock, and asked my husband if he had left a quartern loaf in the morning; he said he had - I left the room a short time, and on returning missed my shawl, which was safe when I left; she was gone.
   ELLEN DORIN. On the 13th of June, at six o'clock in the evening, I met Mrs. Clark, and she had a shawl - she sent me to pawn it at Matthews'; as I was better known there, she thought I might get more on it - I pawned it for 1s.
   LEONARD CLARE MATTHEWS. I have a shawl, pawned by Dorin - the prisoner offered it in pawn at first; we would not taken it in, as she was intoxicated.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had met the prosecutrix, who had sent her to pledge the shawl to procure some liquor; and had given her in charge to save herself from her husband's displeasure.
   ELIZABETH TOOMES. I never saw her till she came into the room - I had no gin with her; I saw her on Sunday morning - she offered me 10s. not to come here, and the Policeman 5s.; I lost the shawl on Monday, the 13th of June - I drank no gin that day with her, nor any liquor; I never saw her in my life till Sunday evening - my husband and her's were together then, and had a glass of beer, I believe, at the Crown, Golden-lane; I did not get drunk with her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Recorder.
 1441.  GEORGE RAIN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the goods of George Perkes , from his person.
 GEORGE PERKES. I am a  warehouseman, and live in Lad-lane. On Sunday, the 26th of June, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I was walking down the Cityroad, with my wife; a gentleman opposite gave me information that my pocket was picked - I felt, and missed my silk handkerchief, which was safe just before; I turned round, and saw three lads running, two close together, and one of the two dropped my handkerchief - I do not know which; I pursued the prisoner - he turned up Baldwin-street; I lost sight of him just while he turned the corner, and saw him again directly - he was stopped at the top of Baldwin-street, and I took him; I said, "You have picked my pocket;" he said, "Don't take me Sir, I have not had any thing to eat to-day;" I secured him, and gave him in charge - the handkerchief was afterwards delivered to me; it has my name in full on it.
   GEORGE GLASSOP. I am a comb-maker. On the 26th of June I was in the City-road, and saw Mr. Perkes there - I heard a cry of Stop thief! went into the middle of the road, twenty or thirty yards from Perkes, and saw the prisoner running away, and another in his company - I saw the prisoner drop the handkerchief, which I picked up; I continued the pursuit, and he was stopped by a City officer - I showed the handkerchief of Perkes, who claimed it.
 ROBERT SMITH. I am an officer of Broad-street ward. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and joined in the pursuit - there appeared to be two boys running; the prosecutor was in pursuit - I came up at the very moment he stopped the prisoner, and took him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was walking behind two boys, who threw the handkerchief down - I took it up; the gentleman ran after me, and I threw it down.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Life.
 Second London Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
