MORRIS then submitted on behalf of Tennant that there was no evidence to go to the Jury on Counts  1 and  5 in the indictment, Count  1 charging with unlawfully conspiring to print and cause to be printed sheets of copyright music without the consent of the proprietors and selling and publishing and disposing of the same knowing it to have been unlaw  fully printed, Count 5 being similar but setting out the names of the pro  prietors and the songs; he contended in all cases of conspiracy to defraud it must be proved that the conspirators must combine together to do something to alter the prosecutor's position; that it was dear that no copyright in this case had been obtained by the prisoners and by Section 3 of the Act of  1842 copies of pirated music did not belong to the owner of the copyright, and that the word " defraud " meant depriving of physical property, which it was clear there was no evidence of in this case; and that as to depriving the prosecutors of profits, they could not be deprived of what they had not got, although, he admitted, there was evidence as to the prisoners having conspired to inflict a civil wrong. The learned Judge held that it was unnecessary to prove that the conspiracy had been successful, the charge not being one of de  frauding, but of conspiracy to defraud, of which there was clear evidence to go to the Jury.
