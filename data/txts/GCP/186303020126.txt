ROSE, Mayor.
MR. DICKIE conducted the Prosecution.
SARAH HENNESSEY. I am the wife of John Hennessey, of Bexley Heat
-on Saturday afternoon, 14th February, about 5 o'clock, I was coming
Woolwich, and met the prisoner-he caught roughly hold of me by the
sboulder, and said, "What have you got ?"-I said, “Pray, do not hurt me
all I have is two shillings, I will give you one "-he said, “I will have it
all'-he threw me into the hedge, turned my pocket out, and took the
money and a new farthing-he said, “What have you got in your bag!"
I said, "My husband's work; pray, do not take that ; you will ruin me
he turned my clothes up over my head, used a dreadful expression with his
hands up my clothes, and said, “Hush ! if you make & noise I will til
you-he then ran away-I saw Woods coming up the hill, and told him
-- man they call Biswell was with him-they went in pursuit-I am sure
the prisoner is the man.
FREDERICK Woods. I am a painter, employed at the Royal Arsenal
Woolwich-on Saturday afternoon, 14th February, I was on Bexley Heute
where I live, and at the top of the hill met Mrs. Hennessey, who made
complaint, and described & man-I looked about, and saw a man running
down the road-I told my friend to follow him, and I ran in a different
direction-I called at the Foresters' Arms and gave information, and then
went down the hill, and saw a man going through the shrubs, stooping, who
answered the description-I went back to see if it was my friend, then came
back and saw the same man who I had seen in the wood, a hundred yards
before me—the landlord of the Foresters' Arms took him, and gave him
in custody--the prisoner is the man I saw in the wood.
Prisoner. Q. How far from the place where the woman was robbed w
I stopped ? A. About half a mile-I went in a circuitous route, thinking
you might take Wickham-lane.
ALFRED BISWELL. I am engaged in the Royal Arsenal, Woolwich
14th February, I met the prosecutrix crying, and in a very confused state
-I went in pursuit of a man who was running, but lost sight of him in
Wickham-lane, as he went down the road-I ballooed out, “Stop him!"
wore & billycock bat and a brown coat-I saw bim & fortnight afterward
among five or six others, and identified him.
WILLIAM BLAYDEN. I keep the Foresters' Arms, Wickham-lane-Iw
working in the garden, received information, and saw the prisoner in the
brushwood, 150 yards off; he seemed to be hiding-I watched him two
three minutes-he came out of the wood and ran down the rond-after be
had run about fifty yards I overtook him, and told him I wanted him-
stopped directly I spoke to him-I took him by the collar, and said, "I
want you to go back with me"-I took him, and met the other man, who
said that he answered the description, and banded him over to him-I asked
the prisoner wbat he had to say for himself-be said, “Nothing."
Prisoner. You asked me if I knew anything about the robbery, and I
said, "What robbery?" Witness. You did not
JOSEPH Moss (Policeman, 'R 151). I took the prisoner, and told him the
charge—be said that he knew nothing of it.
Prisoner's Defence. I think it is very hard for me to be brought here on
suspicion. I never was within half a mile of the place. No money
found on me, and I went into no house,
GUILTY.-Four Years' Penal Servitude.
