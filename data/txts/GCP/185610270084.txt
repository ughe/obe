MR. BODKIN submitted, that there was no case for the Jury, manslaughter
not being an offence to which there could be accessaries in point of law, this
was a doctrine clearly laid donon in Hale; the only question was, whether the
11 and 12 Vict., c. 46, sect. 1, made any alteration in this respect. (M
PAYNE, in opening the case, had relied upon this statute;) he (Mr. BODKIN) COM
tended that that statute only applied to those cases susceptible of an accessorial
charge; it created no new offence, and left the law as to manslaughter as #
esristed before. MR. RIBTON, on the same side, urged, supposing the 11 and 13
Vict. not in question at all, that there was no evidence of manslaughter; the
question did not arise here what offence the woman herself had been guilty of;
but he submitted, that there was no evidence of the death being attributable to
the act of the prisoner, nor any evidence of his being present at the time the
powder was taken, or at the death. MR. JUSTICE ERLE was of opinion, that
the matter involved some diffioulty, and would reserve the point upon the finding
of the Jury.
THE JURY found, in answer to questions separately submitted to them; 18,
that the prisoner bought the sulphate of potass, and gave it to his wife for the
purpose of proouring abortion; 2nd, that her death was caused by taking
part of that which he gave her; and, 3rd, that there was no proof of his being
present at the time she took the sulphate of potass.
