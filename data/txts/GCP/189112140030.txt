MR. A. GILL contended that the false pretences laid in the indictment being
that the defendant had delivered a ton of good coal, whereas according to the
evidence he had delivered a mixture composed of mineral substances other than
coal, but containing a small proportion of real coal, it was for the Jury to say
whether the alleged false pretences were made out. R. v. Foster (L.R., 2 Q.B.D.,
301); R. v. Suter (10 Cox, C.C., 577); R. v. Kerrigan (L. and C., 383).
A false representation respecting an alleged matter of definite fact, knowingly
made, though with regard to quality, was sufficient to constitute a false pretence
within the statute.
The Common SERJEANT, having consulted with the RECORDER, ruled that
the case must go to the Jury.
