   Cross-examined. Q.  What sort of a house is this, is it not a brothel? A.  Of course it is your business to say so, but I know nothing of the kind—I never heard it called a brothel—it is not one, I swear that—there are only two rooms, which are let to two soldiers and their wives, as far as I know—I do not know the two young women who came down stairs—I have a man and his wife lodging in my own house named Evans—they are creditable people. JOHN WELFORD. I am a gunner in the Royal Artillery, the prisoner is also a gunner—he belonged to the same room as me at, the time of this transaction—I missed my cap on the Monday morning in question, and found the prisoner's cap on my bed—I had not seen him that morning—there were two beds in the room—the prisoner slept in the other—he did not sleep at home on Sunday night—I had seen my cap safe on Sunday—I was not in Johnstone's house on Monday evening, I was on duty—this I is the cap I lost.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you known the prisoner some time? A.  Yes—he has borne an irreproachable character for honesty. SAMUEL WATTS. I am a constable. On Tuesday evening about six o'clock, the prosecutor came to me at a public-house, where I was attend ing an inquest on a convict, and applied to me to take a soldier in custody, who had rubbed him—he said he did not know his name, but he had got his cap, and had taken it to the Arsenal—I went to the Arsenal, and this cap was delivered to me—I have had it ever since—the prosecutor was intoxicated then, but seemed to have a knowledge of what he was saying—I took the prisoner up that night, with three others, for being disorderly in the street—he was intoxicated—the prosecutor saw him next morning and recognised him—5 s . 2 d . was found on the prisoner. NOT GUILTY.
