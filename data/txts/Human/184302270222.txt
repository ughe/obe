 1058. ELIZABETH ELWOOD and CATHERINE KELLY were in dicted for stealing, on the   28th of Feb ., 13 yards of printed cotton, value 10 s  ., the goods of Jesse Jones . THOMAS MURRELL. I am shopman to Jesse Jones, a linen-draper, in High-street, Islington. About one o'clock on the 28th of Feb. the prisoner Elwood was brought into our shop with this piece of printed cotton, contain ing thirteen yards—I had seen it safe about five minutes before in the lobby of the shop, between the two windows—it is my employer's—I had seen the prisoners standing about the door for nearly ten minutes. THOMAS GIBBS. I am a linendraper. I saw the prisoners and two other persons at the prosecutor's shop door—I watched, and saw a third person take this print, and give it to Kelly, and she gave it to Elwood—I ran across the road, and seized Elwood with the print—she dropped it from under her arm—I had been watching them for nearly an hour—they bad gone to different shops—I followed them from my own door in the City-road, I should think nearly a mile to the prosecutor's.
   ELWOOD *— GUILTY. Aged 20.
   KELLY *— GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Year .
 1059. JOHN BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the   lst of March , 100 cigars, value 10 s  ., the goods of Robert George Cattell . MARY ANN CATTELL. I am the wife of Robert George Cattell, tobacco nist, Goswell-street. On the 1st of March I missed a bundle of about 100 cigars from a board in the shop window—I had seen them safe about an hour and a half before—the prisoner was brought into my shop—I had seen him in my shop on the Monday before—the cigars have not been found. RICHARD POOL. I live in Whitecross-street. Between one and two o'clock on the 1st of March, I saw the prisoner come out of Mr. Cattell's shop with a bundle of cigars in his hand—he threw them into another boy's apron and they ran away—I am quite sure the prisoner is the boy who came out with the cigars in his hand—I ran to my master's door, and then I ran after the prisoner—I called, "Stop thief"—he turned round, swore at me, and asked what I wanted—I stuck to him—he tried to get away, and struck me—I fell down, and a gentleman took him.
   Prisoner.  He came hallooing out "Stop thief"—I turned and said "What do you want?"—he said, "I will tell you what I want," and then he struck me, and I hit him again. Witness.  I did not hit him, he hit me. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months .
 1060. ELIZABETH AYRES was indicted for stealing, on the   20th of Feb ., 1 pair of boots, value 4 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Thomas Searles . HENRY SIMMONS. I am foreman to Thomas Searles, a shoemaker, in Whitechapel. On the 20th of Feb. the prisoner came to purchase a pair of boots—after trying on several pairs, she got up, and said she would call the next day, as we had none to suit her—after she was gone I missed a pair of boots—I ran after her as far as Whitechapel church, and she had these boots on her left arm under her shawl—she dropped them in the mud, and said they must have been entangled with her umbrella—she had two umbrellas in her hand, but I could swear the boots were under her arm—she has borne a good character before.
