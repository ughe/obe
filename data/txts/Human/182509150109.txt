 1522.  PETER DUFFEY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , at St. Pancras , a watch, value 16 l.; a chain, value 3 l.; two seals, value 1 l.; a key, value 2 s., and a time-piece, value 3 l., the goods of Charles Wilkinson , in his dwelling-house.
   ELIZA WILKINSON. I am the wife of Charles Wilkinson, an attorney, who lives in Arlington-street, in the parish of St. Pancras. On the 1st of September I had been in the parlour all the morning, and went out of it about a quarter past eleven o'clock; I left this watch, the chain, the seals, key, and a time-piece, in the room - I went into the garden and heard an alarm from the servant - I came back and missed them - I went to the street-door and cried Stop thief! - the prisoner was brought back by some persons, and an officer with him - I saw the officer take the articles from him - they had been on the sideboard, and were worth 24 l.
   ANN HILL. I am the prosecutrix's servant. On the 1st of September I went out and left the door ajar, and the gate of the front garden open; I went to the next house but one, and returned in about three minutes; I met the prisoner in the passage coming from the parlour - I asked him what he wanted - he asked me the way to Somers' Town; I asked if he went in to ask his way to Somers' Town - he gave me no answer, but ran away directly - I gave an alarm and so did my mistress. I saw him taken up the street, and am sure he is the who person I had seen in the passage.
   WILLIAM LUMLEY. I am a coal-merchant, and live in the Hampstead-road. I was in Arlington-street, and saw the prisoner running towards me - he then turned off, and ran towards the brick-fields. I pursued, and said to the brickmaker's who had caught hold of him - "Don't hurt him; let us go before a Justice;" the prisoner said, he had thrown down the time-piece; a boy picked it up, and gave it to me. I then asked him where the watch was - he said, he had thrown that away. I said, "I will not take your word;" I put my hand into his pocket, and found it there - the officer afterwards took it out.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   ABRAHAM LORIMER. I am an officer, and took the prisoner in charge - I took this watch from his left hand waistcoat pocket - the time-piece was given to me.
 Prisoner's Defence (written.) I hitherto have borne an unblemished character, which I have witnesses to prove. I have no wish to palliate my offence, but merely to state, that I was taking a walk with a young man - having no money, and being out of employ, I was persuaded to attempt this robbery - an alarm being made he escaped.
 Two witnesses gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 18.
 Recommended to Mercy on account of his good character.
