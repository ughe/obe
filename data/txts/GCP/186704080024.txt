GABRIEL, Mayor.
NEW COURT.-Tuesday, April 9th, 1867,
Before Mr. Common Serjeant,
377. ANN MILLS (17), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin,
Mr. STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution.
HANNAH WORLAND. I am the wife of Frederick Worland, of 303,
High Street, Hoxton-on 19th February, about eight in the evening, the
prisoner came in and bought some eggs, which came to 4d.-she put down
a half-crown-I put it between my teeth, it bent, and I said, “You are
my prisoner; this is bad; you have been here before and given a bad five-
shilling piece to my husband"-she said nothing, and I gave her in charge
with the half-crown.
FREDERICK WORLAND. I am the husband of the last witness-on 5th
February the prisoner came for some eggs-I said that I had none-she
said, "I will have half a pound of that dripping, it looks very nice”-it
came to 4d.-she gave me a crown-I gave her the change, and after she
had gone I found it was bad—I gave it to the policeman-I had no other
in the house.
EDWARD PITTS (131 m). I took the prisoner on 19th February, she
said that she had never been to the shop before ; Mrs. Worland gave me
this half-crown and Mr. Worland the crown (produced).
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Mint-this crown
and half-crown are both bad.
GUILTY.-Confined Six Months.
378. ANN SMITH (45) was indicted for a like offence.
Messrs. COLERIDGE and STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution,
CHARLES PLESTED. I am assistant to Mr. Cox, & butcher, of 34,
Channel Street-on Friday, 22nd February, about four o'clock, the pri-
soner came in for a halfpennyworth of peas pudding-she gave me a shil-
ling-I put it into the till, there was no other shilling there I gave her
11jd. change, and she left-she came again three-quarters of an hour after-
wards, and I spoke to my master, who went to the till and took out a shil-
ling-a half-crown and a sixpence only were left in the till.
Prisoner. Q. Who was in the shop ? A. A woman, who gave me 6d.
-I said something to her about what a disfigurement there was on your face
- you did not pay me with a halfpenny, you gave me a shilling.
JOSEPH HARRADENCE. I am assistant to Mr. Cox-on the afternoon of
22nd February I was in the shop, the prisoner came in for a pound of
beef sausages, which came to 50,--she gave me a florin-I told her it was
bad, and she offered me another—I would not take it, but gave the florin
to Mr. Cox.
Prisoner. Q. Did your master turn me out of the shop ? A. We were
going to let you go for the florin, but when we found the shilling in the till
you were followed and given in custody.

Harradence gave me the bad florin, and I gave it to 438 A with a bad
shilling which I took from the till—the woman went out of the shop, but
the boy said something to me and I sent for her back.
