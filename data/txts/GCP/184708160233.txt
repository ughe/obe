WILLIAM GLADWIN (policeman R 122.) I saw the prisoner in Mrs.
Drouet's house---she gave me this shilling.
JAMES Watson. I keep the White Horse, at Charlton-on the 4th of
June, about nine o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my house and
asked for a pennyworth of spruce-I served bim-he tendered me a piece of
money purporting to be a shilling-I gave him a 6d. and 5d. in coppers, in
change-he left the house-my ostler stood behind me and said, “I have a
suspicion that this is a passer of bad money, he has been here before"-I
never put the shilling out of my hand-I sent my son and the ostler after the
prisoner for a mile, and I followed leisurely after them as far as a toll-gate-
in a quarter of an hour I saw the prisoner again-he had avoided the main
road I took him coming through the gate- I said, " Young man, you have
been to my house and passed a bad shilling for a pennyworth of spruce"-he
denied being at my house at all, and did not know me I took him back to
my house on the road he asked me to let him look at the shilling-I said,
“No, I have got the shilling, you must give a better account of yourself" -
he asked me to give it him back, and said he would give me a good one.
HENRY WADLOW (policeman R 267.) On the 4th of June I apprehended
the prisoner at Mr. Watson's-1 produce this bad shilling, which I received
from Mr. Watson-I found on the prisoner a shilling, a sixpence, and five-
pence in balfpence, all good-he said he had not been in the town more than
two hours.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. You questioned him about having
been at Mr. Watson's, and he said afterwards he had been there? A. Yes-
this case has been standing over for two months.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint.
These shillings are both counterfeit, and have been cast in one mould.
GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Four Months.
1979. JOHN WILLIAMS was indicted for embezzlement.
ROBERT WILLIAMS. I am cashier in the service of Thomas Wilkinson
Kershaw and another. They live at Greenwich; the prisoner was in their
service-on the 5th of July I made a communication to my master, and in
consequence of that six penny pieces, and twelve halfpence were marked
about four or five o'clock that evening I saw the prisoner serving a customer
-be called for change for a shilling, and farthings-I took him five pence,
one halfpenny, two farthings, and a sixpence-I did not see what he took
from the customer- I think it was three farthings-he put it into the till, and
went to another customer and sold her a yard of calico for 4fd.-be called for
change for sixpence-I saw him take 4 d. of the customer-I watched him-he
only put a portion of it into the till-I could not see how much, but I saw
part of it in his hand-he went to another part of the counter, put up some
hose, put his hand bebind him, and dropped some of the coppers from his
hand into his coat pocket-I think it was more than one piece-an officer
was sent for.
