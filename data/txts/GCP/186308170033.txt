CHEVALIER SCHAFER. I am director to the Austrian Consulate-General in
London--this is a one-gulden note, current in the Empire of Austria--there

Cross-examined. Q. Is there no signature wanted to the note ? A. The
signature of the director of the cash department is printed upon it; it is all
printed.
Mr. METCALFE submitted, that no offence was proved; that photography was
not contemplated by the Act upon which the indictment was framed (24 de 25
Vic. c. 98 sec. 19); the offence there pointed at was "engraving and making,"
that was, that something should be cut into the material employed, from which
impressions could be taken; the Legislature did not seem to have had their
attention called to the art of photography, which, probably, was not then con-
sidered an art that could be applied to such a purpose: this was clearly no en-
graving or making, it was a mere shadow, which without having recourse to a
further process was useless, and not capable of producing any impressions ; nor
could it be said to have been done by the prisoner, because the photographer had
not acted according to his instructions, but just the reverse, the prisoner's object
evidently being to use this photograph, not for the purpose of printing from, but to
get an engraving made from it; on these grounds, he submitted there was no case to
go to the Jury. MR. JUSTICE KEATING was of opinion that the case must go to the
Jury, and if they were of opinion that the prisoner did, in the words of the Act,
"in any wise make or cause to be made upon any plate whatever" the note in
question, with intent to counterfeit the Austrian currency, he should hold a plate
of glass to come within those words, and direct them that the offence was within
the Statute; but, as the case was a novel one, he would give the prisoner the
opportunity of raising the question in another place. Mr. METCALPE did not
address the Jury upon the facts. GUILTY. - Judgment respited.
