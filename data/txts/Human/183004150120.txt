 918.  ELIZABETH BUMPSTEAD was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 4 pewter pot, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Edward Butt , and 1 pewter pot, value 1s. 6d., the goods of James Scull.
 EDMUND BUTT. I  keep the  Black Cap public-house, Camden-town. Four of these pots are mine; I know nothing about the prisoner.
   JAMES SCULL. I am a publican. I keep the  Wheat Sheaf public-house. This pot is mine.
 WILLIAM JAMIESON. I am an officer. On Wednesday, the 24th of February, I saw the prisoner in Camden-town; I followed her across the fields - I came up and stopped her; I found these five pots in a stuff petticoat under her apron - she begged me to let her go.
 The prisoner made a long Defence, stating she was a widow with six children, and had not the means of providing for them.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. - Transported for Seven Years.
 919.  EDWARD WILLIAMS and THOMAS MORGAN were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of March , 24 stockings, value 15s., the goods of William Kipling.
   THOMAS FOGG. I am a Thames Police-constable. On the 18th of March I was in Rosemary-lane; I saw the two prisoners in company - Morgan was carrying this bundle under his apron; I asked what he had there - he said, some stockings, which a person employed him to sell for him - I told him to show me the person; I went with them down the lane, and then they said it was of no use telling a lie about it, and Williams added, "We stole them out of Cheapside."
   THOMAS STILL. I am in the employ of Mr. William Kipling, a hosier, in Cheapside. These are his property, and were missed, on the 18th of March, from a stool near the door, in our shop - there are twelve pairs tied up, and they have our mark on them; I did not see the prisoners.
 Morgan's Defence. I was in distress, through my master being a bankrupt.
 WILLIAMS -    GUILTY, Aged 18.
 MORGAN -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 920.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of March , 1 truck, value 3l., the goods of George Sanderson.
 GEORGE SANDERSON. I live at  Edmonton, this truck is mine. and was in the yard in front of my house on the evening of the 22nd of March, and the next morning at six o'clock I saw it in Bunhill-row; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   GEORGE SHEPHERD. I am a Police-officer. On the 23rd of March I was on duty, and saw the prisoner coming with this truck in a direction from Kingsland-road to Shoreditch; I asked where he was going to take it - he said it belonged to a man named Brown, in Tower-street, and he was sent down to a person named Francis at Edmonton, with fourteen pieces of wood - I did not think it was a correct story, and I took him into custody: in the course of a week I found the prosecutor.
 Prisoner. I did it through distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Confined Three Months.
 921.  JOHN WILTSHIRE and JOHN COOKE were indicted for stealing, on the 26th of March , 1 tea-kettle, value 4s. 10d., the goods of John Northfield.
   JAMES TAYLOR. I am a butcher, and live in the Cityroad. On the 26th of March I saw the two prisoners together, in Bath-street, not ten minutes walk from the prosecutor's - Wiltshire had an apron, and when I got close to them, I saw the spout of this tea-kettle; I passed them and saw them turn up a court - I went round Old-street, and saw them again; Cooke had then got the apron on, and the kettle open in his hand - I told the officer; I ran and took Cooke and Barrows took Wiltshire - I found these boots on Cooke, and a duplicate of an article pawned the same day.
   JAMES BARROWS. I am an officer. Taylor pointed out the two prisoners; Cooke had an apron on, and this kettle in his hand.
   ROBERT SANGSTER NORTHFIELD. I am the son of John Northfield, an ironmonger. This is his kettle, it had been outside his house in the City-road, on a stove by the window: it has my mark on it.
   JOHN BEDFORD. I was serjeant of the Police. The prisoners were brought in, and charged with stealing this tea-kettle; when I was taking them to Worship-street, I heard Cooke ask Wiltshire what he should say - he said he did not know: Cooke said, "Say we found it" - Wiltshire said No; Cooke then said, "Say it was given you to carry:" and that was the defence they made before the Magistrate.
 WILTSHIRE    GUILTY. Aged 21.
 COOKE    GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Three Months.
