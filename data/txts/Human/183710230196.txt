but when I accepted a bill, or any tiling, it was in both my names—I never concealed my second name with a view to do wrong to any one—I was once in the King's Bench, about twenty-five years ago—I was arrested for debt, taken to Horsemonger-lane, then removed to the King's Bench—I sold gin, and was sent back to Horsemonger-lane for one month, and came back to the King's Bench—I was arrested fifteen years ago, and paid the debt and costs, and then again I took the benefit of the Insolvent Debtors' Act—I was taken on a charge of stealing a loaf of sugar—the Alderman said he should hold us all to bail, but the Grand Jury were sitting—I went to Newgate, no bill was preferred, and I went out—I was in Clerk en well two days, for an assault, and taken for want of bail—this all amounts to one charge of larceny, and the rest debts and other transactions—I was outside this Court, Mr. Davis had been with Mr. Battle, and he pointed me out, and said, "There is Vincent," and gave me in charge for felony—I was taken that night before the Magistrate at Worship-street—Mr. Goddard, the solicitor, appeared for me—the Magistrate said I had better come up the next day, and I did, and was discharged. WILLIAM FITCH. I am a wholesale stationer, in Budge-row, in partner ship with John Imray. I know Bonnell—about the 6th of August he came to my counting-house, and stated that he wanted to purchase a miscellaneous lot of goods, such as I supplied; that he was occasionally travelling about the country, and could sell such goods as I supplied; and if I would furnish him with 10 l . worth of articles he would pay me down half—he gave me a reference to Mr. Good and my partner called on him—he was supplied with nearly 10 l . worth—about the 11th of August he came again, and paid 5 l ., and gave me a further order of about 12 l . worth of goods—his man, Clark, came and fetched them—I understood that he had a cart wait ing in Smithfield—Bonnell said they would be sent for as usual by the man Clark—Clark came for them and brought two or three baskets—there were day-books, memorandum books, and quills—that was the first order—the second was similar—he said the first goods did so well that he would give me a further order—that was about 12 l .—I never saw him again—about the 17th Clark came and brought me this letter (No. 2)—the goods mentioned in it were rather above 2 l .—Clark took them away—two days after that he came again—I do not know that he brought a letter that time—from
