EDWARD HARRISON TAYLOR. I am Clerk to the Guardians of the Wandsworth and Clapham Union—the prisoner was one of the Relieving Officers for the district of Wandsworth—he had been so since November, 1883—I have the minute book here containing his appointment—he was furnished with the "Relieving Officers' Manual," containing the orders of the Poor Law Board of 24th July, 1847, and the order of the 14th December, 1852.
   Cross-examined.  If the officer gives relief to a person who the Guardians think ought not to be relieved, the account would be submitted to the District Auditor, and if he thinks fit the Local Government Board would surcharge him—the Relieving Officer has a certain discretion in the matter of relief—the Guardians are no parties to this prosecution. SARAH WRIGHT. I am the wife of John Wright, a carman, of 9, Iron Mill Road, Wandsworth—we had a little boy named Edward, aged 17 months—he had been under the doctor—on 1st August he was taken suddenly ill with bad diarrhoea and sickness—be got better, and went on pretty well till the Saturday following; about 9 o'clock that morning I went to the prisoner and said "Please, Sir, will you give me an order for my child, he is very bad indeed with diarrhoea, and might be dead before I get back"—he said "Go and find your husband"—I said "I do not know where to find him, he is away at work"—he was at work that week, but he had been out of work—his wages were 22 s . a week if he went on Sunday morning—the prisoner did not give me any order, he kept saying "Go and find your husband"—he never had given any order to my husband, he always gave it to me—I have nine children now, I had ten with this one—some of them were out in the street—all of them live at home—the eldest is not 14, the next is a little over 12, the next not 11, and the next not 9—my husband leaves home for work at half-past 4 o'clock in the morning, and returns between 7 and 8 o'clock—the child died at 3 o'clock on the Sunday morning—I had no money to pay a doctor—my husband would bring his wages home on Saturday night.
   Cross-examined.  My eldest child earns 4 s . a week, which he brings home to assist in the maintenance of the family—the deceased got a little better on the Saturday morning after I returned from seeing the prisoner, and he was better when my husband returned, between 7 and 8 o'clock—he brought home 1 l . 1 s . on the Saturday night, which he had earned, and the eldest boy brought home his 4 s .—the child was asleep when my hus band returned—I put him to bed with the others, and he went to sleep about 8 o'clock; the diarrhoea began again just after 12 o'clock—he had always been a delicate child from six weeks old—I did not send out my husband or boy for a doctor, we did not think he was so bad; we did not think there was any danger—there was no doctor very near—we occupy the whole house, and pay 6 s . a week, we don't let any of it—my husband had been out of work for a month—I knew I should have 25 s . that night, but I owed a lot out of it—it was no use going to Dr. Nicholson without an order; I have always had orders before—I had no money to pay for a doctor.
