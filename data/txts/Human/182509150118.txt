THOMAS TROTT re-examined. Q. What was the charge you gave to the prisoner? A. To take care of every thing, and work up all the stuff, and not let a chip be taken away, as I would prosecute any one who did. I told him I meant that nothing should be taken away, and I would prosecute him as well as another who took it.
 MR. PHILLIPS to JOSEPH CLIMPSON. Q. Were these little bits of any use but as fire-wood? A. No.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Were they not of use to Mr. Trott? A. Yes, as fire-wood. I would not give a halfpenny for three of them. I never saw the prisoner do it but once; there was no person present but him and me.
   EDWARD KEARNEY. I was employed by Mr. Trott to watch his premises, the week after Russell left. I had had particular orders for two years if I saw any body take any thing away to let him know - this was generally known on the premises.
 Cross-examined. Q. Would you not have lit your pipe with a chip? A. I do not know. I had orders to take the chips home to Trott's, as fire-wood.
   JOHN PROVAN. I was employed by the prisoner on these buildings for about a month; I have heard Mr. Trott mention in Russell's hearing, that no chips or any thing was to be taken away.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1543.  RICHARD TAYLOR was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM GIBBS ROGERS. I live in Church-street, Soho- the prisoner was my porter- he was intrusted to receive money for me. On the 28th of July, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning, I sent him with a cheque for 17 l., on the house of Fry and Co., to pay an acceptance of mine for 15 l., to Mr. Evans, a gold refiner, in Little Love-lane, and bring me the change. He was to act at his own discretion, either to get the cheque changed by Mr. Evans, or at the bankers. In the course of the day I received a letter, but I do not know who wrote it. I did not see him again till he was in custody, about six weeks afterwards.
   JOSEPH THACKERY. I am a clerk in Messrs. Fry and Chapman's. I paid the cheque for 17 l. on the 28th of July. I do not remember the person of the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1544.  JOHN MITCHELL was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , a lace veil, value 8 s., the goods of Jane Elizabeth Wilson , from her person.
 JANE ELIZABETH WILSON. I am  single, and live in Antelope-gardens, Holywell-mount. On the 6th of September, between one and two o'clock in the morning, I was returning from Smithfield with two young men and three young women - between Cloth-fair and Bartholomew Church, three young men passed me; the prisoner then passed me, and I felt my veil torn from my head; I turned round and said "Stop thief! I am robbed;" the officer then had hold of him, and saw him take it. I have never seen it since.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What colour was it? A. Black. I had been to Holborn, to a singing party; I had drank something;  Emma Weightman was with me, and was as sober as I was; I did not see who took it; I had passed the evening at the King's Arms, between Hatton-garden and Ely-place; we had had three sixpenny glasses of gin and water - it was the first day of the Fair.
   GEORGE PROCTOR. I saw the prisoner at two o'clock that morning, between Bartholomew Church and Cloth-fair; he snatched something from this lady's bonnet, but I did not see what; she turned round and said "I am robbed;" I said "I know you are;" I pursued the prisoner and took him about twenty yards off - he had nothing - there were three others with him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you ever ask one Jones if the business could be compromised at all? A. No; I never said I should get 10 l. if this man could be convicted.
 EMMA WEIGHTMAN. I was with the prosecutrix; she had a veil on her bonnet; I did not see the prisoner take it, because there was a crowd, but I saw him tucking a black lace veil into his bosom; I heard Proctor say "I have got the man."
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. I go to service in general, but am out of a situation. I live in Fore-street; I had been at the King's Arms with Miss Wilson - I never told Mrs. Hoyle that the veil was a white one; I slept at a friend's that night; I did not return home till ten o'clock in the morning; I was sober when I went home - my landlady did not turn me out on account of my drunkenness. I never told her I should get 5 l. by it; I state, positively, I never had any conversation with Proctor, in the presence of Mrs. Jones, on this business.
