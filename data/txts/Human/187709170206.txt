NEW COURT .— Thursday, September  27 th , 1877.
   Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 731. EMILY CHARLTON  (33), EDWARD CHARLTON  (37), and HENRY CLAYTON  (40) , Stealing a chest of drawers and other articles, the property of Arthur Leonard Seeley , in his dwelling-house.
   MR. C. MATHEWS conducted the Prosecution; MR. WARNER SLEIGH appeared for Emily and Edward Charlton, and MR. FULTON for Clayton. MART SEELEY. I am the wife of Arthur Leonard Seeley, and now live at 15, Morton Street, Clerkenwell—in June last, I had words with my husband and he went away in a bit of temper, I remained at 2, Hornsey Park Road, where we were then living—Mr. and Mrs. Charlton lived at No. 3—when my husband left me I became intimate with Mrs. Charlton, who encouraged me to drink, and that went on till 5th July, when I removed to 86, Euston Road, by Mrs. Charlton's advice to avoid my husband—Mrs. Charlton ordered the vans and told me to "shut up" as I had nothing to do with it, and she would not allow me to say anything—on 4th July, I was very intoxicated and went to bed all the afternoon, I awoke between 3 and 4 o'clock next morning—I had not then quite recovered—I heard something moving about at the back of the house—I went into the back-room, and saw Clayton with a chest of drawers on his back—he put them over the wall to Mr. Charlton, and Mr. Charlton senior—they had all been packed full of goods ready for removal, by myself and a servant—afterwards Mrs. Charlton came upstairs and said "Make haste, the van is come"—I went to look, and saw a van—I said "How did you get into the house?"—she said "By the window"—I said "I fastened the window"—she said "Oh, we managed it," and when I went downstairs the kitchen window was wide open—I had employed Clayton to remove the things—the removal then took place, Mrs. Charlton taking part; I was about the house till the vans were loaded, when I and Mrs. Charlton got on the top of one to go to Euston Road—we went in a roundabout away to avoid anyone seeing me who knew me—Mrs. Charlton directed everything—she got down before we got to the Euston Road, because a man was following the vans, and she said "I don't know you "—we got there about 7 o'clock; I did not look over the things till a day or two afterwards, when I missed the things mentioned in this list ( pro  duced); afterwards Mrs. Charlton visited me and I asked her if she had any of the things—she said "I have not"—my husband joined me at Euston Road, and on 10th July, Mrs. Charlton came again—my husband was at home then—I begged her to give up the things"—I said "I am sure you had the things"—she said "I had not—I said "My husband will prosecute you unless you bring them back"—she said she had nothing belonging to me at first, and then she said "Yes, I will give them up"—I gave her a dress, and paid her 30 s . for her trouble in removing the goods—I did not pay Mr. Charlton anything—I did not engage him; I paid Clayton 7 s . 6 d .—at the time of the removal I was possessed of 10 l .
