Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. How long had Newitt been in
Pickford's service? A. Something under twelve months—I believe my
masters would not have taken him unless he had a good Character.
EDWARD PARL8. On the morning of 23rd Feb. there were white and
black peas in Messrs. Pickford's loft—I have compared the bag of peas produced
with them, and have not the slightest doubt about them—there is a peculiarity
about them—a few white ones have got among them—the black peas are
peculiarly large, and much darker than the generality—the black peas in the
loft are separated from the white by a partition—a quantity of white peas
got among the black ones, but no black ones had got into the white—that vu
the case in the bulk, and is so with the peas in the bag also.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Anybody wanting peas could have
helped themselves to the white, without having any black ones? A. Yes—
we got them from Smith, Fine, and Smith's, of Upper Thames-street—I
not know that a hundred other people have the same black peas—other pwple
may have some—I will not swear, but I do not know that they are sold to
Other people besides us.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. The white peas are not peculiar? A. No, but
the black are, I have never seen any like them before—the prisoner no
business to have any peas in his cart—the peas are the property of Joseph
Baxendale, and others.
MR. DOANE. How many partners are there? A. Two besides Mi.
Baxendale.
