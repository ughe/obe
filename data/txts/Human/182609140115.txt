1566. FRANCES NEWMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of July , 2 sheets, value 4s, the goods of John Burton , in a lodging room in his dwelling-house.
   SARAH BURTON. I am the wife of John Burton, of Tothill-street. I let a lodging to the prisoner in Baker-street; that house is all let out - it is not our dwelling-house. NOT GUILTY.
 1574.  MARY WILEY was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , 5 pillows, value 16s.; 1 blanket, value 4s.; 3 sheets, value 14s.; 1 counterpane, value 6s.; 2 bolsters, value 12s.; 1 bed-tick, value 6s., and 2 flat irons, value 2s., the goods of Mary Stote , widow , in a lodging room.
 MARY STOTE. I am a widow, and live in  Eagle-court, St. John's-lane. I let the prisoner a room on the second-floor, furnished, on the 6th of May, at 4s. 6d. a week; I had great difficulty to get my rent - she owes me 9s. now; on the 4th of September a person gave me information; I went to the prisoner's door, but she would not let me in; I sent for an officer, she did not refuse to let him in; I said, "Good God! have you been robbing me, with my family?" the officer found some duplicates - she admitted she had robbed me, but refused at first to tell where the property was, but she told the officer afterwards; she works at her needle; a person cohabited with her as her husband, but I let the room to her, and did not see him; the next day she told me her husband paid the rent; he was with her all the time she was there.
   CHARLES CROOK. I am shopman to Mr. Chapman, a pawnbroker, in St. James-street. I have one blanket and one sheet, pawned by the prisoner on the 16th and 17th of June.
   RICHARD PERRY. I am shopman to Mr. Brown, a pawnbroker, I have a blanket and a pillow-case pawned by the prisoner, I believe, on the 7th of July.
   JOHN JONES. I live in Middleton-street, Spa-fields, with Mr. Blackburn, a pawnbroker. I have a bolster and pillow - I cannot say whether they were pawned by the prisoner or her mother, as both were in the habit of pawning goods with me.
   WILLIAM BRADFORD. I am shopman to Mr. Bailey, a pawnbroker, Aldersgate-street. I have two pillows and a bed, pawned on the 15th of July, by a woman, but I do not know who.
   ROBERT CLARK. I am a constable. I took the prisoner - she said if we would stop till Saturday she would send the property back again; I asked her where the duplicates were; she said she gave them to her sister; I took her, and she said she had them of the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. She said, if she had the money, that was all she wanted, and she had it for what they were pawned for.
   JOHN MCTYRE. I am a carver, and live in Tash-street, Gray's-inn-lane. On the evening the prisoner was taken, word was sent to me, that it was for things she had pawned through distress, and if I could in any way assist her, she would be obliged; I went and saw Mrs. Stote - I told her that I could not make up the money to release the things that night, but I could the following morning, and I paid 1l. 12s. 6d. to a man named Ward, who lodged there, with this proviso, that I was to pay 16s. more.
 COURT. Q. Did Mrs. Stote agree, if you paid this money. to forego the prosecution? A. Yes - and I paid 1l. 12s. 6d. to the man, but the Magistrate ordered him to pay it back again.
 MARY STOTE re-examined. Q. Did you ever agree to take this money? A. No, never in my life - I never received a single farthing.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Six Months.
 1575.  THOMAS CUNNINGHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 6 sets of castors, value 3l.; 4 planes, value 8s.; I saw, value 3s.; and 1 hand-vice, value 2s., the goods of Charles Kensell , his master.
 CHARLES KENSELL. I am a  cabinet-maker, and live in Temple-street, York-place. The prisoner had been seven years in my employ - he was at work for me on the 1st of August; he got castors in my name, unknown to me - I knew nothing of it till the clerk came and told me of it; they had not been in my possession - he had pawned them before he got home - I have not paid for them - the duplicate of the planes were found afterwards; he had them to work with.
   EDWARD BELL. I am in the employ of Mr. Reynolds, an ironmonger, of Compton-street. The prisoner came for a set of castors on the 1st of August, in the name of Mr. Kensell, which I served him with, and booked them to Mr. Kensell.
   HERRY STOWELL. I am an officer. I took the prisoner into custody; he denied knowing any thing about the castors, but I told him he did - and asked him for the duplicates - he said, he had none - but in going along he produced one for the castors - I then found others for the different tools, which he said, he got from Mr. Reynolds; he said he had been drinking some time, and did not know what he had been about.
   GEORGE CHAPMAN. I am shopman to Mr. Bailey, pawnbroker, Mary-le-bone-street - I have four castors pawned by the prisoner on the 14th of July.
   CHRISTOPHER GRAVES. I am a pawnbroker. I have two sets of castors, two planes, and a saw - one set of castors I took in of the prisoner - I did not take the rest in - they are pawned in the names of Cox, Wilson, and Carter.
   EDWARD STOKES. I am shopman to Mr. Ross, a pawnbroker - I have two planes, but the person who took them in has left me.
   HENRY KING. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Tottenham-court-road - I have two sets of castors; one set was pawned by the prisoner on the 1st of August - the other on the 14th of July; but I did not take it in.
     NOT GUILTY.
