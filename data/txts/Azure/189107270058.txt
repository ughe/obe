surgeon at Guy's Hospital—the deceased, Mary Tobin, was admitted
there on Sunday afternoon, the 28th June—she was rather in a dazed
condition, as if suffering from the combined effects of drink and head
injury—she was able to walk—she had a scalp-wound on the loft side, it
went down to the bone—it was a contused wound—there was a good
deal of bruising—she remained in an unconscious condition till 2nd July,
when she died—I made a post-mortem—the cause Of death was extrava-
sation of blood and laceration of the brain tissue on tho right side of the
base of the brain—that was the result of the injury—that might have
been caused either by a heavy blow or a fall—I rather gave preference
to a fall, because of the extensive injury to the brain tissue; I thought
the weight of the body was more likely to cause that than a blow—a fall
from a blow would have done it; It was the result of considerable
violence—there were no other bruises and no fracture; it could not have
