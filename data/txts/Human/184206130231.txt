   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not tell the prosecutrix at the time you sold her the furniture that you were to leave the room on the Tuesday? A.  Not that I know of—I did leave the room the following week—I went with you to the pawnbroker's the week before, when you removed the things. JOHN MOORE. I am a pawnbroker, at Woolwich. The prosecutrix purchased the articles she has enumerated of me some time since—I be lieve the prisoner was with her at the time—my young man sold the arti cles—some of them were afterwards brought back by Jennings, and the prisoner, and I took back of the prisoner the bed, carpet, counterpane, and blanket in exchange for other articles—I cannot exactly recollect for what, but believe it was the watch now produced—I sold this watch to the prisoner, I believe, but I have had so many transactions I will not swear that this is the watch—I knew the things again when they were returned—I did not express any surprise at their being returned so recently—he did not offer to pledge them, but sold them, and I gave him other articles in exchange. JAMES PARRY( police-sergeant. ) I took the prisoner into custody at Deal, on the 5th of June—he said he knew nothing about it—on our pas sage up in the steam-boat, he said he had had the 1 l . back from Jennings—I found this watch, three rings, a gold seal, and chain, a knife, four du plicates, and 1 l . 7 s . on him—the duplicates do not relate to these articles. HARRIET HOLDER re-examined.  The prisoner never told me that he in tended to pawn or sell the articles—he never asked my leave to do so, nor ever told me that he had done so—I was quite in ignorance of it—I dis covered it by going to Woolwich, where I bought the things—he did not come forward on the day appointed for the wedding, and I went to where Jennings lived, and found the things were gone—I traced a washing-stand to a pawnbroker's in Artillery-place which the prisoner had sold, and I went to Moore's—the things were disposed of on the 19th of February—I had purchased them on the 10th—he told me in February that he should put off the marriage for six months—he did not tell me so until after the things were bought—it was on the 12th of May that he borrowed the 30 s . of me—I knew nothing of the things being disposed of then—he never told me he had disposed of them—he said he would go into the country to get some property, and on the 1st of June the wedding was to take place—I supposed that the things were all safe in the prisoner's care at Jennings'—I did not know that Jennings was gone.
