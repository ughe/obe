629. FREDERICK LAWRIE (21), was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. POLAND and LAW conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES PECKHAM. I am the landlord at the North Pole public-house
Greenwich—the prisoner came to my house about the first week in April
he called for a half-quartern of gin, which was 24d.--I served him, and he
put a half-sovereign across the counter into my hand—I detected it, and
said directly, "This is a counterfeit"-he told me he did not know it, and
took from his pocket a port-monnaie containg nearly 208. in silver-he took
out a good sixpence to pay me I have not got the half-sovereign-I kept
it about two months, and cautioned all my neighbours, but I have lost it-
there was no doubt about it's being a bad one-I showed it to a constable
the same night.
Cross-examined by MR. COOPER. Q. Did you know the prisoner 4. I
had never seen him before-he has not been to my house since I did not
test the half-sovereign in any way ; but being used to take money I knew it
was a counterfeit directly-the prisoner told me he was a window-sash
maker, and gave me his address in Walworth-lane.
THOMAS HAGGER. I keep the Rose public-house at New Cross-on Satur
day evening, 8th June, at a little after 9, the prisoner came in and asked for
a pint of stout, which came to 4d.--I served him, and he gave me a half-
crown in payment-I took it up, saw it was bad, and told him so, and then
broke it in three pieces the prisoner then made use of rather abusive lan-
guage to me for suspecting him of dishonesty, and with great persuasion I
let him go- I asked him if he had any more, and he pulled from his pocket
some coppers and some silver-I took out a sixpence, and gave him 20.
change-I kept the pieces of the half-crown, and gave them on the Monday
to the constable Snell.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you known the prisoner before? A. I never saw
him, to my knowledge—the Rose is a well-known public house, and fre-
quented by very respectable people-policemen come there sometimes as
customers—there is another public house at some little distance from mine
--the policeman has the half-crown.
