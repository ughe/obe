FRANK BRADLEY. I was present when Mr. Barron gave a man a bill-
head with Ross's name and address-he said that he had no room for the
50 staves—I believe he said the landlord was going to lock the gate up,
and would not let him put them there, and he gave him a note to take
them somewhere else, I don't know the name, and he gave him a bill-
head-there appeared no concealment whatever I am a cabinet maker;
I should not give as much as 31. 108. for the staves, if this one is a
sample-I would not give 18. each for them.
Cross-examined. I have seen Barron several times since January last-
I met him in Curtain Road and in a yard on several occasions during last
year-he told me he was not living at home because he could not ineet
his creditors—he was living in Leman Street and Cable Street during
1884.
PAUL GREENWOOD. I am a timber merchant, and buy a good deal of
timber-on December 13th, 1883, two men came to me offering to sell
some staves--I would not be bothered with so few, and I think I referred
them to Barron, but I do not remember there was & van-I know Ourtain
Road and Leonard Road- I have been there 25 years-wood is certainly
sold from vans there, and used to be much more than now-thousands of
pounds' worth of wood are sold from vans there, and no invoices are
taken- I have bought English timber, which is sent up from the country,
and the agents sell it where they can-I saw nothing improper in it at all
-the prisoner did not abscond; I have seen him in Curtain Road going
about in the usual way—I do not know Cable Street.
