Q. Was not the prisoner's expression, "The child is my own, I am its
father, and will do for it as I always have done !" A. No-I will not swear
it-he said, "The child is mine, and no one shall touch it; I will do for it"
-I do not know that he appeared incoherent and wild- I did not hear
him say anything about starvation, and how funny it was to see people's jaws
work-I did not hear a good deal he said, I dare say, as I was talking to my
friend.
DANIEL Donovan. I am a labourer, and live in Griffiths'-rents, Tooley.
street-I was at Havill's public-house, on the 24th of Oct., between eight
and nine o'clock-I went into the tap-room, near the fire--I have known the
prisoner at least fifteen or sixteen years- I went to school with bim-I was
in the room before he came in-I saw him rather excited in his mind, rather
irritated in his mind and spirits I was in the tap-room writing a letter before
he came in-he came in, called for a pint of ale, sat down in the box where I
was, and poured me out a glass of ale-I drank it, and he drank a glass himself
-I asked him to drink out of my pot, which he did-he remained a few mi-
putes, then went to another company I still remained writing the mother-
in-law came in and abused him, called him Greenacre, a murderer, and
names not fit to be used--the wife came in afterwards with the young infant
in her arms, put it into the prisoner's lap, and said, “There is your child,
take care of it, I will have no more to do with it; I will work, and get my owo
living"-she then turned round to him, and struck him several blows on the
face and head-the prisoner began to cry, and said he was in the power of
the law, and did not wish to strike her, he had come out on two 201. bail,
and what would the men do who had bailed him out ?-the publican came and
turned the mother-in-law and wife out-the prisoner still remained in the
tap-room, with the child in his arms, for a quarter of an hour or more-
younger sister of his wife's came in, and he gave the child to her-it seemed
to be crying-the mother-in-law then came in, and began to blow the girl sp
for taking the infant-she gave it back to the prisoner--the mother-in-law came
to the table where I was, and took up a quart pot to strike the prisoner
it was taken out of her hand by a witness-the publican came, and turned
both the women out the child remained with the prisoner a woman in the
company said, "Give me the child, I will take care of it"-the prisoner
replied, he would take care of it, he had always done for the child, and would
do so now--he then rose from the table with it in his arms, and said to the
publican, “I want to go out, and these people won't let me go away"-
some persons had tried to prevent his going out-the publican desired them
to let him go out-he went out of the side-door with the child in his arms
and returned into the tap-room in a moment or so, with the child in his arms,
and remained in the tap-room four or five minutes-he then went to the bar
out of the tap-room, and stood outside the bar with the child in his arms-
remained in the tap-room-be returned to the tap-room & minute or two, and
returned to the bar again with the child--the tap-room door bad closed, and
I heard a cry outside that the child was murdered, but I still remained in
the tap-room.
