1670. JOHN FORRESTER, burglariously breaking and entering the
dvelling-house of James Clark, and stealing therein 1 eruet-stand, and other
articles, value 208. : his goods.
JANEs CLARKE. I now live in Brook-street, Rateliffe-] formerly lived in
Bermondsey, and kept the house. On 23rd Ang. I came down-stairs, about
six o'clock in the morning; I am sure it was before six-I was the first pery
so0 that came down-I found the window adjoining the shop wide open, and
missed the eruet-stand from the sideboard, the snuffers and tray, and half a
pound of tobaceo-this is the cruet-stand (produced)- saw it safe the night
belore--my wife and sister were up after me; they are not here.
JOHN DAY (policeman, M 187). On Friday morning, 23rd Aug., between
two and three o'eloek, I was near Folly-bridge, Dockhead, and hearing a dog
bark at the back of the prosecutor's premises, I went into the adjoining yard,
and saw two persons-one was attempting to escape over the wall, and the
other, who was the prisoner, was assisting him over-I asked what business
they bad there-they said they came there to sleep in a cartthere were
several carts in the yard- took them into custody but the other got away-I
kept the prisoner, and took him to the station- found on him some tobacco
loose in his pocket, a tobacco-pouch, 9d. in coppery a comb, and a quantity
of lucifer matehes.
TINOTBY SPARROW (policeman, M 45). About half-past six o'clock on
the morning of the 23rd, I received information from Mr. Clark, and examined
bis premises-they had been entered by the back-parlour window saw
marks on the wall where persons had got over] found this cruet-stand on
the adjoining premises in the corner of the yard close by where I saw the
marks-] have found the snuffer-tray, and half a pound of tobacco.
Prisoner's Defence. I went into this place, to sleep in the cart; the
tobacco I had on me I bought with some money I earned during the day; I
know nothing of the robbery.
NOT GUILTY.
1671. JOHN THOMPSON, feloniously breaking and entering the dwell-
ing-house of William M'Lellan, and stealing 1 handkerchief, value 28. his
goods.
WILLIAM MLBLLAN. I live at 26, Sutton-street, Lambetheth occupier
lives in it, and I rent a lodging of him. On Friday, 16th Aug., I got up a
little before six o'clock in the morning, opened the shutters of the room I
slept in, and then left my room for three or four minutes-I left my wife in
the room-when I came back I went to bed again, until near eight-I then got
up, and missed my coat, trowsers, neckhandkerchief, and handkerchief from
the chair-] had shut the door after me, but not locked it-I found it shut at
eight-this is my handkerchief (produced) know it by a darn in the cor-
ner-it was in the pocket of my coat which was taken.
FRANCIS IRBLAND (policeman, L 119). I took the prisoner into custody,
on 19th Aug., and found this handkerchief in his coat pocket asked him
were be got it-he said he bought it about eighteen months ago, over the
water.
Prisoner's Defence. I bought it in Petticoat-lane.
GUITY of Stealing the handkerchief.
