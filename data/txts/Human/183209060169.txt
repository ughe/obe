   SUSAN HIGGINS. I am an unfortunate girl, and live at No.7, Collingwood-street. On the 19th of August, about two or three o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came there, and the prosecutor with him - the prosecutor laid on the sofa, and went to sleep; the prisoner said, "I will go and see what money my friend has got;" he put his hands into both his waistcoat pockets, and took some money out -I saw some silver, but I did not see any gold; he put it into his trousers pocket - the prisoner then wanted to go up stairs with my friend Miss Slater, but she would not have any thing to say to him; he then went out, brought in two Policemen and the watchman, and said that Miss Slater had robbed him; the prisoner took off his coat, and threw it down at one end of the room; the watchman took it, and found in it an old silk handkerchief with a sovereign and a half tied up in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. What house is this? A. A lodging house - there were myself, Slater, and Mr. Farley the landlord there, besides the prisoner and the prosecutor; they did not come to see us - they came, and wanted something to drink; the prisoner went up stairs with Slater, but he did not stop - we were all there when he took the money from the prosecutor's pocket; we did not say any thing to him; I saw some silver in one of his hands - he turned one of the prosecutor's pockets out.
   WILLIAM FARLEY. I was in the room, and saw the money found.
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. A painter and glazier - I let the house in different apartments not to young women in particular - there was one man in the house named Size; the prisoner came to see him - I do not know what trade Size is; I believe these young women see gentlemen, but when I found what house it was I left it altogether - I was not there when the prosecutor and the prisoner came; I came down when the prisoner was making a great disturbance, and begged him to be quiet - I saw a sovereign and a half found in the coat.
   JOHN YABSLEY. I am a private watchman. I went, and found the sovereign and a half in the handkerchief in the coat.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you search the coat? A. In the parlour of the house - it is a brothel; when I said I should search the prisoner, he took some silver out of his trousers pocket, and it laid on the table; there were 8s. or 10s., there was no Policeman there then.
   ISAAC GOUGEON(Police-constable N 27). I took the prisoner, and found on him 11s. 6d.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the prosecutor - he knew perfectly well where he was going, for he named it to me; these two girls and this man were in the room - one of the girls went up stairs, and I after her, leaving the prosecutor on the sofa asleep; I then missed a sovereign and a half from my pocket, and being in liquor, I suppose I took it out of my fob and tied it in the handkerchief - I then called in the Policeman; we went into the parlour - the prosecutor was still asleep; the watchman came in, awoke him, and asked if he was robbed - he said No; he said,"You had better feel," and then he said, "I have lost 1l. 15s." I said,"Well, I will be searched," and I pulled off my coat; the girls said, "Serve him out;" they then beat me, and gave me a black eye.    NOT GUILTY.
 2158.  JOSHUA CHAMBERLAIN and PHILIP CHAMBERLAIN were indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of July , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of John Carter.
   CHARLES BREWER. I take care of horses in a hackneycoach-yard; this coat is Mr. John Carter's property - he was taken ill on the Sunday morning, and I drove his coach - this coat was on the box; I had the care of it - one of the horses slipped down at the corner of Brick-lane, Church-street, between two and three o'clock in the morning - I got down to get the horse up, and when I looked for the coat, it was gone.
   JAMES WALTON(Police-constable H 85). I was in Wentworth-street, and saw Philip Chamberlain come up a little court - he then turned, and went back; I thought something was wrong; I ran up, and saw him speaking to his brother Joshua, who then had a large coat on, and a stick in his hand - there was at that time a noise of some labourers going out to work; I turned to look at them, and the two prisoners were gone, but I knew them both; and soon after I saw Joshua come and speak to his brother without the great coat; two officers came up at the time, and I told them of it.
   CHARLES RANDALL(Police-constable H 68). Walton informed me of this; I went and took Joshua Chamberlain in Essex-street, and found this coat in a dust-hole, up a court, not far from where he had been standing, and this stick not far from him: the other officer took the other prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to).
   Joshua Chamberlain's Defence. I got up at a quarter past five o'clock to go to work; the gates of the yard were shut - I was waiting for some of the workmen, and this officer took me.
 Philip Chamberlain's Defence. I came out with my brother - the gates were not open - I said, "We will go and call our shopmates."
 J. CHAMBERLAIN -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 P. CHAMBERLAIN -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 2159.  ELIZABETH MYNOR was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 1 printed book, value 12s., the goods of Peter Wright.
   JOHN POYNTON. I am son-in-law of Mr. Peter Wright, bookseller, High-street, St. Giles'; I was at his shop accidentally, on the 2nd of August; I broke my watchglass, and went home to get another; while I was gone I was sent for, and missed this book, which I know is his.
   MARTHA WELFORD. I was passing the shop door a little after five o'clock in the afternoon, on the 2nd of August; I saw the prisoner take the book from the door - I followed, and took her and the book nearly opposite the church; I said, "You have stolen that book;" she dropped it - I took it up, and brought her back.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I saw a woman drop it, and I gave it to the officer.
 MARTHA WELFORD. I saw the prisoner take it, she put her apron and shawl over it, and when I spoke to her, she dropped it.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Confined Six Weeks.
