 1310. THOMAS BUCKMASTER PROCTER was indicted for a con spiracy. WILLIAM ATKINSON. I live in Little Warner-street, Clerken well. On the 8th of April the prisoner, a man named Joe , and Mellson, came to my shed— Joe  said he had brought two friends that were just going to begin the cow-keeping line—the prisoner looked at the cow, and asked the price—I said, 18 l .—We went over the way, and had a pot of stout together—Mellson said he was the prisoner Thomas Buck master Procter's servant—he bid me 17 l . 10 s ., for the cow—I agreed to let him have it— Joe  said, "Come to my house at three o'clock, and take it to this cow-keeper's yard in the Mile-end-road"—we drove it to Mile-end—Mellson met us on the road, and we drove it into a cow-yard—we went to No. 9, Percival-buildings, Percivel-street, to take the money—the prisoner, Mellson, and Joe  were there—we all went into the room together—the prisoner said he was disappointed in getting the money, but he would give me a bill—I said I was not in the habit of buying cows for bills, I paid ready money for my cows— Joe  said, "It is right for 100 l .," meaning Mr. Procter was worth 100 l .—in consequence of that representation I agreed to take it—I should not have done so if Joe  had not assured me he was worth so much money—he said Mellson was the servant—I had 2 l . 10 s . put down to me in gold—Mellson said he would get the bill cashed directly, he would go to a house in the Mile-end-road— Joe  took me there—there was no cash there—I showed Teakle the cow-yard.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You got 2 l . 10 s ., did not you? A.  Yes—I signed a bill and a receipt for 15 l ., not for 17 l . 10 s .—this receipt is my writing—I got 2 l . 10 s . and this bill for the remainder—I am sure Mellson was there—it was as far back as April last—I have a distinct recollection that it was Mellson and not some other man, who said he was this man's servant—I had never seen him before—I have seen him since at Lambeth-street, and am sure he is the person—I have always been sure about it—I thought the bill was not right, and went to the house that night to see the parties—I did not go to the place where the bill was made payable, at the time it was due, nor after.
   COURT . Q.  For aught you know this may be a good bill, and will be paid to-morrow morning? A.  I never presented it after it was due—I have made no inquiries about it since it was due. WILLIAM PURKIS. On the 4th of July last I saw Mellson—he stated him self to be a wine-merchant—the prisoner was with him—he said he was his servant—I had a horse—Mellson asked me whether I would sell it—he left Procter with me to take the horse up to No. 9—he went along with me—as he was going along he said he was the servant, and persuaded me to sell the horse to his master—I cannot tell whether I should have sold it if I had not believed him to be the servant—they both over persuaded me—I believed Mellson was the master, and was a wine-merchant—I got 5 s . and a bit of paper for the horse—I went the day the bill was due—there was no money.
