MR. CLARKSON called -
   LAURENCE NATHAN. I am an officer of the Sheriff of Middlesex. I had this warrant in my possession; before I received it I had this warrant in my possession; before I received it I had been to No. 36, George-street, for the purpose of arresting Thompson. I saw this servant, and told her my object; I saw Baker, and told her the same: this warrant is dated the 28th of July - I went to execute it two or three times, but the house was closed; when I went there first the house was fall of goods, but when I entered with the warrant there were no goods on the premises; I endeavoured to follow them, but could not find them - the warrant is against William Thompson and Ann Baker - if I had found any goods I should have seized them, and sold them; I remember being in the Court of King's Bench; I arrested Thompson, and he offered Baker as bail to me - she offered to become bail for the man: I asked if she was a responsible person, and could pay the money, if he should fix her, and she said Yes, her furniture in her house in George-street would pay it.
 BAKER - GUILTY. Aged 43. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Twelve Months.
 THOMPSON -    GUILTY. Aged 47. Transported for Seven Years.
 1788.  MARY BROWNE was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 1 trunk, value 12s., the goods of Adam Margatroyd.
   ADAM MARGATROYD. I am a trunk-maker, and live at Knightsbridge. I lost a trunk from my door on the 28th of July; I followed the prisoner, and took her with it, about two hundred yards off.
   CHARLES THOMPSON. I saw the prisoner take the trunk from the door, and gave information - she was taken.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. Transported for Seven Years.
 1789.  THOMAS JOSEPH ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of Joseph Lawton.
   JOSEPH LAWTON. I am a porter at Mr. Stevens', in Tottenham-court-road. I slept with the prisoner in the back parlour of a lodging-house - on the 8th of September I missed my coat, which I never permitted him to take - he denied knowing any thing about it.
   JAMES ALDONS. I am a pawnbroker. This coat was pawned on the 8th of September - I cannot say by whom.
   THOMAS DUKE. I am a patrol. I took the prisoner, and he produced this duplicate, which led me to the place.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I meant to bring it him before Saturday night, when he came home, and saw me - he said if I would give him up the duplicate he would say no more about it.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Six Weeks.
 1790.  MARGARET WATSON and JANE BATNER were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 11 sovereigns, the monies of Thomas Webb , from his person.
   THOMAS WEBB. I am a seaman of the Maitland. On the 7th of September I had eleven sovereigns and 7s. - I got drunkish, and do not know what happened till I awoke, and found myself in bed with Watson, at No. 2, Shadwell-market; it was day-light - she asked me to get something to drink; I put on my trousers, came down, and she showed me some money in her hand - I had received my money in the dusk of the evening, but I do not know any thing about meeting the prisoners; I missed my money when I got up stairs, after having something to drink.
   ELIZABETH TURNER. I keep the White Lion. Batner came on the Monday evening to me, and Watson followed her, and gave me two sovereigns to keep; Mrs. Scott, who is a witness, was quite drunk outside - she cannot be found now.   NOT GUILTY.
 1791.  WILLIAM JORDAN was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , 1 canvas bag, value 2d., and 4 sovereigns, the property of Joseph Pigg , from his person.
 The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1792.  ELIZA JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 6 sovereigns, the monies of Andrew Oliver , from his person.
   ANDREW OLIVER. I am a seaman of a merchantman. On the 10th of September, at half-past ten o'clock at night, I met the prisoner at No. 1, Dock-street- I went home and agreed to stay all night with her; I was sober - I had eleven sovereigns and 24s. 1d. in my trousers pocket; she put away my trousers at the head of the bed- I am sure the money was in them; in the morning she said she was very bad, and wanted to go down stairs - I told her not to stop long; I then looked into my pocket, and six sovereigns were gone; I do not think the door was locked when we went to bed - I have not seen my sovereigns since; I am sure the prisoner is the woman - she did not return.
 Prisoner. He dropped 12s. 6d. on the bed - I took it up, and put it into his hand; that was all the money he had. Witness. There were 14s. dropped out of my jacket-pocket, but the gold was in my fob.
   MARY WILLIAMS. I live in this house with the prisoner, in Dock-street. On the 10th of September she brought the prosecutor home - he appeared sober; at half-past three o'clock the next day the prisoner told me she had been shopping, and had bought a pair of shoes for 5s., a pair of shoes for a child, and a black bonnet - she then said she meant to go down to her grandmother, and leave her her money to take care of till Saturday, when she meant to go to Chatham; I did not see what money she had; but she said the shoes cost 5s., and the child's boots 5s. - I do not know what the bonnet cost; the prosecutor afterwards came into my room, and the prisoner turned her back to him; he then went away, and she said, "He don't know me again," and burst out laughing - I said, "I don't suppose he does know you?" we were having tea together at the time - as soon as he was gone, the prisoner and another girl got up and ran away; they sent me word in an hour that they were going to a raffle, and could not come back to tea.
