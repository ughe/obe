   JONATHAN LOVETT. I am a watchman. On the 27th of October, about half-past two o'clock in the morning, I was in Long-alley, and in consequence of what I heard, I went to Crown-street and Clifton-street; I saw Morley coming from the new market, down Clifton-street, at a smartish pace; I cannot say he was running, nor yet walking - he was sixty or seventy yards from Austin's house - I took hold of him; he said "What do you want with me? What have I done?" I said "I don't know, you know best, you must come back with me" he said he should not - I took him back, and found Kemp taking Ousby along; I said "Kemp, do you want this man?" he said Yes - he identified him directly; I searched him, and found 2 1/2d. on him.
   RICHARD HATCH. I am a watchman. I was with Lovett, heard a rattle spring, went with him, and saw Morley going at a sharp pace; I saw him taken - Kemp identified him immediately.
   THOMAS BERRY. I am a headborough of Finsbury. -I was present when the prisoners were brought to the watch-house; we searched them - I produce the property found on Ousby.
   GEORGE KEMP. These two spoons were given to me by Edmonds and Dixon - they were found in the street; I found a key on Ousby.
   GEORGE CARPENTER AUSTIN. This is the key of No. 10, and was safe in the house overnight; these are the spoons, and I believe these shoes to be mine, but do not swear to them - this is my poker.
 Cross-examined. Q.How long had Morley lived with you? A. More than two years; I had no character with him - he left me of his own accord.
 MORLEY'S Defence. I know nothing about it; I was taken accidentally walking along, and did not know what for - the other prisoner is quite a stranger to me; another young man was apprehended in the street, just in the same way, and he was liberated before the Magistrate.
 GEORGE KEMP. I saw three men running - I had them before me for some time; I am certain Morley was one of them.
 OUSBY'S Defence. I know nothing of the other prisoner.
 MORLEY - GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 21.
 OUSBY - GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 20. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury, on account of their youth.

 238.  JOHN LOVELL, THOMAS EYRES and EMILY COX were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of November , at St. John, Hampstead, 1 sheep, price 50s., the property of Thomas Billen and William Billen.
 SECOND COUNT, for killing the said sheep, with intent to steal the carcass; against the Statute.
   THOMAS BILLEN. I am a drover, in partnership with my brother William Billen. I had eighty-six sheep in a field in Hampstead parish - they were all right last Thursday fortnight; I saw them all there about the middle of the day - I did not see them again till the Saturday morning; I did not then count them, but on the Sunday morning I missed this one - it was marked J. W. on the near hip, and ruddled between the shoulders; I have seen the skin since - I saw it in the field that morning; the sheep had been flayed in the field, and the carcass taken away - I am sure it is the skin of my sheep; the prisoners were quite strangers to me - the sheep was worth about 2l. 10s.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you any other partner? A. No.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I am a Police-officer of Mary-le-bone. On Saturday, the 22d of November, at a quarter-past seven o'clock in the morning, I was standing in Lisson-grove, at the end of Alpha-row, and saw Cox coming towards me - she had got a long grey cloak on; I saw her holding something very heavy under her cloak - I went up and asked what she had got there; she turned back her cloak, I opened a basket which she had got, and in it I found a leg and shoulder of mutton - it did not appear to have been cut by a butcher: I asked how she accounted for having it - she said a man had given it to her up the grove; I asked her if she knew who the man was- she said she never saw him before in her life; this was about a mile and a half, or three quarters from the field at Hampstead, where the sheep were kept. I asked her again if she did not know the man who gave it to her; she said No, she did not - I then asked her how she came there so early; she said she was going to Kilburn to work- I asked if she knew the party's name who she was going to work for; she said she did not - I asked if she had ever been there before - she said No, but a person had recommended her; I then inquired where she was taking the mutton to - she replied "Down this way," (meaning down the grove) I asked where to - she declined saying, and begged she might have her child before she went to prison; I asked where her child was - she said at her father and mother's where she lived: I asked her where it was - she said towards the Edgware-road, and she took me to the Junction-road; I asked her where she lived - she said next door to the baker's; I left her in charge of Wall and others, while I went to inquire, and at last found the child at No. 20.
