   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  As assistant officer it is part of your duty to serve writs? A.  Yes, I am appointed especially for that purpose—I serve writs for other attorneys besides Mr. Abrahams, all the attorneys that practise in that Court—I never went by any other name than Thomas Vine—I never in my life went by the name of James Vining—I never knew such a person as James Esdale Whittaker—there is not the slightest pretence for saying I was in custody for picking a pocket or stealing of any kind. PERCIVAL NORTON JOHNSON. I am in partnership with Mr. Cock, as gold-refiners, in Hatton-garden. I remember on Easter Tuesday last receiv ing a bar of mixed gold and silver—one of the young Solomons brought it—I do not know his name—the value of the bar was 500 l . odd—I gave the person who brought it a cheque for 400 l ., drawn on the bankers Williams, Deacon, & Co.—I afterwards had the gold assayed—my cheque was returned to me—( looking at it, marked "No.  19")—this is the cheque I gave young Solomon—I had paid some money to one of the Solomons before on that morning—I do not know to which—I was in my lapidary, and Corfield, my clerk, sent up to me to sign a cheque for 60 l .—this is the cheque I signed ( marked "No.  20")—that was paid first in the morning.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Was that the case with both cheques? A.  A silver bar was brought in the morning, and that was paid on account—I did not see the person who brought it—the second bar I took in myself, and saw the person who brought it—that was the 500 l . bar. WILLIAM JOHN COCK. I am in partnership with the last witness. On Thursday, the 4th of April, Henry Solomon called on me, and I gave him a cheque on Williams, Deacon, & Co., for 127 l . 17 s . 6 d .—this is it ( "No.  21")—as balance for a silver bar and a parting bar, that is a bar contain ing gold and silver—that was the balance of the 400 l . and the 60 l ., making together 587 l . 17 s . 6 d . JAMES GILES. I am clerk to Messrs. Williams, Deacon, & Co., bankers Cock and Johnson bank there—on the 2nd of April this cheque of theirs for 400 l . (" No.  19") was paid at our house by another clerk named Thackery—he is away from indisposition—on the 14th of April I cashed this other cheque of theirs myself ( "No.  21") for 127 l . 17 s . 6 d .—I paid it in a note for 100 l . and I believe cash.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  You have the books here where it is entered in your own hand-writing? A.  Yes—I am sure I paid a £100 note with my own hand—I paid it to Solomon.
