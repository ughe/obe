1970. THOMAS TURNER and ALEXANDER CLARK were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , 1 pair of boots, value 4s., the goods of George Green.
 GEORGE GREEN. I am a  shoemaker, and live in Shoreditch. On the evening of the 27th of July I was standing in my shop; my son called out, "A man has stolen some boots," and he ran after Turner, calling Stop thief! I took Turner - my son said, "That is the man that took the boots, but he gave them to another, who is gone the other way;" I looked, but could not find him; in about ten minutes my son said, "Here is the other boy," and he pointed to Clark - I took him; the boots have not been found.
   GEORGE GREEN, JUN. I am the prosecutor's son. Turner came to the shop, and cut the string of a pair of boots; he gave them to Clark, who ran away with them, and then they both ran away - I ran after Turner, and my father took him; I am sure he was the boy.
   JOSEPH RYAN(Police-constable G 216.) About half-past nine o'clock I was in Shoreditch, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I ran up; Clark was given to me, and I took him to the station - Turner was loitering about there, and Clark said to him, "I am pinned for them boots," and from the description I had received I took Turner - I found this knife on him, which the little boy says he thinks is the knife.
 TURNER -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 CLARK -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Seven Years.
 1971.  JOHN THORPE was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , 1 live tame goose, price 4s., the property of Esquire Duke.
   JOHN CANTY(Police-constable N 168). I was on duty in Upper-street, Islington, at half-past one o'clock in the morning of the 26th of August; I saw the prisoner carrying a goose, and took him into custody - he was half a mile from the prosecutor's.
 ESQUIRE DUKE. I know this goose to be mine; I had seen it safe the morning before, in a little paddock at the back of my house - I do not know the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a man on Highgate-hill with some geese in a cart - his trace broke, and he asked me if I had a bit of string; I lent him a bit - a man came and asked if the geese were for sale; he said Yes, they were 3s. each: I said I would have one for half a crown and bought it.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1972.  EMMA VINCENT was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 quilt, value 3s.; 2 sheets, value 2s., and 1 pillow-case, value 6d., the goods of Thomas Overton.
 THOMAS OVERTON. I live in  Exeter-place, Chelsea. The prisoner lodged with me eleven days; she came as a married woman - no man lived with her; I missed my property, and got the officer - she said she pawned them from distress, and gave the duplicates.
   JOHN GAMMON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce the articles.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
 GUILTY. Aged 29. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1973.  SARAH YOUNG was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 shawl, value 2s. 6d., the goods of William Salter ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
   MARY SALTER. I am the wife of William Salter, a porter; we live in Long-acre. On the 1st of August, my landlady called me - I went down and saw the prisoner at the bottom of the stairs; this shawl had been taken off a box inside the door of my room, while I was washing.
   JEMIMA FARMER. I saw the prisoner come along the passage of my house - I called the prosecutrix down; the prisoner had these sheets, but I did not see the shawl at that time - I knew she was a stranger, and asked what business she had there; she said she wanted a person named Taylor - I then asked where she got the sheets which she had under her shawl; the prosecutrix pulled the sheets from her, and this shawl dropped down - there is no owner found for the sheets.
   JOHN DAY(Police-constable C 134). I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction - I know she is the person; I attended and saw her - she was ordered to be fined 1s. - (read).
     GUILTY. Aged 55. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1974.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 1 set of boot-trees, value 10s., and 3 brushes, value 2s., the goods of George Hanson ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
 JOHN NISBITT (Police-constable E 6). I stopped the prisoner near Russell-square on the 24th of July, at halfpast six o'clock in the morning; she had these articles on her - I asked what she had got: she said various articles which she had brought from Charlton-street, Somers'-town where she lived - that she was going to Mr. Ardin's, in Staples Inn, and she was laundress there; I then looked at the things, and asked her if he had said she was to carry a set of boot-trees there - she said she had heard of them, but she did not know whether she was to carry them that morning or not; I said, "I will go there with you:" we went a few steps, and then she said, "I am going to my daughter" - I then took her; this was about five hundred yards from the prosecutor's.
   THOMAS BARRETT. I am a footman to George Hanson, Esq. I lost these trees from a cellar in the front area on the morning in question - the gate is kept locked: I do not know the prisoner.
   GEORGE BENCON(Police-constable D 155). I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction - I know she is the person; she had fourteen days in Newgate.
     GUILTY. Aged 54. - Confined Twelve Months.
