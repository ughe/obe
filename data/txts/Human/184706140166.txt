JAMES JONES. I am in the service of Mr. James Hoby, of Camden-town, I produce a coat—I do not know who pledged it—this is the ticket I gave to the person—I produce a remnant of silk, some knives and forks, two pairs of shoes, a waistcoat, and some blinds, the tickets for which are here. GEORGE MACNALLEY re-examined.  The coat, curtains, silk, shoes, and waistcoat, are mine—these shoes are my son's.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have lived with the prosecutor as a wife for nearly eight years; he has been aware of the things being in pledge; they were not pledged with the intention to rob him; my brother would have redeemed the things for me—part of them are my own. GEORGE MACNALLEY re-examined.  The prisoner was not living with me as my wife at this time, but she had been for about six years—she never had authority to pledge articles—she never told me she had pledged anything till after I found something out about my son's watch.
   JURY . Q.  What was the earliest date on which anything was found? A.  The 2nd of March, 1846. NOT GUILTY.
 1499. FREDERICK JOHNSON was indicted for stealing 17lbs. weight of sugar, value 4 s  .; the goods of James Huxtable , his master, in a vessel in a port of entry and discharge. JOHN SUTTON. I am in the service of the East and West India Dock Company. On the 14th of June I stopped the prisoner going out of the Docks—a person with him was carrying his hammock, which was very heavy—I asked what was in it—the prisoner said his bed and his wet clothes—I put my hand in, and found they were dry—I slacked the lashing, pulled up the bed, and found this bag, with 17lbs. of raw sugar in it—he had got a pass for his things, from which I found he came from the ship Codrington  —I went to the Codrington , and took the prisoner with me to Captain Huxtable, and in his presence showed the sample of sugar, and asked how he could think of taking that from the ship—it was part of the cargo—he said he did not know anything about it—the captain said he had nothing to do with it, and told me to do as I pleased—I took the prisoner to my superior, and then to the Thames Police-office—he was remanded—next day I examined some casks—there was one on the dock which had similar sugar in it—it had a great hole chopped in it, and a quantity of sugar taken—I examined it with what I found in the hammock, and found they did not agree—I then examined some casks on the quay, which I had just seen come ashore from the ship—I had seen them come over the ship's side, and know no others were there—I did not see them all landed—I examined the sugar in them, and it corresponded.
   Q.  What is the captain's name? A.  James Huxtable, I believe—I said it was William at the Police-office—I was told so by one of the seamen, but afterwards ascertained that it was James.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I did not know it was in my hammock, I had just come home, and cut it down. NOT GUILTY.
