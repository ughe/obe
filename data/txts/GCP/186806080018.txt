513. BENJAMIN SPALL (20), Stealing, whilst employed in the Post-
Office, a post letter containing & 51. bank note, the property of Her
Majesty's Postmaster-General. 1 YOTEPS
Messrs. METCALFE and SLADE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM LAWRENCE. I live at Rugby-on 25th April I had ocorsion to
send & 5l. note to London I posted it on the 24th-the number was
"70511,"date" December 27th, 1867"-I put my initials, “W. L.," at the
back--this (produced) is the note- I put it into a letter and addressed it,
“Mr. W. F. Fisher, Clement's Inn, Strand, London, W.C."--I sealed the
envelope, and posted it between 9 and 10-in consequence of something I
heard I applied to the Post-Office authorities.
dos
WILLIAM COLLINS. I am a clerk in the Post Office letter posted in
Rugby in the evening would arrive in London in the morning, and would go
first to the West Central Office, if it was directed “W.C.," at Holborn.
PALIP RALLY. I am a sorter in the Post-Office-I was at the West
Central Office on the 25th--the mail bag from Rugby arrived about 5-I
opened it and took out the letters.
DANIEL FITZPATRICK. I am a letter carrier at the West Central District
Post Office, Holborn---the prisoner was also a letter carrier in the same office
---his walk was the Clement's Lane walk--there are three carriers in that
walk-the prisoner, myself, and another named George Smith on the
morning of the 25th we were all three at the office it was the prisoner's
duty to divide the letters for the Clement's Inn walk between himself,
George Smith, and myself-Clement's Inn itself was in George Smith's part
-I know the prisoner's writing- I believe the writing on this note, B.
Spall," to be his a good many buildings have been pulled down, and that
has lightened the prisoner's part of the district a good deal.
Prisoner. Not one morning out of six do I divide the letters since I have
been on that walk. Witness. It is his duty to do so, and he did it on that
morning.
