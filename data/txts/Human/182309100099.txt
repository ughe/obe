   FRANCIS JOHN GATES. I am patrol of St. Giles's. On the night of this fire, between ten and eleven o'clock, I was on duty in George-street with Riley, and the prisoner crossed the street, coming down Stacey-street, in a direction from the fire. I stopped him, and asked what he had under his coat. He said he had nothing there. I put my hand to his side, and found this pocket-book full of papers. He said it was his own, and contained a few letters from his friends. I asked what they were about. He said he did not choose to tell. I found nothing else upon him. The direction on the letters led me to the prosecutor.
   PHILIP RILEY. I was with Gates. His statement is correct. When I took him to the watch-house, he persisted that it was his own property, and after questioning him, I asked where he found it. He then said he found it at the end of Plumtree-street, between Charlotte and Streatham-streets.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Life.
 1226.  JOHN CLARKE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , a pair of boots, value 12 s.; a pair of shoes, value 7 s., and a shilling and a sixpence, the property of John Hawkins.
   JOHN HAWKINS. I am a shoe-maker, and live in Bull's-head-court, Great Peter-street. The prisoner lived two doors off, and worked with me. I had not seen these boots for a month, but missed the shoes on the 4th. The money was marked, and was taken from the till. On Sunday, the 12th, he was taken up, and I charged him with taking 1 s. 6 d. from the till. He immediately threw it back on the shelf, and said it was only 1 s. 6 d. I had been looking through a hole, and saw him leave his seat, and heard the till unlocked. He was the only person in the room. His wife gave me a duplicate.
   LEONARD MEEDES. I am shopman to Messrs. Graham and Co., pawnbrokers. On the 11th of June the prisoner pawned the boots, and on the 21st the shoe were pawned by somebody in his name.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Eight Months.
 1227.  ELIZABETH DAVIS was indicted for stealing. on the 9th of August , two table-cloths, value 6 s.; a knife. value 3 d., and a fork, value 3 d., the goods of William Acton.
   WILLIAM ACTON. I live in Maiden-lane, Covent-garden, and keep a cook-shop. The prisoner was my charwoman; we missed things at different times. I went to her lodgings with a warrant, and found a knife and fork, which I knew, and two table-cloths in pawn, which I know to be mine. I claimed them in her presence, and she admitted them to be mine.
   JOHN GRINDLEY. I am shopman to Messrs. Townsend and Co. pawnbrokers, Russell-street. On the 4th of July the prisoner pawned a table-cloth, and another on the 9th.
   ROBERT DUKE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner into custody, and found the duplicate of the table-cloth on her; she said she only took one - and when the second was produced, she said she only took two; she appeared distressed.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1228.  WILLIAM DENNISON was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of June , four books, value 3 s., and a waistcoat, value 6 d., the goods of Matthew Weldhen.
   MATTHEW WELDHEN. I lived in Red Lion-street, Holborn. The prisoner is no acquaintance of mine - but I know his features. A fire broke out in the house a little after eight o'clock - I was out at the time, and only know that these things were on the second floor at the time. On the Saturday following I found the prisoner in custody at Marlborough-street with them.
   JOHN KENDRICK. I am a beadle of St. Giles's. A watchman brought the prisoner to the watch-house in Smart's Buildings, on the night of the fire, about eleven o'clock, with a bundle containing a waistcoat and four books; I asked what he had there - he said it was his own; before it was untied, I asked what was in it - he then said somebody gave it him in Holborn. I took him to Marlborough-street. The prosecutor claimed them, I found other things in the bundle, which were lost at the fire; he could not tell what was in it; he had a coat on his back which he said was given him at the same time - but he could not tell who by.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had them given to me. I was taken a quarter of a mile from the fire.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Transported for Seven Years.
 1229.  JOHN FUZDEN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , four half crowns, four shillings, and seven sixpences, the monies of John Ingram , from his person.
   JOHN INGRAM. I live in Lambeth-street, Whitechapel. On the 28th of August, about two o'clock in the morning, I came home quite sober - my money was in my breeches pocket - I left my door ajar - I got up at eight o'clock in the morning, and the money was gone.
   MARY INGRAM. I am the wife of the prosecutor. I got up a little before eight o'clock, and his money was gone. The prisoner had been in the house helping my son, and was there the day before. He usually came to call my son up.
   ANN GOLDING. I lodge on the second floor - I came down about six o'clock in the morning, and saw the prisoner come out of Ingram's room - I knew him very well, but did not speak to him.
     NOT GUILTY.
