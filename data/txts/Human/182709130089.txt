Third Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1724.  JULIA HASWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 3 blankets, value 3s.; 2 pillows, value 2s.; 1 sheet, value 2s.; 1 pair of snuffers, value 3d., and 1 snuffer-tray, value 3d., the goods of Elizabeth Chorley.
   ELIZABETH CHORLEY. I am a widow, and live in Grove-place, Bethnal-green. On the 3d of July I let the prisoner a furnished room; on the 18th I went into the room, and asked for the loan of a pillow - she hesitated; I thought all was not right; I sent for an officer, and called her into the parlour; we went up and missed these articles - the duplicates of them were found in a cupboard in her room - she said she intended to return them.
   FRANCIS SOAMES. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Green-street, Bethnal-green. I have a blanket pawned on the 12th of July, for 1s., and a pillow, on the 13th; and some cups and saucers, in the name of Haswell. I cannot swear to the prisoner.
   EDWARD HALES. I am a pawnbroker. I have a blanket, pawned for 1s., and two or three other articles, they were all pawned by the prisoner.
   JAMES ESSEX. I am an officer, and took her; I found duplicates of the property in a pocket-book in her room.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Three Months.
 1725.  JOSEPH LEE was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 2 sheets, value 4s., the goods of Isaac Mussell.
   ANN MUSSELL. I am the wife of Isaac Mussell. The prisoner lodged on our ground floor; I missed these sheets from my first floor room.
   HENRY DUPLIEX. I am a pawnbroker. I have two sheets, pawned by the prisoner on the 28th of August.
   WILLIAM ALLEN. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house; he said, "I acknowledge I took them, but do not know what I did with them;" he bears a good character.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I beg for mercy.
 GUILTY. Aged 3 . Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 1725.  GEORGE WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 2 shirts, value 30s., the goods of James Soper.
   JAMES SOPER. I live at Edmonton. On the 24th of July, on coming home, between seven and eight o'clock, I heard our drying ground had been robbed. I applied to the constable, and next day I saw a pawnbroker, who described a person to me. I saw the prisoner carrying a bundle on the road, and had him secured, and taken into a public-house; I asked what he had in the bundle; he said two shirts; I opened them, and said they were mine. I had the fellow shirt on - I never saw him before.
   JOSEPH DOSSETT. I am a constable. I took the prisoner with the bundle, containing two shirts.
   MARY MAINE. I am a servant to Mr. Soper; I hung the linen out about one o'clock, and missed it between two and three.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.

 1728.  LUCY CATHERINE BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of August , 2 blankets, value 5s.; 2 pillows, value 4s.; 1 table-cloth, value 1s. 6d.; 2 pillow-cases, value 9d.; 2 spoons, value 4s.; 2 sheets, value 6s., and 1 candlestick, value 6d., the goods of James Rowell.
   MELYAR SOPHIA ROWELL. I am the wife of James Rowell, a cabinet-maker: we live in Smith-street, Somers'-town. The prisoner hired some apartments of me, ready furnished - she paid me very well. I went into her room two or three days previous to her being taken, and missed this property - some of it has been found.
   WILLIAM BAKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Somers'-town. I have two blankets, a sheet, and a candlestick, pawned at different times by a woman, in the name of Brown. I have a table-cloth, which I can swear was pawned by the prisoner for 1s. 6d., in July.
   GEORGE HAVILL. I am a constable. I was sent for on the 23d of August, and took the prisoner - she gave up some duplicates of this property, and said she intended to redeem them on Friday.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. How long had she been with you? A. Between four and five months: she might be poorly, but never had any medical attendant - there was 2l. 13s. due when I spoke to her; she had given me notice to quit, and the expiration of the notice would have been in two days. I will swear that the first time I missed any thing from her apartments, was on the Tuesday night previous to the Friday on which the notice would have expired. I spoke to her of it on the Wednesday - I gave her into custody on Thursday.
 Prisoner's Defence. My remittances did not come in, and I pawned some sheets, which the prosecutor knew of- and the day I was committed here, she told me she was not a fool, she knew I had done it. Poverty alone induced me so far to forget myself.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined Three Months.
 1729.  MARY LANG was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 1 piece of gold, of the value of 10s.; 1 sovereign, and 3 pieces of foreign silver coin, value 4s. 6d., the property of Michael Moore.
   MICHAEL MOORE. I am a sailor, and Italian. I came here in the Saucy Jack, and have got no ship since. On the 15th of August, about eleven o'clock at night, I was in White's-yard; I met the prisoner, who took me to a house - I was very tired - I took off my jacket, and put it on a chair. I had some foreign gold and silver in the pocket - I fell on the bed, and went to sleep directly - she was then in the room. I awoke in about half an hour, and saw her over-hauling my things; I jumped out of bed, felt my pocket, and the money was gone; she put the money into her mouth; I tried to get it out, but could not.
