736. MARY ANN MIDDLETON was indicted for stealing, on the
22nd of December, I set of bed furniture, value 10s. ; 1 blanket, value 6s;
2 sheets, value 103.; 1 bolster, value 6s. ; 1 looking-glass and stand, value
28.; 1 table-cover, value 23. ; 1 candlestick, value 18.; 1 pillow, value le
6d., and 1 pair of bellows, value Is. 6d. ; the goods of John Kent.
John Kent. I live in Bowling Green-lane, Clerkenwell. The prisoner
was a lodger in a furnished room in my house-she came sometime in De-
cember-in consequence of suspecting that my property was missing I
took her into custody one Sunday morning, and the duplicates, the key of
the street door, the key of the room door, and a small knife, were found on
her-I missed a blanket, a locking-glass, table cover, sheets, and other
things—there was from ten to twelve pounds of feathers taken out of the
bed-I had observed the bed before she came, and she acknowledged to
taking them out-she paid a fortnight of the rent, the rest is owing-these
articles that I missed were in the room, and let to her as part of the fur-
niture.
WILLIAM HARVEY (police-constable G 83.) I live in Greville-street. I
took the prisoner, and found these duplicates on her-she said she had cer-
tainly pawned some of the prosecutor's things.
WILLIAM ROBERTS. I am in the employ of Mr. Cordwell, a pawnbroker,
who lives at No. 31, Exmouth-street. I produce a table-cover, a blanket,
a swing-glass, a pillow, and bolster-the duplicates found by the officer
are the duplicates of the things I have the counterparts—they were
pawned on different days, from the 28th of December to the 3rd of
February
John Kent re-examined. This is my property-(looking at it.)
WILLIAM GARDENER. I am in the service of Mr. Fleming, a pawa-
broker, in Farringdon-street. I produce two sheets and one single sheet
-the prisoner pawned the single sheet--this is the duplicate of it-it cor
responds with one which was found on her by the officer.
WILLIAM HENRY KELL. I am a pawnbroker. I have a candlestick, 8
