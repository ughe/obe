EDWARD SMITH OWEN. I am in the galloon trade, in partnership with Sibley Foster, and live in Goldsmith-street. On the 1st of November, between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, I sent a lad with a parcel to the Bell and Crown inn, Holborn - it contained the articles stated in the indictment.
   SAMUEL MOORHOUSE. I am errand-boy to the prosecutors. On the 1st of November I had this parcel to deliver at the Bell and Crown, and was going down Skinner-street - the prisoner stood outside Muncaster's (the pawnbrokers) window, and called to me; he asked if I would go and fetch him a coach - I had another boy with me: he said he would give us 6d. a piece - he gave us 1s.: I put my bag down, and said to the little boy, "You mind the bag," and while I was going I met the little boy by the Saracen's Head; I had been absent four or five minutes: the boy told me to bring a chariot - I immediately ran back to where I left my goods, and the prisoner was gone, bag and all; this was on Saturday - I saw him on the Monday morning, and knew him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Of course you pointed him out the moment you saw him? A. Yes - I saw him come out of the lock-up place; the officer met some gentleman on the Saturday night, in Gracechurch-street, but I did not think right to go near them; I do not know whether that was the prisoner - I did not see his face - I was about three yards from him, but was not looking that way; we were going along and met him - I was about a yard behind the officer; the officer tapped him on the shoulder, but I did not see him do that - he stopped, and talked to him, and then I went away; it was rather dark- I did not hear what passed, and did not know it was the prisoner.
 Q. Then you were looking that way? A. They were looking towards Fenchurch-street, and I towards Bishopsgate-street; I described the person who robbed me as being above forty years old, and of very low stature; I had never seen him before, to my knowledge - I went to St. Andrew's watch-house, and gave information directly, and about half-past five o'clock saw the officer tap the gentleman on the shoulder; I was robbed about four.
 COURT. Q. What do you mean by a man of low stature? A. He was not tall.
   ENOCH JACKSON. I was with Moorhouse; a man stood at a door in Skinner-street, by Seacoal-lane, and a man at the top said, "A man wants you there;" we went to him: he said he would give us 6d. each to fetch a coach - I staid by the goods, and he said to me, "Go and tell him not to fetch a coach but a chariot, and make as much haste as you can;" I went, and when we came back the bag and man were gone; we went to the watch-house directly - I left the bag with the prisoner; he is the man.
 Cross-examined. Q.Had he the same clothes on then? A. No, he was dressed in black - he had black trousers on; I never saw him before - I was keeping my eye on the goods: I was with the prisoner about nine minutes.
 Q. Have you been repeating your story to any body? A. No, Sir - Yes, Sir; nobody told me to say it was nine minutes - the man appeared about forty, but not more, and of a low size; nobody told me to say he was about forty.
 JURY. Q. What age do you think the prisoner is? A. About forty, and of low stature.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not cry, and tell your father next day that you were not there at all? A. Yes.
 COURT. Q. Why did you tell your father so? A. I was afraid he would beat me.
   THOMAS SHELSWELL. I belong to Lambeth-street office. On Saturday night, the 1st of November, I apprehended the prisoner at a public-house in Rosemary-lane, and on Monday these two boys said he was the man.
   JOHN THOMAS GRAY. I am an officer. About a quarter-past four o'clock on Saturday a lad came to St. Andrew's watch-house, and said he had been robbed; I went in pursuit, and met two persons in Aldgate High-street that night - the prisoner was one; he said, "Mr. Gray, how do you do?" I said, "Very well, thank you," and walked away; the boy might be a yard or two off: I went to the office, and gave information.
 Cross-examined. Q. He knew at the time you were looking for the robber? A. Certainly - he was walking in the same line with me; he might pass the prisoner face to face - he might be a few feet behind, but he must have passed him; it was near a shop window, with a gas-light to it.
     NOT GUILTY.
