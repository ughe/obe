This letter of mine of 2nd May acknowledges the receipt of the balance
sheet (read) on 13th May, 1875 I sent Mr. Oakley a further accept
ance for 2001. I afterwards received the balance sheet for the quarter ending
30th June, 1875, in due course, I read it, and afterwards wrote this letter
of 6th August, to Mr. Oakley. (This alluded to some anonymous lettere con
taining unfavourable remarks about the bank.) I received the balance-sheet
for 30th September, 1875, and read it on 29th October, 1875, I wrote this
letter to the defendant. This also alluded to the persistent attacks on the
bank both anonymously and by the public press, but espressing confidence in
the undertaking). During the year 1875 I received regularly the “Co.
operative and Financial Review," I subscribed to it six months in advance,
according to the terms printed on it--the balance sheet was never pub
lished till about five weeks after the date-I had further correspondence
with the defendant about the attacks made upon him in the newspapers -
was asked by him to guarantee a sum of 801. for the prosecution of "The
World" on the part of the Keokuk and Kansas Railway Company, and on
10th December, 1875, I wrote to him this letter. (This agreed to the request
for the 801. guarantee.) I received this little pamphlet from the defendant,
I can't say when, I presume when it was published-my first interview with
the defendant was on 29th January, 1875; I then asked him about the
bank, how it was progressing, and he spoke of it most favourably in every
way--I might have had two or three more interviews with him after that,
I think one was in May, 1875, and one on 1st December, 1875; there might
have been one between May and December, but I don't remember the date,
and I am not sure that there was one---at those interviews I always spoke
to bim in reference to the bank; he always assured me that everything was
satisfactory and in a most flourishing state and that it was sure to prosper ;
he inspired me with full confidence at every interview--the interview on the
1st December, was at the bank, I spoke to him then about the condition of
the bank, and he assured me that it was most prosperous and everything was
well in spite of the attacks in the different papers I had at that time seen
the balance-sheets for March, June, and September-1 paid in 4001. in the
name of one of my daughters as an investment at that time, and just as
I was coming away, he said to me "By-the-bye, General, I have to accept
ance of yours now, it will be of use to me," and I had such condence in him
that I gave it immediately, dated 1st December, for 2001. at three months,
that acceptance ought to have been renewable by the bank, but the bank
was closed, and of course I had to pay it when due-I never received any
part of the 4001. out of the bank at the time I invested that money and gave
the acceptance I had no idea of what I have since learnt as to the real
state of the bank; I had confidence in the state of the bank, not only frðm
the assurances of the defendant, but from the balance sheets; I trusted
entirely to the balance-sheets being certified and signed by public
accountants.
