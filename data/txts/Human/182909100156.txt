 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was Mrs. Keefe a woman rather grateful, as to acts of kindness done to her? A. I never thought her so - she made no will; I have no recollection of her saying any thing to me, but"There the notes are for you to put your hand on;" she did not tell me to give them to Dr. Farre, but I concluded that was her meaning - I saw her last on the day Dr. Farre did; she certainly appeared to require nursing very much, but appeared better, and I was prevented from going to see her - I am not aware that Mrs. Carter sat up one night with her - Mrs. Keefe had a stick to knock with to call her in the day time; I am not aware of her doing that in the night; Mrs. Carter did not appear to me to be particularly fatigued on the day of her death - she did not appear pregnant; I visited Mrs. Keefe almost daily in Carey-street - she was a very odd woman; her apartment at one time was in a filthy state: her dress was more like a pauper than a lady; she was a self-willed obstinate woman -I do not know why she returned to lodge at Carter's; I was surprised to hear of her going back, because I understood she had left there in anger - she had lived there before for two years; if she had wanted the desk in my absence, she could not lift it herself, but must have some one to bring it to her; Mrs. Carter's words were that Mrs. Keefe had told her to shut the door against every body, and take charge of every thing; she did not say she had given her every thing.
 COURT. Q. Did she on any occasion insist that the notes, or any other property, had been given to her? A. No; she only said she was to take charge of every thing.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you never hear Mrs. Keefe say she intended to make Carter a compliment? A. Once, when I applied about the shilling a day, she said, in an angry manner, "I will make her a compliment;" I did not take out all the papers when I first examined the desk - I turned them over in the desk, and opened every West Indis letter; there were a great many papers; it was not full- I did not put any papers on the steps, or on the bed, nor put them on one side; there was no room to do so - there were a great many loose papers - I examined them, and am certain there were no Bank notes among them; I might be ten minutes rummaging the desk.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Do you live in the house of Dr. Farre's father, as one of the family? A. Yes - I frequently went to see Mrs. Keefe, when Dr. Farre did not; Mrs. Carter knew I was a member of the family, no doubt - if the desk was brought to Mrs. Keefe, she could open it with the key, and put the key away in its proper place; Mrs. Carter said she was assisting her out of bed, that her head dropped on her shoulder, and she died immediately - when Mrs. Keefe said she would make her a compliment she did not fix any time for it, in words or otherwise.
   FREDERICK JOHN FARRE, ESQ. I live with my father, Dr. Farre. On the 8th of June I went with Miss Springweiler to Mrs. Carter's house - I said I had no hesitation in telling her that notes to a considerable amount were missing from the desk, and I was sure, for her own sake, she would be anxious to afford some explanation of it, as it appeared to me very mysterious; she said she knew nothing about them, and could not be answerable for them - I saw her again on the 10th and 26th - I believe she was asked by Miss Springweiler whether she could afford any further information about the paper - she could not; the desk was opened on both occasions before me - Mrs. Carter was the only person in the room; I saw Robert Carter, Jun, write at Charterhouse-square - I stood over him, and observed his writing; I am fully persuaded that the two papers found in the desk are in his hand-writing.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you visit Mrs. Keefe at any time? A. Yes; I visited her on the Sunday week previous to her death, not afterwards - I am studying medicine; Mr. Franks was with me on the 24th and 26th, and on two other occasions; I have not been in Court during the examination of the other witnesses.
   RICHARD FRANKS, ESQ. I live at Mill-hill, and am a Magistrate of Middlesex. I went to Carter's with Mr. Farre and Miss Springweiler, on the 18th of June, and saw Mrs. Carter - Miss Springweiler desired her to come up stairs; she came up - I asked her where she took the key of the desk from: she said, "Out of the drawer, by the bed-side, and it was in a purse;" I asked what made her take the key; she said she thought she had a right to take it; I told her she did very wrong; she had made herself liable by so doing - I did not say to what she was liable; she was agitated, and shook very much - I said all she ought to have done was to close the eyes of the deceased, tied a handkerchief round her mouth, locked the door, and sent for her relations - she made no answer: I asked to see her daughter Catherine, who is about twelve years old, and asked her, in her mother's presence, whether Mrs. Keefe had laid quiet in the night she died, and if she was out of the room the whole night - she said she laid very quiet, as usual - I asked if she had asked for the desk; the daughter said she had asked for it on the Friday before the Sunday, and she had handed it to her on the bed - I asked what Mrs. Keefe took out of it; she said a sovereign, and gave it her to get change; I asked if she had left any papers or things on the bed, after she had locked the desk - she said she had not left any thing, and Mrs. Keefe told her to put the desk where it was, and the key and purse in the drawer where she usually kept it - I then requested Miss Springweiler to open the desk, and look into every part of it very particularly - it was searched, but no notes found; the notes were not mentioned - Miss Springweiler asked Mrs. Carter if the ear-drops had been in the desk; she said she would go and fetch them - I said, "Don't move out of the room, you may send for them;" and she sent the child, who brought them up -Mrs. Carter said she found them under the grate: I wished to see the nurse, who had attended Mrs. Keefe that night - they sent for Catherine Williams, who came;
