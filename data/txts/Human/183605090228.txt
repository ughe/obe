 1345. CHARLES HARDING was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of April , 4 chain traces, value 4 s  .; 2 gate books, value 1 s  .; and 1 sack, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of William Morriss . WILLIAM HURDISH. I live at Lee, in Kent, and am foreman to Mr. William Morriss. This is one of his sacks—I missed a great many—the prisoner worked for me a little while at hay-making. GEORGE HARRIS( police-constable R  158.) On the 29th of April I saw the prisoner in High-street, Deptford, with this sack on his shoulder, turned inside out—I followed him to Flagon-rew, and stopped him—I asked what he had got—he put then sack down, and I found these traces in it.
   Prisoner . I told the officer that I had it of a man named Jones, who told me he worked for Mr. Morris. GUILTY. Aged 41.— Confined Six Months.
 1346. LUCY SHILL was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of April , 1 quilt, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 2 blankets, value 3 s  .; 2 sheets, value 3 s  .; 11 look ing-glass, value 3 s  .; 1 tea-kettle, value 9 d  .; 1 pillow, value 3 d  .; 1 pil low-case, value 3 d  .; and 2 candlesticks, value 2 d  .; the goods of John Wilkinson . MARY WILKINSON. I live in Charles-street, Woolwich; my husband's name is John. The prisoner lodged with me—on Saturday, the 2nd of April, she had been out all night—her husband lived with her, but he was drafted away the Wednesday before—she came home at eleven o'clock, and I asked her for an iron which I had lent her—she said she would go and fetch it, and she did not come back again—I heard a noise in he room between twelve and one o'clock—I thought it was her sister's little girl—I went down—the door was not quite closed—I went in, and missed these things—she did not return—these things are all mine.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .) WILLIAM BOWERS. I live in Church-street, Woolwich, and am a broker. I produce two duplicates, which I bought of the prisoner's niece through charity—I gave her 3 d . for them—she asked me 6 d .—I did not want them—I have repeatedly told her I would have nothing to do with them—I did not buy them with the intention to take the things out—one if for the glass for 1 s . 6 d ., which is not worth the money; and the other a gows for 4 s .—the girl was to have them again at ten o'clock—a duplicate of two candlesticks was also bought at my shop, but not by me. EDWARD JOHN TALBOT. I have one blanket, which was pawned for 1 s . 6 d ., and one blanket and quilt for 2 s . 6 d .—they were pawned in Fe bruary—I cannot recollect the person. HARRIET LUCY COOK. I am the prisoner's niece. I took a quilt from my aunt to the pawnbroker, and I sold these duplicates to Mr. Bowles—I told him I was to fetch them again, as my aunt was going to Blackheath to get some money—she sent me with them—I pledged these candlesticks for 8 d ., which I took to Mrs. Shill.
