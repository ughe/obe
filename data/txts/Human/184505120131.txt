I gave cash for this check for 21 l . 2 s . 6 d .—I paid it across the counter. JOHN DAVIES. I am clerk to Spooner and Co. I paid this check for 13 l . across the counter, I do not know to whom. JAMES SELLER. I am in the employ of Sir William Magnay, who has one partner. The prisoner was in their employ as out-door clerk—it was his duty to collect orders and to receive money, which it was his duty to enter in his own book, and pay it over to me the same day that he received it—the book he kept was one in which he ought to enter the goods he sold, and the money he received; and in the evening he would say what monies he had received, and pay them to me—he did not pay me a check for 21 l . 2 s . 6 d ., nor the proceeds of it—he did not at any time pay me a check of 13 l ., nor the proceeds of it—if he had paid me the checks they would have gone through our bankers—these checks have not been through my hands at all—he never paid me 8 l . 13 s . in cash on account of Mr. Watson—it was his duty to pay checks and money to me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you, the cashier? A.
