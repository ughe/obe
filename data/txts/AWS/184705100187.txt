stealing 6 blankets, value 31: 8 sheets, 11. I Tug, 123. and 1 pair of
drawers, 68. the goods of William Brown, their master; and that Sullivan had

WILLIAX BROWN. I live in Stratton-terrace, Shadwell. The two, pri-
soners were in my service--Munican left me on Saturday, the 10th April,
and Sullivan left on Monday the I2th-when she had left, I missed the
articles stated-) had Munican taken up-she said she took two blankets and
pawned them in one shop, and one blanket at another shop, and had shared
the money with Sullivan.
MARY ANN SYRELL. I live servant with Mr. Brown. On Saturday, the
10th April, Sullivan took a blanket off the bed-ighe took it into the back
room, folded it up, and gave it to Munican, at the bottom of the stairs-I saw
her do it, and Munican directly afterwards took it out.
Cross-eramined by Mr: PAYNE. Q. Where were you A. Up stairs, in
the same room-it was taken before me-it is a lodging-house for sailors-
there 'ar not many persons coming in and out.
SAMUEL SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. I have two duplicates of a blanket
pawned for half-a-crown, in the name of Ann Brown--the person who pawned
it had one duplicate and I had the other-it was pawned on Saturday the 10th
of April, and taken out on the Monday- cannot say it was by either of the
prisoners.
EBRNEZER CLARK. I am assistant to Mr. Hall, a pawnbroker, in High
street-| have the duplicates of a pair of blankets pawned by a female, but
redeemed again.
