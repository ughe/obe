   Cross-examined. Q.  You knew what cracking  a crib  meant, why not go and tell the police? A.  He was half tipsy, and I did not know whether it was true or not—I do not know now whether it is true or not—it is what he told me—I did not tell the police—I told the prisoner to take it away, that was all I wished—the policeman did not come and talk to me, nor did I give any information—I told my son about it, and it was him that gave informa tion—he was not by at the time the prisoner said he had cracked  a crib  —he does not live with me—my husband was present, but he is deaf, he could not hear what was said—he was before the Magistrate on the first occasion, but was not examined, and on the second he had nothing to say—he is not here to day—all that he heard was the prisoner saying he had got work from Mr. Batty, of Shoreditch—he was sitting at his work about two and a half yards from the prisoner—I did not say when I was first spoken to about it, that I thought the bag had the letter "B" on it—I did not think anything about it—I said the leather had "G" on it—if anybody comes in with work we always look as it—I told him I had looked at the bag—my son was examined before the Magistrate—the police never spoke to me about this—my son spoke to me about it two or three weeks ago—I think it was this day three weeks that my son gave information about it to Mr. Pool—I and my son have not had a great deal of talk about this—I only told him about cracking  the cirb  —he went of his own accord to give information—I did not hear that 10 l . reward was offered till this day three weeks, when I was at the Court—my son did not tell me of it before—I did not bring him here—I saw him here this morning. GEORGE DYER. I am in the employ of Mr. Pool. On the 13th of Feb., when I went to work in the morning, I missed about eleven dozen of work in an apron similar to this one produced—it had the same mark as this, and a diamond—this is produced to show what it was like.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have not found your apron? A.  No. WILLIAM MOSS( policeman. ) I took the prisoner—I told him the charge—he said very well, he would come—Mr. Poole had sent for him—I told him the goods stolen were Clarence fronts, and some other things, and I under stood he had offered some of the same description for sale to a Mr. Hoare—he stood he had offered some of the same description for sale to a Mr. Hoare—he said, "Very well; I am very much obliged to you for telling me what has been brought against me—he was told the charge again at the station, and said, "I never offered any for sale, and never had any in my possession."
