was to get it from Mr. Power. It was going round again in a circle
(To the Judge.) As to the amount that was due from Gordon to
Power, say £25, if Gordon paid that to Power, Power would let him
have a very much larger sum & new loan. (To Mr.
Graham Campbell.) It would be large enough to take up
this cheque and to give Gordon also a considerable amount. The £60
according to my belief was a further accommodation cheque of mine
for Sharp, and it was for Sharp and Gordon to arrange between them-
selves what that was to be for. As I was obliging Gordon by giving!
an accommodation cheque, he would oblige me by obtaining a loan
for me. I had already obliged him the day before by giving him
cheque for £55. As to the £60, what I understood from Gordon was
that Sharp, in fact, wanted the whole amount. I asked Gordon
where the money was coming from to meet the £60 cheque, and ne
informed me from the same source Mr. Power. Gordon has been
financial agent, and I imagined he could raise £700 or £800 if he
wished. Counterfoil No. 012543 (Exhibit No. 1) is in
my handwriting. The cheque belonging to that counterfoil
was a post dated cheque given to a musio-hall artiste named Bracombe,
which he (Gordon) asked me for, which he wanted to show to some
body, and that he himself would find the money to pay the cheque.
Gordon asked me for it, and it was given to a music hall artiste.

foils of July 6 cheques are not in my writing. One, £60, was the

connection with some business that he had. I stated yesterday in
