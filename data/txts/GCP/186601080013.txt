WILLIAM BUTTERICK (City-policeman, 108). At 8 o'clock in the evening
of 18th December, I was at Mr. Wilson's, 8, Bunhill-row--he is a cap-
blocker--I got from him six dozen of "tops" (produced )-I waited in the
shop about ten minutes--I met Wagner in Bunhill-row--I told him I was
an officer, and should apprehend him for stealing twenty-seven yards of
velvet-he said he knew nothing about it.I then took him into Mr.
Wagner's shop, and he said he bought the velvet of a lad named Higgins-
Higgins was given into my custody the same evening, charged with being
concerned at the station, and in the presence of Wagner, he said that they
both went through the passage of the house in Jewin-street, that they got
in at the window, and that Wagner helped himself to a box off a shelf in
the shop, that they went into Aldersgate-street, and that Waguer there
gave him sixpence for the velvet,
