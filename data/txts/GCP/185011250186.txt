Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Where had you come from last
before you saw her ? 4. From Trinity-street- I had been there to see
if I could get some poultry-1 had some friends coming to dinner the next
day—there is a poulterer's shop there, on the right hand side going from
the Borough-I had previously been to Partridge's, my grocer's, to buy
my grocery- I did not leave my office in Stamford-street till nine o'clock
-I am reader to a printer-) then went to Oakley-street for an umbrella,
it was not done; I had to wait for it till ten-I then went to Shep-
herd's, opposite Newington Church, where I had two glasses of ale and a
biscuit for my supper-I staid there till a little after eleven-I then went
to Mr. Partridge's, a grocer's- I got there about a quarter before twelve-
I did not meet any one on my way--I staid there till a little after twelve.
till I got my grocery-I then went back to Trinity-street, to buy some
poultry- I did not get any- the shop was shut up-I then returned on
my way home--when I got to the end of the road, I saw the prisoner
Joitering about-she walked a quarter of a mile with me, against my will
-I saw do policeman-she had hard hold of my arm-if I had used
force, I might have got rid of her-she was talking a parcel of rubbish, and
about the Lord Mayor's show; there was nothing of an amatory nature-
when I got to the end of the quarter of a mile, I stopped to get rid of her
-I said I would not go any further--I then gave her some halfpence-1
had my umbrella in my hand, and my grocery in a parcel - I did not put
it out of my hand till I got to the station-- I know there were several
gates, I did not want her to go in at any of them-I found afterwards
that I had given her 11d-1 gave her that to get rid of her-she did not
go immediately- I was pointing to the policeman's light, and I said,
"There is a policeman, you had better go”-she did not go for about a
minute-I had these trowsers on which I have now, they have no button
on this right-hand pocket, and the pocket is very shallow-I had a
paletót on-she had gone three or four yards from me when I missed my
purse-she did not run, she walked - I turned back, and came to her; I
did not run-she was very unwilling to go till she had robbed me- I had
received these half-crowns from Mr. Partridge, the grocer-his shopman
is in the habit of changing my gold for silver every Saturday-night-there
was not a wicket-gate that I wanted the prisoner to go in at, after I gave
her the 11d., I felt too keen a sense of my moral situation as a
married man.
MR. BRIARLY. Q. Do you persist now that you had received no
service of any kind from her ? A. Not any I had not taken the
slightest liberty with her.
