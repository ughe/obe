 955. NAOMI FROST , sealing 3lbs. weight of bacon, value 2 s  .; the goods of Thomas Mason . RICHARD GOLDING( policeman, V  31.) On 17th Feb., about twelve o'clock in the day, I met the prisoner at Merton Rush, going from Wimbledon to wards her home—I stopped her, and asked if she had anything belonging to any one else in her basket—she said "No"—I said I must see—she said she had a piece of bacon—I found she had two pieces, and said, "I must take you to the station"—she said she hoped I would not take her to Mr. Mason's—I took her to the station, and then took the bacon to Mr. Mason's, and saw it fitted to some bacon, it corresponded. THOMAS CHILTOM. I am shopman to Thomas Mason, of Wimbledon. On 17th Feb. the prisoner came and bought some tea, soap, and salt, but no bacon—I served her—there was some bacon on the next counter, two yards from her—I went to the back of the shop for the salt—she paid me and went away—Golding afterwards brought me some bacon—I then missed a piece—this is the piece which remained ( produced )—it fits exactly—I am quite sure it had been in the shop—I am not certain of the other piece.
   JURY. Q.  Can you swear that one piece was in the shop when the prisoner was there? A.  No.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I picked it up in a hedge and put it into my bundle; I said to the policeman, " Take  me back to Mr. Mason's, where I have been; i have not stolen it." RICHARD GOLDING re-examined.  I am sure she did not say so—I saw her coming out of Mr. Mason's that morning, and should have stopped her them, but a gentleman stopped me. NOT GUILTY .

   ALLEN  pleaded GUILTY . Aged 14.— Confined One Month . MARY ANN ALLEN. I am the wife of George Allen, of Suffolk-street, Southwark—the prisoner is my son—this shawl, frock, and two aprons, are mine ( produced )—my boy was charged with stealing them—he said he had taken them.—( MR. METCALFE objected to any confession of Allen's, implica  ting Challander, being given; Allen not being on his trial [see Rex v. Turner, 1 st Moody's Crown Cases, page  347.]— Allen's confession was not received. )—in consequence of what he said to me, I went with Wise to Chal lander's house—he asked her if she had bought a shawl—she denied it—she had the apron on—Wise searched, and found some pawnbroker's tickets—he said, "Here is a ticket for a shawl"—she said that was not the shawl; that was her own—we went to the pawnbroker's, and found my shawl.
