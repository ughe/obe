 953. JOHN THOMAS was indicted for stealing on the   3rd of April , 1 jacket, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of William Keen : 1 bag, 2 d  .; and 1 mat, 10 d  .; the goods of Edward Duncan , in a vessel in a port of entry and discharge. WILLIAM KEEN. I am a labourer, and live in Well-street, Stepney. On the 3rd of April, I was at work on board the Tory , in St. Katherine's Dock—the cargo was being discharged—I took off my jacket, and laid it on a water cask on deck—I saw the prisoner on board—he went ashore about two o'clock—about a quarter of an hour after, an officer came on board with my jaoket—this produced is it. ALEXANDER MOFFATT. I am an apprentice belonging to the Tory.  The prisoner had no business on board and was ordered off—as he was going over the side a constable stopped him with a bag belonging to the ship—it contained this jacket, and a mat belonging to the ship—Edward Duncan is the captain—the things were in his care. JOHN JONES. I am a watchman in St. Katherine's Dock. On the afternoon of the 3rd of April, I was called on board the Tory  to turn the prisoner ashore—I stopped him with this bag—I asked if it belonged to him—he said, yes—it contained the jacket and mat—I asked if he put them in—he said, no, he knew nothing about it.
 (  The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that the mate had allowed him to take some rubbish, and he put the articles in the bag thinking them of no value, that it was not likely he should steal them, knowing he must be searched on leaving the Dock. ) JOHN JONES re-examined.  Labourers are searched when they leave the docks, and are stopped if they have property in their possession not their own—I have seen the prisoner in the Docks.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) NOT GUILTY.
