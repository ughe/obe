Ann M'Cabe. I am the wife of James M'Cabe. I was in Captain Liy.
ingstone's service some time before his death-in the beginning of Sept. Charles
Davis and Trigg were there on Saturday evening, the 30th of Sept., previous
to the captain's death, there was a ring at the bell --we were down stairs
went up, and my husband and Charles Davis, to the captain's bed-room-he
was in bed in a very weak state-Trigg, Charles Davis, my husband, and I
were in the room- the captain said he wished us to come to his bedside-he
called to Charles Davis-he went to his bedside-we all went--the captain
said, "Charles, come here, I want you"-Charles went-he held the keys up
to Charles, and said, " Take these keys, they are yours, the furniture, and
every thing in the house is yours; take possession of it, and keep Mrs. Cook
out"-I am certain, as near as I can recollect, that these were the words used
-Charles said, “Thank you, I am much obliged to you"-he took the keys,
and put them into his pocket-we shortly after all went down but Trigg, who
remained with the captain--when we got down Charles said he was very much
obliged to him, the captain always promised to be kind to bim for his kind-
ness to him, and the captain had often repeated to me before this that he
