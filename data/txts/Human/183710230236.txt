THOMAS ALLEN I am a private in the 1st Battalion of the Rifle Bri gade at Woolwich. I went with William Allen to the public-house in question—he was no relation of mine—I have heard him called by the name of William Allen—he was not sober when he entered the house—I was sober enough to know what passed—the prisoner sat in the same box with us—they tossed for a pot of beer, and Allen charged the prisoner with cheating him—that brought on a quarrel—the prisoner was not sober—he went out by the direction of the landlord—I and Allen came out after wards, and found the prisoner at the door, and he took and knocked Allen down, directly he got his foot on the step to go out—Allen did not say any thing about fighting the prisoner before he received the blow—I have heard what Barlow has said—it is not true—he did not express any wish to fight the prisoner—he did not say so in the public-house—I did not see him take his coat off—I was in the same box with him—Allen fell to the ground from the blow—I helped to pick him up—the prisoner's son came out of the public-house, and knocked me down—I have since seen the body of William Allen dead—I did not hear Allen express any wish to fight—I do not think he could have taken his coat off unobserved by me—no blow bad been given in the tap-room—the prisoner gave no notice to the deceased before he struck him, nor say any thing to put him on his guard.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What Barlow has stated you say is not true? A.  No, it is not—Barlow was not drunk—I was a little in liquor, but knew what I was doing—I had been with Allen about two hours and a half, drinking with him—he was drunk before I fell in with him—he lived seventeen days after this. ROBERT GIFFIN. I am a labourer, and live near the Dockyard-gate, in New King-street. I was standing in front of the Rodney Arms on the evening in question, when some people were running to the house—I ran over, and saw the prisoner strike Allen in the face—I cannot say in what part—he fell with the blow, and hit his head against the curb-stone—I did not notice whether the prisoner remained or went away—I was looking at the deceased—I heard a voice before they went up, but could not distin guish from whom it proceeded. THOMAS ROSE( police-constable R  154.) I observed some persons col lected in front of the public-house on the night of the 8th of October, and found the deceased lying on the ground, bleeding profusely from the nose and mouth—I took him to the station—I took the prisoner into custody at the end of Brunswick-square, New-road—he came to me and asked if I wanted him—he said, "I hear you have been looking for me"—I said, "Yes, wanted him," and said, "It is an unfortunate job you have got into"—he said, "I should have supposed the blow never could have injured the man in the way described," and he said he struck but one blow.
   Cross-examined, Q.  Did you know him before? A.  Yes, for nearly eight years, as a hard-working, industrious man—he is given to drink, but I never knew him quarrelsome or troublesome—I have always found him ready to assist me in the execution of my duty.
