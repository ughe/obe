ELIZABETA SUNDERLAND. I am the wife of James Sunderland, of 2,
Queen's Terrace-on the night of 23rd February Meek was at my house-I
saw him come in-he asked me to allow him to wash his hands and face,
and I got him a basin and water--the prisoner came in and Meek said Mr.
Lamprell, do you know anything about my wife," and he said "No, and
don't want to know"--the prisoner used bad language to me I was just
outside the door-Meek told him not to use bad language-he said that it
made no odds to him--the prisoner said " If that was & sister of mine I
would punch your head," and made a rush at Meek--I did not see any more,
because the table went over and the lamp went out-Meek said “Bring a
light, I am stabbed."
Cross-examined. My husband was at sea--the prisoner said "What are
you doing with that man in your bedroom and Jim at sea ?"-he had been
in my room about five minutes before that-he lives down by the Victoria
Dooks, I do not know how far off that is I had not seen him before on that
day, but I had the day before, when I met him by aceident, and we had s
glass of ale at the Woodstock-when he came in he took the light out of the
room where we were, and took it into the bed-room--I went in after him,
when I heard them having a few words; the table was not overturned then;
they had not began to fight then--when the prisoner made the rush the
table was overturned and the lamp went to the ground, but I saved the
bottom of it with my hand-they then fought in the dark-I did not see
the prisoner with a knife.
Reexamined. - I was standing against the door-the prisoner's back was to
me as he talked to Meek.

DAVID BEATSON MURDOOK. I am a physician, of 9, Queen's Terrace-I
Gramined Meek and found a small angular wound of the shape of the letter
L between the seventh dorsal vertebræ and the lower part of the blade bone,
about 24 inches from the spine; it was composed of two incisions, each
about an inch deep-there was no danger from the wound itself, because it
struck against the bone; if it had penetrated the chest it would have been
very serious.
Cross-examined. Anything very sharp would do it, but not a piece of
glass, because the clothes were cut so clean the point of an iron nail would
not do it ; the edges were clearly incised.
By The Court. He had on a woollen outer jacket, & waistcoat, and two
Boirts, and they were all cut through-the outer jacket had two cuts, but it
might have been rucked up.
GEORGE MELLISH (Policeman K 459). I took the prisoner the same
evening and charged him with stabbing Meek—he said "I am sorry," and
asked to be allowed to finish his writing-I searched him, but found no knife.
GUILTY of unlawfully wounding-Recommended to mercy by the Jury-
Fourteen Days' Imprisonment.
