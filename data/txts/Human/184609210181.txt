EDWARD PERRONET SELLS, JUN. I am carrying on business with my father and Mr. William Jones, at Bankside, in the parish of St. Saviour, Southwark. We have a stable at White Lion-wharf—we keep fourteen or fifteen horses—Jacobs was horse keeper in our service, and resided on the premises—I know Simpson by sight, and I know his stable—the distance between that and the back door of our stable is thirty yards—it was measured by me and my partner—that back door is to allow the horses to escape in case of fire—it ought never to be opened on any other occasion—it was flat intended for any purpose of business—on the 29th of  Aug. my attention was called to Jacobs being in custody—I saw him in the yard—in the presence of the policeman, and asked how he came in that position—he said he was very sorry indeed, it was the first time he had ever done anything of the kind; he hoped I would not lock him up; he would be sure to remain on the premises till he was required—I told him he was in custody of the police—I went to Simpson's premises, and found a bin with six or seven bushels of corn in it—three sacks, if full, would contain twelve bushels—I compared the corn in the Ma with that on our premises—it was exactly the same in ray opinion—there was chaff and corn lying on the floor besides the corn in the bin—! noticed the oats that were mixed with the chaff—I think they were part of the lame parcel—I heard that there had been some marks of oats seen outside my stable—I looked in the course of the day, and saw a few oats—I did not trace them.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  You have a great many men? A.  We have many—we have lent sacks to persons we know—our men are not allowed to lend sacks off the premises—I do not know whether these are a common quality of oats—it is the first time we had such, to my recollection—it is not uncommon to borrow sacks—if we wanted them we should borrow them ourselves.
   COURT . Q.  Are these oats that are black in these samples damaged oats? A.  No, I do not know that they are—I find the same proportion of shaded oati in both samples—they appear precisely the same—I cannot tea the least difference. ROBERT BUNSFORD( police-constable M  101.) I was with Ross during part of that morning—I took Simpson—I told him what he was taken for—he said he had just heard of it in going along Bankside—he said he  borrowed the sacks of Jacobs for the purpose of carrying pollard and oats to Richmond, where he had a large job, and the oats he bought of Mr. Meek, yesterday—lie said he was in the habit of buying oats of him almost every day. JOHN MEEK. I am a corn—chandler, and live in Holland—street, Blackfiriars—road. Simpson deals with me—my attention has been called to Saturday morning, the 29th of Aug—I sold Simpson bran and beans on the Friday evening—I had not sold him any corn myself—I had not been at home in the daytime—I have ascertained that corn bad been sold to him almost everyday in the week, a sack or two—I am not able to say whether I sold him such corn as this—I had about twenty quarters of such oats as these in my possession, with some black oats mixed with them.
   COURT . Q.  Had you any such corn as this? A.  I think these are Dutch oats—I had about twenty quarters—I think there are more black oats in these than in the Dutch oats I had—I think these are not my Dutch oats, but I had Polish oats—these are not my Polish oats.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Do you mean these are not oats you might have sold him? A.  I cannot say—I have sometimes forty quarters a week, and sometimes do not untie a sack to see what they are.
