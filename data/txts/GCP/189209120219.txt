901. MARY ANN RUTTER (32), Stealing a watch and chain and
other articles, of Henry Wall, in his dwelling-house.
Mr. LYNE Prosecuted, and MR. HUTTON and MR. SANDYS Defended.
SUBAN ALICE WALL. I live at 3, Walpole Street-my husband is an
invalid, and in an asylum-I had his watch, chain, and medal-I put
them away on August 2nd, and did not go to the drawer again till the
27th, when they were gone—the drawer was locked, but the woodwork
above it was broken, but it could not be opened without a key-on 16th
August the prisoner came to the house with my lodger in a cab-I left
the house, and in about ten minutes they passed me in a cab-I have
seen the watch, chain, and medal since, and identified them.
Cross-examined. I only lived in the house six weeks--I have already
prosecuted the other woman, my lodger, who is the prisoner's sister.
HENRY SUTTON. I am a pawnbroker's assistant, of 95, King Street,
Hammersmith-on August 16th two women came; one of them pledged
these articles, and the prisoner was with her.
Cross-examined. I only recognise her as standing in the shop-I had
no difficulty in identifying her at the Police-court.
ELI PRITOHARI) (220 V). On August 30th I went to 1,
Palmerston Road, Kilburn, and I saw the prisoner there-I told
her I was a constable, and should take her in custody for being con-
cerned with her sister in stealing a watch, chain, and medal from Mort-
lakeshe said, "I know nothing about it; I have not been at Mortlake
for a long time”-I said, “ Were not you at Mortlake, and did not you
go to the pawnbroker's ”-she said “No”-I said, "You will have to
accompany me to Barnes Police-station "-on the way she said, “I had
better tell the truth; my sister came to me about a fortnight ago, we
went to her room; she said, I have got no money, but I know where
there is something I can make something with;' she went into Mrs.
Wall's bedroom, and I kept watch. She came back with a watch, chain,
and medal, and we went and pledged them for £1; I destroyed the
ticket in the presence of my sister, because I told her if we kept it she
might get into serious trouble."
Cross-examined. I took a note afterwards--I did not ask for any
questions, she said it voluntarily-I have since heard that she is a
woman of not very strong intellect-her sister pleaded guilty-the
prisoner said that she had no felonious intention Not GUILTY.
