 1709.  ELIZA SUTTON was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 4 pairs of shoes, value 14s., and 1 pair of boots, value 4s., the goods of William Moore.
 WILLIAM MOORE. I am a  boot and shoe-maker, and live at No. 15, Upper King-street, Bloomsbury-square. The prisoner was servant to a lodger of mine for one week, and her mistress was about to discharge her - some shoes of mine were brought down stairs; I sent for an officer, who searched her box in my presence, and found in it a pair of boots, and a pair of shoes; I took one pair of shoes off her feet - two pairs of shoes were found under her bed - I asked her about them - she denied all knowledge of them; a few days before this I went out and double locked my shop door, and the passage door - on my return I found the shop door on the single lock only; I then missed some property.
   WILLIAM JAMES. I am headborough. I searched a box, which the prisoner acknowledged to be her's; I found a pair of boots and a pair of shoes in it; these two pairs were under the bed, and one pair on her feet.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I leave myself to the mercy of the Court.
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 22.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, believing it to be her first offence. Judgment Respited.

 1710.  CAROLINE HOPPER was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , at St. James, Clerkenwell , 1 watch, value 7l.; 1 watch-chain, value 3l.; 1 watch-key, value 4s., and 3 seals, value 3l., the goods of Thomas Haywood Lamb , in his dwelling-house.
   MARY ANN LAMB. I am the wife of Thomas Haywood Lamb; we live in Sidney-street, Goswell-road, in the parish of St. James, Clerkenwell. I know the prisoner, she lived in the neighbourhood. On Friday, the 4th of August, between 3 and 4 o'clock in the afternoon she called at my house, and seemed to have some pressing call upon her; I showed her to the water-closet, on the first floor; she then went away and said she would return in an hour, and then, she supposed, her kettle would boil, and I should take tea with her, or she with me; I went to the room on the first floor soon after she was gone, and missed, from a drawer, a gold watch, a gold chain, three gold seals, and a key, which had been in that drawer on the Monday before; I had not seen them after Monday; they were my husband's; the prisoner had been in that room a fortnight before, and I had shewn them to her in the drawer; these are the articles.
   CHARLES SANDERS. I am a pawnbroker. I produce all these articles, except one seal; they were pawned with me on Friday, the 4th of August, by the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. How came you to be so sure of her: you only said you believed so before the Magistrate? A. Yes, but I spoke to her after she left the bar, and her voice convinced me that she was the person; I am certain of her.
   THOMAS THOMPSON. I am a constable. I took up the prisoner on Thursday, the 10th of August, in Blackfriars-road. The prosecutor had given me the information on the Sunday, and I went to look for her, but she had left her own house; when I took her I told her I was a constable, and I had come respecting a watch; she said she had never been happy since she took it, and she had written a letter to the prosecutrix or her husband, I do not know which; and meant to give herself up that night.
 COURT to Mrs. LAMB. Q. How long had you known her as a neighbour? A. About seven or eight weeks - she first came to inquire about a room in my house; she lived with a man who was a carpenter.
   SAMUEL SHARWOOD. I live in St. John's-street-road, and am a pawnbroker. I produce the other seal pawned by the prisoner on the 4th of August. GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, supposing it her first offence, believing her to have had a good character before, and appearing sorry for what she had done.
 1711.  THOMAS POWELL and WILLIAM PERCIVAL were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Warren , about six o'clock in the forenoon of the 28th of June , at St. Leonard, Shoreditch ( Henry Warren being therein,) and stealing 5 cages, and 5 singing birds, value 27s., the goods of the said John Warren.
 JOHN WARREN. I live at  Old Haggerstone, near King's-land-road, in the parish of St. Leonard, Shoreditch. On the 28th of June I saw my birds and cages safe at four o'clock in the morning, when I and my wife went out to work - I had then given them water - there was an outcry in the field about half-past six o'clock, and I missed my birds and cages; the back door was open - I saw them again in about ten minutes; but I did not see either of the prisoners till they were in custody about seven o'clock.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Was it light when you went out? A. Yes; I found my baby, Henry Warren, safe in his bed - he was not awake.
   WILLIAM WILSON. I am a servant to the Gas-light company. On the morning of the 28th of June, I saw the two prisoners at Warren's house, trying the padlock of the door - they could not get in at the front door, and they went to the back - I saw them come out with some property tied in two handkerchiefs - I followed them to Hackney-road, and gave an alarm; I saw them stopped with the property on them.
