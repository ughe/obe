MR. BARON MARTIN was of the same opinion. After alluding to the
peculiar form of the plea, which omitted to aver the identity of the crime now
charged, with that of which the prisoner had been acquitted, and which omission
in his opinion, was fatal to the plea, he referred to that portion of the
argument founded upon the maxim in Hale and other authors, that no man
could be tried twice for one and the same crime; that maxim presented a true
criterion by which to test this question ; was the crime here one and the same!
now the offence for which the prisoner had been tried was one of intent, and
was therefore complete the moment the stab was given; whereas, the offers
for which he was now indicted, could only be consummated by the death
the party. To the mind of any lawyer, this must be deemed conclusive
as against the plea, which, on the grounds already referred to, was not to be
supported.
MR JUSTICE WILLES gave judgment to the same effect. In order to support
this plea, it must be shown that the former acquittal was an acquittal of all the
state of facts which might constitute the party a murderer. Now, on comparing the
two indictments, it was clear that the Jury had not so acquitted him ; all the
was then disposed of was, that he did not wound with intent to kill; it could
not be assumed that they negatived the wounding; therefore, if the wotending
coupled with circumstances not showing an intention to kill, might constitute

be the case, was clearly shown by the fact, that persons inflicting wounds while
