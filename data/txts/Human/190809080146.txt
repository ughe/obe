   BEFORE JUDGE LUMLEY SMITH .
 (Monday, September 14.) Charles PURKIS  ,, and Arthur SINGER  , ; both stealing three ice cream freezers, the goods of Thomas Reynolds .
 Mr. Forrest Fulton prosecuted; Mr. W.W. Grantham defended. THOMAS HILLYER, 849, Forest Road, Walthamstow. On July 4, I was lodging in Purkis's house; the other prisoner was not there; I could not tell you where he was. (The witness was cautioned.) I went to bed about 11 o'clock. Purkis slept in the same room. I was not wakened up in the night. I saw Purkis get into bed and saw him get up in the morning about 9. I could not tell exactly when he came to bed. I know No. 2, Jeffrey Square, but I saw nothing in connection with that house.
 Mr. Forrest Fulton now drew his Lordship's attention to a state  ment made by the witness in presence of police officers, which was voluntary, and he asked to be allowed to treat witness as hostile.
 Judge Lumley Smith said that it was not likely the jury would act on the statement if it was contrary to what was said at another time.
 Witness (to the Judge). The statement was made because I was paid for it by the police; it is not true. His Lordship said that no jury would convict on the evidence given by a man who declared that what he had said the other day was untrue. There being no other evidence, he therefore directed a verdict of Not guilty. Frederick RICHARDS  , (26, coster) ; stealing one horse, the goods of John William Ashton .
