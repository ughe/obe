ROBERT PARRIS. I am a surveyor, and live at 5, Walsingham-terrace,
Lainbeth-the prisoner came to me in July, and said she wished to speak to
me about a little business—she was a stranger to me, and I asked who
she came from, she said she was recommended by a person of the name of
Wright, and she produced two letters purporting to come from a person of the
Dame of Thomas Henry Wood, of 13, Royal-crescent, Bath; precisely the
same as the evidence Mr. Davies has given-she asked my advice as to the value
of the property-I told her I could not tell her anything about the value,
unless I saw it-and I suggested that I should go and see it—the letters
stated that the land was four acres, and that an offer of 954. an acre had
been made-she mentioned that she was entitled to some money from Mr.
Wood, and he was in the habit of sending it by Post-office orders, and the
person wbo had formerly received it for her was dead, and she wanted to
know if I would receive it for her, I said I would- I think the sum of
141. 6s. 6d. was mentioned in the letter-I forget whether the prisoner
mentioned the sum or not-she said it would come on 29th--this was about
a week before that date-sbe wished me to write to Mr. Wood, and tell him that
I would receive the money-she asked me to let her post the letter as she
was anxious to give Mr. Wood an immediate answer, as she always did I
said I would post it myself I wrote to Mr. Wood, stating she wished me
to receive the money for her, addressed it to the address she gave me, and
posted it myself—I received an answer on 26th, but have destroyed it-the
substance of the letter was that he considered the land of more value than
bad been offered for it, that he would transmit the money to me on 29th.
in three Post-office orders, and also mentioned about her aunt being ill at
Carlisle, and requested her to go immediately- I called on the prisoner twice,
and she was not at home-she came to me in the evening-I told her that
Mr. Wood considered the property was worth more than had been offered
as there was building about I think she said she should be entitled to
property from her aunt to the amount of 360l. a year, but I would rather
not say what amount she said she said she could not go to Carlisle until she
had her money, and I said I could advance her the money, and did so on
the strength of getting it on the following Tuesday-I advanced her the
precise amount, 141. 68. 60.-1 received no Post-office order, and my letter
came back from Bath through the Post-office-I went to Bath myself, went
to 13, Royal-crescent, but could not find Mr. Wood, or any account of him.
Cross-examined. Q. She never asked you to let her have the money!
4. No; I offered it to her-I should not have done so only she said she
could not go on account of not baving it-she was a perfect stranger to me
Horsford did not go to Bath with me-- I went as much to see Bath as any-
thing.
Mr. Bodkin. R. Would you have advanced the money if she had not
told you she expected the 141. 6s. 6d. ? A. Decidedly not.
