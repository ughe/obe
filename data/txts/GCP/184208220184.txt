WILLIAM MURPHY. I am porter at the University College Hospital.
On the 4th of July the deceased was brought there-his head and face ap-
peared very much swollen, and particularly the right cheek-there was
small bruise or cut on the right side of his nose--he did not point that out
to me-he said something about it.
HENRY BURFORD NORMAN. I am house-surgeon at the University
Hospital. On the 15th of July, about twelve o'clock in the day, I saw
the deceased-his face was then very much swollen from erysipelas, parti-
cularly on the right side-the right eye was almost closed-I saw nothing
more than a slight contusion of the eye lid-it was difficult to say how
much of the swelling belonged to the blow, and how much was caused by
the erysipelas-the whole of the right side of the face was swollen, and it
was extending on the left side also-- I found that he was a person of
drunken habits--I cannot say whether he was then perfectly sober, from the
change produced by disease-I talked to him, and he stated he had been
struck the day before in the eye, but I did not inquire very particularly
into it-he said he had been drinking porter and gin for the last eight or
nine days, and she believed he had not eaten a quarter of a loaf in that
time, or a quartern loaf-he died on the 19th from erysipelas, which I
supposed to be caused by the blow.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You cannot undertake to say that?
A. No-erysipelas is a disease very little understood as to its cause--there
is no doubt that he died from erysipelas-it will sometimes seize on a person
of intemperate habits without any exciting cause, or it may arise from natu-
ral causes-it is more likely to arise from an open cut than from a contusion
-I did not see him till the 15th-the first surgeon who saw him is not
here-I should not say he had delirium tremens, only simple delirium,
which is the natural result of intemperate habits-it would be the more
likely to result to a man who had had no solid food for eight or ten days
I cannot undertake to say the blow had anything to do with his death.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. I believe erysipelas very frequently follows
wounds ? A. It does-I saw no cut-it might not be a cut though the
skin is broken--if the skin is abraised it would be likely to be followed by
something of this kind-if it should turn out that there had been abrasion
of the skin on the 13th, I could not say with confidence that this blow was
the cause of death-I cannot say with confidence that the blow was the
immediate cause of the erysipelas, but I should be inclined to attribute
it to it.
RICHARD Quain. I am surgeon at the University College Hospital,
and am professor at the College. I saw the deceased on the 15th of July
-I saw his body after death-I attributed his death to erysipelas cade
not state with confidence that the blow on the nose was the cause that
produced the death-if I had seen him from the time of his receiving the
blow I might have been able to say.
NOT GUILTY.
