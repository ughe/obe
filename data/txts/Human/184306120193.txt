   Q.  Upon your oath, if you had received an assignment from the prisoner of the money due to him, should you have prosecuted this case? A.  Yes, just the same—I told him I should prosecute him—I did not give him in charge before the Tuesday, because I was waiting till after the mother's funeral—she was not buried till the Sunday week; but it was on the Sunday after her death I had the conversation with the prisoner—I did not give him in custody on that Sunday, because I was in so much trouble—I will swear I had not been asking him to execute an assignment of that property—I dined with him and his friend in Moorgate-street on the Monday—his friend had received a cheque for 30 l . from Mr. Shad well—I do not know whether the dinner was paid for out of that money—the prisoner had 5 l . of it—it was an understanding with his friend that he would procure him a situation for 25 l .—I do not know that the prisoner preferred trusting that friend to me. I am the carpenter of the brig Britannia, lying in Bugby Hole. On the night of the 15th of June I met the prisoner in Ratcliff High way, and went home with her between ten and eleven o'clock—I gave her 3 s . and went to bed—I put my jacket, and trowsers, and waistcoat on the floor—I had a pocket-handkerchief in my pocket—I awoke about two in the morning—I got up and said to the prisoner, "Where is my jacket?"—she said, "It is all right"—I said, "You had better give it me now"—she refused—I came out and gave her is charge—the officer searched another apartment and found my jacket—this is it—I have not found my handkerchief.

It is not true that I had no money—I asked her for my jacket—she said she would split my head with a weight—I did not ask her to look at my head. GEORGE HAILES( police-constable K  54.)
