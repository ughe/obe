 1347.  WILLIAM DIXON was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of June , one sheet, value 7s.; four handkerchiefs, value 2s.; three shirts, value 7s.; three pair of stockings, value 4s., and one apron, value 1s., the goods of Richard Rawley.
 RICHARD RAWLEY I am a  baker, and live in George-street, Spitalfields. On the 24th of June, about half-past nine o'clock in the morning, my child alarmed me, and said there was a man in the first-floor room, I ran up; when I got to the top of the stairs, my street - door was opened, and a person said a man had ran out of my private door with a bundle of linen. I ran out, but lost sight of the person. I sent for an officer, the people said the prisoner was up a passage in the street - we were examining the house, and heard a noise in the street; the officer ran out and took the prisoner. I found my room-door open, and the articles stated in the indictment gone.
   THOMAS REYNOLDS. I am a cheesemonger. I heard of the robbery. I watched outside while Hardy, the constable, searched a house three doors from the prosecutor's. I saw the prisoner come out of another door of the prosecutor's house in Thrall-street, I pursued, and stopped him in Fashion-street, and found the sheet tied round his body, and the rest of the things in a bundle.
   THOMAS HARDY. I am a constable. I was searching a house, I heard he had run from another door; I saw him running, and secured him in Fashion-street. I found the bundle on him. He said he broke the door open and took the things out of the room; that he lived by thieving, and if he had his liberty to-morrow, he would thieve again.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I hope I shall be sent abroad for life.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1348.  SARAH CULLUM was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , five sheets, value 12s.; two shirts, value 2s.; two aprons, value 1s.; one umbrella, value 6d.; one table-cloth, value 1s.; one waistcoat, value 1s.; two shifts, value 3s.; one frock, value 1s.; one bed curtain, value 3s.; three handkerchiefs, value 3s.; one shawl, value 1s.; one pair of boots, value 1s., and one napkin, value 1s., the property of Philip Elias.
   ESTHER ELIAS. I am the wife of Philip Elias, who is a furrier, and lives in Bell-lane, Spitalfields. I buy and sell old clothes in the street; the prisoner had lived servant with me about a year and a half; I missed things several times, and asked her about them; she said she knew nothing about them. I examined my drawers, and missed these things; I asked her about them, she said they were being mangled. I had got my things from the mangler's the Friday before. I told her to fetch them; she returned, saying, I should have them in half an hour. I went to the mangler's; in consequence of what I heard I examined, and missed all the property stated in the indictment. I got an officer, and the prisoner gave me about sixteen duplicates, I told her she had more; she then gave me a purse containing thirty-three, eight of which were her own, the rest related to my property, by which means I found it. She said she spent the money in gin.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am servant to Mr. Pearl, who is a pawnbroker, and lives in Whitechapel. I have an apron, a sheet, a shift, a frock, a table-cloth, a waistcoat, and a curtain, pledged with me by the prisoner. The duplicates found on her are our's.
   JAMES HAVEL. I am a pawnbroker. I have twelve articles pledged with me by the prisoner.
   WILLIAM FLETCHER. I have two articles pledged with me by the prisoner.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I intended to redeem them.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
