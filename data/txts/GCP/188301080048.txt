WILLIAM PERCIVAL. I live at Brixton- I am corresponding clerk to

Mr. George Mayor called me in-I must have seen the bonds—in con-
sequence of what Mr. Mayor said to me I telephoned to Messrs. Burt,
the money-changer's--that was in the public telephone office-I took
their reply back to the prisoner-I said the bonds were worth about 171.
each if any one is in the private room they can hear what is said in
the pul lic office if they listen-I believe Mr. Mayor had left before that
- the prisoner hesitated, and then said I must confer with my friends"
-I asked for his address, and he gave me this envelope out of his
pocket, “8, Cowley Street, Palace Yard, Westminster," and to that
address I afterwards communicated-he mentioned the name of Stephen-
son and Co., of Westminster, in connection with the bonde, leading me
to think that they were connected with them-he left the office pre-
sumably t. see if his friends would accept the value I quoted, and on

-thet was in the private room where Mr. Mayor had been-some one

Cross-examined by MR. COTTINGHAM. The envelope has been through
the post-Stephenson and Co. are engineers at Westminster-it did not
occur to me to telephone to them, the case being handed over to me by
Mr. George Mayor-I wrote down “Stephenson and Co." in a rough,
hasty way, simply the initials—I took no note of the conversation—the
prisoner did not leave the bonds-I said before the Magistrate, “You
left the bonds with me simply for the purpose of ascertaining their
value"-I think he left the bonds with me the two interviewe lasted
about 10 minutes each--the production of the bonds had a great deal to
do with my parting with the cheque-if they had not been produced I
