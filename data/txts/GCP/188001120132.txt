GEORGE MOOKE. I am a gupner in the Royal Artillery-on the 27th of
December last, about 6.30, I was passing the barrack-room; I heard a noise,
and opened the door I saw the prisoner with a sword-he stabbed Murphy
once, and making a second attempt-I went for assistance when I came
back the disturbince was at an end.
Cross-examined. I saw a table on one of the beds Baldwin was there
drunk, and looking like a man waking out of a horrified sleep-I could not
hear any distinct words - the door was shut, but not locked.
ROBERT SMITH. I am a surgeon at Woolwich on the 27th December I
was called in to see Murphy and Dewdney, about 7.15 p.m.-Murphy had
án incised wound in the neck; and another incised wound in the
side of the mouth on the left cheek- they were serious at one
time, but not the next morning - there were no after symptoms of
erysipelas, or anything of that kind -Dewdney had three wounds,
one in the Deek at the top of the breast bone, another in the chest,
and a third on the shoulders-Murphy I detained in the hospital, Dewdney
I sent out the same night about an hour afterwards—his wounds were not
dangerous.
Cross-examined. Wounds to the same extent would not have been made
simply in defence—Dewdney's wound was three inches deep, but went upwards
under the skin agaiust the chest wall; it could not penetrate- I noticed
the condition of the men-Mnrphy was considerably the worse for drink-
Dewdney was less so, but more excited.
JOAN BALDRY (Policeman R 27). I was on reserve duty at the Wool-
wich Police-station on 27th December, about 10 p.m.--the prisoner was
brought in, and charged with feloniously cutting and wounding Murphy
and Dewdney-the prisoner said "What I did, I did in self defence."
The Prisoner received a good character.
