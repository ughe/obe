   Q.  Just look at this note, and tell me when you sent this to the pri soner? A.  It is dated Thursday evening; I believe that was on the 15th, five days after this transaction. ( Reads )—"I am desirous that you should call upon me, and pay for the goods you have had, or else give such reference as will give satisfaction. Punctuality is the very hinge of business."
   Q.  And on the 17th he sent you a bill for 18 l .? A . Yes; but I had not had the order—he had goods after the blue transaction, but I did not know how this was—he did not come to me after he had had the blue—he wrote me a plausible letter on the 14th of January—the 18 l . bill would more than cover the blue transaction and the other, but I am not satisfied with the bill—I do not think it is good—it is not due yet—if it should be paid, I shall take legal advice to know whether I might deduct the money for the blue. FRANCIS BISHOP. I am clerk to Wrench and Co. I manage their bills—I am generally employed in the counting-house—I never authorised the prisoner to draw upon them for any money—I have never paid any draft he has drawn—he has never drawn any—he has never given us cre dit for fourteen days—we never had any goods of him—if he ever had authority to draw upon us, I must have known it—I never authorized him to get a cask of blue, nor have Wrench and Co.—he did not send us in a cask of blue on the 10th of January—there is no truth in any part of the statement.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is Mr. Wrench here? A.  No—there is no other clerk there—a gentleman was assisting at the time in arranging the affairs of the late firm of Turner and Wrench—that firm was put an end to on the 31st of December, 1833—Mr. Wrench could not give any orders in town; he was at Newcastle-upon-Tyne at the time—I bought goods at that time—I have not the books of the house here—Mr. Jacob Wrench is the only person in the business—it goes by the name of Wrench and Co., but there is no partner. WILLIAM DAGGET. I am clerk to Messrs. Fothergills. My em ployer had a cask of blue, on the 10th of January, of a man named Hen derson—it came a little after four o'clock—we gave 42 s . a cwt. for it—it came to 4 l . 7 s . 4 d .—it weighed 233lbs.—Mr. Roberts has seen the same cask. ME. ROBERTS. I have no doubt it is the same cask. CHARLES TRIMMINGS. I was hired by a man named Henderson, who was with the prisoner, to draw a cask, which he said was coming from Mr. Roberts, of Fish-street-hill—he hired me down by the iron bridge—I took the cask from Mr. Roberts' to Fothergills'—I went up Fish-street-hill—I could not get up, and the prisoner helped me—I met Henderson at the corner of Little Trinity-lane—he told me to follow him, and he led me to Fothergills'. GUILTY. Aged 21.—See Kent Cases, page 668.
