1034. JAMES SMITH, forging and uttering an order for the delivery of
9 printed books; with intent to defraud Thomas Jones Ellison : other
COUNTS, with intent to defraud James Burns.
WILLIAM Ellison. I am a bookbinder, in Are Maria-lane. On Monday
erening, 13th March, the prisoner came to my shop, and gave me this order
-read-(Thursday, 18th March, 1848. Mr. Ellison, please to let the bearer
have six French Revolutions and three Don Quixotes, and you will oblige
Mr. Burns, Portman-square)-We work for Mr. Burns asked the pri-
soner who sent him-he said, "Mr. Burns"-I asked if he was employed
there-he said, "Yes"-I asked how long he had been there-he said,
"Six months" -I detained him while I went to Mr. Buros.
Prisoner. He said at Guildhall, I told him Mr. Buros had written the
order. Witness. You might have said 80-I asked you a great variety of
questions-I did not ask if Mr. Burns wrote it-I think you said Mr. Burns
gave you the order, and when Mr. Burns came, you said a man bad sent
you in with it-you told a great many falsehoods.
JAMES Burns. This order is not my writing I do not know the pri-
soner-I never saw him before I never gave him this order, or authorised
him or any one to write it.
WILLIAM MILLS (City policeman, 352.) I took the prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. A man said he would give me sixpence to take it.
Guilty of Uttering. Aged 17.-Confined Four Months.
