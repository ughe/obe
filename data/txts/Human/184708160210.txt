CHARLOTTE SOPHIA CLIFTON re-examined.  This needle-case is mine—I know it, because I bought it myself—there is no mark on it; it was in my dressing-room—I have a person to come and work and mend my clothes—this necklace is mine, it was given me as a present, it is made of Roman pearls—I should think it was worth half-a-guinea, that was what the lady told me when she gave it me—there is no gold about it, it is a peculiar sort of necklace—it was on my dressing-table; she must have taken it off my dressing-table, from one of my china-jars—this scent-bottle I know; I have had it a long time, and the pincushion—I never gave them to the pri soner—they were not found in her trunk—the prisoner was dressed very neatly, and was very clean in her person while in my service—I did not supply her with clothes or ornaments of any sort—I got her character form a friend of hers, living at West Drayton—she was never in service before—I did not ask her age—my reason for parting with her was not on account of the dis pute about the sovereign—she was going to leave me on the Saturday night; her month was up—she had not given me warning, she said she was not going into service any more—I had told her long before, that she was not ser vant enough for me; she could not keep the house clean
   MR. METCALF. Q.  Who is the person whom you said you had to work for you—I understood you just now that you had no one in the house but the prisoner and a lady? A.  She was not there on the Saturday; she was there on the Tuesday, to mend the clothes—she has been in the habit of coming there ever since I have been in the house, which is fourteen years—I have very few ladies come to visit me, and I never receive gentlemen, not one—the prisoner's bed-room was down in the kitchen, but as the lady had her lady's maid she said she would lay on the sofa, in the front parlour, I was in the back—the lady that was with me had the second-floor, and I had the ground-floor—there is no bed-room on the drawing-room floor—there are two draw ing-rooms—I was present sometimes when the prisoner went to bed, no one else was present—she has never had to go to bed in the presence of any one besides me—when I went to bed, I saw her on the sofa—if I was at home, we went to bed together—she has never had to go to bed in any one else's presence, unless it was my sister's; she was not asked to go to bed in any one else's presence—we were alone—she never went to bed, if any one was in the room—we were quite alone on Friday night—if I went out, she went to bed, and I let myself in—the ladies I had with me, just came to stop for an hour, and went away again—when I went out and let myself in, perhaps I have gone to a party, or to the theatre, with ladies; I do not require gentlemen when I go out at that time of night—we go to the theatre in the evening, and come home in proper time, without any gentlemen—the time I come home, depends upon where I go to—if I am at a ball I come home at one or two o'clock—I do not often go to balls—I do not think I have been at theatres once a month.
   Q.  Who is the professional lady living in the house? A.
