   ROBERT WARD. I am a watchman of St. Mary-le-bone. The prisoner came to lodge in the same house with me in Jubilee-court, Chelsea, at the latter and of March; he said he had spent a great deal of money, and was very much distressed - he offered to sell me a duplicate of a watch, two seals and a key, for 2l.; I bought it of him, and told him if he would come to me in two or three months and give me my money, he might have it again - I went to redeem the watch with my pension money, and the pawnbroker would not give it me.
 Prisoner. you were to keep it for me. A. No; I bought it but I said you might have it again.
   JOSEPH HARRIS. I am a pawnbroker. A watch, two seals, and a key, were pawned with me for 2l. on the 24th of March, by a man in the name of John Smith - I do not know the person, but I gave this duplicate; Ward applied to me, but I had heard something.
 JOHN MASHFIELD. I am in the employ of Mr. Cubit; I lived in  Francis-street: Mr. Charles Marriott kept the house. On the 17th of March the prisoner came there for employ; he was recommended by a clergyman, and I put him to work - I provided him with tools and clothes, and let him have some money - On the Friday following he came and said he had seen his brother and he should decline working any more, as one Mr. Scott, a banker in the City was dead, and he was entitled to 1200l. - he said he was to be buried at Norwood, and he shewed me some clothes for mourning, which he got from a tailor: I lent him 23s. to pay his expences; he went on Sunday morning, and he promised to return on the Sunday afternoon about four o'clock, but he did not: I lent him the watch in question expecting him to return- I got some information, and found it at Mr. Harris's; I did not find the prisoner till the 25th of August, when I took him in a field where he was at work.
 Q. Where did you lend him this watch? A. I did not lend it him; he took it himself, and said, "You may as well let me have this to appear respectable; I will return it in the afternoon.

 1805.  DANIEL SULLIVAN was indicted for feloniously assulting Thomas Warner , on the King's highway, on the 23d of May , putting him in fear and taking from his person, and against his will, 5 sovereigns, and 6 shillings, his monies.
   THOMAS WARNER. I am a book-binder. Between eleven and twelve o'clock, on the night of the 23d of May I was coming from Duke-street across Smithfield to the East-side; I met two females near the Bear and Raggedstaff - they caught hold of each arm, and said "We have got you now, we will not let you go without you give us some gin;" I tried to get away, but could not - I then gave them a glass of gin each - I did not have any myself; we remained but a very short time in the house - they then turned into Smithfield - I remained a minute or two at the door, and said, "Now for Clerkenwell;" the prisoner and another man were there - the prisoner said to me, "You live in Clerkenwell;" I said, yes - he said, "You are not far from it;" I said, I know it - I looked at him, thinking he was a neighbour, and we went on together towards Bath-street, where I live - we crossed the way towards Cow-cross, and I turned up Peter's-lane- the prisoner was at my right-hand, and I had no sooner got to the top where the posts are fixed, than I received a blow on my left ear which knocked my hat off - I turned round and said, "That is what you mean is it?" I seized my money out of my pocket into my hand - there were six sovereigns and twelve shillings; I tried to save myself, but could not- I was thrown down, and the prisoner seized my hand, and said, "You b-g-r let go;" I was obliged to let go, and he received the money into his left hand - he turned back my hand, and took it; I saw it as plain as I see you now - I never saw my hat afterwards; the prisoner went off to my right-hand: I called Watch! and at length one came from St. John's-lane - I told him I had been robbed - I did not go back the same way, as I thought it might be worse for me; we went up St. John's-lane - I am certain the prisoner is the person: my eyes were fixed on him several times in walking along, when we came to a gaslight.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How many gaslights did you pass? A. I do not know; the young ladies turned into Smithfield - I had not seen them before; I did not take any of the gin myself; I had been drinking two or three pints of beer, but no spirits; I was not considered tipsy when the watchman came up, nor I should think the worse for liquor; the prisoner was not taken up till the 1st of September; I gave information on the 31st of August - I did say that I had been treating two women, and I might be a little tipsy; Phillips asked me if I knew the person that robbed me, and I said I did not - he is not a particular friend of mine; I borrowed a hat of him, and called on him a fortnight afterwards and paid him for it - he asked me if I had had this person apprehended; I told him No, but I could have him apprehended at any time; the reason I did not have him apprehended was, he was a character so well known - he was out of the way; I was taken ill, and could not go through such a fatigue: I mean I had not got his name and place of abode. On Saturday, the 6th of September, I called again on Phillips; I told him I had been to Hatton-garden Office, and had parties apprehended for this robbery; I think he asked if they were the parties I had been speaking about before, but I did not say "No, it was a neighbour of mine" - I am quite sure I did not tell him they were not the same parties I was speaking about before; I said the watchman knew the prisones, and he gave me the first intimation, knowing the prisoner and one of the women passing his beat repeatedly, but he did not know the prisoner's name; I will not positively say whether I said it was upon the watchman's information that I took this man up.
