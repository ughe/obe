2493. ELIZABETH GOODYEAR was indicted for stealing, on the
28th of August, 2 shirts, value 2s. ; 4 bandkerchiefs, value 38.; 1 waistoont
value 28.; 1 bonnet, value 38. ; 1 shilling, and 13 pence; the goods and
monies of Ann Goodyear.
ANN GOODYEAR. I am a widow, and live in York-street, Westminster
The prisoner is my daughter-she was not living with me on the 28th
August-I cannot say how she got her bread or where she was at that time
-I went out on the 28th of August, at seven o'clock in the morning
leaving the property stated locked in a drawer in my room--the room dock
was not locked-I returned at eight o'clock in the evening--my drawer
was locked then, but the property had been taken out, I cannot tell how-
I had seen it safe there in the morning, before I went out-I found somed
it at the pawnbroker's.
MARY ANN GOODYEAR. I am the daughter of the prosecutrix, and
am nine years old. The prisoner is my sister-I remember my mother
going out at seven o'clock in the morning, on the 28th of August, and
eight o'clock in the morning the prisoner came to my mother's room,
went out, and left her there for about an hour-she was there wben 1
came back-she went away afterwards.
WILLIAM THOMAS BARTON. I am foreman to Mr. Cotton, a pawnbroker.
I have two shirts and two handkerchiefs, pawned by the prisoner on the
28th of August, in the name of Ann Williams.
JAMES WEBSTER JONES. I am an officer. I took the prisoner-I pro-
duce some of the property, which was given up by a pawnbroker at the
office.
Prisoner's Defence. I am not guilty-I have been turned out several
times when I go near my mother she accuses me of taking things.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutrit.
Transported for Seven Years.
