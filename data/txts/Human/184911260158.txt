MR. HORRY called ANN BENHAM. My husband is a labourer, at Sydenham—I have known the prisoner eight or nine months, but have known very little of her; she lodged with me three weeks—I always found her sober and honest—after she left Mrs. Whitaker's, I went there to oblige Mrs. Whitaker till she got suited—the tea and sugar I bought for the prisoner on the Saturday as she went to her new situation on the Monday—she was to have 16 l . a year, and find her own tea and sugar. GUILTY. Aged 34.— Strongly Recommended to mercy by the Jury.— Confined One Month,
 149. SARAH STANTON , stealing 2 gowns, 1 waistcoat, 1 comb, and other articles, value 3 l  .; the goods of Charles Louis Amie De Bergue . SOPHIA DE BERGUE. I am the wife of Charles Louis Amie De Bergue—I live with my brother Frederick William Billings—the prisoner was our servant—on the 18th Nov., in consequence of missing some articles, I went into her bed-room, and found two silk dresses of mine between the mattress and the bed—I told my brother; and in the evening I charged the prisoner with stealing them—she produced a comb of mine out of her pocket—she was taken into custody; and next day I went with the sergeant to the Servants'-Home in Millman-street—some boxes were opened there, in there prisoner's presence—she had given us the keys the night before, hoping that we should not give her into custody, and said we should have the things back—I found two handkerchiefs, two neck-ties, and a pair of scissors of mine—I missed a coat of my husband's, and five tea-spoons, which I found in a drawer, into which a box of my mother's had been emptied—the clothes have not been found—she was only with me a few days. JOHN MAY( policeman ). On 15th Nov. I took the prisoner—I received some keys from Mrs. De Bergue, and went with her and her brother to the Servants'-Home—I opened some boxes which the prisoner had described to me, and found this lace, handkerchiefs, and ornament ( produced ); and between her bed and mattress I found these two dresses—the value of the whole is 7 l . or 8 l GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined One Year .

 150. MARIA CRONIN, JOHN BROWN, THOMAS JONES, and MARGARET THOMPSON were indicted for unlawfully uttering counter feit coin. MESSRS. BODKIN and DAWSON conducted the Prosecution. SARAH HAGGERTY. I am the wife of James Haggerty, and am servant to Mr. West, at the Marquis of Granby, High-street, Woolwich. On 18th Nov., about half-past eight o'clock, the prisoners came and had a pot of por ter, and some bread and butter—I served them; they gave me 1 s .—the two women went out, and returned again in about ten minutes—they stopped till ten, and all went away together.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you know them? A.  No; I did not notice the womens dress. SARAH HARRISS MURRAY. I am the wife of John Money Murray, tobacconist; of Woolwich. On 18th Nov., between eight and nine o'clock at night, Cronin came for half an ounce of tobacco, it came to 1 1/2 d .—she gave me a crown-piece, which was bad—I told her so—she said she was not aware of it, a man gave it her, and she would leave a mug which she had and fetch him—I allowed her to go, and kept the crown-piece—a policeman came, I marked it, and gave it to him.
