 503.  HENRY WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , 1 coat, value 10s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 5s.; 1 waistcoat, value 3s.; and 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s.; of Thomas Awdos Maling , being in a vessel in the port of London, the same being a port of entry and discharge.
   THOMAS AWDOS MALING. I belong to a brig from Sunderland - she was lying in Gun Dock, Wapping- on the 19th of February I first missed a coat, and then the other things which had been taken from the cabin - the prisoner was taken on board, but the property was found at the pawnbroker's - he has been my apprentice since June, 1832.
   EUGENE KEARDON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce the coat, trousers, waistcoat, and handkerchief, which were pawned by the prisoner in the name of John Howard, on the 18th of February.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Three Months.
 504.  MARTHA BRICKLE was indicted for stealing, on the 6th February , 1 coat, value 1l.; 1 pair of pattens, value 6d.; 1 gown, value 6d.; and one pair of boots, value 1s. 6d.; the goods of George Brickle.
   GEORGE BRICKLE. I am cutter to a tailor, and live in Chapel-street, Curtain-road- the prisoner is my daughter, and was living at home with me - on the 6th of February she stole a coat, a pair of pattens, a gown, and a pair of boots - my wife got up in the morning and missed them.
   ANN BRICKLE. I am the prosecutor's wife - the prisoner stole these things out of the room where they were kept - I work at the tailoring business - she is my own daughter, and is seventeen years old - I have one more child.
   JOHN BOARD. I am a pawnbroker, and live at No. 42, Shoreditch - I have a coat and pair of boots pawned by the prisoner on the 6th of February; but, as I stated at the Police-office, I thought she had been sent with them by her parents, that they might come and get them for nothing - some time ago this man was living with another wife, who, I believe, was his lawful wife, and he in a very bad way obtained a jacket of me; but I gave it up rather than have any more trouble about it; I never heard any more of him till the morning the prisoner pawned these goods; and soon after she had pawned them, I saw him come and stand opposite my shop; he went away and came and stood there again for some time, and the next morning I heard that these things had been stolen - I said I was sorry I had taken them in, but I would not give them up, for I had given away pounds worth of things sooner than come here - when the prosecutor was at the office, he stated that he had not seen the girl all day, but I feel certain from his coming before my door that he had seen her just after she pawned them.
   JAMES M'MICHEN(police-constable H 131). I took the prisoner on the evening of the 6th of February; I found on her two duplicates which led to these things.
   JOHN BOARD. These are the duplicates - she pawned the coat for her father, but the boots in the name of Ann Harding.
   GEORGE BRICKLE re-examined. The jacket that he states I got from him was one he took in unmade - I know not why he states what he has, unless it is because he was forced to give up that jacket - when I got up in the morning, I went out to look for my daughter, and I did this in order that she might be taken from her bad companions - I married my present wife in 1810, there was another woman who passed as my wife.
 COURT. Q. Where is that wife? A. I don't know.
 Q. In the course of your life how many wives have you had? A. This one and the other I lived with.
 Q. Do you mean that you have not had two wives at once? A. Yes, I have; and they are both alive - I was tried here for bigamy, and fined 1s. - I allow my other wife 1s. 6d. a week for the child.
     NOT GUILTY.
 505.  JANE BAXTER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February , 2 silver forks, value 1l., the goods of William Abbott.
   CHARLES BURFORD. I am in the service of William Abbott- he is a gentleman, and lives in Wynyat-place- the prisoner used to come to visit the cook - she was there on the 8th of February - we missed some property; I suspected her - we inquired, and some property was found at the pawnbroker's - she has been in the habit of coming there for three months.
   JOHN MATTHEWS. I am a pawnbroker - I have two silver forks, pawned by the prisoner on the 10th of February - I have no doubt of her person.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing about them further than washing them up and leaving them in the kitchen.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Transported for Seven Years.
 506.  CHARLES COSTER and JOHN COSTER were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of February , 10lbs. of copper bolts, value 10s.; and 20lbs. of copper rivets, value 7s., the goods of Edmund Pontifex and others.
   CHARLES SCOTCHMER(police-constable G 125). On the 15th of February, at a quarter before eight o'clock at night, I was in Playhouse-yard - I saw the two prisoners pass me - Charles had a bundle under his arm - I suspected and followed them to a house in Playhouse-yard - I asked what they had in the bundle - they said, "Nothing," and they had no bundle - I searched John, and found under his coat these bolts now produced - I called another constable, and took them to the station-house - the bundle contained the ten pounds of copper rivets - they said two men on Clerkenwell-green gave them to them to sell.
   RICHARD JENNINGS. Charles Coster worked with me - he was my hammer-man, in the employ of Mr. Pontifex - these rivets are some that he and I were employed in making that week - I know nothing of John - the name of the firm is Edmund Pontifex, Son, and Wood.
   JOHN BROWNING(police-constable G 174). I took Charles Coster - these copper bolts were found on John - this bundle contains the other property.
   SAMUEL LANE. I am in the employ of Messrs. Pontifex - Charles was employed there, but not John.
