ELIZABETH ELLIS. I am the wife of David Ellis - the prisoner lodged with us in Queen's-court, Holborn. I missed these things, and have never seen them since; I inquired why her husband did not come; she said he was in the country - in about a fortnight afterwards she left her shop of work, and I heard from another lodger that she had not been in my house for two nights; I went up stairs and looked for the key of her door, but could not find it - I looked through the key-hole, and missed the clothes off the bed; I had the door opened, and there was nothing in the room but an old shift of my daughter's, which she had to mend - I know these articles had been in the room, and the prisoner only, had the key of it.
 Prisoner. I went out one morning, and when I returned in the evening I found the door had been broken open; I missed the things - her daughter admitted that she had broken open the door and taken the things.
 THOMAS THOMPSON. I had been looking for the prisoner for six months - I found her about the 30th of December; when I asked her about these things; she said they were too far gone.
 Prisoner. I said I knew nothing of them - as to the shawl. she gave me that.
   ELIZABETH ELLIS. I did lend it her once - she returned it, and then took it again: three months afterwards I found where she lodged - I took a Police-officer to the place, but she could not be found - she afterwards gave herself up to another officer.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined One Month.
 479.  ANN READ was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of January , 1 pair of boots, value 16s., the goods of William Huddard Dutton.
 SAMUEL GWYNN. I am a boot-maker, in the service of Mr.  William Huddard Dutton, in Norton-court. On the evening of the 6th of January I saw the prisoner lurking about the place; I went out, and asked if she wanted any thing - I looked round the shop, and she dropped the boots at her feet; she had a large cloak on - I had not seen them in her hand; Mr. Groom came by, and we gave charge of her - she said, "I am very sorry for what I have done, and I hope you will look over it."
   JOHN GROOM. I took the prisoner - she begged Mr. Dutton to forgive her, and said it was the first time.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined One Year.
 480.  THOMAS MANNING was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of January , 1 feather pillow, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Sladden.
   THOMAS SLADDEN. I am a broker, and live in White's-row, Spitalfields. On the 8th of January I lost a pillow from my shop - a little boy told me it was gone; I went out in pursuit of the thief, but could not find him - the prisoner was afterwards pointed out, and I took him; I have never seen the pillow since.
 Prisoner. There were several persons round the place; I was coming by between five and six o'clock, and he took me. Witness. Yes, there were several boys.
 THOMAS SAVAGE. I sell fish. I saw the prisoner take the pillow - he shook it, put it under his arm, and went down Rose-lane; I saw nobody with him - he came up Rose-lane again with some other boys.
 THOMAS HART. I received the prisoner at the watch-house - he said he knew nothing of the pillow; I found l on him - he has an industrious poor mother.
     NOT GUILTY.
 481.  ROBERT BEHARRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of January , 1 shovel, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Morris Fenton.
 MORRIS FENTON. I am working at the  New College at Somerset-house. On the 8th of January the prisoner came there and asked for a job - I said we had none; I saw him go away with my shovel under his arm - I did not go after him, as the watchman said if I did I should not get in again till twelve o'clock, and I should lose half a day: I afterwards found him at work with my shovel - this is it; I know it by three notches on the handle, and the iron being turned up.
   EDGAR ANDERSON. I am a Police-officer. The prosecutor told me the prisoner had his shovel, and I took him with it - he said he bought it in the street, and gave 2s. 9d. for it; he then said he gave 3s. for it.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   JOHN WATTS. The prisoner lodged with me. This shovel came into my house bran new six weeks ago.
 MOSES WATERS. I cannot swear to this shovel, but six weeks ago last Thursday, I bought one at the corner of Parliament-street, for 2s. 8d. - it had no mark on it; I sold it to the prisoner for the same price - he gave me 3s., and I gave him 3d., which was all I had; I believe this to be it.
 JOHN BERRY. The prisoner came to the place for work, and I saw him go out with his hands in his pockets - he had no shovel.
     NOT GUILTY.

   WILLIAM HOOKER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on the 10th - he gave the duplicates up, in my presence, to the prosecutor.
   LEONARD CHARLES MATTHEWS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Whitecross-street. I have some brass ornaments and watch movements, pawned on the 7th of December, by the witnesses; the officer gave the duplicate up to me - I put it on our file, and have not been able to find it.
   MARY CANNON. I have known the prisoner three years - he gave me these articles, and I pawned them for him with Matthews.
