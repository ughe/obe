HARRY HUNT. I am a stove manufacturer of 43, Newington Butts-
on 18th March I received this letter. (In this Miss Taylor requested him to
und a small stove suitable for a studio.) I wrote for information as to size
and received this. (Naming 19 inchos square as the size and suggesting the
"Argus.") In consequence I sent a stove value 88. and received this
letter. (Acknowledging receipt of store and asking for a price-list of kitohenors.)
I wrote that it would be necessary to see the kitchen I sent my
account with the stove or next day to Miss Taylor; I have not been paid
-I went to The Towers in May-Mrs. Taylor met me in the grounds-I
said "I have called for my account for a stove supplied to Miss Taylor”
-she said Miss Taylor was not at home, she had gone to Bagshot-I
asked to see the stove-she said her daughter had taken the key with
her-I asked about the kitchener, if I could see the kitchen-she said
she had not yet let or sold the house and could not settle anything about
the kitchen-I asked if she could settle the account --bhe said her
daughter would attend to it and that she expected her daughter would
be in soon-I offered to wait, and I waited perhaps half an hour, but
went away without seeing the daughter-I think I called back again to
see if the daughter had arrived I was answered in the negative.
Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN. It was & portable stove, but was
too heavy to carry about; it could be used for any room-I saw the case
in the paper, and wrote to Inspector Smith--I said at the police-court "I
have not ascertained the stove was obtained by fraud, I have not been paid
for it"-that is true to that extent.
SAMUEL MOORE. I am a silk merchant at 78, Paternoster Row-in
April, 1885, I received this letter. (Miss Taylor requested Mr. Moors to
send by post black sill, plain and broché, to The Towers.) In consequence I
sent some patterns of both sorts and then received this letter. (Requesting
him to send by return a lady's numbrella, best quality, and 10 yards of broché silk
according to the marked patterns.) I sent them; the total value was 21. 198. 8d.
- this is the silk, it is in the same condition as when I sentit-I received
this letter. (Miss Taylor acknowledged receipt of the first parcel and requested
him to send 20 yards of black silk and an umbrella, and also patterns of fabrios
for a tennis dress.) I executed that order, it came to 71. 28. 60.--I sent an
account with each parcel, the total was 10l. 28. 2d. I have never been
paid I have never seen the second parcel of silk again that was of
superior quality, such as would be used by ladies of title, few ladies
would wear it-I did not understand the silk would be pawned; I
thought it would be used in the ordinary way by people living at such a
house as I thought The Towers was.
Cross-examined by Mr. GEOGHEGAN. I belong to the Trade Protection
Society-I made no inquiries, but sent the goods on the mere receipt of
the letter: the people were perfect strangers to me the silk is made for
anybody who can pay for it-I imagined it was for a lady of society by
"The Towers" at the top of the letter--the neighbourhood is respectable,
I have customers there, but no one from whom I could make inquiries
they are superior houses there, and each house bears its own name; they
are not in streets-it was the name of the house, the letter, and the
neighbourhood, that induced me to part with the first parcel of goods.
