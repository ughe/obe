   JOHN KEY, ESQ. My partner's name is Jonathan Muckelstone Key - he is the only partner I have; we examined our stock of paper made by Coles, and missed ten reams; we received the paper from Mr. Morgan, of Fleet-market - the lots were from No. 522, to 612; I have examined the paper that was found - five of the wrappers have been destroyed, but I have examined the wrappers which were found, and in every instance we have the preceding number, and the succeeding one; we lost ten reams - they are worth about 9l. 10s. - I have no doubt these are ours.
 Cross-examined by MR ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you see the numbers on those papers yourself? A. Yes; we wanted them for a particular purpose, and I waited till they came in - fifty-six reams were sent out the next day; when I heard of these being found, I went to the place they were sent to, and found fifty-two of them remaining at His Majesty's stationery office - I found the preceding, and the succeeding numbers to these, on those which remained at home; I am sure these had not been sent out.
 Coleman's Defence. I left work at half-past four o'clock, and went to Tomes' house to have a coat repaired; I had not been there five minutes, when the officer came and took me - the paper, and the covers laid on the table - I took up one cover to look at, and threw it down.
   Richard Tomes' Defence. I was coming down London-wall; I cast my eye up a gateway, where I stopped, and saw this paper lying in the wet - I brought it down as far as the gateway, and a boy was coming by - I asked him to lend me a hand to carry it, which he did; when we got to the place, it was heavy, and he let it fall off his shoulder - there was a young woman coming by, and I asked if we might leave it at a chandler's shop; the woman said No - I said we would take it up stairs; I did take it up, and tore off part of the dirty paper, and was going to put it into the box, to be out of the way; Cook and my sister did not come in for half an hour.

JAMES BUTLER and JOHN PICHOTT were indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , 2 rings, value 4s.; 18 napkins, value 12s.; 2 shifts, value 4s.; 4 table-cloths, value 16s.; 3 shirts, value 15s.; 1 veil, value 3d.; 6 remnants of lace, value 2s.; 6 gowns, value 1l.; 1 smelling-bottle, value 2s.; 1 necklace, value 10s., and 4 pairs of sheets, value 3l., the goods of James Bellingham.
   REBECCA BELLINGHAM. I am the wife of James Bellingham; he had a shop in Regent-street, in June last, and is a glass-cutter- I took a lodging at the prisoner Pichott's, in Chester-terrace, Regent's-park, in the beginning of August last; I sent a trunk and box there, by a porter - they contained wearing-apparel, and the articles stated in the indictment, which have been found, but none of the wearing-apparel. On the 14th of September, I heard that Pichott had been to my house on the 13th; I then went to his house, and found my boxes, which were both open, and empty - when I sent them, one had been locked, and both were corded: there was a blanket laying on the floor- Pichott showed them to me, and he had left word at my house that he wanted me to go up there: when I went, he opened the door, and said he had been robbed, and all my things were gone - he then showed me my trunks, which were empty - I took Schofield there the next morning; he went out, and when he returned he said he had very good grounds for taking Pichott, as he believed Pichott or his wife, or some one in the habit of coming there, had committed the robbery; it was no stranger, he was quite sure - I then asked Pichott who was in the habit of coming there; he said, "Nobody but respectable people, indeed, none at all" - on the Thursday, I received information that Butler visited him; I went to Pichott, and told him what I had heard - he said, I must be very much mistaken, as he was a very respectable man; he said he did not know where he lived, but he believed his wife did - his wife was then called; she said she did not know - he then said he believed it was No. 80, Drummond-street I said I should go there the next day, and left the house - I went with the officer to No. 80, Drummond-street the next day - I saw Mrs. Butler; she said her husband was not at home, but a neighbour opposite said he was at home - I saw round Mrs. Butler's neck a coral necklace, which was mine, and had been in one of my boxes; directly we went in, she caught up a duster, and threw it round her neck, but it did not entirely conceal the necklace - the officer had some conversation with her; I said I had every reason to believe that was my necklace- she said it was not; I said it was, and described it, the clasp as a gold one, with a ring at each end and twisted; the officer told her to take it off, but she would not; I believe this veil and smelling-bottle to be mine, and these napkins - they were in the trunk - I afterwards went with the officer to Pichott's, and he said I must be mistaken, as there were so many necklaces alike, that I could not swear to it - the officer asked him if he had sent his wife to tell Butler; he said Yes, and he should again, if he liked - the officer said it was very improper, after having said he would assist me.
