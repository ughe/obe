JOHN TUCKER was indicted for burglariously breaking and en tering the dwelling-house of Samuel John Davis , about the hour of twelve in the night of the   22nd April , at   St. Mary, Lambeth, Surrey , with intent to steal, and feloniously and burglariously stealing therein, 1 looking-glass and stand, value 4 s  ., his property. SAMUEL JOHN DAVIS. I live at No. 46, White Hone-street, Cornwall-road, New-cut. It is in the parish of Christchurch, I think—I do not pay taxes—I sometimes sell fish, and sometimes fruit—I do not rent the house, it is let out in tenements—I am a lodger—the landlord does not live in the house—I have one room to myself, separate from the other lodgers—on the 22nd of April, between eight o'clock at night, and one o'clock in the morning, this happened—I locked my room-door at eight o'clock, and was absent from eight till one o'clock—my wife was with me—we went towards Greenwich fair—when I went away, I left the prisoner sitting on the steps, outside my door—I am quite certain I locked the room-door—we returned between twelve and one o'clock in the morning—I found my room-door open, and missed a looking-glass off the drawers—I knew the prisoner before, by his brother being with me—he lived nearly opposite to me—I got a constable and went after him, and found him in bed—he lives with his mother, who is a widow—I told him I had lost the glass—he denied it—I took him to the station-house in Tower-street, and after the Inspector put a few questions to him, he said he had taken it, and sold it to a woman named Rogers, for 4 s .—I went to her house, and found it there. ELIZABETH ROGERS. I live in White-horse-street, near to the prose cutor. On the night of the 22nd of April, at a quarter-past nine o'clock, the prisoner came to my house, and asked if I would buy a glass of his mother, for she was in distress, and wanted to past with it—he wanted 4 s . for it—I said I had not got 4 s .—I gave him 2 s ., and said I would give him two more on Saturday night—the policeman came to my house about one or two o'clock in the morning, and I gave the glass up to him—I am sure he is the boy who sold it to me.
