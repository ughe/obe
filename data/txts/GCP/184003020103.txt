daughter's which had no lock on it-I corded it—the following morning. I
sent my wife to hire apartments-that was in consequence of what passed
between me and Ellis Caspar.
Q. Well, where were the tin boxes with the ore in them afterwards taken
to? A. To Mansel-street, by my directions, and from thence by my sister-
they were afterwards taken, I believe, to Petticoat-lane, and from thence the
ore was brought to me at Oxendon-street, where I was then concealed I
had not the boxes at Oxendon-street--the ore was taken out of the boxes
before it was brought to me--the last time I saw the tin boxes was at my
place in Mansel-street--it was to Davis's house in Oxendon-street that I
went.

Q. What became of you after this? 4. I went to Brentford for two
days, and afterwards to Peckham, to Fishwick's I never saw the prisoner
after that remained at Fishwick's a fortnight, and then surrendered to
the officer-I supplied myself with the lodging at Fishwick's I saw no-
thing of the prisoner at Davis's house I did not take the ore to Davis's-
it was brought to me out of the boxes I desired Davis to go for it to my
sister's, in Petticoat-lane- I told him they were there, and he said he should
fetch them I delivered up some money to the officers when I surrendered
-I received that money from Davis it was notes I think it was 1401.
COURT.-Q. Do you know of your own knowledge what became of the
ore? A. Only what I was informed of-I never saw it after it was taken
from Oxendon-street-I did not receive the 1401 till nearly a fortnight after
-it was given to me by Davis in the coach as I was coming here to surren-
der myself—I do not know what has become of Davis I saw him once at
the office.
