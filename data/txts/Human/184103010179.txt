   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  He said he kept the house? A.  Yes; he said he lived at Mrs. Hambrook's, and she kept the house—he said he was commander—he said he lived in the house, and looked after Mrs. Hambrook—I cannot say that he used the word "commander"—if the pri soner bad wished to strike Wiseman again, when I went out of the house, he was at his mercy—I had been in the house about four hours and a half—Smith went with me—we bad been drinking—Mrs. Hambrook fetched what we had, at our desire—we had come from home—I and Smith both had the clothes-pole—it was broken in two, and I and Smith both took a part—this was after we had escaped out of the house—it was standing in the yard—I first laid hold of it when I escaped out of the door the second time—I and Smith did not make use of any expressions when we came back, no fur ther than saying, if he was to come out we would serve  him out  —I do not remember saying, "Come out, you b—"—I will not swear I did not say so—I swear positively I did not use such an expression, nor Smith either—we might have said, "We will have your life, or you will have ours"—I believe we did say it—the prisoner had not been up stairs in bed—he came in after we had been there four hours—Wiseman did not attempt to make a blow at Mrs. Hambrook, and the prisoner interfere to prevent it—Wise man did not do, or try to do, any thing.
   COURT . Q.  Did Wiseman use any violence towards the prisoner? A.  No; no one had done any thing to the prisoner, more than hold his hands, before he got the shovel—it was after the blow was given that we said, "We will have your life, or you will have ours."
