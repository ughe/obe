 2254. MICHAEL M'KEW and SILVESTER FLOOD were indicted for stealing, on the   8th of September , 117 yards of bed-tick, value 5 l  ., 16 s  ., the goods of William Dickenson . JAMES TIDMARSH. I am a policeman. In consequence of information I received from Williams on the 8th of September, I watched a house in the Maze, at South walk, from half-past nine o'clock till about half-past eleven at night, when I saw two men come out—we let them pass us, and a third person came out, which was M'Kew—we took him into custody, and went into the house, and found this property in a room there—the other two men did not answer the description we had received of the men—Flood was one of those two. RICHARD WILLIAMS. I am a steam-boiler-maker, and live in Queen street, King-street, Borough. On the 8th of September, between nine and ten o'clock, I saw two men and a woman come from under an arch of London-bridge—M'Kew was one of them—he had a parcel on his shoulder—I followed them down to the church, in Tooley-street, and lost sight of them by the side of the rail-road—I gave information to the officer—the paper round the parcel was torn, and I could see by the gaslight that it was bed-ticking—I cannot speak to Flood—he wore a cap similar to the other man, but I thought the man was shorter than him. SUSANNAH MULHEEN . One Catherine Flood lodged with me in the Maze.
