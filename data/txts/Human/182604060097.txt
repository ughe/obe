WILLIAM HALL. I am a constable. On the 10th of March, about half-past two o'clock, I saw Hutton going along Hounslow with a basket. Mrs. Hancock was close behind him - I followed him half a mile, and asked what he had got - he said some lead from Mr. Hancock's house, and he was going to Brentford to sell it - at the further end of Hounslow I got an officer, and we took Hutton - Mr. Emmerson and I went to Hancock, who is a shoemaker, and lives at the end of Hounslow - both the doors were fast - he went to the front door and I to the back; Mrs. Hancock said her husband was gone to Harlington, but a man said he had seen him just before - we then broke the door open and found him. I said to Hancock "You are my prisoner, you must go with me;" Emmerson went up stairs, and brought some lead, which Hancock said had been in Taylor's room; we asked where we could find Taylor; he said "If you go to Crawford-street, you will find him;" Emmerson went there and found him - we got a search-warrant a few days afterwards, and found some more lead in Taylor's box; as we were coming to London on the 11th, Hutton said "If we have taken the lead from Mrs. Canham's gate they can't swear to it now, because it is cut in pieces" - the other two prisoners were close to him, but made no observation. I believe they all lodged in Hancock's house.
   JOHN EMMERSON. I am a Bow-street patrol. We apprehended Hutton with the lead - we then went to the house, and I found four pieces more, which Hancock said was Taylor's - I found two more pieces in Taylor's box - I took them to the office, and took two or three tradesmen to see where the lead was taken from; the lead here produced matched exactly to the top of the building; I knew that Taylor lived at Hancock's, but I did not know what room he occupied; I heard Taylor say he knew the lead came into the house the same night it was lost off the lodge - there were 87lbs. of it.
   DANIEL BERKSHIRE. I am bailiff to Mrs. Canham. I saw this lead fitted on the building, and it matched exactly - I missed it on the 8th of March - I had seen it safe the day before.
 HANCOCK's Defence. I let this man the room, and he had got the lead. I know nothing of him nor what was locked in his room.
 HUTTON's Defence. Hancock asked if I had any thing to do - I said No; he asked me to carry it to Brentford for him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 814.  THOMAS PEARCE was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , 15 yards of flannel, value 10s., the goods of John Fowke.
   JOB CLIFFORD. I am shopman to Mr. John Fowke, a draper, in Oxford-street. On the 14th of March, about three o'clock, a woman gave me information - I went out and found the prisoner about twenty yards from the door, with fifteen yards of flannel which had been standing within the door, surrounded by a chain.
   THOMAS MORRIS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I am very sorry - I was rather in liquor.
 GUILTY. Aged 73. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Months.
 815.  JOHN MACQUIRE was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of March , 2 pewter pots, value 2s., the goods of John Hinton.
 JOHN HINTON. I  keep the  Hare and Hounds public-house, St. John's-wood. These pots are mine.
   JAMES LAWS. I am a shoemaker. I saw the prisoner in St. John's-wood-lane, about eleven o'clock, with two quart pots. about 190 yards from Mr. Hinton's house - he put them down, took them up again, then walked round to the back of a house, and got in at the window - I heard him beating something - I went up to the place, and saw him beating one of the pots with a brick; I said"What are you beating these pots for?" - he said he was going to take the other pot home; he went out at the front of the house; I called to a person, and had him secured.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been for a pot of beer, and brought it down to the gardener - I went into the building, and put the pots down, but I did not beat them.
     GUILTY. Aged 50. Confined One Month.
 816.  THOMAS MALONEY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of March , 1 pair of steps, value 5s., the goods of John Clough.
   GEORGE WADDINGTON. I am a Bow-street patrol. Mr. Clough keeps an oil-shop, opposite Southampton-buildings. On the 27th of March, about seven o'clock in the evening I saw the prisoner looking into his shop; there was a pair of steps at the window to keep the people from the paint - they were open, he closed them, and put them under the window - he then went a little way, returned, and took them up, and put them down again - then took them up and went away - I secured him.
   JOHN PARTIGER. I am porter to Mr. John Clough. I know these steps to be his.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming down Holborn, very much intoxicated, and a man gave me part of a pot of beer to carry these steps over to Southampton street.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Confined Two Months.
