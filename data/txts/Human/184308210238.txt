WILLIAM COCKRILL( police-constable L  108.) On the 14th of Aug., about half-past three o'clock in the morning, my attention was called to the prose cutor's house, by seeing Palmer and Lutchford talking together at the foot of Westminster-bridge—when I got up to the corner of Stangate, Lutchford came towards me, and said he thought there was something wrong at Mr. Mackintosh's, we had better go round to the back, and see if it was safe, which he did—I went up to Palmer—he said something to me—I looked to see if there was any light through the doorway—it was all in darkness—I then went to the side door and heard some whispering and footsteps more from the direction of the bar towards the side entrance, leading to the landing of the stair-caseat that moment Lutchford returned and stated something—I sent him back again—after a short lapse of time I called out to Mr. Mackintosh several times, and as I rang the bell Mr. Mackintosh opened his bed-room window—I told him I thought thieves were in the house—he said, "I thought I heard them a quarter of an hour ago"—the next moment I heard the swing door which leads to the back entrance of the premises pulled back—it works on a pulley, and when the door closes again the pulley makes a noise, which would let me know that the door was closed—about a minute afterwards one of the witnesses came up to the corner of Stangate, and called out to me to send further assistance—I then sent Palmer round to the back of the premises—shortly afterwards I saw Lutchford in the road—he called out to me for assistance, in consequenoe of being stabbed by a knife—I ran to his assistance, and saw Trenate in the act of standing over him, and Lutchford hanging on to the waistband of his trowsers—Trenate had a knife in his hand—Lutchford was on the ground—he turned over twice while I was running from the bridge to where he was lying—I hit Trenate a blow with my fist on his head, and the knife fell down on the left, and he fell to the right of Lutchford—I secured the knife and the prisoner the next mo ment, before he got up from the ground—at the same moment there was a cry for further assistance at Mr. Mackintosh's—a drayman came up, and being a man I knew well,
