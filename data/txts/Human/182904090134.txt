 JURY. Q. Is the court a thoroughfare? A. No; there are about eight houses - the doors of which open to the court.
   DANIEL HICKS. I am shopman to Mr. Holmes, a hatter, of Aldgate. I bought some trimmings of Appleby; they were silk and cotton hat-bands - the officer received them from me.
   THOMAS WATERS. I am a Police-officer of Worship-street. On the 17th of March I took the prisoner at his own house in Princes-court, Gibraltar-walk, Bethnal-green; I asked him if he had sold any ribbon or hat-hands to Appleby - he said he had; I asked him where he got it - he said he found it about a fortnight ago on Farthing-hill; I shewed him one of them; I told him I should take him on suspicion of the robbery - Towers was with me; he denied the charge - I went up stairs to the room which he said he lived in, and found a quantity of cotton and a small quantity of silk, some in skeins and some on bobbins; he said they were all his own, and he had bought them; I told him he had better produce the bill of parcel before the Magistrate; the prosecutor said he had lost things of the quality, but would not swear to them; he identified the pieces of hands before the Magistrate - I got one of them from Hicks, and the other from the prosecutor himself.
   JOHN TOWERS. These hat-bands were manufactured by a person I employed; I know the make, because it is one colour on the edge, and another on the body of the work; it was returned to my hands by Appleby's brother a month before the robbery - it was damaged, which was the reason I had them laying by me; I am certain they were in my own possession before the house was broken open - the person who manufactured them is not here; I had seen it that day in a large drawer, with thirty-two pieces, which were taken at the same time (looking at the hat-band); the drawer they were in was about half way open, and the lock hanging on one screw; it was in its proper state when I left the house - the prisoner's father was taken into custody, and discharged; he had never worked for me - some things were found in a room in the father's house, but the prisoner said he rented it of his father.
 MR. CHURCHILL. Q. How many locks of drawers had been forced open? A. Only one; it had been done without much noise, for they had laid mats on the floor, to prevent their being heard - a noise would not be heard on the top story; we had a suspicion of the very man who manufactured these, but did not have him taken up; he had an opportunity of knowing the premises - he did not work for me at the time, and did not come above once a week - it is two years since he worked for me; he then worked in my shop - what I speak to this by is the difference in colour; it was a mistake of mine in putting two colours in - I never saw such before, nor has any other man I should think; I heard the noise about eight o'clock- I only heard one noise.
 JURY. Q. Had you sold them to Appleby's brother? A. He ordered it of me, and returned it on my bands on account of the badness of colour; and after the robbery they were taken to his brother for sale; he knew it, and gave me information.
   JOHN APPLEBY re-examined. Q. Is that the band you received from the prisoner, and offered to your brother, which he rejected? A. This is one of them - I did not take them to my brother, but sent them to him; he did not choose to have them; I could tell they were not well manufactured the instant I saw them; I shewed them to several hatters, who rejected them, as being badly made.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is that the first badly manufactured article you ever saw? A. No, nor the last.
 JURY. Q. Did you ever see any with the same defect in it? A. Never exactly, but very similar; it would be foolish to manufacture as this is - the body of the work is good, but the edges are another colour, thick, and clumsy; it is possible to make the same mistake.
   JOHN TOWERS. The value of the property taken is 20l. and upwards; the articles mentioned are worth about 20l.
 Prisoner. I leave my Defence to my Counsel.
   JAMES ABERY. I remember the 14th of February. I saw the prisoner at half-past three o'clock that afternoon, and until eight in the evening, at the Half Moon public-house, in Bacon-street, Bethnal-green, which is no great way from Phoenix-street; I can walk it in five minutes - nothing particular occurred to make me remember the date; I left the place at eight o'clock, leaving him behind me there.
 COURT. Q. What day was it? A. It was on a Saturday - I do not wear a watch; Spitalfield's church bell rings at eight o'clock, and it rang as I was going home; I left as it was ringing; it always strikes out at eight - I left while it was sounding.
   ROBERT LOBB. I am a broad silk-weaver. I know the prisoner, and saw him on the 14th of February, about half-past six o'clock in the evening, at the Half Moon; I found him there when I went in - I staid there till ten- he went away about eight, while the bell was ringing.
 JURY. Q. How long was it before you saw him again? A. The next Saturday - I have known him six years.
   WILLIAM LOBB. I am brother to the last witness. I saw the prisoner at the Half Moon; I was with my brother - I went there about a quarter before seven, and remained there with the prisoner till a little after eight o'clock; he then went away - it might be five minutes past eight; the bell rings till a quarter past eight.
 COURT. Q. Did you look at the public-house clock? A. Yes, I am quite sure of that; I generally look at the clock - the prisoner went away about five minutes, or it might be two or three minutes after eight; the Half Moon is about one hundred or one hundred and fifty yards from the prosecutor's house.
   ROBERT BENNETT. I am a broad silk-weaver. I saw the prisoner at the Half Moon on the 14th of February; I went about half-past six o'clock, and he went away near upon eight.
 COURT. Q. Do you mean a little short of eight? A. About that - I am certain be staid till eight.
