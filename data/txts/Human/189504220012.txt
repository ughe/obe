KATE MCNEILL. I live at 28, Euston Crescent—about 9th March, on the Saturday before I gave evidence at the Police-court, at half-past seven p.m. the prisoners came to my place—a week previous the male prisoner had engaged my back-room—the female prisoner stayed there from the Saturday; I believe the male prisoner stayed there one night, but I have not seen him—they brought this white portmanteau with them.
   Cross-examined by Alfred Bellmy.  I never saw you eat in my house; I think you slept there one night—your wife was ill in bed; she went to bed the morning she came. MARTHA DALLY. I am the wife of Henry Dally, of 17, Portland Place—the male prisoner called at my house and asked if there were any letters for him in the name of Bellamy; none came—he called again for letters in another name, and said he had advertised, and ten or fourteen came; he called for them—he afterwards asked me to take in letters in the name of Madame Lamotte, and two came—it extended over a fort-night or three weeks—it was in February, I think—I have the letters.
