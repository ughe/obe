WILLIAM COOPER (Police-constable F 82). I heard the cry, and found the prosecutor at the house - he charged the persons there with stealing from him a sovereign and a half, and two half-crowns; he had his senses, and described all the circumstances as he has now - I found the prisoner Martin and Smith there; I took them all, and went in search of the man, but could not find him - there was a passage through the house, so that a person could go through; Cooper denied the charge, but in going to the station she said if we would go back we should find the money under the chair, but it has not been found - she offered the prosecutor 5l. to let her go, and offered me the same; the other two were remanded three times and then discharged.
 Prisoner's Defence. I said I would sooner have given 5l. than such a thing should have happened - when the prosecutor went into the back parlour, Smith said to him,"Will you come into the bed-room, for the sofa is not fit to sit down on;" when they were in the bed-room there was some quarrel and Smith called me - I went to the door; I found it locked - Smith said, "This man is quarrelling with me and using me very ill;" I said, "I am sure you should not, if you are tipsy you should not ill use her;" she then took his coat and waistcoat from the nail - the prosecutor and her stopped for the money which fell down; the prosecutor said, "It is my money."
   THOMAS HENMAN. I did not pick any money up, nor did I state so - my coat and waistcoat were handed to me by Martin and I threw them on my arm. GUILTY (of stealing only.) Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 234.  EVAN CAPE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 1 cloth cap, value 2s., the goods of Charles Oswin , from the person of Charles Holley Oswin.
 CHARLES HOLLEY OSWIN. I am the son of Charles Oswin - he lives in Harley-street, and is a  dentist . On the 24th of November, I was in Vere-street, taking a letter to the Post, and the prisoner passed me - he took my cap, and ran on to Old Cavendish-street; there he threw it down, and I took it up - the officer followed, and took him in Oxford-street.
   GEORGE MILLS(Police-constable D 84.) I saw the prisoner running - Oswin charged him with taking the cap- I followed him to Oxford-street, and took him.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - Transported for Seven Years.
 235.  JOHN DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of November , 1 Venetian blind, value 12s., the goods of Joseph Lavender.
   JAMES BRADFIELD. I live with Mr. Lavender, a blind-maker, in Oxford-street. I placed this blind in his window half an hour before it was taken.
   HENRY BAKER. I am an officer. About eight o'clock in the evening of the 12th of November, I saw the prisoner and another loitering about the shop in Oxford-street - the other went in, and came out again; the prisoner then took the blind, and came out.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a person come out of the shop with this blind - he ran, and placed it six doors off against a door-post; a woman said, "I think that man has been stealing something;" I went upto him - the Policeman came, and took me; I never touched the blind, and was not near it - I said, "You are mistaken;" he said," It does not matter, you will do quite as well," and then the woman who saw it followed us, making a noise, and kept saying, "That is not the man;" the officer threatened that if she followed him he would take her, or I should have had a witness here.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Months.
 236.  GEORGE FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of November , 2 tea-trays, value 1l. 7s.; 1 breadtray, value 3s.; 1 pair of snuffers and tray, value 1s. 6d.; 1 pair of candlesticks, value 4s.; 2 curtain-bands, value 4s.; 5 brass finger-plates, value 4s.; 2 brass knobs, value 1s.; 3 brass hooks, value 6d.; 1 milk-pot, value 2s. 6d.; 1 sugar-basin, value 3s. 6d.; 1 salt, value 1s. 6d.; 2 egg cups, value 9d.; 5 apoons, value 10d.; 2 tin pans, value 2s.; 1 brush, value 2s., and 1 hasp and staple, value 3d., the goods of Joseph James Sheffield.
   JOSEPH SHEFFIELD JAMES. I am an ironmonger. The prisoner had been my errand-boy for six or seven years; in consequence of the loss of some pigeons, I went to his father's residence on the 15th of November - he sent a young woman on Thursday morning to say he was not able to attend to his duty - I found at his father's, the whole of the property stated in the indictment, some of which has my private mark on it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOSEPH BEAL(Police-constable 252 K.) I found the property at the prisoner's father's, where I am informed he resides.
   WILLIAM GEORGE SMITH. I am a biscuit-baker. I am an acquaintance of the prosecutor's, and knew the prisoner lived with him - I met him on the 28th of November, in the Minories - he saw me, and turned down John-street; I followed him through several streets, when he turned the corner, he ran, as I could perceive, by the distance he got- I at last came up to him, and said it was useless his trying to run away, as I should give him in charge - he said he would go with me; I delivered him to the officer; I said he had acted very foolishly in going on as he had - he cried, and said the only thing he had taken was a seal.
   JAMES JOHN CONNERLY(Police-constable 135 H). The prisoner was given into my charge by Mr. Smith; I made him no promise or threat, but said, "You are a very foolish young man;" he said he had taken a tea-board or two, and some other articles, which Mr. Sheffield valued at 4l., but he had got them back again.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Will you swear you did not say it would be better for him to tell you? A. No. I did not.
   GEORGE AUGUSTUS. I am a house-agent. I know the house the property was found in; I let it to the prisoner, and he paid the rent of the premises.
 Cross-examined. Q. He had a good character? A. I believe he has.
     GUILTY. - Aged 22. Confined for Three Months.
