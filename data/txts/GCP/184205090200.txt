1663. JAMES BOURNE was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of
April, 1 saw, value 8s.; and 1 square, value 8s. 6d. ; the goods of Ben-
jamin Wildsmith.
SHOST
BENJAMIN WILDSMITH. I am a carpenter. I lost this saw on the 8th
of April, from my shop, up stairs—I can swear to it, by a particular mark
--this square is also mine-my name is on it-the prisoner formerly
worked for me, and I always found him honest.
JAMES ARCHER. I know this saw and square to be Mr. Wildsmith's.
WILLIAM EPPs (police-constable N 303.) I met the prisoner in the
Lea Bridge-road about eight o'clock, on the 9th of April, with this saw and
square-I asked what he had got-he said he had bought it-I asked him
where-he said in Sun-street, Bishopsgate, about eighteen months ago.
Prisoner's Defence. I went to Woodford to look for work; as I was
coming down Snaresbrook I met a carpenter, whom I had never seen be-
fore; he had the square and saw, and asked me to buy them, which I did,
for 38.; when I found the name on the square it confounded me; I did
not know what to do; I thought I would take it to Bow, and send it to
Mr. Wildsmith; I was never in trouble before.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY: Aged 78.--Strongly recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor

1664. WILLIAM WINDLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 15th
of April, 2 loaves of bread, value 9d., the goods of John Macomie.
John MACOMIE. I had a wheelbarrow in the street--I went to a gen.
tleman's house-I left my bread in my barrow-when I returned I found
two loaves gone.
CHARLES PENNYPATHER. I saw the barrow at the gate--I saw the
prisoner go away with two half-quartern loaves- I called him to stop-he
would not.
Prisoner. The gentleman came to me; my brother was in doors ; he
said he stole the bread; my brother said he did not steal it. Witness.
did not charge the brother-there was another one with him, who also bad
a loaf-I could not swear to the other.
GUILTY. Aged 18.-Confined Three Months.
