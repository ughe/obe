   COURT. Q.  Were you lodging there before the prisoner? A.  Yes—nobody used this razor-case but him—I took notice of this end of it, so as to be able to identify it—I cannot say it is the same razor-case that he used, only that it is like it.
   MR. COOK. Q.  Did you describe to Ward the shoes you had lost before they were shown to you? A.  Yes. JAMES WARD( policeman, N  144.) I produce a pair of shoes which I get from the prisoner when he was in custody at Hford gaol—he was wearing them—I had previously, on that same day, received a description of a pair of shoes from Howe, which corresponded with these shoes—they are very remarkable—there are two rows of nails outside the heel, one row inside, one row round the sole, and a few knocked out of each row, besides a patch on the right shoe, where there are two holes—Howe told me of all this before, and, finding they corresponded with his description, I took possession of them. HENRY WHALE( policeman, N  148.) On Tuesday, 2nd Nov., I saw the prisoner on the road to Chingford—I asked if he had heard about the woman that had had her throat cut at Chapel-end—he said he had—(I did not at that time know it was the prisoner)—I said, "I suppose, then, there is some truth in it—he said he did not know much about it, for he had only heard it himself—I asked where he lived—he said at Hale-end—I asked where he slept on the night of 1st Nov.—he said he had slept in the forest—I saw that he had his left hand in his pocket, and when it was taken out I saw that he had lost two fingers—upon that I said that he was the very man that was suspected to have done it—he did not say anything, but appeared very much agitated—I took him into custody—as we were going along he asked me if the woman's throat was cut very badly, and whether she was dead—I told him I did not know, I knew very little about it—he said, "Ah! I was drunk at the time"—as we were going along, my attention was called away from him, and he ran away—I overtook him—he had no neckerchief on when I took him.
   COURT. Q.  Had he when you first saw him? A.  No—he had on a pair of blucher boots like those that were afterwards in Ward's possession—I can not tell whether they were the same. GEORGE HUNTER( policeman N  357.) On Monday, 1st Nov., I saw Mrs. Cressy—I afterwards searched round the premises, and in the privy of the adjoining yard found these boots, which I produce—there is a row of houses—the back premises of Mrs. Cressy are only separated from the adjoining yard by a small temporary wooden fence, which was broken down, so that anybody could get over—I saw footmarks leading from Mr. Soames' farm-yard, across a ploughed field, to the back of these yards—some of the foot-marks were perfect enough to enable me to notice the marks on them—after finding the boots I went with Serjeant M'Culloch to the field—I had not noticed the impressions at the time I found the boots, but about twenty minutes afterwards—the boots were out of my possession about five minutes—Sophia
