 Q. When you told Mrs. Carter to send all the things to Charterhouse-square, did not she tell your Mrs. Keefe had left her every thing in the house? A. No, nothing like it - Mr. Franks, of Mill-hill was present, and Miss Springweiler; Mr. Franks is a Magistrate - she did not state this to me, nor in my hearing, but denied positively all knowledge of the notes; she did not say any thing had been left her by Mrs. Keefe - I claim on the property as sole administrator, to answer all claims as the whole of the estate; I have a sister who claims as well - we are her next relatives; I am a creditor of 150l.
 Re-examined. Q. Were you and the deceased on good terms? A. Perfectly so - we never had any difference; I thought she would recover on the 1st of June, and only required good nursing and nourishment - I had a better opinion of her then, than at any time from the commencement of her illness, and did not provide a nurse on that occasion, because I was satisfied with Mrs. Carter's attention up to that time; she had enjoyed the 200l. a year from 1821.
 MISS  ANN SPRINGWEILER. I am single, and was intimately acquainted with Mrs. Keefe - I am no relation, but have been acquainted with her many years. I remember her going to live at Carter's on the 2nd of April; she was then in a bad state of health - I had visited her during her illness about twice a week till her death; I saw her last on Monday, the 1st of June - she died on the 8th; I know she was in possession of about 225l. in Bank notes - I had counted them for her on the 10th of April, and had them in my hand on the 15th; I did not see them on the 1st of June - they were deposited in her desk, of which she kept the key herself: it was put into a purse in a little drawer by her bed side - there was also a 200l. bill of exchange, four sovereigns and a half, and some ear-drops: I had seen those in April and May - I saw the bundle of notes about three weeks previous to my last visit; they appeared just the same in bulk as when I had opened them - the drops of her ear-rings were in the desk, and were gold; I saw them there in May - she was very particular about opening her desk; I frequently brought it to her, wishing her to open it herself if she wanted to go to it, but I used to open it when I was there: she used to direct me to take the key from the purse in this drawer, if she did not take it herself - when she wanted a note changed she employed me to do it, and was very particular about it; I changed a note for her last on the 10th of April, when I saw them altogether - about three weeks previous to my last visit or after that, she used to point to her desk and say to me, "There they are for you to pat your hand on in case any thing happens to me;" but generally added, "I am not going to die yet." On the 8th of June Mrs. Carter's son came to me at No. 1l, Charterhouse-square, and informed me of her death; I went almost immediately - Mrs. Carter told me she died about nine o'clock that morning very suddenly, and had been very angry in the night at her employing a nurse to attend her instead of herself - I asked why she employed a nurse without sending for me; she said she had been too ill to attend to her herself - I asked her for the key of the desk, saying I knew there were papers of consequence there; she said she had the key in her possession, went down, and brought it up - I opened the desk in her presence; I found it on the steps by the bed-side - I found all the papers in confusion; the bill of exchange was there, but neither the ear-drops nor any one of the Bank notes - I then said there was a parcel missing, which was of consequence to Dr. Farre; she replied if they were merely papers they could not be of much consequence, and stated that Mrs. Keefe had told her she had no money, but there was a bill of exchange coming due in October when she should be paid for her lodging - the bill in the desk is due in October; Mrs. Carter had told me, previous to this, that Mrs. Keefe was to pay her 13s. a week for lodging, and desired me, on one occasion, to apply to Mrs. Keefe to allow her 1s. a day more for her attendance - this was about the 10th of April; I made the application to Mrs. Keefe, who was very angry - she said 13s. was quite sufficient, and she would allow nothing more - on the 8th of June Mrs. Carter told me Mrs. Keefe had desired her to shut the doors against every body, and take charge of every thing; she did not say for what purpose - I left the desk there, taking home the 200l. bill and the West India letters; I went there, I believe, every day but two, till the funeral; notice was sent to Dr. Farre, who came to town that day week - I think I had no more conversation with her myself about the desk, but I went back on the day she died, with Mr. Frederick Farre- he stated to her, in my presence, that the property was missing; she said she knew nothing about it - he asked who had been in the room; she said she knew nothing at all about it - on the 18th I went in company with Mr. Franks, and opened the desk; I was showing him, in Mrs. Carter's presence, the manner in which the notes used to lay there, and where the drops were in a small box in the desk - Mrs. Carter said she had picked the drops up in the fire-place, and sent her child down for them;
