STEPHEN BROWN. I live at Mr. Upjohn's, at Highgate - I know Bacon, and knew his cows. On the 3rd of August, between four and five o'clock in the morning, I was about half a mile from Bacon's field, and saw his two cows - I have seen them since, and know them to be the same; Edwards was one person who was driving them, and Duffy the other - I knew Edwards in Bacon's service, and thought he was going to take them home; I am sure of the other prisoner - I had never seen him before, but took notice of him, and said he was the man when he was apprehended.
   GEORGE CORK. I am an assistant to Mr. Moyes, a salesman, of Smithfield-market. On Monday, the 3rd of August, about eight o'clock, Edwards brought me two cows, and delivered them to a drover's charge, to tie them up; the drover asked who was to sell them; he said,"The salesman that stands before the Bear and Ragged Staff;" we usually stand there - at one o'clock Edwards came to Mr. Moyes, and the cows were taken away - I never saw Duffy.
   JOHN WARREN. I am an officer. I was on duty at Dulwich, on the 3rd of August, and saw Bacon, who informed me of the robbery - I only know that two cows were turned out in a field at Dulwich.
   SAMUEL CHAMBERS. I am a Bow-street patrol, and was with Warren.
   JOHN BACON. Edwards left my service on the 17th of February, and lived with Mr. Marsh, of John-street, Kent-road.
 Duffy's Defence. I neither had any thing to do with what I am charged with, nor do I know any thing about them.
 DUFFY -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 20.
 1831.  CATHERINE WINGROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of May , 80 yards of carpet, value 14l., and 15 yards of oil-cloth, value 2l., the goods of Samuel Hollingsworth , in his dwelling-house.
   SAMUEL HOLLINGSWORTH. I live in Bedford-street, Bedford-row. I have lost a quantity of property, carpets, and rugs - the prisoner's husband was our porter, and lived in the house, but he had left when this property was taken - I never saw the prisoner there; the property must have been taken through the connivance of my son, who, as well as my porter, have both been convicted.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1832.  MARK JOEL was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Bloom , in the Liberty of Norton-falgate, on the 27th of May , and stealing therein 1000 yards of silk, value 500l., his property.
 2nd COUNT, calling it a warehouse instead of a dwelling-house.
 3rd COUNT, for stealing 100 yards of silk, value 20l., the goods of the said John Bloom.
 MR. BRODRICK conducted the prosecution.
   SUSANNAH BLOOM. I am the daughter of John Bloom, who lives in Spital-square, in the Liberty of Nortou-falgate. On the 27th of May my father went out, about ten o'clock in the morning - the warehouse is part of the dwelling-house, and under the same roof; in consequence of information from the servant, I went and looked at the warehouse at half-past four o'clock in the afternoon, and saw that a wooden window of the warehouse, which is in the passage, was taken down, and laid in the passage - my father came home about five o'clock, and I communicated to him what had happened.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. How many persons were in the house? A. Three; my mother, the servant, and myself - it is our dwelling-house; the family live there - I might have seen the property before, but I did not take any particular notice of it.
   JOHN BLOOM. My house is No. 24, Spital-square, in the Liberty of Norton-falgate; it is extra parochial. On Wednesday, the 27th of May, when I came home, about half-past five or six o'clock, in consequence of information, I went to the warehouse, and found it broken open - a wooden window, which had originally been a door, and slides up and down, was taken down - I found every thing on the counter as I had left them in the morning, and did not examine the rest of my stock till Saturday morning, when I had to execute some orders, and the very goods I wanted to send out were gone - I reckon that above nine hundred yards of silk were gone; I was in the habit of seeing it every day, and am sure it was there on the Tuesday and Wednesday morning - I know it was there; I missed fourteen pieces, containing about nine hundred yards - the value is about 320l.; the shortest piece was worth at least 12l. - I am certain no piece was worth less than 5l. On the 7th of August I was going along the Quadrant, in Regent-street, and saw part of one of my pieces of silk in the shop of Mr. Poulson, a tailor; I went in, and asked the price of it - I examined it, and was sure it was mine; Poulson gave me a pattern of it, and on the 20th of August I went there with an officer, and found the piece in the same state as when I first saw it - I brought it away, and there was a short length of another piece; the officer has them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you any other name? A. No, nor any partner; I reside at the house - the silks were on the ground floor.
 Q. How does it happen that you did not discover the robbery the next morning? A. Being trustee to an estate I was obliged to go out next morning early - the property might have been stolen between the Wednesday and Saturday, for what I know; I was in the habit of seeing those fourteen pieces of silk every day before Wednesday - I was busy out of doors on Thursday and Friday- when I was at Poulson's I fixed on one piece of silk, which I said belonged to me; and it did not afterwards turn out that it had been in the shop for six months previous- I went to Mr. Shoolbred's and saw a good many pieces of silk, but none of mine; there were several young men there- I do not recollect seeing a little boy; I saw Mr. Shoolbred's brother - I fixed on a piece of silk there I thought was mine, but found afterwards it was not; no little boy spoke to me about it - there was a young man who said it had been in the shop six months.
 Q. Had you not ascertained that it had been there previously, and did not belong to you - would you not have sworn as positively to that as you do now to the silk in the indictment? A. No; because I found it was a different colour - mine was gold and blue, and that was yellow and blue.
