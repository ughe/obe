MESSRS. CLARKSON and ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD STERRY. I am an oilman, and live in High-street, Borough; my son and two others are partners with me. I had some lead on the roof of my own private house at Croydon—I had a new roof put on that house—the old lead had been removed—I sold it to Warner and Sons—Salmon was a carman in our service—he was sent to Croydon for the purpose of removing the lead, and he was ordered to call at our place in the Borough—he would have no business in Bermondsey-street with the cart on the 31st of Aug.—it is not in the direction from Croydon to my house in the Borough, or to Mr. Warner's—this lead worth 17 s . a cwt.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  I suppose you do not know where Salmon lives? A.  No.; I never knew where he lived—I understood he was in the service of Chaplin and Horne, before he came to us—I do not think we had a character with him—he was employed as a temporary carman while our man was ill—he drove the wagon about a month.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q.  Who cut the lead at your country house? A.  It was cut by a labourer—I sent a person to the police-office to identify some lead—I was there at the time—he was examined before the Magistrate—he is not a witness in this case—he answered the questions put by the Magistrate, but his testimony amounted to nothing. GEORGE ADAMS( policeman  156 M. ) On the 31st of Aug., between five and six o'clock in the afternoon, I was in Bermondsey-street, and met Salmon driving a wagon—he was coming in a direction from Payne's house—he asked me to drink, and I drank with him—he asked me to mind his wagon—I did so, and he went away for nearly ten minutes—he then came and turned his horses round, and went back, past Payne's shop—in Salmon's absence I had looked into his wagon, and found it contained lead—I received some information, and went to Payne's shop—I asked him whether he had bought any lead—he said no, he had not—Salmon was passing the door at the time, and I said to Payne, "Your bought some lead of that man"—he said yes, he had, but he had not paid him for it—it then left Payne, and went and overtook Salmon at the corner of Tooley-street—I told him he had been selling some lead to Mr. Payne—he said he had not—I said he had, for Mr. Payne had told me so—he said he hoped I would say nothing about it—he produced 5 s ., and begged of me to say nothing about it—I threw the five shillings on the ground, and Salmon got into his eat and drove away—I knew him very well, and where he worked—I then returned to Payne's shop, and told him I should go and inform Mr. Sterry of the circumstance—he followed me out of the shop, and begged me not to do it, and offered me half a sovereign—he followed me to Snows'-fields, urging me to receive the money and say noting about it—I did not take the money.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  How long have you known Salmon! A.  I think nearly three years—he lives in Cherry-garden-street, Dockhead, or somewhere near there—that is below where I saw him—I did not see his wife or children in the cart that day—the cart was coming back towards High-street when I first saw it, as if he had been further down—we had a pot of beer together before he left me—I should say it would take him half an hour to go from where I met him, to his own house—I do not know is whose service he was—I have seen him repeatedly driving a van—the cart appeared to have a full load.
