MARTHA ELIZABETH CHARLES. I am the wife of Ambrose Charles. The prisoner came to assist my servant, who was ill - she came on the Monday, and staid till the Sunday night, when she absented herself; I missed this money from the parlour closet - I have seen the tablecloths, apron and cap since.
   SAMUEL HASLOP. I am a pawnbroker. I have a table-cloth, a pair of woman's shoes, and a shift, pawned with me; the table-cloth on the 10th of February, by the prisoner, I believe.
     THOMAS SWEATMAN. I am a grocer. I tied up these papers of halfpence, and paid them to Mr. Charles on Saturday evening, the 13th of February; I know these are the papers.
   MARY COLLINS. I lived servant at the place. The prisoner took these shoes, and this collar of mine.
   JOHN WHITE. I am an officer. I have a cap which I took from her, and this apron and copper money I found in a box which the landlady said was hers - these papers were about the money; one was tied up, and the other had been opened.

 927.  WILLIAM STEPHENS was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April , 2 doors, value 1l., and 1 wooden partition, value 14s., the goods of Samuel Emsley.
 SAMUEL EMSLEY. I am a  stay-maker, and live in Kingsland-road. I began building a manufactory seven months ago; I found all the materials - the prisoner was to find labourers, and do the carpenter's work. On Saturday, the 10th of April, I went to see if the workmen had come - I met the prisoner coming from my premises with some beads and timber - I asked him why he did not go on with his work; he said, "I do not mean to take these things, Mr. Emsley;" I said, "It looks very much like it"- he then said, "I did take them," and he went back and threw them down; I then went to another place, where he had a contract and was to find the materials - I there found two doors, which I knew belonged to me - he followed me- I said I should not leave him till I had given him in charge; he went with me to Bethnal-green, and I gave him in charge for stealing two doors, which he denied, and said he had them by him for twelve months - but at Worship-street he acknowledged he had taken them; I do not recollect the words he first used, but he said be bought them of Pain, my foreman - I knew the doors, and on the Tuesday following I found a partition that he was painting- I ordered him to desist, as it was mine; he said it was not, but he afterwards, before the Magistrate, said he bought them of my foreman.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Have you any partner in this building? A. No; the prisoner is a master carpenter - he walked with me till I gave him in charge; the exterior of the doors was the same as they were before he took them.
   THOMAS PAIN. I am foreman to the prosecutor. I never sold these, or any thing else to the prisoner - I was ordered to go and take two doors which the prosecutor considered belonged to him, which I did, and gave to the officer.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you never received any money from the prisoner? A. No; I never received 12s. 6d., nor ever sold him any thing.
   JOHN COLLINS. I work for the prosecutor. I know these doors are his.
   JOHN COBHAM. I am in the employ of the prosecutor. I know these doors are his.
   JAMES MASON. I employed the prisoner to build a cottage for me, at No. 30, Camden-gardens; he was to find the materials - that is where the doors were found; they had been there two or three weeks, and had not been altered or disfigured.
   JAMES HAWKNAGE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - he said he did not recollect who he bought them of- he had had them twelve months.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   ROBERT HILL. I am a carpenter, and live at No. 10, Reliance-square, New Inn-yard, Shoreditch. I worked for the prisoner on those premises; I saw him take these things away, and Pain handed them outside the door to him - I think it was this day month; there was only the prisoner, Pain, and myself on the premises.
   THOMAS PAIN. He might have seen me giving them out, but with no intention of his taking them away, nor did I know he was taking them; the frames were made to enclose the doors.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do not you know they were taken away? A. Yes, but not by my orders; I gave them to be made use of.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 28. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor. Confined Eight Months.
 928.  WILLIAM STANLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 penknife, value 2s., the goods of William Elliott.
 MARY FELTS. I am servant to William Elliott - he is a  tobacconist. On the 17th of March the prisoner came into his shop, and asked the price of some tobacco; he was told the price, and while Mr. Elliott turned away, he stole a penknife off the counter, took it out of the shop, and gave it to another boy - I called the Police-officer, and brought the prisoner back; it was a good knife.
   THOMAS MIDDLETON. I am the officer I took the prisoner; I found 1s. 6 1/2d. on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went and asked the price of tobacco, came out, and went about a hundred yards; this lady came and said I had stolen a penknife - I said "I will go back."
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - Transported for Seven Years.
 929.  SARAH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April , 4 lbs. of beef, value 2s., the goods of Charles Baker.
 CHARLES BAKER. I am a  butcher. On the 10th of April a person told my man a woman had taken this beef from the shop; I had seen it about ten minutes before - the man brought the prisoner back, and she had my beef; I said to my man, "This is the woman I told you to take care of."
   ANN BALLIS. I saw the prisoner take the beef from the shop, which she placed under her arm; she then took up another, took up her cloak at the corner, and laid that one down - I told the man, and he took her.
