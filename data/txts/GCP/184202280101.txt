CHARLES SAVIN. I am an artificial-flower-maker, and live in South
Audley-street, Grosvenor-square. The prisoner was in my employ in
1840, in Berners-street, and Madame Roret also-Madame Roret went
home ill in June, 1840-about October, 1841, I had a conversation with
the prisoner about Madame Roret-he never showed me 241.-he told me
he had received 241, from Madame Roret--some time before that he told
me Madame Roret held the book of the savings' bank, and that he
would have the money-he said she had got the book from her husband,
and that he would make her draw out the money from the bank-I asked
him how he could do so-he said he would make her sign for her husband
-I told him she could not do so without putting herself into trouble-he
said, "Never mind, I must have it"-this was not a conversation about
making up the sum of 801.--that was before--he said that Mr. Roret was
a man of money, and had 2001. or 3001., and perhaps more, and he would
have it all I spoke in French to him I told him, by Madame Roret
signing her husband's name, she would get into trouble, meaning that she
would be injured, and it would go against her, put her into prison, or any
thing, by the French laws--the expression I used was, that if it came to
the knowledge of the bank they would proceed against her.
COURT. Q. Had you any thing to do with the transaction of the 802.? 4.
Yes--the goods had been bought from me I think some was paid in cash,
and some by a bill for a week, but I do not recollect exactly the amount
altogether was 801.--the prisoner paid me part first, and part afterwards
he paid me all-no part was paid by the prosecutor.
