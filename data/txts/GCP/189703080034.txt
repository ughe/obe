245. STANLEY FOXHALL FITCH, Unlawfully publishing a false
and defamatory libel of and concerning Francis Augustus Dod, knowing
it to be false.
MESSRS. HORACE AVORY and Rooth Prosecuted, and Mr. KAPADIA
Defended.
Tom Ford (Inspector N). At 7.30 on January 30th, I was stationed
at Stoke Newington, when the prisoner came in and said, "My name is
Stanley Foxhall Fitch ; I want to give myself up for conspiring with Dod,
Coles, and Wilson to set fire to the place, 63, High Street last May twelve
months ; Wilson is the man who set it alight; there was not £30 worth
of stuff burnt"-I said, "Are you aware of the seriousness of what you
say?"-he said, “Yes, I know what I am doing ; I have had this on my
mind long enough, and I mean to go through with it now”-I said, " If
that is so, I will take your statement down in writing"-he repeated the
statement, and I took it down at his dictation-I then read it to him, and
asked him if he would sign it- he said, “Yes," and he did so..
Cross-examined. It is usual to take down statements of this description
I should say the prisoner had been drinking, but I should not call him
drunk - I asked him if he would sign the statement-I believe I should be
liable to blame if T did not take down such a statement.
Re-examined. It is no duty, but only a practice, for the sake of
accuracy, and in case a man should deny his statement the next morning
--there is no statute requiring such statements to be taken down; there
is a police regulation to that effect.

go and fetch the others, and I can prove it; I have got some papers in
my bureau at home which will fix them up "-after he was charged be
