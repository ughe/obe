 870.  JOHN WATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of February , 53lbs. of cheese, value 35s., the goods of George Fitch and Thomas Fitch.
 GEORGE FITCH. I am in partnership with my brother Thomas, we are  cheesemongers, and live in Leadenhall-street- this cheese is ours - it was on a pile near the door.
   JAMES DODD. I am in the prosecutor's service. On the 28th of February I missed something from the door; I went out and saw the prisoner with the cheese under his left arm - I called Stop thief! - he dropped it and ran with all speed; I pursued him till the officer stopped him; I had seen this cheese safe about half an hour before.
 The prisoner put in a written defences stating that Dodd, at the time, could not identify him, but said he thought him the man.
 JAMES DODD re-examined. I am sure he is the man who dropped the cheese; I lost sight of him as he turned round by the five lamps, and was taken in Fenchurch-street.
 GUILTY. Aged 19. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 871.  SUSAN JAMESON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of March , 2 half-crowns, 6 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the monies of Benjamin Fish , from his person.
 BENJAMIN FISH. On the 26th of March I was in  Little Moor-fields, between twelve and one o'clock at night, and saw the prisoner; she said "Young man, I want to speak to you, come down this little alley;" we staid there some time, and as soon as she was gone I missed my money from my right hand trousers' pocket; I had unbuttoned my trousers; I did not give her any money - she was taken to the watch-house in about ten minutes - my purse was left in my pocket and two sixpences in it - six shillings were taken from the prisoner's bosom, and a sixpence from her mouth; I had been drinking, but was perfectly aware I had lost my money.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you give me 6s.? A. No; none at all.
   JOSEPH HORTON. I am a constable. I found five shillings and two sixpences in the prisoner's breast - I asked her if that was all she had got - she said Yes; but I thought she talked rather thick, and found two half-crowns and one shillings in her mouth - the prosecutor had stated that his money was 12s., in half-crowns, shillings, and sixpences.
     NOT GUILTY.
 872.  ANN WILKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , 1 lace cap, value 6s., the goods of James Edwards.
 JAMES EDWARDS. I lost a lace cap on the 14th of March, from my front room on the first floor. I was sitting in the back room and heard a noise; I went into the room - I saw the prisoner - I asked what she did there - my wife came in and missed the cap - I sent for an officer, but before he came she pulled the cap out of her pocket, put it on her head, and said it fitted her very well - she was in liquor.
   SARAH EDWARDS. I went into the room, and the prisoner took this cap from her pocket.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. Confined Two Months.
 873.  JOHN SAVAGE was indicted for stealing on the 23d of February , 1 cask, value 8s., and 8 gallons of Cloves cordial, value 3l. 16s., the goods of John Gillyatt Booth, and another, his partner.
   JOSEPH SMITH. I am servant to Messrs. John Gillyatt Booth, and his partner - they live in Cow-cross-street. On the 23d of February, I was in White-cross-street- I went up a yard, and left my waggon with some casks in it; I came back, and saw a man in the waggon, who gave the prisoner a cask out, and ran away; the prisoner went down Whitecross-street, with the cask on his shoulder - he walked as fast as he could, and I stopped him - he brought the cask back - it contained eight gallons of cloves cordial.
   JACOB COHEN. I saw the prisoner with the cask - he said he had to carry it to Smithfield for sixpence.
   JOSEPH HORTON. I am a constable. I was coming down Whitecross-street and saw a number of people - the witness had hold of the prisoner by the collar - I took charge of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was employed to take it to Smithfield.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 874.  LYDIA LEGGETT was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of January , 111/4 yards of thread-lace, value 4l. 6s.; 3 yards of blond-lace, value 3s.; 3 yards of ribbon, value 1s. 6d.; 61/4 yards of muslin trimming, value 5s. 2d.; 2 bands of work, value 2s.; 1 garnet, ring, value 1s., and 1 ivory fan, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Elizabeth Dimes, spinster, her mistress.

 Cross-examined by Mr. LAW. Q. Where did you live at the time? A. I lived part of the time in Chapel-street; I found part of my property at Mr. Arthur's, in Bird-in-hand-court, Cheapside; the fan and trimming were found in a box which she had bought of me before she left, and some were found under her bed; there are still many things missing, which I have not found.
   SUSANNAH DOCKER. I was in the prosecutrix's service. I went with her to the room in Bird-in-hand-court - the ring and blond lace were in a small box under the prisoner's bed.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was this at Mrs. Arthur's? A. Yes; the prisoner had no character when she came, but a sister of her's lived with Miss Dimes - she went from there to Mr. Arthur's.
