JOSEPH BARBER (Policeman, C 59). The prisoner was given into my
custody, with this part of a bad shilling- I asked him where the other part
was, and he said, " Find it"-I did not find it I found on him a half
crown, six 4d. pieces, and a penny, all good-he was taken to Monmouth
Street, and discharged-the party did not appear--he gave the name of
Richard Cook.
HARRIET PRICE My husband keeps the Norfolk Arms, in the Strand.
On 1st Aug. the prisoner and another man came in, about half past 12
o'clock at night-I served the prisoner with a pint of porter-he paid me
for it—he then asked for a pickwick, and his friend put down a penny to
pay for it—the prisoner said, "Take up your penny," and he put down a
shilling—I took it up, examined it, and found it was bad- I told the
prisoner so, and told him I should give him into custody-he wanted the
shilling back-I said no, I should not give it him back-he said he would
not believe it was bad-I sent for the policeman, and they were both taken
in charge-I marked the shilling, and gave it to the policeman.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE . How long did the prisoner remain
in your house ? A. About ten minutes-there were no other persons there
-We are obliged to keep open late--some of the houses about us are open
all night-I told the prisoner I should send for a constable-he remained
till the constable caine.
JOHN FENN (Police sergeant, F 27). The prisoner was given into my
custody- I received this shilling from the last witness the prisoner asked
me to let him look at it, and said he did not know it was bad- I took him
to the station, and found on him one good shilling and one penny.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This part of a shilling is bad, and this shilling is
bad, and they are from the same mould. GUILTY.-Confined Nine Months.
