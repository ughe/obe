 284.  WILLIAM DAVIS and JOHN HUMPHREYS were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of December , 1 stove, value 9s., the goods of Thomas Read.
   THOMAS READ. I live in Thomas-street, Brick-lane, and am a broker. On the 1st of December I missed a stove from outside my shop - I found it at a broker's in Club-row next day.
   ANN GILL. I live in Club-row, Bethnal-green, and am a broker. On the 1st of December Davis came to my shop, and asked if I would go and look at a stove - he said he lived in Phoenix-street; I got my bonnet, and went with him - it was standing against the wall inside, Humphreys was sitting by the fire; he turned round and said, "The reason we want to part with it is, the other is fixed" - they asked 5s.; I offered 4s.; they said "No, 4s. 6d." - I was going out, and Davis said, I should have it; I said, "You must bring it home," which Davis did - it was put out the next day, and Mr. Read came and claimed it; I went with him, and the officer - I pointed out to them where the prisoners were, and they were both at home.
   JOHN GREEN. I am a Police-officer. I was applied to by the prosecutor, and took the prisoners.
 Humphreys' Defence. I was standing at my mother's door in Phoenix-street; a man came and rested this stove by the side of the door, and Davis asked if he had it to sell; we bought it for 3s.
 The prisoners received good characters.
 DAVIS -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 HUMPHREYS -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined One Month.
 285.  JOHN HOPKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of November , 3 spoons, value 20s., the goods of William Lawrence.
   WILLIAM LAWRENCE. I keep  Serle's coffee-house, Carey-street, Lincon's Inn-fields. On the evening of the 30th of November, the prisoner came into the coffee-room about a quarter before six o'clock; he staid there ten minutes or a quarter of an hour, had a glass of ale, and was going out, when I heard a sound of silver; I waited at the door, and allowed him to pass me, and saw obtruding from his right-hand coat pocket, a spoon or prong; I followed him: he began to run, and run towards Bell-yard - I came up and caught him by the collar; I heard some silver fall, and all the observation he made was, "I have no plate."
 Cross-examined by MR. STURGEON. Q. You are not certain whether it was a spoon or a fork? A. No - he did not appear intoxicated.
   JOHN WARREN KEETLEY. I live at Derby, and am a conveyancer. On the 30th of November I was sitting in the coffee-room, and saw a person walk across the room from the adjoining box, towards the door; he went about a yard past the table, in the centre of the room, which is used as a sideboard - he then took a number of spoons from the sideboard, went towards the door, and was putting them into his pocket - I was rising from my seat, and at that moment Mr. Lawrence opened the half door; the man ran out at the other door - two of the spoons fell from his hand or his pocket.
 Cross-examined. Q. You do not know that the prisoner is the man? A. No.
   JOHN WEEKS. I am waiter to Mr. Lawrence. I saw the prisoner in my master's coffee-room - he had a glass of ale, and then asked me to give him another - I left the room to do so; there were some silver spoons on the centre table of the room - when I returned with the ale I picked up two spoons at the door; the prisoner was then just gone out, and my master after him - I took the two spoons to the table, and found that one was then missing.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you sure there were no forks among them? A. Not among the spoons; there were at another part of the table - he did not appear intoxicated.
 MR. LAWRENCE. I saw the prisoner stopped, without losing sight of him; the third spoon has not been found - these are the two.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going out, but finding I had left my gloves upon the table I went back, and called for another glass of ale; finding I had had enough before I went out without it - I made a stumble, and pushed the spoons from the table on the floor.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Weeks.

 287.  THOMAS CORMACK was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 iron trevet, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Bennett.
   THOMAS BENNETT. I am a journeyman weaver, and live in Brick-lane, Spitalfields- the prisoner is my cousin. On the 26th of November he came and drank tea with me and my wife; we went to wind some quills at the other end of the shop - the prisoner was then leaning against my loom-post; he then went down stairs, and I went to my loom and missed a trevet, with which we cut the plush; I afterwards saw him in custody,
   ELIZABETH BENNETT. I was present while the prisoner had tea - we missed the trevet before he was down the second pair of stairs; I saw him in Spital-square on the Tuesday, and gave him into custody.
   ARTHUR SOWERBY. I live in Wheeler-street, Spitalfields - I assist my brother, who is a pawnbroker. I have a trevet, pawned on the 26th of November, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, by the prisoner, for 2s.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 25.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
