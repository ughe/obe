On the 26th of February I went to the prisoner's place with a constable—I told the prisoner he had taken my hay, and he had used me very ill—he said if I would say nothing about it he would pay me for it—he is a carrier, and does jobs with a horse and cart—I believe the hay and straw found at his place to be mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  He does not live far from you? A.  No—his stable is about 200 yards from my house—he had been a customer of mine, and was in the habit of paying me on the Saturday night for what he had had in the week, and he had done so on the Saturday before that—I have not told persons who have come, to go and get things, with out sending my servant with them.
   COURT . Q.  Did you ever let him go and take what he wanted? A.  No-nor any body else. JAMES SUNNIE. I am in the prosecutor's service. I fastened the door at half-past nine o'clock at night, on the 25th of February, and about seven o'clock the next morning I got up, and missed some hay, a truss of straw, and one truss of clover—I received information—I spoke to my master, and we went to the prisoner's—I saw some hay there, and believe it to be my master's.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When had you seen the hay last? A.  On the Saturday night—our business is not over at half-past nine o'clock—we go on till twelve o'clock—this was like other hay—I had not cut it nor bound it. JOHN HASLOP. I am a private in the Grenadier Guards. I was on duty at the Dock-yard-gate, at Woolwich, at half-past six o'clock on that Sunday morning, and saw the prisoner come along with some hay—he went into his stable in Church-street, and came out again with nothing—I saw him return again, and a lad with him, who had a bundle of straw with him, and the prisoner had something, but I could not see what.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How did he carry the hay the first time? A.  On his shoulder—he came towards me—there was a lad with him each time. JOSEPH BUTTERFIELD. I am a constable. I went up to the prisoner's loft, and found two trusses of hay, one of clover, and one of straw—I then went to Orchard-street, and found the prisoner—I said, "Do you know the errand I come on?"—he said, "No"—I took him to the stable, and there the prosecutor owned the hay—as we came down, Mr. Shott's brother saw a bunch of keys in the prisoner's window—I took them, and one of the keys locked and unlocked Mr. Shott's gate.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you try that key to the prisoner's gate? A.  Yes, and it fitted it. WILLIAM SHOTT re-examined.  My gate was not broken—it had been opened by a key—I found it locked in the morning.
   MR. DOANE stated that the prisoner being in want of some hay, went that morning to the prosecutor'st found the door open, and took what he wanted, but intended to pay for it. )
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 32.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Six Months.
