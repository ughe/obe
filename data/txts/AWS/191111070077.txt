The Common Serjeant said that he would state a case to the Court of Criminal

Mr. Watt stated that he thought it most in the interest of his client that a

Williams and Green were then tried for possessing 20 counterfeit
florins with intent to utter.
Police-constable WAUTER SELBY, G Division. At 6.30 p.m. on
September 22 I saw Green and Williams among a crowd waiting for
the trams at the terminus in Moorgate Street behaving suspiciously.
I told Green I was going to arrest him as a suspected person attempt-
ing to pick poakats; he said nothing. In the meantime Deteotive Gill,
who was with me, arrested Williams, and we took them to the City
Road Police Station. Oh the way I heard Williams, who was behind
me, shout out, Get rid of them." Gill then said, "Look after his
hand." I looked towards Green's left hand, but he was too quick and
flung a newspaper packet behind him. Gill picked it up; it contains 20
counterfeit florins (produced). Williams was very violent. Prisoners
remained at the tram terminus for half an hour.
Cross-examined by Mr. MeDonald. When Gill called out to me
Green had his right hand in his trousers pocket; he threw the packet
away with his right hand. I had him by his left hand and could not
have dragged his hand and the paoket out of his pocket.
Cross-examined by Mr. Watt. I was about a yard and a half 10
front of Williams when he shouted. There was no crowd.
Detective ARTHUR GIL, G Division, corroborated the last witness.
SIDNEY WILLIAM SMITH, assistant assayer, M.M. Mint. The 20
florins produced are all counterfeit; 11 are made from one mould and
nine from another.
