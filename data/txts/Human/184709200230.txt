WILLIAM SAMPSON. I am a labourer, and live at Croydon. I removed a quantity of old lead from Mr. Sterry's house there, about the 30th or 31st of Aug.—this piece looks like the same sort of lead—it was taken away in three loads—Salmon took one load on the 31st of Aug.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  The lead was generally in large pieces, was it not? A.  Some of it was—there were pieces from 1lb. to 1cwt.—it is cast lead, not generally used, not in one house in thirty.
   MR. ROBINSON. Q.  Can you tell which was the heaviest load? A.  I suppose the last load—it was the largest in bulk—the first was lighter, the second heavier, and the third the heaviest. THOMAS HURST. I am in the service of Mr. Warner, of Cripplegate. I received three loads of cast lead from Salmon in Aug.—the last load was on the 31st of Aug., at past seven o'clock in the evening—I told him I thought he was very late—he said he had made all the haste he could—I said, "Do you request to have it weighed to-night?"—he said, "Yes"—I said, "Well, we will weigh it"—the weight of the first load was 39cwt. 3qrs., the second 31cwt. 3qrs. 26lbs., and the third 27cwt. 2qrs. 21lbs.—the second lcad was 8cwt. lighter than the first, and was 4cwt. heavier than the third.

JOSEPH BROWN. I am pot-boy at the Prince's Head public-house, a Richmond. I had a sovereign, a half-sovereign, and a gold pin, on the 20th of Aug.—on Saturday night, at six o'clock, the window of my room was safe, and everything all close—on Sunday, between twelve and one o'clock, I went to get my clean things in my room up stairs, and saw the window was open, and one pane of glass was broken—I went into the room, and found my box was broken open—I looked into it, and found a sovereign and a half had been taken out, and a gold pin and two brooches—when I had done my business I went to look for a policeman, and told him what I had lost—I told him had suspicion of the prisoner—when I came back the prisoner was in the tap room—the officer questioned him where he was the night before—he said he was at the Waterman's Arms till a quarter to eleven o'clock, and then he went to the Queen's Head, and was there from a quarter to eleven till a quarter is twelve on Saturday night, and while he was there he saw the pin offered for sale.
