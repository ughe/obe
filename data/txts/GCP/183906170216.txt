Court. Q. Then did Dean assist in all the meltings? A. Yes.
Mr. SERGEANT BOMPAS. Q. Did you put the rest into the crucible, or
did he? A. We did it together--my son Isaac was attending in the front
shop-I do not know whether he was there all the time or not-all the
gold was melted in Mrs. Abrahams's presence-each melting took from
half to three quarters of an hour-about half an hour to the best of my
recollection-as soon as it was melted she went away to fetch some
more at each time- the last parcel was brought, I think, between
five and six o'clock, to the best of my recollection, but I did not
notice the time the time it was finished, was between five and six o'clock,
the time she went away last-it was early in the afternoon that I sent
Isaac out I do not remember sending any body else out that afternoon-
I have no recollection of it I do not know whether I did or not-it was
about three or four o'clock in the afternoon that I paid the 4001., and after
the third melting-there were two or three meltings afterwards-Mrs.
Abrahams came next morning (Wednesday)- I think it must have been
between twelve and one o'clock- I cannot say exactly the time, because I
did not pay attention to it, but it was between twelve and one o'clock, or
about one o'clock--the bars which I sent to Messrs. Bult were melted on
the Wednesday morning-I am certain of that it was not on the Tuesday
-I mean the re-melting-Mrs. Abrahams saw the original melting, but
this was the re-melting--the gold was all re-melted on the Wednesday
morning, except the bar sent to Cock and Johnson's, and a bar of 70 02.
melted on Tuesday-I sent two bars to Messrs. Bult, which I melted on
the Wednesday morning--that is what I put some copper into--that was
all I put in, to the best of my recollection--the copper I put in myself and
my servant Dean-I was afterwards summoned and went before the Magis-
trate.
Q. Was what you swore then true or false ? A. I did not swear there
true--it was false-I think it was to some question about some 1001. notes
that I objected to answer any thing more to, to the best of my recollection
-I was not thinking all the time, but I swore falsely to protect myself
certainly-I was not thinking all the time I was questioned-I swore
falsely to screen myself.
Q. Have you come here now to screen yourself? A. I have come here
to give evidence of the truth now.
Q. Are you come to swear to what you say, in order to get yourself off?
A. All I have stated here to-day is the truth-Yes, certainly it is to
screen myself, I tell the truth, I would not tell a falsehood-I wish to
make every reparation in my power-I feel very great remorse for what
did before the Magistrate-I feel very great sorrow-I was taken into
