694. CHARLES WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of
January, 1 bridle, value 108.; and 1 set of harness, value 41. 108.; the
goods of George Smith, his master.
GEORGE Smith. I am a harness-maker, and live in Ropemaker-street,
Finsbury. The prisoner was my journeyman on the 15th of January I
gave him leave to take out a harness and bridle to sell, as he said he thought
he knew a widow at the West-end who he could sell it to-he was to get from
41. 108. to 51. for it-I told him to bring the money to me; and if he could
not sell it, to return it to me-I did not see him again till Sunday evening,
the 20th, when he was at the station-house-he never gave me the money
for the harness and bridle.
Prisoner. I could not sell it-and when I came home in the evening
he said he was very tired, and wanted the money, and I pawned it-I un-
fortunately met with some young men who proyed to be bad characters,
and they seduced me away. Witness. I never authorized him to pawn it.
JOHN WARREN. I am shopman to Mr. Ashman, a pawnbroker in
Long-acre. On the 16th of January, about five or six o'clock in the
afternoon, the prisoner pawned the harness for three guineas, and bridle
for 58.
Joun WOODYET (police-constable T 84.) On Sunday, the 20th of
January, I took the prisoner into custody, in King-street, Drury-lane-I
asked him what he had done with the harness-he said he had pawned it,
and his mother afterwards gave me the duplicates at the station-house.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined One Month.
