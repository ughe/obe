he gave me two bills, and cash amounting to 125l., but we did not balance on that day - on the Wednesday following, I was still in town, and I required him to balance his account as usual; he begged it might stand till the next morning; and on that day, which was the 30th, he made up the account in his own hand-writing - here it is from the 22nd to the 29th of August; it ought to have included the receipts of all monies within that time; it is entered here, "Received of George Boulton, on the 28th a two month's bill 13l., a thirty-day bill 9l. 10s., and cash 29l. 7s. 8d., making a total of 51l. 17s. 8d.;" there is no other account of any further receipt from Mr. Boulton, as of that period - in November last I had a customer of the name of Fairburn; in this cash account, which begins on November the 14th, and ends on the 22nd, and is made up and balanced in the prisoner's writing, (and was sent to me at Birmingham,) I find this entry,"November 20th, from Robert Fairburn two month's bill 18l., cheque 8l. 10s., total 26l. 10s." - I find, on the 26th of November, "from Robert Fairburn 16l. 8s. 9d.," but that is another account, it has no reference to this; here is no account of the receipt of 9l. 10s. from Fairburn; this 16l. 8s. 9d. is the balance of the account to July - I discharged the prisoner on the 29th of May last - a book was kept in my counting-house, containing the entries in both the cash-books; this is the book, it is in the prisoner's writing - the balance he makes due to me at the time I discharged him is 60l. 5s. 1d. - I called his attention to it, as it was unusually large, he appeared confused; I looked stedfastly at him, and said "I wish you to produce the money, lay it on the desk;" he said if I wished him to produce the money, I should have given him three or four days' notice; "Not I," said I, "I wish you to produce what you have" - he put his hand into his pocket, and produced 5s. 1d. - I asked what he had done with the remainder, he said he had made use of it; I replied,"That being the case, I cannot think of employing you any longer, I therefore now discharge you, and request you to give up your keys," which he did - he said if I would forgive him, his friends should pay the money; I told him that was out of the question - he then left the warehouse - I made no criminal charge against him for that balance - about the middle of July I discovered he had received monies which were not entered in the book, and I had him apprehended - he had 150l. a-year salary.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. How long have you been in the Birmingham business? A. More than twenty years - the firm was originally Jenkins and Hands, Jenkins was my father, and Hands was his cousin - I was an apprentice there, and so was the prisoner; I don't recollect whether it was it the same time - the prisoner was not engaged for a rival house when I had him taken into custody, to my knowledge.
 Q. Upon your oath, did you not know that he was engaged in executing the commission of a rival house, and were you not expressly informed of it by his brother? A. On my oath, I did not know it - I do not know that he was engaged for any house in the same business; I did not know he was engaged to sell on commission, or in any other way for any house in Birmingham or any where else; I know Mr.  Paul Moore of Birmingham; I did not know that the prisoner was engaged in business for him - I never heard that he was, or was about to be; I heard in the early part of July, that he had been in negotiation with Mr. Moore - Mr. Moore is a manufacturer in the same line of business as I am; I never employed the prisoner by commission - I offered him a commission to take place subsequent to Midsummer last; that was the subject of conversation in the early part of this year; I did not offer him that after the 29th of May - I am quite sure of that - I cannot say positively how much my returns have increased since the prisoner came into my service; he met me at Stony-Stratford in September, 1831, to look over the business of the establishment, but nothing was then mentioned about increase of salary - he mentioned that I had not given him credit for the present I had been in the habit of giving him - I wished him to leave the house of mine which he occupied, and I told him I would give him £30 a-year in lien of it; I cannot tell whether £52 a-year was mentioned, it might be matter of conversation - I never said that I would give it him; his salary was £120 a-year, and £30 additional for leaving the premises in Queen-street, where he had resided free of rent, coals and candles.
 Q. Did you ever say that you had been studying how to improve his situation, and that the conclusion you had come to was, that instead of his receiving £120 a-year as formerly, that from the 1st of January, 1832, he should be entitled to a commission of one per cent on all monies received? A. There was a proposal for a commission on his part, to which I did not consent - I did say I had been studying how to improve his situation; I had been in the habit of giving him a present, and I might have given him more - I had a very good opinion of him; I did not succeed to the business of his friends at Birmingham, they live in another part of the town - I swear positively and without reserve, that I never agreed to allow the prisoner any commission of any kind on any occasion; on the 30th of March, 1832, I sent him a statement of accounts desiring him to examine and return them, but he never returned them - I don't recollect his writing to me to say that the statement was not in accordance with my agreement, to allow him a commission of one per cent; I have no knowledge of his objecting to that statement.
