   MR. GODSON . Q.  Are you any relation of his? A.  No—he lived with me about two years—his business was making tools for masons—he had old and new iron rods—I used to see a great deal about—I did not take par ticular notice of them—he rented the upper part of my house at 20 l . a year, and had his shop adjoining—while he was at Westminster he was in the habit of making all the tools for Lee, of Westminster. GEORGE BARNES. I live at Woolwich—I am a mason, and a contractor for the Board of Ordnance. I have known the prisoner eight years—he bore a very honest character—I never was in his shed—he is employed to make and sharpen tools there—he has sharpened picks, and axes, and other tools for me—he is in the habit of making tools of other persons.
   MR. GODSON . Q.  Name any other person for whom he has made tools? A.  I cannot call one to mind just at present—at the time he was at West minster he made tools for the masons that worked in the new houses of Parliament—I cannot call to mind any one for whom he has made tools at Woolwich—I am a contractor under the Board of Ordnance—they do not allow their workmen to carry on trade on their own account, that I am aware✗ of—I live about a quarter of a mile from the house where the prisoner lives, at Woolwich—I have been in his garden since he has been in custody—I went to pay his wife a bill for sharpening some tools.
   MR. PARNELL . Q.  Do you know Mr. Bell and Mr. Prentice? A.  Yes—I know the prisoner has worked for Mr. Prentice—I am not sure whether he has for Mr. Bell—I have been employed by the Board of Ordnance—I am doing jobs for myself now.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 39.— Fined 5 l . and Imprisoned Three Months.

 1297. EDWARD HOLTON was indicted for stealing 2 shillings the monies of Edward Batchelor :—2nd COUNT  , stating them to be the monies of Philip Langford . MATTHEW M'GUIRE. I am a Greenwich pensioner. I met the prisoner on Saturday, the 1st of May—I went to a public-house and had a pot of the ale with him—I put half-a-crown on the table—the boy brought the ale and took up the half-crown—the prisoner got up and met the boy before he came back, and asked him for the change—he said the half-crown was his own—it was not his, it was mine.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you put it down on the table or did I? A.  I took it out of my pocket and put it down—I swear it was mine—you did not put it on the table. EDWARD BATCHELOR. I am servant to Mr. Philip Langford, who keeps the public-house. I took the half-crown from the table and went for the change—the prisoner came out and asked me for the change of his half-crown—I did not know whose it was—I gave him the two shillings.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I put the half-crown towards you? A.  No—it was lying on the table—I did not know whose it was. GUILTY.* Aged 21.— Confined Two Months .
