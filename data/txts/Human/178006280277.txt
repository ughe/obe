I followed her to the stairs; there she met Mary Pickott and gave them to her and bid her take them to her own house. Pickott said, what must I do with them? Then the prisoner said, carry them to-morrow morning to pawn, and you shall have half what they fetch. I went to her on the Friday following and asked her whose the black cloak and the other things I saw her have were? I told her I would be obliged to her to tell me. She said, yes, She would blow Mary Pickott for she had not given her half the money. She gave me a direction to Pickott. I went to Pickott; she gave me the things out of pawn on my paying for them; the blankets cost me sixteen shillings about six months ago.
   MARY NESBIT sworn,
 I saw the prisoner come out of Conner's house with some things in her lap; I followed her a little way, but the mob was so great I could not follow her far.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 Nesbit was in my room and wanted to lie there with a man.
 Nesbit. It was my husband.
 Prisoner. No, it was not; I would not let her lie there, and she went down stairs and picked up these things and put them in my room. Mrs. Conner came up and had what things I had saved, and the next day she asked me if I knew any thing of her cloak? I said, I did not. She went to this girl's house and found the things, and she said I gave them to her. I know nothing of it.
     GUILTY. Imp. 6 months.
 Tried by the First Middlesex Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
 426.  ANN SHERWOOD was indicted for stealing a child's linen shift, value 3 s. two linen gowns, value 5 s. a stuff petticoat, value 1 s. a pair of men's leather shoes, value 3 s. a linen handkerchief, value 1 d. and a cheque apron, value 2 s. the property of John Hill, June 19th.
   CHARLES TOWNSEND sworn.
 On Monday was three weeks, as I was going to work, Sarah Hill, the prosecutor's wife called out, stop that woman, she has stole my things! I followed the prisoner and stopped her in Penny-field, Poplar, with the things mentioned in the indictment, in her apron. She said they were her sister's. I brought her and them back to Mr. Hill's.
 (They were produced in court and deposed to by the prosecutor.)
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I leave myself to the mercy of the court.
     GUILTY. W.
 Tried by the First Middlesex Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
 427.  EDWARD M'CLOUD was indicted for stealing a wooden cask, value 2 s. and five gallons of English geneva, value 25 s. the property of Richard Hooper and William Hooper, June 27th.
   RICHARD HOOPER sworn.
 I live at  the Cross Keys-Inn, St. John's-street, there is a communication from the inn into Charter-House-lane. On the 27th of June I saw the prisoner going out into Charter-House-lane with this cask of geneva. I followed him and stopped him; he trembled and dropped the cask.
   CHARLES NAYLOR sworn.
 I live at Mr. Hooper's; while I was on the necessary, I saw the prisoner take a six gallon cask and go out; when I came out my master had him in possession.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 Going through this place, a young man asked me to help him with this cask.
     GUILTY. Fined 1 s.
 Tried by the First Middlesex Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
