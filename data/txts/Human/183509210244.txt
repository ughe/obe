   COURT Q.  Did Bray appear to be the aggressor? A.  Yes; by con stantly crossing over to them. JOSEPH MASON. I am a chemist, and live at No, 26, Oxford-street, I have had opportunities of observing the conduct of the omnibuses—I have seen Bray's drive as rapid a pace as the others—If one of the others stopped, Bray's would stop also—I have seen that more than once or twice, and I have seen them run the pole very near the other people's door—he has not stopped for a passenger—I have not had that idea—I have not seen any passengers—when the others went on, Bray did so also, and followed them up or down the street, keeping very near indeed to them, as near as they could—I observed that in the first month—I should say Bray's was the aggressor.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALL . Q.  When you have seen Bray's did you see it with others? A.  Yes; the "Emperor"—I cannot state any others, but I have seen it with two or three others.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was it following them or before them? A.  They were altogether.
   COURT . Q.  Did those other omnibuses appear to you to act towards Bray, as Bray did to them? A.  I should say there was no difference in that respect.
   JURY . Q.  Were you subpœnaed here? A.  No; I am here quite dis interestedly—It was intimated to me a friend of the parties that Mr. Holding wished me to come, but I should have come without.
   COURT . Q.  Who is Mr. Holding? A.  He was a very respectable che mist in the same shop—I heave no motive in giving evidence but to clicit the truth. JOHN WILLIS. I am a hosier, and live at No. 371, Oxford-street. I have had opportunities of observing the omnibuses that drive up and down from paddington to the Bank for the last five years—those with "Aaron Bray" on them have been conducted in the most did orderly way imaginable—previous to the omnibuses of Aaron Bray coming on the road, they were conducted as orderly as it was possible for vehicles of that description to be; so much so, that I allowed them, two years ago, to change horses at my house—since Aaron Bray began running up the street, to my coat, I have found that the disorder has been so great that ladies could not come in their carriages to my shop—they were afraid—I have seen the other omnibuses stop to take up passengers on several occasions—I have seen Bray's omnibuses pull up with the pole against the door of the other omni buses, to prevent a passenger from getting in, in more instances than one—from what I have seen, Bray was decidedly the aggressor, because I con sidered he was in opposition to the others—before Bray came, the road was orderly—they were going quitely about their business.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  When this took place, was the "Emperor" the one before or behind it? A.  That I could not say; but I have noticed Mr. Bray's being a blue omnibus.
