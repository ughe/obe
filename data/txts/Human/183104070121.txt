 969.  WILLIAM WALKER was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS DINSDALE. I am an agent and clerk at St. Katharine's-wharf - the prisoner was occasionally my agent, or servant; I paid him as he worked. Some rags were sent to me for sale; he told me he had sold them to Rackley for 19l. 6s. 1d., according to their weight - I gave him a note to Rackley for the money; I had sent the goods by my team on the 31st of March, about twelve o'clock in the day, and was to have the money back the same day; I authorized the prisoner to receive it for me- I had paid him 6s. that day for previous work which he had done; he never brought me the money - I told him to bring it to me or my employer; I did not see him again till the morning following, when I went with an officer, and took him out of bed.
 Prisoner. Mr. Dinsdale received 1l. on account. Witness. I received 1l. from Rackley the day previous to his getting the money; I got it to pay the prisoner the 6s.
   JOHN RACKLEY. I bought the rags of the prisoner as agent for Dinsdale - they came to 19l. 6s. 1d.; I had paid 1l. on account, and paid the prisoner 18l. 6s. 1d. on the 31st of March - here is a receipt which he signed for it in my presence (read); I paid him the whole amount, but finding 1l. had been paid, he returned that to me.
 MR. DINSDALE. He was to take the money to my employer if I was not in the way - I have not received any of it.
 Prisoner's Defence I received it on Good Friday, and on Saturday was going down to pay Mr. Dinsdale -I got intoxicated, and was robbed on Saturday evening; on Sunday morning I took the party who robbed me to the station-house in Wellclose-square; I was to appear against them at Lambeth-street on Monday, but Mr. Dinsdale came with an officer and took me out of bed.
 MR. DINSDALE. After he was apprehended, he took a German there who could not speak English, and said he had robbed him; the Magistrate was three hours investigating the case, and then said there was not the least charge against the man, and I must prosecute the prisoner - the prisoner said the man and him bad had a glass of rum together, and that ten or twelve people were in the house when he was robbed, but it was proved he was not robbed at all.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. - Transported for Seven Years.
 970.  SARAH BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of April , 1 sheet, value 7s., and 1 spoon, value 2s., the goods of John Gibson ; and 1 spoon, value 2s., the goods of Ann Garlick.
   ANN DUTT. I live in Hornsey-row, Islington. Mr. John Gibson and Ann Garlick lodge with me - the prisoner had been rather better than twelve months in Mr. Gibson's service; I had reason to suspect her - the officer searched her box on the 3rd on the April, and found several duplicates.
   JOHN NEAL. I am a Policeman. I found six duplicates in the prisoner's box, one for a shirt and a spoon, and I found duplicates of five more spoons on her person.
 GEORGE STOWELL. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Islington. I have a shirt and two spoons, pawned by the prisoner at different times.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 24.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. Confined Three Months.

 972.  JAMES SMITH was indicted for bigamy.
   DANIEL HONE. I live in Castle-street, Bloomsbury - I am a hair-dresser. I went to the parish church of St. Martin in the Fields, with the prisoner, my wife, and another person - the prisoner was there married to my sister, Elizabeth Hone; she was at that time single - she is still alive; they lived together till last Spring - I think in April last he left her.
   SUSAN TIBBS ROWEL. I live in George-street, Portman Chapel. I have known the prisoner two years last August - I understood him to be single, and that he was living in Marylebone-lane; I went to Christchurch, Spitalfields, with him on the 25th of last May - we were married there; the pew-opener and the clerk were present - I was married in the name of Susan Rowel; a friend of mine heard the prisoner was married, while I was in the country with him, at Overton, in Hampshire, and when I came to town, seven weeks or two months after we were married, I heard of it.
 Prisoner. I was with her drinking in Tottenham-court-road, two or three days before I was married, I got nearly tipsy, and I told her I was married. Witness. No, he did not - I never knew it; I have been with him there several times, and have taken a glass of ale, but have never been intoxicated - he never told me he was married.
 Prisoner. I told her the very day, that I had not money to provide for the marriage, and she had better put it off to a future time. Witness. I did pay for the marriage, but I never understood he wished to put it off - I was living in Carpenter's-buildings, London-wall, in service, up to the day of the marriage.
