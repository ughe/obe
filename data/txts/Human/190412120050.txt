I am a tailor of 10, Upper Baker Street, and let part of my premises on May!), 1903, to the prisoner and Leech; they took two rooms at £10 a year; this is the agreement, it is yearly with quarterly notice, the prisoners told me they were carrying on the business of house agents, hotel brokers &c.; he said Leech would be answerable for the rent, and he would be lessee; Leech signed the agree ment, and the prisoner witnessed it—the rent was paid up till the December, 1903, quarter, that was the last—when they left they owed about five months' occupation—I asked for the rent as it became due; then I distrained after the Michaelmas quarter, I think at the beginning of June—there was some furniture there which was sold for £8 2s., the costs were £3, so I got £5 2s., and there was money owing for gas.
   Cross-examined.  The prisoner was away for about two months in the spring, Leech told me, with a broken ankle—Leech carried on the busi ness while the prisoner was away—I do not know that while the prisoner was away Leech sold some furniture.
