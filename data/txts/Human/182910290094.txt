   JAMES ROGERS. I am a servant at the Horse Shoe and Magpie. I saw the prosecutor at our house last Wednesday night rather tipsy; the prisoner and another soldier were with him, and they drank together.
 FERDINAND JOHNS. I am a constable.  Prisoner's Defence. I was at a house in Brownlow-street; I left a quarter before nine o'clock, and came straight down to the barracks - I had not been at that part of Westminster the whole day. On Friday a man in a red jacket came to me and said, "Soldier, I will sell you a bargain;" he took me aside, showed me this watch, and said he would sell it for 1l. - I said I had not 1l., but I would give him 15s. for it, as I was no judge of watches; I had never had one in my life - I bought it, and then, as I had to go on furlough, I pawned it to get the money.
 ROBERT DUFF. I am pay-serjeant of the company to which the prisoner belongs, in the 3rd regiment of Foot Guards - he was present at the roll call in the King's`mews barracks, at nine o'clock on Wednesday night last; he has been above four years in the regiment, and had a good character for honesty.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Life.
 2088.  JAMES WARD was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of October , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Joseph Dudley Webb , from his person.
   JOSEPH DUDLEY WEBB. On Friday last, between four and five o'clock, I was going up Queen's Head-lane, Islington; three boys followed me, and the eldest, who was this prisoner, pushed against me, and looked me in the face - I put my hand to my pocket, and my handkerchief was gone; the boy had then got on five or six yards before me - I cried Stop thief! ran on, and seized him; I said, "Where is my handkerchief?" he said, "I have not got it, search me;" a youg girl, who stood at a door, said," Your handkerchief is on those steps" - I dragged him back, and took it up; he had passed the others, but they were behind me when I missed my handkerchief; it must have been thrown there before he passed me; I had seen him with two others for a minute or two before.
 ELIZABETH HARDING. I live in Queen's Head-lane. I saw this gentleman there; the prisoner picked his pocket, and threw the handkerchief on the steps - I saw it done, and told the gentleman of it; he took up the same handkerchief the prisoner threw down.
   JAMES ROOMES. I am a watchman of Islington. I took the prisoner, and gave him to Mr. Cope the officer.
 THOMAS COPE. I received the prisoner, and this handkerchief.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. There were two boys behind me, and I walked on before the gentleman - he then came, took me, and said, "You have my handkerchief;" I said I had not - the other two boys ran away; the little girl and a gentleman, who was there, said it was not me.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Life.

 2089.  WILLIAM WILSON was indicted for embezlement.
   JOHN BARHAM. I am a baker, and live at the corner of Baker-street, New-road- the prisoner had been four weeks with me as a weekly servant; I gave him 12s. a week - he was to take out bread, and to receive money and account for it every afternoon: I was not satisfied with him, and gave him notice to quit - he left me on Saturday, and on Monday I discovered this.
   FRANCES DELL. I deal with the prosecutor - I paid the prisoner on a Thursday or Friday, about the 25th of September, 15s. for his master; it was a weekly bill - he wrote this receipt on it (read); I gave him a half-sovereign, two half-crowns, or 5s.
   JOHN BARHAM. He did not bring me this money; he said they wanted change for a sovereign, and he could not give it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I received the money, and paid it to the shopkeeper - the prosecutor was there at the time; he stopped 5s., and said he would make a debt of it.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
 2090.  WILLIAM MESSENGER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , 8 feet of deal plank, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Richard Chapman ; and that at the delivery of the King's Gaol of Newgate, holden for the County of Middlesex, at Justice-hall, on Wednesday, the 25th of October, in the 56th year of the reign of George III, he was convicted of felony by the name of William Passenger.
   JOHN HARDY. I am a watchman of Islington. At a quarter-past four o'clock in the morning of the 13th of September, I met the prisoner in the back road with this piece of timber under his arm - I said, "Mate, what is this?" he dropped it, and offered me 1s. 6d. to let him go, but I would not; he was then within a few hundred yards of the prosecutor's.
   RICHARD CHAPMAN, JUN. This was my father's property; his name is Richard. I had seen it safe in the brick field, but I cannot say when; the prisoner had been in our employ three years, but when we found he was a returned transport we discharged him, and since he has been in our employ we have lost twenty-two planks.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took this bit of plank by order of the foreman to lay over a dirty ditch, for some of my children to get a penny if they could; the foreman said I might have it if I brought it back at night.
   WILLIAM ELLIS. I produce a copy of the conviction of the prisoner by the name of William Passenger (read).
 WILLIAM GALE. I am a timber merchant. I remember the prisoner being tried on that indictment; I attended on the part of my employer - I have no doubt of his being the man.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. Transported for Life.
