ELIZABETH BEDYORD. I pass by the name of Hockham, as the prosecu-
tor's wife. About one o'clock in the morning of the 27th of Oct. I was with
him in Stamford-street-just opposite Bennett-street be turned round-1
looked round, and saw Fury with this handkerchief in his hand--the other
two prisoners were there-Hockham said to Fury, "That is my handkerchief:
you are not going to rob me of that"-Fury up with his hand, and struck him in
the face-he fell-Fury fell also, and as soon as he got up he struck me a vio-
lent blow in the face, knocked me down, and knelt on me; and while I was
down, M'Duff caught me by the throat, and pinched me I said, "Ob they
are throttling me!" and Keating came up and kicked me in the ribs- be-
came insensible, and do not recollect anything more for a long time-1 after-
wards went to the station in a cab-I was too ill to walk-I afterwards
went to a surgeon-this must have occupied several minutes I have no
doubt of either of the prisoners.
Cross-examined. Q. How came you to call yourself the prosecutor's

my name was Elizabeth Bedford I was not asked-I did not take bold of
