MR. PURCELL conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL NEWMAN. I am in the service of Mr. Coltman, who keeps the Old George public-house, Bethnal Green Road—when I went to bed at about 1.30 a.m. on 21st September everything was all right—I left in the bar 2 l . in silver, 4 s . in copper, and 2 l . 15 s . in coppers sealed up in 5 s . packets, and some cigars—at five o'clock I was called up, went downstairs, and missed all the money and the cigars, some grocery, 10 s . in small money in a purse, a pair of shoes, nearly new, and a newspaper, which were on the sofa the night before—I found two pairs of old shoes in the yard, which were not there the night before, and I missed one pair of shoes of another man's—these are my boots ( produced )—I saw them on the prisoner's feet at the station—there were four tiles off the back kitchen, which is a lower roof, and that would give access from one room to another—I gave information to the police. ALFRED DYKE( Policeman K  343). At about 2.30 on the morning in question I was in Blyth Street, at the top of which is the prosecutor's house—I saw the two prisoners go from 47, Blyth Street, in the direction of the George public-house—I was about four yards from them then—I saw them again about three-quarters of an hour afterwards, coming from the George public-house to 47, Blyth Street, where they lived—I asked them what they were doing at that time in the morning—Brown said "We are late"—about five o'clock prosecutor made a communication to me, and I went to 47, Blyth Street with another constable—the landlord called the prisoners, and they came down—Brown was wearing these boots, and Lloyd these others ( produced )—I asked Brown where he got his boots from—he said "I bought them this morning"—I asked Lloyd where he got his—he said that he bought them three weeks ago—I took them to the station, and on Brown I found 10 s . in silver, and 14 1/2 d . in bronze—on Lloyd four postage cards, a cigar case, three cigars, and a key ( produced ). SAMUEL NEWMAN( re-examined ). All these articles are mine but the postcards. JAMES GULLIVER( Policeman K  9). I went to 47, Blyth Street with Dyke, and saw the prisoners wearing the boots—I went up to their room—Brown wanted to go up; I prevented him—under the bed I found 30 s . in silver tied up in a piece of old flannel—they lodge in that house, and occupy the same room—Brown's sister lives in the same house—I searched the two bedrooms all 'over—I found no rolls of copper coin—I watched the house from 6 till about 7.30, and then searched.
   Brown's Defence.  This is the first time I have ever done anything '—I have a very good character, and want to get some work when I get out of this.
   BROWN  — GUILTY — Six Months' Imprisonment each .
