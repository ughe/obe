I took the examination of the prisoners, (after the usual caution,) word for word, as they delivered it—they did not sign it—they were not asked to do so—we do not always ask them to do it—it is signed by the Magistrates—I read it over to the prisoners before the Magistrates signed it—( read )—"William Matthews says, 'On Satur day morning I was up at Norwood, digging potatoes in a field, from seven o'clock until about three, or twenty minutes after three o'clock—I went to take some potatoes to Mr. Page, of Dulwich; and it was half-past three o'clock when I got to the George the Fourth, at Norwood-hill—I took the potatoes to Mr. Page, and then went home—about five o'clock Smith brought a horse, and asked me to buy it—he said it was to be killed, for it came from a gentleman, and he was to take the tail back—I said I did not kill horses—this took place in Dulwich-street, in the village—he asked 2 l . for the horse—I offered 23 s . for it—he said he must have the tail, and would come on the Sunday week for it—I borrowed 4 s . of a young man to enable me to pay for the horse—we had some beer together, and then he went away, and I did not see him again—I afterwards tried all I could to find Smith—yesterday week I bought another horse of Smith, which I also sold to Mr. Giddens.'"
 "The prisoner, William Smith, says, 'On Saturday morning, about one o'clock, I was desired to stay under Greenwich-park wall, at the top of Maize-hill, with four horses—I was desired to do so by Thomas Smith, my master, James Batchelor's foreman—I staid there till two o'clock—this black cart-mare was brought to me, and I was directed to take it straight across the heath, through Lewisham, and take it to the man where I had taken the other to; and I was to ask him 40  s ., for it, but to take what he bid me—he bid me 23 s ., which I took, and gave to Thomas Smith, where they are making the railway, by the canal—he gave me 2 s . for myself—he put me on a horse, and sent me to Croydon with four horses—I had seen Matthews twice before this.'"
