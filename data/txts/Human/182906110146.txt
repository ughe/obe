 1318.  WILLIAM TENSEY was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   JOHN SIMON ENGLEHEART. I am a pork-butcher, and live in St. Martin's-le-Grand. On the 25th of April, the prisoner came to me for a leg of pork, for Mr. Kingsford, of the Swan with Two Necks, Lad-lane, who deals with me - I never saw him before; I supplied him with it - it came to 6s. or 7s.; I asked him for the book which Mr. Kingsford generally sends - he said he had come away in a hurry, or something: on the 30th of April he came and asked for another leg of pork for Mr. Kingsford, which I gave him, and asked him for the book; he said he had forgotten it, and come away in a hurry; on the 1st of May he came again for a leg and a loin, for Mr. Kinsford; I let him have them - he came again in the afternoon for a loin; I had my suspicions then, but being busy I let him go away with it; and on the 7th of May he came again for a leg and loin - I gave it him, and sent my man to follow him; he stopped him - the value of all the meat is 26s. or 27s.
   WILLIAM KINGSFORD. I keep the Swan with Two Necks, Lad-lane. The prisoner was never in my service - I never saw him till he was apprehended.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Six Weeks.
 1319.  THOMAS TADDELL was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   WILLIAM CLARK GOLLAND. I live in Watling-street, and am a warehouseman. On Sunday, the 19th of April, a little after eight o'clock in the evening, I was coming from St. Bride's church, and nearly opposite St. Dunstan's church, I felt somebody at my pocket; I turned round, and felt the prisoner's hand in my pocket - I collared him; I had a handkerchief in that pocket - he drew part of it out in endeavouring to extract his hand; I said,"You rascal, you are trying to pick my pocket:" he immediately said I was a rascal, and wanted to rob him - I held him; some persons collected, and wanted me to let him go, which I should have done, but somebody struck me behind - I turned round, and he struck me himself with all his might in my mouth, which bled profusely - he got from me, and ran up Bell-yard; he was secured in a minute - I have not the slightest doubt of him; he used the most abusive language a man could.
 CHARLES READER. I am a law bookseller, and live in Bell-yard. I heard a cry of Stop thief! turned round, and secured the prisoner, who I had seen shortly before this happened - I had taken hold of him three minutes before for picking a gentleman's pocket, but the handkerchief was returned, and the gentleman declined prosecuting; I secured him, and a party kept soliciting me to let him go, as there was nobody to prosecute him; I let him go, and immediately found my own pocket had been picked - he did not get from me after Golland's robbery.
 THOMAS LIGHTFOOT. I am a beadle. The prisoner was given into my custody - I saw nothing of the transaction.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Confined Two Years, and Publicly Whipped.

 NEW COURT, Seventh Day.
 First Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1321.  MATTHEW SKRUSE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of June , 1 table-cover, value 12s., the goods of James Grant.
 JAMES GRANT. I am a  licensed hawker. On the 1st of June I was at the White Horse public-house, in Maiden-lane; I staid there - the prisoner came in soon after I got there, and sat at the next table; I went out between four and five o'clock - I went into a field near Kentish-town; I put down my pack it being heavy - I was awoke by the rattling of the papers which my tablecovers were in, and saw the prisoner with my table-cover in his hands; I laid hold of it - he dropped it, and struck me; the officer took him.
 WILLIAM LADWICK. I am an officer. On the first of June, between four and five o'clock, I was in the field; I saw the prosecutor, the prisoner, and three others - the prosecutor had hold of a table-cover, which he claimed, and said the prisoner had taken it; the prisoner said that he awoke the prosecutor up, or he would have been robbed by the others, and that he had been drinking in the same house with them, but the prosecutor denied it; the prosecutor said if he would go to the White Horse, and the landlord would deny that he had been there, he would forgive him; we went - the landlord said he had not been there, but the landlady said he had; I took the prisoner to the watch-house - he struck me in the face on the road, and was rescued at the Bellisle; I took him again at eight o'clock the next night.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you, on the first examination, swear to him as the man? A. Yes, and he was remanded, that I might find the parties who rescued him - I found a sixpence and some copper on him.
 JAMES GRANT re-examined. The others were his comrades; I said to him, "Are not you the man who was drinking with me at the White Horse, a few minutes ago?" he said, "No - I am pot-boy at the King of Prussia public-house, and am gathering my pots;" he struck me a violent blow, and said he had saved me from being robbed by the others, but it was no such thing - he afterwards said he had been at the house.
 Cross-examined. Q.Were you not asleep? A. I was nearly asleep; my eyes were shut; he had the cover in his hand when I awoke; I had not drank much - I was not tipsy.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Confined Three Months.
