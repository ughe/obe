I heard a cry of Watch! went up the passage and saw the prosecutor, who said he had been robbed and ill-used; we went up stairs and found the prisoners in bed - Banks was undressed; Winter said, "That is the young man who ill-used me;" we ordered him to get up and dress, which he was very loath to do; Solomons would not get out of bed, but seemed fumbling about to put something away; she got up, we took them both to the watch-house, then returned to the room, and found two sovereigns, eight shillings, and three farthings, in the handkerchief.
   RICHARD BRIDGE. I was with Phillips - what he has stated is correct.
   JOHN DANGATE. The prisoners were brought to the watch-house; the watchman afterwards brought the money in the handkerchief - Solomons claimed the handkerchief before the Magistrate.

 BANKS' Defence. Bridge said he had seen the prosecutor in Bell-yard with two women; he was locked up, the prosecutor being disorderly; I had been to a fair, as I returned I met Solomons, who asked me to go home with her, which I did; I saw the prosecutor there - I was going to retire; she said, "No, don't go away, wake him, and he will go;" I awoke him - he got up and put his things on, and went to strike me; I put him out and called the watchman.
   WILLIAM BRIDGE. I had seen Winter with two of his comrades, but no women.
   ROBERT PHILLIPS. The prosecutor was detained to give the charge, not for being drunk or disorderly.
   JAMES WINTER. I had nothing off but my jacket when Banks was rumbling about me; my trousers pocket is cut where they got my money out.

 1823.  JANE EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., of John James Hodgson , from his person.
 JOHN JAMES HODGSON. On the 28th of July, I was in  Bath-place, New-road- I left my handkerchief go, turned round, and saw the prisoner with it in her hand; I seized her, but she was too strong for me; I called the watch, but no one came; I pulled a second time, and she let go of it.
 Prisoner. I did not take it from his person - I am an unfortunate girl, and was with him for half an hour - he told him to feel in his pocket to see if he had any money; he said I carried him across the road; and he said "You hussy, if there was a watchman I would give you in charge;" I was on the other side of the way - he saw a watchman by the Adam and Eve - he crossed over, and gave charge of me; I have no parents or friends.
 JOHN JAMES HODGSON. I was not in her company at all - she did drag me across the road, and asked me for 2d.; I would not give it her, and she took my handkerchief.
   JAMES DAYTON. I am a watchman. The prosecutor came up to me, and gave the prisoner in charge - at the watch-house, he gave me the handkerchief from his pocket.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1922.  GEORGE JURDEN was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 1 handkerchief, value 6s., the goods of William Foreman , from his person.
 WILLIAM FOREMAN. On the 22d of July, I was going up  Broad-street, St. Giles', between twelve and one o'clock in the morning - I had spent the evening out with some friends - three or four females came up and asked where I was going; the prisoner then came up and took my handkerchief; I made a snatch at it - he drew it from me and ran off; I pursued him thirty or forty yards, and overtook him at the corner of Middle-row, but my handkerchief was not found.
 Prisoner. Q. What part of Broad-street was this? A. A few yards from George-street; I was not accusing the girls of taking it; I gave charge of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been up late settling some business - I was returning home, and saw the prosecutor pulling three girls about, and accusing them of taking his handkerchief - I passed on, and soon heard the cry of Stop thief! I turned round, he came up, and accused me of taking it; if I had, it must have been found on me or in the street.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1923.  JOHN LUKES and JOHN BUCKERIDGE were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of July , 1 necklace, value 2s., the goods of Christopher Taylor , from the person of Martha Taylor.
   ANN TAYLOR. I am the grandmother of Martha Taylor- she is nineteen months old, and is the daughter of Christopher Taylor, my son. On the 15th of July, I took her out with a necklace on - I did not miss it till a gentleman asked if I had lost it.
   THOMAS HOBBS. On the 15th of July, I saw Buckeridge go up to this child, which Mrs. Taylor was holding by the hand - he unclasped the beads, and Lukes took them off the child's neck: they both ran off; I pursued, and took Lukes, and he then dropped these beads at his feet - I took them up.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I received Lukes in charge with the beads.
