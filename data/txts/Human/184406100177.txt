JAMES JACOB SPENCER. I am foreman to Mr. James Fougere, a tailor, in Nelson-street, Greenwich. On the morning of the 18th of May, about nine o'clock, we had some dressing-gowns in our shop, tied on the back of a chair and to a cross-rail fixed in the shop—they were about six feet within the street door—when I left the shop, about nine, they were safe, and on returning at a quarter to ten they were gone—these now produced are them—they are Mr. Fougere's property, and are worth 8 l .—it is his dwelling-house. GEORGE ROUSE. I live at Greenwich. On the 18th of May, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning I was passing Mr. Fougere's door, and saw the two prisoners coming out of the shop with this bundle—I think Frankland carried it—I called to Dyer to assist in taking them, which he did, and we secured them—I am certain they are the men. THOMAS DYER. I am a gardener, and live in the Hampstead-road. On the morning of the 18th of May, I was walking near Greenwich church, be tween nine and ten o'clock, and saw Mr. Rouse with the two prisoners—I stopped Frankland—he had this bundle on his back, and Ferguson had hold of it behind. JOHN WHETLE( policeman. ) I was on duty near the church at Greenwich, and saw the two prisoners running from Nelson-street towards the church—Frankland had the bundle—they were together—I saw Dyer take them—he gave them into my custody with this bundle. JOHN SPRING( City police-constable, No , 315.) I produce a certificate from Mr. Clark's office, of both the prisoners' former conviction—( read )—I was present at the trial, and I know them to be the persons.
   FRANKLAND  — GUILTY. Aged 25.

JOHN BAKER was indicted for feloniously assaulting Mary Ann Wright , and cutting and wounding her on her right arm, with intent to do her some grievous bodily harm.—2nd COUNT  , calling her Mary Ann Baker . MART ANN WEIGHT. I live at Tanner's-hill, Deptford. I have lived with the prisoner two years—we are not married. On the 12th of June he came home between eleven and twelve very drunk indeed—I never saw him so drunk—I began to blow  him up because he had spent some money—I blowed  him up very much indeed, and after I had done so some time he hit me with his fist in my face—it left no mark—I went to bed—I had no candle or fire—he was not in bed—I blowed  him up again very violently indeed, and then I felt a cut on my arm—I screamed out, and went out of the house into another person's house—the cut does not affect me at all—it is quite well now—there was a little chopper in the house—I had not seen it for a week, not having occasion to use it. ANN JARMONSWAY. I live next door but one to the prisoner. On the night of the 12th of June I heard cries of "Murder," and went into his apartment—the door was open—Wright was lying on the bed—I persuaded her to come into my room—she held up her hand, and I said, "O, Mary, look at the blood"—when the policeman came into the room with a light I found her arm was cut. WILLIAM WELDON( police-constable R  40.) I apprehended the prisoner between one and two in the morning—I asked him if he was the man that had been ill-using the woman with the hatchet—he said, "Yes," he was—I asked what he had done with the weapon—he said he had put it away so that I could not find it.
