   Cross-examined by MR. METCALF. Q.  What was in the corn-bin at the Swan? A.  Chaff, split beans, and oats—it appeared to be clover chaff—I saw some loose meadow hay about—I did not see the contents of the which I saw handed down—I wrote nearly a hundred pieces of paper, which I gave to Mr. Gammon on Wednesday morning—I found only this one in the corn-bin at the Swan—I searched for nearly two hours—it was about half-past eight on Wednesday morning that the prisoner stopped and handed down this sack—I was eighty or ninety yards off—I do not know whether he saw me.
   COURT. Q.  You saw him take two sacks from the cart on that Wednes-day one full, and the other partly full? A.  Yes—when I afterwards west into the coach-house, I saw some coke in sacks, and some shot down on the ground. HENRY DOWSETT( policeman, D  181.) On Thursday morning, the 18th, I saw the prisoner come out of the Swan public-house—he got on the front of the cart, and banded a sack half full of something to a man named Brewer—he took it down the Swan Livery-yard—he returned, and then the prisoner handed a sack three parts fall down to Brewer—he took that down in the same direction—I went down the yard, and saw Brewer come out of the stable with an empty sack—he took it up the yard, and threw it on the cart—the prisoner was on the cart, and he then went on—I afterwards went with Attride and another officer to the coach-house, and found one sack full of coke, another sack three parts full, and about three or four sacks of coke had been shot down—the corn-bin was about half full of beans, oats, and out clover-chaff—there was about a bushel and a half—Attride gave me this small piece of paper—I was standing by the side of the bin, when he found it in a handful of corn, which he took up in his hand—I saw the prisoner about three hours after that, he was proceeding towards the gas-works with his cart—I told him that Mr. Grissell had received information that he was in the habit of leaving coke on his way to Bayswater, and he thought it was not correct, and I was instructed by Mr. Gammon to order him to proceed to the yard with his cart—he said, "Let us go to the Gas-works first"—I told him he had better go to the yard—he wished to go to the Gas-works to see if he had not paid for the coke—he said he had bought it—he went on to Mr. Grissell's, and I followed—I saw Mr. Gammon there, and I told him in the prisoner's presence that I had seen the prisoner that morning come out of the Swan, about half-past nine o'clock, and hand down two sacks, one half full, and the other three parts full of something, and they were carried down the Swan-yard, and in searching the corn-bin I found this paper—I took a sample of corn from the prosecutor's yard, to compare with that in the bin at the Swan—they corresponded, but they got mixed together by mistake at the police-office—they are not distinct now.
