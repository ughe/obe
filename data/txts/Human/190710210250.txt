 Cross-examined by Mr. Marshall Hall. I did not search 2, Token-house Buildings after the arrest of Perryman. I have produced all the documents which I think important in this case. At Pinner's Hall I found nothing belonging to Perryman except the furniture. I found on Perryman some cards of 41, Charing Cross Road. He said it was an old address; that he used to live there. I did not search 41, Charing Cross Road, nor did I find any documents relating to it. Excepting in Harris's office I found nothing at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings; there was nothing in the room marked "C. W. P." I found 42 cheque books containing several thousands of cheques. I have only produced one cheque filled up by Mabel Long, dated Oc  tober 8, 1906, to Allen for £65, and another to Nixon and Arnold of June 18. There may be others. I have only seen postcard and letter of Mabel Long's admitted handwriting. I have been told she is in Calcutta. I did not find at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings thousands of circulars from outside brokers—I found a very few from persons unconnected with the prisoner's businesses; there were, perhaps, half a dozen from Martin, Bull and Co., and Mackenzie. (Witness admitted the payment of several sums to customers, referring to the accounts in Perryman's handwriting.)
 Cross-examined by Mr. F. E. Smith. When I called on May 9 at Tyler's office I was not asked my name by Miss Latus. When arrested Tyler did not say, "I am responsible"; he said, "I am nominally responsible." I cautioned him when I was asking him with regard to the account books as to where they were. When I arrested Tyler I found certain testimonials and certificates of character at his ad  dress in Great St. Helens. I took possession of these with other papers and have handed them over long ago. I made a schedule of all the documents found upon him, and they will be on the charge sheet. I went afterwards to Tyler's residence in Harvest Road and made an examination of the papers there.
 To Mr. Marshall Hall. A bundle of Stock Exchange accounts was found at Tokenhouse Buildings. The majority of them were in the name of Perryman.
