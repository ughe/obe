 476.  JOHN WALKER was indicted for stealing on the 15th of December , 4 loaves of bread, value 1s. 4d., the goods of George Dowsett.
 GEORGE DOWSETT. I  keep a chandler-shop, in Rahere-street, St. Luke's. On the 15th of December, about two o'clock in the day, I was watching, and saw the prisoner and three others; another boy took four loaves out of my window; the prisoner stood next to him, and took the loaves of him - they were together - the prisoner ran away - I pursued him; he threw down the loaves just before I took hold of him - I had not lost sight of him.
 WILLIAM LEWIS. Mr. Dowsett gave me the loaves, and I took them to the station.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Seven Years.
 477.  THOMAS WARREN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of December , 1 leopard skin, value 30s., and 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Margaret Walters.
   MARGARET WALTERS. I live in Long-lane. This leopard skin and handkerchief are mine, and had been in a drawer in my kitchen - the prisoner lodged in the house.
   THOMAS THOMAS. I am a Police-constable. In consequence of information I went to a furrier's shop, on the 12th of December, the prisoner was standing at the door, and his son was inside offering this skin for sale, at a very low price - I went in and took him; he did not hear what I said to the boy - I took him to the station, and from what he said, I went and took the prisoner to the station; I questioned him, and he said it was given to him by a young man named Watson, in Long-lane, to sell.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. She said, "Let me put this skin into the drawer, and the Captain shall never have it any more;" this passed on for a month, and one Sunday, John Watson came to the door, and I let him in - he said "Is Margaret up?" I said Yes; she came to him and said, "How did you leave Henry?" he said, "In good spirits;" she said "I have the skin, do you think he will ask for it when he comes home" he said No; then said she "I will have it sold, and divide the money between us," and they gave it to me to sell.
 MARGARET WALTERS. That is not true - it was given to me by Captain Monro; I had shown it to the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 53. - Confined Two Months.
 478.  SUSAN WHITELOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of December , 4 pairs of clogs, value 4s., the goods of Nathan Blake.
   NATHAN BLAKE. I keep a shoe-shop in Edgware-road. I lost these clogs on the 31st of December, but I did not see them taken; I had missed them about half an hour before the Police-constable brought them.
   CHARLES CLARK. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner about two hundred yards from the prosecutor's house. I found three pairs of clogs and an odd one on her, but she struggled very much, and I think we lost one.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. It was want made me do it - I have not a friend in the world.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. -    Judgment Respited.
 479.  JOHN WILLIAMS and BENJAMIN THOMAS were indicted for stealing, on the 25th of December , 7 half-crowns and 4 shillings, the monies of James Gadsby , from his person.
   JAMES GADSBY. On Christmas morning I was drunk, and the Policeman put me into the watch-house; I then had seven half-crowns and four shillings - I am sure of that; I was sober enough to remember that when I was put in the cell I had the money - I fell asleep, and when I awoke I found the two prisoners by me; I believe I was put in there about half-past twelve o'clock on Saturday night - I staid till about nine on Sunday morning, and when I went up I missed all my money.
   THOMAS FARRANT, I am a Policeman. I had taken the two prisoners at eight o'clock on the Saturday night, for attempting to pick pockets - I put them into the cell, but before that I searched them minutely, and on Thomas I found two handkerchiefs, a sixpence, and 1 1/2d., and on Williams, a sixpence, a halfpenny, a farthing, and one handkerchief - this was before Gadsby was brought in there, as I understood, but I did not see him that night.
   WILLIAM TRODD. I was on duty in St. James' watch-house. I saw the prisoners brought in - they were searched, and what has been stated was found on them in my presence; I put them into the cell, and when the prosecutor was brought in, I put him into the cell - when he came out he complained of having been robbed - I called the prisoners, and searched them; I found on Thomas four half-crowns, three shillings, and this box, and on Williams three half-crowns and one shilling - I had seen the money in the prosecutor's tobacco-box the night before, but I did not count it.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Had you not seen this box on the prisoners the night before? A. Yes - I do not know whether any of the prisoners' friends had been there; the prosecutor was not so drunk but that he knew what he was doing, or he could not have taken his money out and shown it to me.
 JAMES GADSBY. I believe this to be my money.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you receive it? A. I had taken it; there were twelve or fourteen persons in the cell.
 Williams' Defence. It was money my master had paid me on Saturday night.
 Thomas put in a written Defence, stating that the money found on him was his own, and that he had it in a box, which was not opened when he was searched.
 WILLIAMS -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 THOMAS -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
