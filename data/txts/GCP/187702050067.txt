Dr. Ruys WILLIAMS. I am an M.D., and member of the Royal College
of Physicians of Edinburgh; I am resident physiqian at Bethlehem Royal
Hospital, and lecturer on mental diseases at St. Thomas's Hospital - in my
personal experience I have come across many cases of epilepsy--the medical
profession recognise epileptic vertigo as a disease---some years ago I sawa
gentleman who simply suffered from headache oooasionally; at that time I
had no suspicion of epilepsy; one morning suddenly whilst at breakfast he
made a violent attack upon his wife ; when I saw him shortly afterwards he
could give me no account of it, nor any reason for so doing, he did not
after that show any symptoms of mental disease, but he had subsequently
at various intervals, some nine or ten epileptio fits-he had one about a
month after he made the attack upon his wife; he continued under medical
care about fourteen months ; I heard of him about two years ago, he had
then resumed his ordinary occupation and was perfeotly well, and had no
return of the fits, and he had had ten or eleven in all I heard of him
about eighteen months ago, he was then well-I have not heard more
recently-that is the only case I have seen that appears to me remarkablo;
I have seen others-a person attacked with epileptio vertigo is unconscious
while he is committing the act, unconscious of what he may be doing, not
Insensible from my own observation I can't say whether they can speak.
they do acts automatically-all they do is automatio, that is the state they
are in ; I ground my opinion upon that; that is the definition of it; they
act as mere machines, and when they recover they are utterly unconscious
of anything that has happened-sometimes there may be perfect conscious-
ness, perfect sanity before the attack-while under the influence of the
vertigo they know not what they do, but I have seen cases where there is
an impression afterwards of having done something, but they cannot tell
what-the length of the attack varies very much, the longest I have ever
seen lasted about an hour, the party being unconscious the whole of that
time, but as a rule they are of very much shorter duration I have been
present during this trial; the symptoms described of constant headachęs,
pusing water unconsciously during the night, and the pains described,
correspond exactly with cases which I have had under my care,
and which have been undoubtedly epileptio vertigo-I think they
are characteristic of it-epilepsy, I think, would tend to shorten life,
but not insanity-epilepsy is a disease of the brain--if there is an heredi-
