Early in April I was introduced by my brother to the prisoner, at the London Coffee-house—he said he had a large estate in India, and another in Cornwall, andwas going to Plymouth to receive a legacy—in consequence of what he toldme I allowed him to live at my house, and provided him with clothes, linen, a gold watch and chain—one morning be went away, intending to take pos sessionof his estate—I went with him to the railway station—when Ireturned I missed from a drawer, which was broken open, a chali  dress, abracelet, and some satin.
   Prisoner. Q . Did you not promise me a dress to send to New York? A.  No—I did make up a parcel for you to take, but it was intended youshould purchase the goods—you bought other goods worth 10 l .—you saidyou were going to Plymouth, instead of which you went to Liverpool. RACHAEL LYONS. I saw the chali  dress and bracelet safe in the secretary-fewerfive days before—both drawers were broken open.

No; I do not keep them there—I found the drawer openthe same evening—there was a piece out, as if it had been taken out with aknife. THOMAS KELLY( policeman, H  2.)
