   COURT . Q.  Will you swear this is your pad? Witness.  Yes, this is my pad—I cannot swear to this handkerchief—the handkerchief I lost was just like this. DANIEL M'CARTHY. I lodge in the same house with the prosecutor—on the night of the 5th of Jan. I met with the prisoner at the Noah's Ark—he was in soldier's clothes—he went to the lodging, and passed the night in the same room with me and the other prosecutor—the next morning we went to work, and left the prisoner in bed. WILLIAM FINCLOTH. I slept in the same bed with the prisoner that night I went out about eight o'clock in the morning, and left the prisoner in bed—I did not take notice whether the clothes were safe.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did any conversation pass between you and I? A.  None at all—you were asleep, or shamming sleep. MARY CALLAGHAN. I keep the house where the prosecutor and the pri soner slept—I went into the room in the afternoon of the 6th of Jan.—the soldier's clothes were left, and the prisoner was gone—he had come in sol dier's clothes. JOHN ROSCOE( police-sergeant, R  9.) I apprehended the prisoner on the 15th of Jan.—I told him I was going to take him for stealing M'Carthy's clothes—he said if he stole them, the best thing I could do was to find them—I took this handkerchief from his neck, and this pad was stuffed in his cap.
   Prisoner's Defence.  In the morning when I awoke, Fincloth and I were in bed together; I told him I was an absentee, and I wished to go to an acquaintance to get some money; I said if he could provide me with some sort of an old suit, I would pay him for them, and he provided me with this old suit; I gave him half-a-crown; I laid, "I have no handkerchief," and he brought me down this old red handkerchief; I put it on, and went out; on the Thursday I was taken, and was in the guard-room as an absentee; the prosecutor came and looked at me, and then they went away, and were away a quarter of an hour; if I knew that handkerchief to be stolen, I had plenty of time to have made away with it. WILLIAM FINCLOTH re-examined.  What he says is all false. GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined One Year .

 629. HENRY WARNFORD was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Smith , and stealing 1 coat, value 1 l  . 10 s  ., his pro perty; and 3 coats, 6 l  . 10 s  ., the goods of Henry Jonas Smith . JOHN SMITH. I live at Deptford, and am a hatter—it is my dwelling-house, and is in the parish of St. Paul, Deptford—on the morning of the 19th of Nov. I got up about seven o'clock—I did not miss anything—I did not look at the entrance to the cellar, but my wife did—I know this coat to be mine HENRY JONAS SMITH. I am the son of John Smith—on the night of Tuesday, the 18th of Nov. I went to bed about one o'clock—my brother fas tened up the house in my presence—I saw the front door fastened—I cannot swear whether the cellar door was—these three coats are mine—I saw them safe that night—two of them were in the room in which we dwell, and one was on the rails of the stairs—the prisoner used to come backwards and forwards to our house—my mother got up first on the morning of the 19th.
