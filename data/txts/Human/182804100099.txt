   HENRY MARSHALL. I am a tripeman; a person resembling Ellis hired a stable of me in Great Garden-street, that is the stable the officers searched - he took it in his own name.
   CHARLES WORGAN. I am carman to Mr. Emery, of Gun-square; I met Ellis in Whitechapel - I knew him by the name of Bullen; he asked, where I was going? I said, I had nothing to do; he asked if I would go with him to sell some old iron, and he would give me some beer - I went with him to McDowell's, and took the iron; there was a round plate and some other iron.
 Prisoner ELLIS. Q. What day was it? A. I believe it was Wednesday - it was the latter end of February; your mother went by the name of Bullen - I have been in your loft and saw a pair of shafts there, I think you said, you were going to build a van with them in the summer for yourself; that might be three or four weeks ago.
 COURT. Q. How long was it before you went to the Magistrate? A. About a fortnight; I only judge of the time I went with him by master having nothing to do - it was towards the end of February.
 Prisoner ELLIS. Q. Was not the iron sold on the Wednesday week before I was apprehended? A. I think so; I was examined on the 17th of March, and think it was on the Wednesday week before that.
 MR. BUTTLER. I saw the shafts, they are mine, and so is the sweep-plate, and some pieces of the cart I can swear to; I know the shafts by some plates and some hooks on them - I had had the van about two years; I bought it - it has been mended once, and I also had done something to it myself; I had put some plates on the shaft, and there are several parts of my mending which I can swear to.
 Prisoner ELLIS. Q. Did you ever take the sweepplate off? A. No, but I have looked at it many times.
 ELLIS'S Defence. He is a false swearing man, it is not his iron; I dealt in iron, and sold some there before - I was first examined on the 29th of February, and then Worgan said it was the Wednesday week before.(See Fifth Day.)
 ELLIS -    GUILTY. Aged 24.
 BOYDEN -    NOT GUILTY.
 WILLIAMS -    NOT GUILTY.
 998.  SARAH DAY was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , 1 oz. and 5 dwts. of silver, value 6s., the goods of Clement Cheese.
 CLEMENT CHEESE. I am a  silversmith, and live in Clerkenwell, the prisoner was in my service. On the 1st of March I found I was deficient in my stock, and on the 3d I was deficient again; I told her on the Sunday that I was deficient, and I would know who it was - on the Monday I told her I was short again, and I would see in her box; she said, she was never called a thief before - I said, if she had nothing of mine, she could not object to my seeing her box; she hesitated - I went up stairs with my errand-boy; she opened her box and took out a small paper parcel, which she tried to put into her pocket - the boy stopped her hand, and we found it was this piece of silver; I told the boy to empty out the box, and we found some more silver - she fell on her knees and begged forgiveness; here is an ancient coin among it, which I know well, and a piece of an ingot; she only lived eleven days with me, and came from the office in Holborn.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   BENJAMIN DUKE. I am Mr. Cheese's errand-boy; I saw the silver found in the prisoner's box - she asked forgiveness.
   WILLIAM BROWN EDWARDS. I am an officer, and took her in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 999.  WILLIAM HATTON was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of March , 1 knife, value 9d.; 3 shillings; 1 six-pence; and 104 farthings, the property of Thomas James.
   ANN JAMES. I am wife of Thomas James; he keeps a shop in Pitfield-street, Hoxton- On the 12th of March, I was in the parlour and heard money jingle, and presently I heard it again; I then looked towards the shop, and saw something black, which I thought was the dog; but presently I saw a head - I called my husband, who was in bed in the parlour - he went and took the prisoner in the shop. I had seen 3s. 6d. in the till shortly before - I found the till open. and the box containing the silver taken out - I found the money under a mat behind the counter; he had got a knife up his sleeve.
   ESTHER BLATCHLEY. I was at work with the prosecutrix; I went into the shop, and served the prisoner with nuts; I opened the till then, and saw some silver and farthings in it - he went out - I went into the room and sat down - we afterwards heard money jink twice; we went out, and I saw the prisoner behind the counter; 3s. 6d. was found under the mat; the farthings lay at a little distance; nobody came into the shop after him; he is a sweep.
   GEORGE WILMOT. I am an officer, and took the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Confined Three Months.
