the prosecutor, on the 3rd of June, to Limehouse, and apprehended the pri-
soner-I asked what he had done with the coat-- he said he had pawned it at
Mr. Dicker's, in Three-colt-street, Limehouse I went with him, and found it
-he said he was very sorry for what he had done—he hoped the prosecutor
would look over it.
Cross-examined. Q. He made no secret of it? A. No—the coat is worth

STEPHEN WILLIAM KITCHERN. I live in Lamb's-buildings, Limehouse.
I saw the prosecutor about eleven o'clock at night on the 29th of May, when
he was about to take water-he and the prisoner staggered down the shore,
and the captain tumbled over a boat-I was about to pick him up, but he got
up, and they went off-I saw the prisoner come back, but I did not go to his
| boat—he is regularly at those stairs-on the following morning I heard from
two or three, who were standing at the stairs, that the captain had left a coat
in the prisoner's boat, and if I saw the captain I was to remind him of it-I
saw the captain on the Friday--he did not appeal to me, but he sent a person
I knew very well to find the prisoner.
