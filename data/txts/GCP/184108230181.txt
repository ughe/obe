Moses Cox. I am in the service of Johnson and Webb; they are
brewers, in Bowl-yard. On the 14th of August, between one and two
o'clock in the morning, I fell in with the prisoner-I asked her if she knew
of a lodging-house, and she took me to No. 10, Church-street-I am mar-
ried, but I could not get in at my lodgings-the prisoner went to bed with
me-I gave her a shilling, and paid for the bed - I undressed and went to
bed-1 had a shilling, two sixpences, and two or three halfpence in my
trowsers' pocket-I had a waistcoat, and that I put on the box--I did not
go to sleep-I was not in bed above three quarters of an hour when the
prisoner got up-she put on her gown and shawl, went to my pocket, took
my money, and my waistcoat as well-I saw her just going out of the
door and called to her, but she went off-I saw her on the Sunday evening
following in Broad-street-I accused her of robbing me-she said she
knew nothing of me, and had not been in the house-the waistcoat pro-
duced is mine.
Prisoner. You gave me 18. 9d., and said you had no more money-I
told you I was not going to stay with you for that, and then we came
down stairs.
GEORGE JOHN RESTIEAUX. I am a policeman. On Sunday night I re-
ceived the prisoner from the prosecutor-she told him if he would give her 4s.
she would give him the waistcoat-he refused - I took her to the station-
she then said, if he would forgive her she would give the waistcoat-she
then said it was at Biddy Ryan's- I went there, and found it between the
sacking and the bed.
Prisoner. I said, if he would give me the rest of the 4s, that he said he
would give me, I would get it him; he took me to a house, and sent for a
pot of porter, and sent the boy for a policeman.
Ann Clark. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former convic-
tion, which I got at Mr. Clark's office-(read)-I searched the prisoner
at that time, and was present at her trial-she is the person.
GUILTY. Aged 28.-Transported for Fourteen Years,
2250. ELIZABETH DUNKS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
August, 1 shirt, value 38.; 2 sheets, value 58.; and 1 apron, value 18.;
the goods of John Constantino Lombardy; and that she had been before
convicted of felony.
DOROTHY LOMBARDY. I am the wife of John Constantino Lombardy-
we live in Ray-street. I have known the prisoner some years—she acted
as my servant off and on-I left home on the 4th of August, and returned
on the 14th-the prisoner was in and out at my place during that time-
when I returned I missed the articles stated the prisoner was at her own
lodging-I suspected her, and I gave notice to the officer-he found this
shirt and two sheets of mine-this is all I have got.
WILLIAM ALEXANDER CHAPMAN. I am a pawnbroker, and live in
Clerkenwell. I have a shirt and two sheets--they were pawned with me,
but not by the prisoner--this is the duplicate of them.
CHARLES ELLIOTT (police-constable G 132.) I heard of this, and saw
the prisoner coming down Turnmill-street on the 18th of August-I told
her I wanted her for a robbery in Ray-street-she said she did not do it-
the next day she said she was induced by another girl to steal the property,
and gave it to her to pledge-I found the duplicate on the prisoner, and
the articles are pawned in her name.
