WILLIAM Mc KEWAN, I am the assistant manager of the London and
County Bank, in Lombard-street--the prisoner has been in the employment
of the bank for about four months-his salary was paid monthly no altera-
tion has taken place in the payment of it--the last payment I made to him
before this transaction was on 31st March-on 14th April last Messrs. White,
tobacconists, of Shoreditch, paid a sum of money into the bank-I did not
Teceive it, but I made the first entry of it-I have the book in which I made
it (referring to it)-it was 2191. 18. 6d., of which 941. was in money, and the
remainder in notes and checks--when notes and cbecks are received from &
customer they are folded up and passed into a box, called the waste box, os
cash box-there are clerks whose duty it is to keep waste books, to take the
particulars of those notes and checks respectively, and enter them in the waste
books-the prisoner kept one of those waste books-1 folded up the notes and
checks received from Mr. White, and put them into the box-this is the waste
book which the prisoner kept (producing it)-I find here an entry in his hand-
writing of the notes and checks received among the notes there is an entry
of one 101. Bank of England note, No. 21,125, dated 10th Jan., 1853-that
entry must have been made from the note itself—it is our practice to make
up our balances every nigbt-on the night of 14th April I am informed there
was 101. short; I was not present-on 23rd April the prisoner was charged
with stealing the note--he denied it-I asked him what time he left for dinner
on 14th April I think he said between 1 and 2-the ordinary time allowed
for dinner is under an hour-he said the entry that I have referred to in the
waste book, must have been made soon after 12, or about 12-after making
the entry he would pass the notes and checks into another box wbich stands
before him, and then they would either go into the till, or be paid inte the
Bank at night ; generally speaking, 101. and 5l. notes go to the Bank of
England -- they are not often paid away, unless they are new notes--after the
prisoner had been charged with stealing the note, he said that it came from
Messrs. White-that is the note that had been missed I see the name of
"James Duke, 19, Ebury-street, Pimlico," on this note-I bave seen the
prisoner write- I believe that to be in his handwriting.
