MICHAEL HIGGINS (police-constable F 91.) I apprehended the prisoner-
I told her Mrs. Hancock charged her with taking a piece of print, and leaving it
at the King William the Fourth-she said she did not know anything about it
- I told her I should take her to Bow-street station, where I should take Smith
as well-on the road she said she had a piece of print in her possession, and
asked me to let her go to her husband-I said I would not let her run about
at that time-she said she was sent to the box by Mrs. Beeston, the landlady,
but she did not say that to me--this is my deposition-I signed it-it was
read over to me-1 afterwards got Smith to the station-I did not pay any
attention to what she said.
Cross-examined. Q. Did not you direct the locket and the handkerchiel
to be put into the indictment? A. Yes I mentioned before the Magistrate
the conversation that took place in going to the station--this is my writing to
the first examination-I mentioned the conversation, whether they took it
down or not, I cannot say I did not desire, on the 10th of Jan., to make an
additional statement-what I stated the first day was read over to me, and I
signed it-no questions were put to me at all-I think I signed my name two
different days—I made the same statement on the 10th as the 5th-I have
been in the police fourteen years.
WILLIAM WELLESLEY MEDLYCOTT (police-inspector F.) I remember the
prisoner being brought to the station-Smith said she saw the prisoner at
the prosecutrix's box-she denied it, and denied taking the print.
CHARLOTTE BEEston. I was landlady of the King William the Fourth.
