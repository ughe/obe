JOHN FIELD. I am a carpenter. I lost my tools on the 4th of August, from Staples' Inn, where I was at work - they had been safe at twelve o'clock, and I missed them at half-past twelve.
   SAMUEL DOYLE. I was working at the same place, and lost a saw and two chisels.
   WILLIAM HENRY WALSHAM. Between twelve and one o'clock, on the 4th of August, I was passing Staples' Inn, and saw the prisoner coming across the garden, with a basket; I am a bricklayer, and seeing he was a stranger, I asked what business he had there; he said, a man at the end of the street had sent him for his tools - I asked him to go and show me the man - we went, and he could not find him - he then said, "Pray let me go - I have a wife and seven children;" I said I thought I should not be justified, and took him to the Police.
   THOMAS PERRING. I am a Police-officer, and took the prisoner; he said a man in black gave him 6d. to go and fetch his tools.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been where there were some men at work, and a man asked me to go and open a gate, that I should find some tools in a basket under the trees, and he would give me 6d. to bring them down to him; I met this man, who told me to go and put them back, which I did.
 Witness. He did not put them down.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Transported for Seven Years.

 1744.  JAMES TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of June , 1 ass, price 2l., the property of William Wilkins.
   MARTHA WILKINS. I am the wife of William Wilkins, he lives at Bedford. I was going to Hornsey, and met the prisoner with an ass - we had lent it to Mr. Smith, at Harpenden, on the 17th of May - I knew it well - I had driven it hundreds of miles; Mr. Smith was to keep it a month, and in June he sent word he had lost it - the prisoner had it in a cart; I looked at it - he said to me,"You look very hard at it, do you want to buy it?" I said "No - but where did you buy it?" he said, "Of a man named Blake, at Barnet;" I said, "Buy it of who you would, it was stolen from Mr. Smith, and it is mine;" I went to a Policeman, and asked if he knew the man, he said No - but did not notice the name on the cart - I said No, I did not.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not make any mistake about the ass? A. No, never - I did not describe one which did not turn out to be mine - I described it as a small, genteel black ass - and one was produced which had a white face, and mine was a black one - I have never seen mine since - the one produced was not like mine.
 COURT. Q. How long after you lost it did you see it again? A. Not till July.
 - DEATH. I live with Mr. Smith - I knew the ass; it was a black one; it was turned out in the grassfield on Saturday night.
   WILLIAM HOLT. I am a Police-officer - the prosecutrix told me about this - I saw the prisoner in the junction-road, the next day, and took him - he said he had sold the ass, but he did not know to whom - she did not say it was hers, but she thought so.   NOT GUILTY.

 1746.  MICHAEL FLING was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the the goods of John Annis.
   JOHN PEEBLES. I am in the employ of Mr. John Annis. On the 10th of July this handkerchief was taken from the door - it was brought to me about six o'clock in the evening; I had seen it safe at two.
   GABRIEL BURROWS. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. The prisoner came to me with the handkerchief, and wanted 1s. 6d. or 2s. for it - I think our house is three quarters of a mile from the prosecutor's; I questioned him - he said it was his mother's, and she had bought it at Mr. Mill's, in Kingsland-road; I asked what this was at the corner - he said, "That is where my mother ties up her money;" I then saw some pin-holes, and then said,"Is this where your mother ties up her money?" - I went to the door, and he ran out; I pursued him twenty yards, gave him in charge.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Confined One Month.
 1747.  ANN BECKLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 2 pieces of ribbon, containing 32 yards, value 25s., the goods of James Shoolbred and Gregory Cook.
   OLIVER DEATH. I am in the employ of Messrs. James Shoolbred and Gregory Cook, of Tottenham-court-road. The prisoner was in their shop on the 31st of August, and a person was sent to tell an officer to stop her; I did not serve her - another person was with her; they were brought back in custody of the officer - I saw the prisoner put her hand into her bosom; I followed her into the counting-house, put my hand into her bosom, and found one piece of ribbon; there are thirty-two yards of ribbon in all.
   ELIZA WATTS. I serve in the shop. This woman and another came in - I attended to them - they asked for ribbons, and I showed them some: they bought a remnant, which amounted to 10d.; it was about a yard and a half - they went out of the shop; I had not suspected them, and did not know why they were brought back; I had not sold them this piece of thirty-two yards - I know the mark on it; I do not know where it was.
