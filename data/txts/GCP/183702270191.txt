JOHN HASLOp. I am a private in the Grenadier Guards. I was on
duty at the Dock-yard-gate, at Woolwich, at half-past six o'clock on that
Sunday morning, and saw the prisoner come along with some hay--he went
into his stable in Church-street, and came out again with nothing I saw
him return again, and a lad with him, who had a bundle of straw with him,
and the prisoner had something, but I could not see what.
Cross-eramined. Q. How did he carry the hay the first time? A. On
his shoulder- he came towards me-there was a lad with him each time.
JOSEPH BUTTERFIELD. I am a constable. I went up to the prisoner's
loft, and found two trusses of hay, one of clover, and one of straw-I then
went to Orchard-street, and found the prisoner-I said, "Do you know
the errand I come on ?"-he said, "No"-I took him to the stable, and
there the prosecutor owned the hay-as we came down, Mr. Shott's brother
saw a bunch of keys in the prisoner's window, I took them, and one of
the keys locked and unlocked Mr. Shott's gate.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you try that key to the prisoner's gate? 1.
Yes, and it fitted it.
WILLIAM SHOTT re-examined. My gate was not broken-it had been
opened by a key-I found it locked in the morning.
(Mr. Doane stated that the prisoner being in want of some hay, went that
morning to the prosecutor's, found the door open, and took what he wanted,
but intended to pay for it.)
(The prisoner received a good character.)
VUILTY, Aged 32.- Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.
