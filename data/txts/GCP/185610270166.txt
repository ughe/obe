from the butcher's? A. Just across the road, a very little slanting-there
were a good many people in the butcher's; it was Saturday night, at 7. or
8 o'clock-she was at the side of me; that was the reason that I accused
her-she followed close behind me from Mr. Hughes's I was ten minutes
in the butcher's shop, during which time people came in and out, but ther
had not to pass where I stood-I saw Rebecca Cooper directly I went out.
and I saw the prisoner then ; I could see her all the time-nobody would
help me-I asked Mr. Bland, but he said that he could not leave his busi:
ness—the money was four half crowns, six shillings, two sixpences, and
farthing—the prisoner heard what I said to Mr. Bland, and ran out directly
-I charged her with robbing me before she ran.
REBECCA COOPER. I am the wife of William Cooper, of Queen-street
Woolwich. I was standing outside Mr. Hughes's door, waiting for my
laughter, and saw the prisoner come across the road in a hurried manner, as
if she had been doing something wrong-she did not run, but she hurried
--she went to the middle of Mr. Hughes's shop, with a shawl over her arm
--Mrs. Wane came in, and said, “You have robbed me of all my money,
178. and a farthing"- the prisoner said nothing I said, "Hold her fast; do
aot let her go"-she turned round and said, "I am not afraid ; I can stand
to my tack"-I afterwards saw her in custody.
Cross-examined. Q. When she was first charged, did not she say, "Go
along with yon, I have not robbed you" ? A. Yes it is a very busy
place, like a market place.
