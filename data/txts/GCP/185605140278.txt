WILLIAM BLACKBURN. I am a plumber employed at the General Post
Office. On the morning of 15th April I was directed to go to the water
closets on the basement-I went there about a quarter past 6 o'clock-Peak
was not with me, he was in the air shaft; he could see me I washed out
the closet and stopped it, I put a sheet of brown paper into the trap; it is
a self acting closet, the seat is on a spring, and when a person rises the
water flows in-there is a trap below the pan-I washed that trap out-it
is a ten inch trap, made of lead, and contains & gallon of water--the contenta
of the pan go into that trap in the ordinary way, and when the person rises
the fall of water washes out the trap-I put the brown paper in the
trap for the purpose of preventing anything from passing down it without
my knowledge-after doing that I left the closet, leaving nothing but water
and the brown paper in the trap-Peak was then at the grating; about a
quarter past 7 o'clock Peak came and fetched me, and I went to the same
closet-1 examined the pan, I found that it had been used in the usual
way-I cleared out the trap-I had an instrument made of two pieces of
wood like a pair of tweezers, and with that I pulled up the contents of the
trap with the sheet of brown paper, there was soil in the trap-I took out
some torn pieces of paper and pieces of card, I picked out the pieces, put
them into a sheet of brown paper, took them into my shop and washed
them in Smee's presence—these are them-Smee wished me to mark one or
two of them, which I did, and gave them to him-here are my marks-my
attention had been called to this water closet the day before, and I took out
seven letters from it.
Cross-examined. l. What did you take out of the water closet when
you went at & quarter past 7 o'clock, with your wooden tweezers? A. I took
out the torn pieces of paper and some fragments of printed paper besides
these-some parties had been to the closet, I cannot say who I have no
doubt it had been used in the ordinary way, I merely picked out the letter,
I did not want those, I put the pieces of printed paper into the trap again,
with the brown paper-the rushing of the water into the pan makes a noise,
and can be seen by the person using the closet, about a gallon and a half
of water flows in—when the water is turned off, there would, of course, not

water-I washed out the four letter carriers' closets that morning—there
