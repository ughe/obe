   JURY . Q.  Did this fit the place? A.  Not exactly, there is some wanting. THOMAS BAYLEY. I am a plumber. On the 11th of November, about 5 o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my shop with some pipe—he asked if I would buy it—I asked him if it was his own—he said yes, and he wanted to sell it, as his wife given herself up to drink lately, and had made away with some of it, he thought he had better turn the rest into money—he said he had had it by him some time, and that he had been at work for a former master who gave him this lead instead of wages, as he was thinking of having water laid on at a future time—he told me where he lived, and that he worked for Mr. Oliver, on Black health-hill—on the Wednesday following I heard Mr. Oliver had lost some lead, and as it was similar to this I gave information.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you hear any thing of the reward? A.  I did, I read the bill—that was how I learned that Mr. Oliver had lost lead, the reward made no impression on me—it is twelve months yesterday since I was here—I know the Hospital at Greenwich had some lead stolen from it—Mr. Edward Bayley is my uncle—I was placed in the dock, but discharged without the least stain on my character—I can be trusted now for a thousand pounds—I have not the least doubt that I shall get the reward if the man is convicted, but not if he is acquitted—I believe my brother James was in the shop when the prisoner came in, but I cannot be certain—we have not conversed about the evidence we were to give—we came here in company from Deptford—we never uttered a word about it on the road—there might a word drop now and then, but we had no long conversa tion—the shop is very large—I have not asked my brother whether he was there—I did not see the prisoner in my shop that day afterwards—I re mained in the shop till half-past six o'clock—I believe I told the Magi strate that he said his wife had taken to liquor—I gave information on the Wednesday evening following—this was not reported currently through the village—I have known the prisoner I suppose ten years, or else I should not have bought the lead of him—I have bought lead once of him before—not more to my knowledge. JAMES BAYLEY. I am brother to Thomas Bayley. I was in the shop on the 11th of November, about half-past 8 o'clock—I bought some lead pipe of the prisoner—it is here—I asked him nothing it—I gave him 3 s . 3 d . for it—it weighed 33lbs.
