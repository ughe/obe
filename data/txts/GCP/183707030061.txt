steal, and stealing therein, 8 spoons, value 27. 12s.; 3 handkerchiefs, value
98.; 1 coat, value 11, 108.; 1 pair of shoes, value 58.; 2 pairs of specta-

WILLIAM Tomlin. I am a lighterman and coal-merchant, and live at the
Newcastle Coal-wharf, Limehouse-fields, in the parish of St. Anne, Lime-
house. On the evening of Friday last I saw the three prisoners, and an-
other, sitting on a bank, about 100 yards from my house_Weatherstone
was pointing towards it, which excited my suspicions I went to bed about
eleven o'clock-I examined my house before I retired, and left it all safe
-I was called up about three o'clock, and found my desk laying on the
wharf, broken open-there were several papers, a £20 bank-note, and two
fourpenny pieces gone from it, I had bored one of the fourpenny pieces
with a drill which I have in my pocket-I tried to get it on a ring, and
cut it a good deal in trying to get it on-I had left the desk in my sitting-
room when I went to bed - I looked about the house, and missed my coat,
two silver table spoons, five tea-spoons, one mustard-spoon, and & silk
handkerchief-I examined the house, and found two chisels with the desk
-I compared one of them with the marks on the desk, and it fitted the
marks-the kitchen-window was broken open—I compared a knife which
I found in the garden with the window-the putty was dug away with the
knife, and the glass was taken out sufficient to put a hand in, and unfasten
the catch-it was near the catch--the whole pane was not taken out-they
had got a hand over the shutter, and opened it by lifting up the bar -- I have
seen the property, and know it to be mine- I went to bed again, and in
about an hour my daughter called me-I got up and looked, and saw Wil-
