THOMAS HAYWOOD(Police Sergeant). On the morning of the 15th I received
information from the prosecntor, and went to the prisoner- I told him
the Railway Inn had been broken into, and that he was strongly suspected
of committing the robbery-he said he knew nothing about it, I searched
bis jacket, and in the pocket I found ten cigars, and in what is termed
the long bag, part of the kit, I found some more broken, about the same
quantity-I asked him how he accounted for the possession of the cigars
- he said he bought them at some shop, he could not say where--85
cigars were afterwards handed to me in this handkerchief produced)
they were found secreted in different parts of the room-they were given
to me in the room in the presence of the prisoner by the sergeant of the
regiment-I think he said he found them in the coal-box, near the
fire in his rooin--the prisoner admitted that was his hankerchief they
were tied up in.
ROBERT PARBOX Davis (Superintendent of Police, Canterbury). The
prisoner was given into my custody on the morning of the 16th-seeing
he was a young soldier, I asked him what service he had, and he told me
-I asked him if he was aware of the nature of the charge, and if he was
properly charged and cautioned, and he made some evasive answer;
** but," he said, “I never took the cigars, sir; they were given to me out
of the front door after closing hours on the Sunday night. When in the
house on the Sunday morning I asked the landlady to give me some
cigars, as I was going out to India. After the house was closed
on Sunday night I came and threw a small pebble at the window,
and the landlady came down and handed me the cigars"-I then said to
him “But you have written a letter to the landlord, asking him to forgive
you,” and he admitted to me that he had-here is the letter written by
him to the landlord, and signed by him-I got it from the landlord. (The
COURT was of opinion that as there was no evidence of the handwriting the letter
could not be read.) I was at the hearing before the Magistrate there was
a remand, at which I was also present-the wife of the prosecutor and
the servant were there by my order, and the prisoner declined to question
them.
