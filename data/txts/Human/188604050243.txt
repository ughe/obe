Witnesses for the Defence. PATRICK FITZGERALD. I am a carman to my father—he has a wharf at Dock Street, Deptford, called Fitzgerald's Yard—I saw Cantlin the morning of the day he was locked up, about 6 o'clock, in the yard—he came for work—he had worked for us before—he had been in the yard since 4 o'clock—the morning after his mother came to me and spoke to me—then I went to the solicitor, and afterwards to the police-court.
   Cross-examined.  I was sleepy on the 9th—I said before the Magistrate I could not tell what the time was because I was drowsy—I had not left work till 2 a.m.—Cantlin had worked for us two years before—he was always in the yard.
   Re-examined.  My boy comes at 5 o'clock—he was in the yard, and the chaps—the horses are harnessed at 6—Leary was in the yard that morning—I saw him soon after 5 o'clock. MICHAEL HOWARD. I live at 9, Barnes Alley, Deptford—I am one of Fitzgerald's carmen—on Tuesday morning I went to the yard at 5 a.m.—I cleaned the horses in the stable—I saw Cantlin at 6 o'clock or a little after, speaking to my foreman, young Fitzgerald, the last witness—I had seen him before.
   Cross-examined.  I said before the Magistrate I had known Cantlin for eight or nine years—he has worked for the same master as I have—he used to come every morning for a job between 5 and 6 o'clock. JOHN LEARY. I am a carman at Fitzgerald's Yard—I was there at 5.30 on this Tuesday morning getting my horses ready—I saw Cantlin at 5.30, and again at 5.45—after 1 had put the horses in I went down to the manure factory—it is close to Deptford Creek—Cantlin rode with me as far as Noah's Ark Corner, the top of High Street, Deptford, then he said he was going to breakfast—I knew him before this—there is a clock in Fitzgerald's Yard.
   Cross-examined.  The clock was in the engine room—I walked round for a bit of string, and I saw through the door, which was ajar—I wanted the string to tie a pole chain—I found it on the ground. MICHAEL HICKEY. I live at 24, Queen Street, Deptford—I am one of Fitzgerald's carmen—I was getting my horses out on this Tuesday morn ing—I went about 5 a.m.—about half an hour after I saw Cantlin come in the yard and speak to young Fitzgerald—10 minutes afterwards the engineers' bell struck, which goes at 5.45—it rings for 10 minutes, then leaves off, and rings again at two minutes to 6—I afterwards saw Cantlin go through the yard, and with a boy, in the direction of the manure factory—I have known Cantlin some time.
   Cross-examined.  I have known him eight or nine years—I have worked with him—I said before the Magistrate that young Mr. Fitzgerald, my master, had sent Cantlin to the manure factory—I heard him say he might get a job if he went there.
