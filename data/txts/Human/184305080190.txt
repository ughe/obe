GUILTY.— Transported for Seven Years .
   Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1687. RICHARD WELHAM and WILLIAM CONNOR were indicted for uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and ESPINASSE conducted the Prosecution. JAMES HOWORTH. I am in the Royal Marines, at Woolwich, and am em ployed in the barber's shop there. On the 31st of March, Welham came to be shaved—I asked him for the money which he owed me—he said he had not got any, only sham money—he produced two half-sovereigns to me out of his purse, where I saw he had ten or a dozen more—he gave me two, and told me to keep them till he came again; not to part with them, or he would never pay me what he owed me—he was immediately called away by the corporal, and I put the half-sovereigns in a piece of paper into my pocket, and when Suttle came I gave them to him.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Where did you put them? A.  In a cupboard, under lock and key—they remained there some days—the police man came about three weeks alter—my mate has got a key to the place as well as me. WILLIAM INGLISH. I am a private in the Royal Marines—I have a key to this cupboard—I did not take any half-sovereigns out—I never saw them. WILLIAM SUTTLE( police-constable R  260.) I received these two half-sovereigns from Howorth. ANN SONNEX. I am the wife of John Sonnex, who keeps the Duke on Horseback, in High-street, Woolwich. On the 11th of April I saw my hus band clear the till of all the gold coin—there was none left in the till—Con nor was employed in waiting in the house as an extra hand during the fair. Between four and five o'clock in the evening of the 13th of April, Welham came in—he asked for half a pint of rum, which came to 1 s .—I put it on the counter—Connor ordered it—Welham came up, and said to Connor, "Bill, is this for me? "Connor said, "Yes, Dick "—Welham said, "Here, take this half-sovereign, "and Connor took it from him, and gave it to me—I gave 9 s . in change to Connor, and he gave it to Welham—I put the half-sovereign into a bowl in the till—there was no other half-sovereign there—when we closed for the night, I took the coin out of the bowl and put it into a bag—there was no other half-sovereign but that, and I took it up stairs—on the 14th of April Welham came again in the evening—it was his general practice to come of an evening—he was in and out a good deal that day, and treating persons with liquor—he asked me for half-a-pint of gin-and-raspberry, which came to 8 d .—I served him—he gave me a half-sovereign, and I gave him 9 s . 4 d . change—Connor stood by, but bad nothing to do with the change—in the course of that night Connor came for a pot of beer, and gave me a half-sovereign—he said it was for Dick , the marine—I gave him the change, and saw him give it to Welham in the passage—Welham said, "It is all right, Bill, you shall never want a shilling while I have one"—on Saturday, the 15th, Welham came again—in the course of that evening I took two half-sovereigns from Connor—he said they were from Dick , the marine—on the 16th Welham came again with a young woman—he gave me a half-sovereign, and I gave him 9 s . 6 d . change—Welham and Connor were always together—that even ing Welham proposed to leave a half-sovereign with Connor, wrapped up in paper—Connor said, "Leave it at the bar, I will have nothing to do with
