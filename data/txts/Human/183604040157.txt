I found him in the solicitor's office—he told me at once that he had letters in his drawer—two letters, of a private na ture, which were found on his person, were returned to him—I believe these were the only public official letters found on his person; they are di rected or "William Tickell, seaman on board His Majesty's Ship Canopus, Malta or elsewhere."—" Mr. John Leain, on board His Majesty's Ship Revenge, Malta or elsewhere"—both the letters are marked, "put in without postage"—it appears they are common ship-letters—I do not know whether there is any postage on them.

I went to Mr. Barnes's house, and to his private apartments in the Post-office—he pointed them out to me himself—they consisted of a sleeping-room and sitting-room—I do not know, of my own knowledge, whether he sleeps in the Post-office when on duty—I searched his house at Peck ham diligently—I discovered no thing to further this prosecution that I am sware of—I believe, when he spoke about resigning, he said something about the disgrace which would fall on his family, by his being tried at the Old Bailey—I found offi cial stationary and tape in his drawers, with the letters—there was a par tition in the drawer—the drawer was locked—the partition merely divided the drawer—it had no cover to it—the contents of the drawer could be seen the moment it was opened—there were papers and other things in both parts of the drawer.
