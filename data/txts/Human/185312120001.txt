 117. WILLIAM MORTON was indicted ( with Frederick Rosenthal Crutchley ) for unlawfully obtaining money by false pretences, and for conspiring to cheat and defraud Caroline Burton .
 (  The defendant Crutchley did not appear at the commencement of the cast, but afterwards arrived in custody of an officer of the Queen's Bench Prison. MR. BALLANTINE did not call upon him to surrender, and the trial was pro  ceeded with against Morton alone.)
   MESSRS. BALLANTINE and ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. CAROLINE BURTON. I am the wife of Thomas Jones Burton; I was married to him in Feb., 1853; before that my name was Onslow. I reside in Gloucester-place, Portman-square—early in 1852 I was introduced by a person of the name of Stanborough to Frederick Crutchley—about that time there was a loan from me of 20 l . to either Crutchley or Stanborough; I lent 18 l . to receive 20 l .—it was either to Crutchley or Stanborough; it was a joint concern—a bill was given to me for the amount—previously to that bill becoming due, I saw Crutchley on the subject; that was very shortly after the loan of the 20 l .—on that occasion Crutchley produced to me another bill for 300 l .; that was accepted by a Mrs. Nash—( The witness being asked what Crutchley said on that occasion , MR.
