1658. SAMUEL MAYCOCK and JOHN JENNINGS were indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of June , I live tame rabbit, price 2s., the property of James Summers.
   JAMES SUMMERS. I am a plumber, and live in Quaker's-gardens, Kensington. On the 23rd of June, at five o'clock in the morning, I missed two rabbits from the garden behind my house; I had not seen them since the 22nd, at noon I had three more; they were in hutches, which were closed; when I went in the morning one was left in the hutch which the two were taken from.
   WILLIAM BRAIN. I live in Stingo-lane, and deal in rabbits. On the 23rd of June the prisoner came and offered to sell me three young rabbits, and an old one; I bought them of Maycock, and paid him for them - I cannot say who brought them; I gave 4s. for the four - Jennings took no part in the bargain; they both came in, and Maycock spoke first.
   JOHN POINTING. I am a Policeman. On the 23rd of June, I took the prisoner's at Fulham-bridge; I turned them out of a yard; Jennings had 4d.: there was a boy named Perryman with them - after they got to the watch-house, Jennings told me they had sold the rabbits in Stingo-lane, and divided the money between the three, which was 1s. 4d. each.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALESBY. Q. Where is Perryman? A. He was not committed; he had no basket - Jennings did not say, in his presence, that he had a share of the money.(Property produced and sworn to)
 Maycock's Defence. Between eight and nine o'clock in the morning I met this young man and Perryman together; Perryman asked me to buy a rabbit; I said No, but I would tell him where to sell them - we all three went together; I went in to ask the man if he bought rabbits - he said Yes; we took them in, and sold them.
 Jennings' Defence. I met the prisoner in Queen-street; he asked me to buy a rabbit - I said No.
 Jennings received a good character.
 MAYCOCK -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Confined Three Months.
 JENNINGS - GUILTY. Aged 14.
     Judgment Respited.
 1659.  WILLIAM MEATYEAR was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of June , 12lbs. of veal, value 5s., the goods of John Thomas Miller.
   ANN BARTON. On the 23rd of June I was in Spitalfields-market, and passed Miller's stall; I saw a man take a fillet of veal - he turned round by Mr. Gregory's greenstall; I only saw his back.
   SAMUEL MILLER. I was servant to John Thomas Miller, a butcher. I was standing inside the shop at the corner of South-street, and saw the prisoner with a bundle in his apron, ten or twelve yards from the shop; the Policeman took him.
   JOHN BURROUGH. I am a Policeman. I was in the market, and saw Miller running after the prisoner, who was secured, and as I took him to the station, he threw away a fillet of veal; I got possession of it, and showed it to Miller.
   JOHN THOMAS MILLER. I am a butcher. I saw the prisoner at the corner of South-street; the Policeman took him - I knew the veal to be mine, by the cut; I missed it from my shop.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in the market looking for work - I wanted bread for my wife and four children; a man asked if I wanted a job, and said he would give me 6d. to take the meat to the Nagg's Head, Whitechapel, and he would be there, as soon as me.
     GUILTY. - Confined One Month.
 1660.  WILLIAM BRIAN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of July , 3 pairs of shoes, value 12s., the goods of John Crispin.
   JOHN YOUNG. On the 6th of July I saw the prisoner run from Mr. Crispin's shop door, with some shoes in his hand - he had more than one pair; Crispin came out, and gave an alarm - the prisoner kept on, and turned up the first street; I lost sight of him, but afterwards saw Crispin bring him back.
   JOHN CRISPIN. I am a shoemaker, and live in Tottenham-court-road. On the 6th of July, at night, an alarm was given - I ran out, and followed the prisoner, calling Stop thief! till a man stopped him; I found nothing on him - as I returned, I saw three pairs of shoes picked up by Hill, in the direction he ran; they were mine - I had seen them in my shop less than an hour before; he was not out of my sight.
   JOSEPH STANNARD. I took the prisoner in charge -Hill delivered me the shoes.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I never had them.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Months.
 1661.  ALLEN CHARTERS was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , 1 bridle, value 5s., and 1 pad, value 3s., the goods of William James.
   ABRAHAM JAMES. I live in Russell-mews with my son William, who is a livery-stable keeper- the prisoner was in his employ about eight months ago. On the 29th of June, about half-past six o'clock in the morning, when I went into the stable, he was there, and asked how I did; I said, "I don't know you;" he said, "Yes you do, I worked here for your son some time ago;" I said, "I have no knowledge of you;" he said, "He told me he had a set of harness for sale;" I said, "I know he has not, for his harness is in use;" he said, "This is the harness with the lion on it;" I said that was not for sale, for it was a glass-coach harness; he unbuckled the pad; I went up stairs to speak to my son, who was asleep, and when I came down he was gone, with the bridle and pad; I found him about a week after, and asked him about it - he said it was not sold, but he knew where it was; I asked him to give me the address where it was - he said he would not; he did not say it was in pledge; I afterwards found it at a pawnbroker's, in Seven-dials.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not take the bridle and pad, and put them on my arm? A. I did not; I never said I would not prosecute you if I received 8s.
