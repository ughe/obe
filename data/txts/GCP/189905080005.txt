WILLIAM ALBERT FINCH, I am post-master at this post-office-Miss
Blackman called me, and I saw the prisoner—she said that he had given
her a coin for a postal order, and she thought it was bad-I examined it
and found it was very light-he said that he came from a race-course, and
thought it had been taken for a debt-I asked him his name and address,
wrote it down, and said, "It is my duty to report this to the police; wifi
you go with me to the station !"- he said that he did not wish to-I said,
« Then I must call in a policeman"-eventually two constables came.
CHARLES MATSAY (158 G). I was called to this post-office, and saw the
prisoner; I asked him how he came by the coin; he said, “I don't know;
I attend race-courses to get my living; you don't suppose I should have
stayed here if I had not been innocent"-he gave his address at a lodging-
house he was charged and made no reply.
GEORGE ROBERT FRENCH. I am employed at a post-office, 151, New
Kent Road-on April 21st the prisoner came in for a postal order for
78. and tendered & 5s.-piece, 2 shillings, and a penny-I tested the
crown, and told him it was badhe said that he obtained it from Epsom
the previous day-I spoke to the post-master; he broke it, and returned it
to the prisoner - I wrote up about it, and on May 3rd I was asked to go to
the Police-court; and at the rear I saw 8 persons, and the prisoner was
the third; I identified him at once-he made no remark.
Cross-examined. I went past the whole row, and put my hand on you
and said, “That is the man » _I did not say, "I don't know you."
WALTER SELBY. I was present when French was brought in; the pri-
soner was placed with 7 others and identified-he said, "I don't know
you "-no race meeting was going on at Epsom on April 20th.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am Inspector of coin to H.M. Mint - this
coin is bad.
Prisoner's Defence: When the lady told me it was counterfeit I said I

charge.

ALEXANDER WILLIAM Grant. I am a medical student, of 2, Vernon
Square, King's Cross Road-on March 9th I was with Mr. Winslow, just
at the back of the hospital-we saw several men, and I received a blow
behind my ear; I turned round and saw Mr. Winslow surrounded by a
crowd, and saw him knocked down-I felt my watch-chain pulled, and saw
the prisoner distinctly striking my friend with his fist-two men were
taken in custody, and the prisoner was detained.
HORACE CLARK. I am a dealer, of Featherstone Place on March
31st I was near an alley behind Charing Cross Hospital, and saw Mr.
Grant and another gentleman go up the alley, and the prisoner ran up
and knocked Mr. Grant down from behind.
