1074. JOHN SOUTH NICHOLS (31), Embezzling the sums of
1997. 103., 131., and 141. Us. 5d., received on account of Juseph Phillips
and others, his masters.
MR. Grain Proscouted: MR. GEOGHGAN and MR. ARTHUR GILL Defended.
JOSEPH PHILLIPS. I carry on business as a tobacco manufacturer and
dealer in cigars, 112, Commercial Street, Whitechapel--the firm is
Godfrey Phillips and Son--the prisoner entered our service at the begin-
ning of March, 1886, As country traveller for the Eastern Counties district
--his sulary was fixed by agreement--this is the agreement; it is
stamped. (This was dated 1st March, 1886, and was in the form of a lotter
from the prison-r, agreeing to an arrangement made that he should travol for
six months at a weekly salary of 51. and expenses, and a commission of 21 por
cent. on sales of cigars and cigarettes, and $ per cent, on tobacco. I think
there was another agreement after that--this one is his writing (This
was dated 4th October, 1886, on similar termne, to 25th December next, and
agreeing to forward on receipt all moneys collected by him.) This is another
egreement signed by him, dated 27th June, 1887. (It was stated that the
alleged embezzlements were previous to this latter dato, and it was not put in.)
His duties were to travel on the Eastern Counties ground, take orders from
our customers, receive money and send it to us immediately-he had no
authority to endorse cheques for us--we bank at the Shoreditch branch
of the London and County Bank-in May last we had a customer at
Norwich named Postal, who owed us a balance of 1291. 108-the
endorsement to this cheque is in the prisoner's handwriting that cheque
was not sent to us-since the commencement of these proceedings I
understand it has been paid in to the National Provincial Bank at
Peterboro', with 126. 3d. in addition, and was passed through to our
account at the London and County Bank, to cover 15 items which the
prisonur sent up as having been received by him--the letter in which he
sent it was dated 10th May, in his own handwriting-it was not sent to
us in the ordinary way these two receipts (produced) signed "Nichols"
are in the prisoner's handwriting. These were receipts for 131. and
141. 08. 5d.) We have not received either of those two sums-this letter
is the prisoner's writing. (This was dated 1st August, 1887, in reply to
letter from the witness, and stated that he had received from Postal
the 1291. 108., also 621. 98. from Mr. Norton, and if the witness would
make out his commission account, he would call at the office to-morrow to
balance.) I first heard from Mr. Postal himself that he had paid the
1291. 16s., and from Mr. Norton a day or two before 1st August-in
consequence of what I heard from Mr. Postal, on 27th July I coinmuni.
cated with my firm, and wrote a letter to the prisoner, of which this is &
copy: "As Mr. Postal disputes the amount of his indebtedness, we must
ask you to attend here on Tuesday morning next without fail”-our chief
