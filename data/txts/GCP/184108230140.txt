morning-I am not able to say whether this lead was melted in this ladle

ladle ? A. I could not swear to it-it has the appearance, from the im.
pression on the lead.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do you believe that lead had been scooped out
by the ladle, out of the pot ? A. I have no doubt of it, I would not
have sworn it came out of the ladle unless I had seen it in the ladle-
the marks on it seem to correspond very well.

2170. WILLIAM TAYLOR and WILLIAM JOYCE were indicted
for stealing, on the 15th of August, 20 yards of oil-cloth, value 31., the
goods of Christopher Teesdale.
CHRISTOPHER TEESDALE. I am an oil-cloth manufacturer in Crawford-
street. The prisoners were not in my employ-this cloth was taken from
my premises-the officer showed it to me at the office-I know it to be
mine--it was just inside the threshold of the front door.
PATRICK JENNINGS (police-constable E 123.) I met the prisoners on
the 15th of August, in Alsop-place, at a quarter past nine o'clock-Taylor
had the oil-cloth on his shoulder, about a quarter of a mile from the pro-
secutor's-he carried it about thirty yards, then Joyce took it, and car-
ried it on--I asked where they were taking it-they said, "To a cigar-
shop in Regent-street"--they did not know the number-I left Joyce with
the oil-cloth, and took Taylor to the house when I got near the place he
ran off-I took him, and went back, and got Joyce and the cloth.
Taylor. We were tipsy at the time we never went into Crawford-street
--we picked it up at the corner of Gloucester-place-it was lying against
the railing
(Joyce received a good character.)
