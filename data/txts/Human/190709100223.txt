 Detective-sergeant HENRY DAVIS, stationed at Tottenham. On August 7 I went to "The Gun" beerhouse, where I saw a pony cart, and some harness which I recognised as belonging to Henderson.
 Detective-sergeant WILLIAM PHIPPS, recalled. On August 13 I was at Waltham Abbey Petty Session Court. Luck was charged with stealing and receiving a pony and cart, the property of Mr. Hender son. He replied in the presence of Friday: "I bought it of a man I know as Tom Smith. There were others there." Friend was then charged, and replied, "I am innocent of it.  (Defence.) THOMAS LUCK(prisoner, on oath). I have been in business in Pixham Lane, Dorking, for 18 years. I have never before this been in the hands of the police or been charged with any criminal offence. I employ two or three carts in my business. They are painted with blue bodies and yellow wheels. These are my regular colours. About the end of March I purchased the horse, trap, and harness, which was sold to Saunders at the "Ram Inn," Dorking, of a man called "Tom" for £16 10s., which in my judgment was a fair price. Saunders stopped me as I was driving past his place, and we had some conversation which led to the deal. A little later I heard rumours about the pony, cart and harness and had the conversa tion with the superintendent which he has related. I first met Friday (or Smith) on the Thursday before I bought the pony, cart, and harness with several other dealers. They were down for a periodical horse sale which is held twice a year, but had made a mistake in the date. The spring sale has generally taken palce on the Thursday before Good Friday, but it was a fortnight later this year. They had horses for sale. I bought a horse off one of the other dealers.
