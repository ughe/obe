   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Where were you living? A.  At Bristol, as bar maid, where he is perfectly well known—I do not know whether he paid the last quarter's rent of the house in Eldon-street—it was paid up to that time. BETSY NEWLAND. I am nearly fourteen years old I live at No. 9, Wen lock-street, with the prisoner and the last witness—I recollect the day he went into the country—they dined that day at one o'clock—I waited on them at dinner—they had beer to drink for dinner; I fetched it—I remained down in the kitchen—while they were at dinner I went out for a halfpenny-worth of tripe—I went out before tea-time with a note—he wrote a note and told me to take it down to his brother—that was a few minutes before four—I went down and brought the tea-kettle up—the things were set on the table, and I went out—I did not wait on them at tea—I saw two gentlemen there that day, but I do not know them—I got back after taking the letter, about five o'clock—when I got back into the room I found nobody at home—I did not see ray master's carpet-bag—I never saw this bag before to my knowledge.
   Cross-examined. Q . How long have you been living in this house in Wen-lock-street? A.  About a month or five weeks—they removed there the same day that I went to it—I went to Eldon-street the same day to clean the place—it was on a Thursday, and master went away on Monday—it was not on the Thursday before that Monday that they came into the house—I do not think it was the Thursday before—I think it was more than three or four days after they moved from Eldon-street. WILLIAM DOUGHTY. I drive a cab . On the 2nd Oct., I was fetched from my stand by the prisoner—I have been in that street, and driven the pri soner before—on this day, I drove him for 1 s . 6 d ., to go to take up at No. 9, Wenlock-street, and go to Gracechurch-street—I took up in Wenlock-street, at ten minutes before four o'clock—he kept me a quarter of an hour at the door, and I went to the Spread Eagle, Gracechurch-street—he had a coat, a carpet-bag, a little box, and another bag—we had a quarrel about the fare when I was at the door—I said, "I cannot stay here all this time, and then go to Gracechurch-street for 1 s . 6 d ."—he said, "Never mind, go on."
   Cross-examined. Q . What time was it? A.  Ten minutes before four o'clock, by Hoxton clock—I got to the Spread Eagle at twenty minutes after four. CARL MORIE DORMAN, ( through an interpreter ). I am a gilder. I know this carpet-bag, and these rags that are in it—they belong to a man named Gotchalk, who has worked by me—here are three watches—I have seen three watches that Gotchalk had, but they were not so bright as these—I am quite certain about this bag—I can swear that these two cloths belonged to Gotchalk, I do not know where he is—this bag hung on a nail in my kitchen on 2nd Oct.—Abraham Gotchalk and Herman Gotchalk were in my room—Abraham had a little box in bis possession, which he showed me—the box was sealed, and he broke the seal—there were several rings in it—one ring he put on his finger, and showed it to me.
   Jury. We think we have had enough of it . NOT GUILTY.
