 Cross-examined by MR. PREDERGAST. Q. There was no particular time at which he was to have possession? A. No; I believe he said the rent would commence on the Monday after the Thursday that the partition was taken - he did not apply to sleep there before - he was to sleep there on Thursday, the 1st of March - he had taken my bed-room at the same time; he did not say the partition was useless to him - I had no other fixture, except two or three shells - it had been a fishmonger's, but those fixtures were not mine - he did not say I had better take them away - he said the partition must be moved about a yard towards the fire-place - he did not afterwards say that space would not do - he gave me this paper (looking at it) on the morning of the day the partition was taken down - he paid me no money; he left my daughter in care of the place while I was out, and a friend of his proposed to cut out part of the partition: I said if they did, they must make it good - I refused to let him move it without the landlord's leave - it was not to go out of the house - I never said he might do what he pleased with it.
 This paper was here read; it was an agreement from the prisoner to make good any alteration made upon his leaving the premises.
 COURT. Q. Did he know you were going out? A. He saw me, and I saw him with the carpenter, and thought he was come to make alterations - I did not know where he lived; he had given a reference to the Surrey-road.
   HENRY TALMAGE. I was sent for, being the landlord, and found the partition gone - there were twenty-five squares of glass in it; it was worth 5l. - I saw the prisoner on the Wednesday. he came there to sleep; I asked what he had done with it - he said it would be all right tomorrow.
   JOHN WITHECOMB. I was sent for, and asked him what he had done with the partition - he declined answering.
 Prisoner's Defence. If I have done wrong, it was an error in judgment.
   JAMES FERGUSON. I went to this house on the Thursday evening, and found the partition standing up - the prisoner asked if I would buy it of him, which I did, and took it down - the prosecutrix was present, and saw it down, and said she thought it would be best to let it remain a little longer, to see if the shop would do - the prisoner said he should put up another which would answer the purpose better - she made no reply - it was loose then; I took it away; it was about seven o'clock in the evening.
 COURT. Q. Did you see the prosecutrix there when you was taking it down? A. Yes, and the young lady brought the candle down when the prisoner knocked at the door - the prosecutrix was there till it was laying down.
   JANE SANDERS. The prisoner applied to me before my brother came home, and I went with him to the house - I was there when the fixture was removed, and the prosecutrix was there - my brother opened the door to let her out - I did not hear any thing pass about removing it - it was taken out into the court - I minded it while he went for somebody to carry it.
 MRS. CHAMBERLAIN. On my oath I did not see it moved - it was fixed when I left the house, I swear that.
   SARAH CHAMBERLAIN. I am the prosecutrix's daughter - my mother was out when this partition was taken out - I was up stairs with some friends - I did not see her leave the house.
     NOT GUILTY.
 999.  ELIZABETH HARWOOD was indicted for stealing on the 20th of March , 1 rush basket, value 3d., and 48 brass handles, value 5s., the goods of William Moore.
 WILLIAM MOORE. I am a  brass-finisher. On the 20th of March these handles were in a basket - I called at a public-house, and while there, it was taken away.
   THOMAS BAGSTER. I am servant at the Fortune of War public-house, Guildford-street. The prosecutor came in there - the prisoner was sitting near him - I saw her leaving the house between two and four o'clock, with this basket in her hand - I did not know who had brought it in.
   THOMAS CROPLEY. I am a coal-dealer. On the 20th of March a customer came to borrow a few halfpence, and left this basket - I kept it till the 22d, when the prisoner and prosecutor came there together - she had not left it.
   WILLIAM MOORE. The prisoner sat near me - I missed the basket directly she went away - I was told she had taken it, and found her at her lodgings with her husband; I did not know her before; she went and showed me Cropley's house, and said she wanted to take it to get a drop of gin.
     NOT GUILTY.
