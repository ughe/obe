MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. SARAH BRAY. My husband is a tailor. I live next door to the prisoner in Birdcage-alley, Borough. On the 2nd of March, about a quarter-past nine o'clock in the morning, I was hanging my things in the yard, and heard a terrible fighting in the second floor in the next house—the prisoner keeps that house, and lets lodgings—he hawks things in the street—I never saw Mrs. Hobbs but once, but I know she lived on the second floor of that house—I went and knocked at the street door, and demanded it to be opened (as I heard screams of murder) four or five times—it was then opened by a woman—I said, "What is Mr. Carroll doing up stairs?"—the door was slammed in my face—I went into my yard again, heard the blows repeated, and "Murder" called—I demanded the door to be opened again, and saw the prisoner stand ing on the stairs, and Mrs. Carroll by the deceased's side—I said, "What are you about? if you don't let the woman come out, I will fetch a policeman"—he told me to mind my own business—I saw Mrs. Hobbs come down stain with a blow on her forehead, and the blood trickling down her neck—she called the prisoner many bad names, and used many bad expressions—she asked him for her boots and her husband's—Mrs. Carroll said, "Betsy, I will give them to you when you have paid for the crockery you have broken"—the deceased called her several names, and continued calling out for revenge, twenty times over, and aggravated them very much—they both used bad words—he said if she did not leave him alone, he would cut her head off—she had a fork in her hand, beating it up and down—I did not see anything in the prisoner's hand—the door was slammed in my face again—I went in doors, went out again, and heard her call him bad names, and say, "Carroll, don't strike me with the poker, I am not a man, but a woman."
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was not the deceased of ex tremely drunken habits? A.  I think she was in liquor that morning—the fork was the size of a dinner-fork—she was beating it up and down the stairs at the prisoner, and aggravating him very much. WILLIAM ALLEN. I am a coal-porter. I lived with Mrs. Carroll. On the 2nd of March I heard a terrible noise in the two-pair room, where Mr. and Mrs. Hobbs lived—I lodged on the third floor, over their room—I heard the cry of "Murder" two or three times—I came down stairs, and found Carroll standing on the third or fourth stair down from the landing—Mrs. Hobbs was standing on the landing, a very little distance from him—she had a fork in her hand, and tried to job it at him—she made several attempts to run it into him—Carroll said, "If I had a poker I would knock your head off"—Mrs. Carroll was not by then—I saw Sarah Bray down stairs in the street—when I came down, I said to Carroll, "Come down stairs with me," and he followed me down stairs—I passed Mrs. Hobbs on the landing—after Carroll had been down stairs about half an hour, Mrs. Hobbs came down stain, and called him all manner of names—he went out—Mrs. Hobbs went up stairs again—Carroll came back again, and there was another row  —she wanted her boots—he said when she paid for the things, he would give her the boots, and all at once he called out, "She has run the fork into my eye"—he ran to the fire-place and took up a piece of broken tongs, and ran into the passage where Mrs. Hobbs was standing—I did not see him strike a blow—I did not see Mrs. Hobbs after that till half an hour after, when I found her dead in the room—I did not go into the passage—I was binding my arm up—I had had my arm broken—I did not hear any blow struck, but heard her hal looing at him, "You murdering old curse," and all manner of things.
