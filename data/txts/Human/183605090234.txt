Witness. These is not the slightest truth in it. JANE BUTT. I live at No.3, Baron's-buildings, Waterloo-road. On the morning of the 27th of April, a little before seven o'clock, I saw the prisoner in Webber-street, near the Riding-school, about five minutes walk from the prosecutor's house—she had her apron full of something, and two cap-strings, hanging out of it—I said to her, "Did you get the situation?"—she said, "Yes, and thank you"—she had come to my mother's shop and inquired for a situation, and we recommended her to the prosecutrix.
   Prisoner. Q.  What kind of Cap-strings were they? A.  Two strings with edging—I could swear to them. GEORGE BEARCHELL. I am a policeman. I apprehended her on the 27th, about nine o'clock in the evening, in the tap of the Duke's-hand public-house—Mr. Burchell gave her in a charge—she said; "Do not handle me, I will go with you quietly, I know what you want"—I took her to the station-house—I have not found the things.
   Prisoner's Defence . I said I would not go without he told me what he wanted of me—I did not know what it was about—the prosecutrix know I was going to leave her—it is a house of ill-fame—when she found I would not stay, she said she would take my liberty from me., GUILTY.*Aged 18— Transported for Fourteen Years.

CHARLOTTE ROSS. I am house-keeper at the Aathaeneum, in Pall Mell, Several table napkins, which I had the charge of, were lost—they have been traced to various pawnbroker. JOHN DODDS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Mount-street. I have two parcels of napkins, which were pawned at various times by the prisoner—all within fifteen months—two parcels, of six and four napkins, were pawned on the 30th of April and the 7th of May. WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer. I was employed to make inquiry respecting this property—I issued some hand-bills, and received information from the last witness; who said they were taken in from Penfold, No. 9, Isabella-street, Oakley-street, Lambeth—I went there, and apprehended Mrs. Penfold, and her husband, who had been a waiter at the Athenaeum club—at that time I saw the prisoner in the front parlour—she lodged with Mrs. Penfold—I asked the prisoner if she had seen any napkins there, and whether she had every pledged any—she said she had not: that she had pawned a gown and shawl for Mrs. Penfold, but never any napkins—I left Mrs. Penfold in care of Goddard, while I went to look for her husband—I directed Goddard to take Mrs. Penfoldd to Bow-street; and there the pawnbroker said Mrs. Penfold was not the person—he described the person to me—I went back to Mrs. Penfold's house—after waiting so me time the prisoner came in—I went out with her, and, in Westminster-road, I said I had cause to suspect she had pawned some of napkins—she said she had not—I said, "I must take you to the pawnbroker's to see"—I took her there, and the directly said,
