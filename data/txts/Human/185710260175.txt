Witnesses for the Defence. CAROLINE LEE. I am the wife of James Lee, who lives at Stratford. On Wednesday, 21st Oct., a pig came into my house—I was lighting my fire, and I heard a noise in the front room; I turned round, and saw two pigs there—I drove them out; I had lighted my fire when one came in again, and I drove it out into the back yard—I let it remain until my hus band caine home—it broke a glass lamp, standing on the table, and a case of stuffed birds—I was trying to drive it out with a broom, when my husband came home I told him a stray pig had come in; he asked me what mischief it had done—I told him; he said, "Let it remain," and he would send to the police station, and give information about it—it was there from the Wednesday until the Friday—on that day the prisoner came for the pig—my husband was not at home, and I gave him the pig to keep until the owner was found; that was the agreement between my husband and him.
   COURT . Q.  Did he come for it, and drive it away from your premises? A.  Yes; he tied a string to its leg, and drove it off—that was, I think, about half past 6 o'clock at night.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'CONNELL . Q.  How far does the prisoner live from you? A.  I do not know the distance—he lives at Bow—I gave him the pig to keep until an owner was found, because we had no place for it—we were afraid we should not be allowed to keep it—he has a different place for keeping it, a field—I did not see him from the time I gave him the pig until I saw him in custody. JAMES LEE. I live at Stratford, and am foreman in Mr. Burford's manu factory. I have been there ever since I was nine yean of age—when I came home on the day in question, I asked who broke the case of birds; my wife said a pig came in the place and ran underneath the table, and upset the case—I was very angry, and said I would keep the pig until I found the owner, and would make him pay for it—I asked my lodger to write a letter for me to take to the station house, on the Thursday; he said he would take it; he delayed it—I asked him for it on Friday; he gave it to me, and I took it to the station as soon as I had done work, about 9 o'clock—I went to where my brother-in-law works, and asked him if he would take the pig until I could find an owner, as I had got no place to keep it—he did so—when I went to the station they said they knew nothing about any pig.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where did you see the prisoner about taking care of the pig? A.  At the wharf, where he was at work—it is about half a mile from my place. NOT GUILTY.
