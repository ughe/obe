The Inspector (continuing) said it was alleged that Perryman
attempted to swindle a bookmaker by backing a horse after knowing
the result of the race, and it was decided to expel him, but that
decision was reconsidered. Soon after he started a bucket-shop
business, and later on in the year promoted a bogus concern in which
the publie were defrauded. In 1890 he was expelled from the
National Liberal Club in consequence of his connection with a gold
mining company which was exposed by the Financial News," who
were sued by Perryman in the Queen's Bench Division, when the
jury found the company to be a swindle. He was subsequently at
this court sentenced for libel to two months' imprisonment as first
class misdemeanant. Numerous instances were given by witness as
to the shady transactions in which Perryman had been mixed up,
and he stated that many complaints had been received in connection
with the present business.
