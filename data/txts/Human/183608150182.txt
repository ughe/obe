   MR. DOANE . Q.  What is your husband? A.  A porter, and resides at No. 60, Broad-row, Prospect-place—I was not in the habit of visiting the prisoner—the Swan public-house is on Fish-street-hill, just by the foot of the bridge—I recollect this was Wednesday, because she was taken the next night—I did not hear it till Friday morning—she was taken on Thursday night—I have never stated this till now—I said I was quite surprised, how could that be, when I saw her there?—I said so at her mother's—I do not live far from her mother's—I was in the habit of going there—I am not related in any way to any part of the family—I am quite sure it was the Swan, to the best of my knowledge—I have known Mrs. Neale a long time—it was quite accidentally that I saw the prisoner there—I got there about five o'clock—Mrs. Neale and I sat together, and this woman and her husband and two young men, Richard Smith and John Flemming, were there—no one else was there that I know of—there might be other persons—I know these young men—I do not know whether they were friends of Mrs. Neale—she appeared to know them—we all conversed together about different things—the prisoner mentioned that her feet were blistered with walking, that she had been out with her husband—she did not say where she had been—we talked of other subjects which I should not like to mentioned—I said, "I must go home—it is getting late—I am afraid my husband will get home before me"—I had some porter to drink—we all drank together out of the same pot—nothing else besides porter—no gin or brandy—I and Mrs. Neale went first away—we left the two young men, and this woman and her husband there—I put down 4 d . for one pot, and paid no more—if Mrs. Neale paid any thing I did not see it. BRIDGET NEALE. I know Deborah Brown. On Wednesday, the 6th of July, I went into the Swan public-house with Brown about five minutes to five o'clock, to the best of my knowledge—there were several persons in the house, men and woman—the prisoner was there with her husband—I think it was a piece of duck she had—she had a green merino bonnet, a dark cotton gown and a brown shawl—we staid there till seven o'clock—we had porter to drink—Mrs. Brown paid for a pot of porter—we drank it between us—the rest were drinking also, to the best of my knowledge—I have known the prisoner fourteen years—I cannot be mistaken in her person—we alkted together.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  How came you to go this place? A.  I went to Billingsgate; and on returning we went into the Swan—I did not expect to meet any one there—I took notice of the time because I heard of the prisoner being detected—I said to Mrs. Brown. "I won't stop very long, because my husband will be here," but conversation came on, and we delayed—we were talking about family affairs—her husband was there—the prisoner and I sat together, and her husband—not exactly on the same form, but we kept together—Smith and Flemming were there—the prisoner complained about her corns or something, or her shoes pinch ing—I am not at all related to her—I did not hear of this till Saturday—my husband told me—I saw her husband after she was in custody, on Mon day
