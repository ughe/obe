THOMAS GARFORTH( policeman, L  151). From information on Friday, 8th Nov., I went to Mr. Simpson's shop in Lower-marsh, Lambeth—I found there three dozen of these brass taps—I afterwards went to the shop of Henry Fisk, a general outfitter for tobacconist's shops in the New-cut, where I found two dozen and eleven of these taps—I found one tap at the house of Mr. Bliss, a gas-fitter—on Saturday, 9th Nov., I saw the pri soner go into a public-house in the New-cut—I went in after him, and said, "You must consider yourself in my custody for having had a quan tity of brass taps in your possession"—he said, "I have been to Doulton and Watts this morning"—he did not say for what purpose—I had not mentioned the name of Doulton and Watts to him—I asked him how he came by them—he said he bought them at a sale in Leicester-square—I asked him if he knew the man he bought them of—he said he was a dark Jew-looking man, with a thin nose—I asked him if he had a catalogue of the sale—he said, "No," the sale had commenced about an hour before he got there—he afterwards said he bought them at Jones's sale-room on Monday the 4th, in the evening—there is an auctioneer of that name—there are two auction-rooms, but Mr. Jones's is not in Leicester-Square, it is two doors down a passage—the prisoner described the place where he bought them—he said, "Jones's is at the further end of Leicester-square," which would be the further end from the police-court—I went to another auction-room, Mr. Thomas's—I got a catalogue from Mr. Thomas".
   COURT . Q.  There is another auction-room in Leicester-square? A.  Yes, Mr. Thomas's, which is three doors from Green-street; that is in the square—I was informed by the foreman that the prisoner had been to Messrs. Doulton's. HENRY ADAMS SIMPSON. I am a marine-store dealer, of the New cut, Lambeth. On Monday, 4th Nov., I saw the prisoner in my house about twelve o'clock in the day—I have known him fifteen years, he attends sale-rooms—I never knew anything wrong of him—he buys lots, and sells them in the trade to ironmongers, marine-store dealers, and furni ture dealers—he called on me that Monday about twelve o'clock, and said he was going to Jones's sale-room for twelve dozen of taps to be sold, what would I give him for them—I know Jones's sale-rooms; they are called in Leicester-square; they are within two doors of it—I do not know whether they have any window which looks into Leicester-square—I said I could not tell what to give for them unless I saw them—on the following Thurs day he brought me a sample tap—I told him I would take three dozen at 7 s . a dozen—he came back in about two hours, and said he had sold three dozen to Fisk, and six dozen to another place, and I should have the three dozen, and I had them—he came back about five o'clock, and brought the three dozen with him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have known him fifteen years? A.  Yes, he has been dealing at my shop—I always thought him a respectable young man—it is a common thing for parties to buy things at sales, and sell them at a profit; that is the way he has got his living—he was well known in the trade—all the brokers in the Cut know him—I never bought taps of him before.
