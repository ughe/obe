ROBERT SEAGER (Detective Inspector, City). On Thursday, June 15th,
I received a warrant for the arrest of Beall-I nade inquiries from that
time with a view to finding him, and on June 20th I was with another
inspector, Davidson, at Chapel Street, Edgware Road, and I saw Beall there
-he came towards us-I said to him, "You know us, Mr. Beall," and he
said, “Yes”-I said, “I hold & warrant for your arrest for fraud ; shall
I read it to you here or at the station?"-- he said, " At the station, if you do
not mind "-he said, “I heard of it last night, and was on my way to the
City, and thought I might have seen some of you at my office”-he was
then taken to the station, and formally charged and made no further
reply-on the same day in the afternoon I saw Lambert at the Detective
Office, Old Jewry; he was brought there by Mr. Beyfus, his solicitor-
Mr. Beyfus spoke to me, and I then said to Lambert that I was a police-
officer, and held a warrant for his arrest; I read it to him-he said, "As
soon as I heard it I went to my solicitors, and then we came on here at
Once"-he made no further reply-about half-past five o'n that same day
I saw Wain at the Detective Offices in Old Jewry, in charge of Sergeant
Willis-I told him that I was a police-officer, and had a warrant for his
arrest, and read it to him-he said, "There must be some mistake
about that first date, as I was not connected with the company till some
time after that "-the warrant put the conspiracy at the date of March
1st, 1892-then he was taken to the station, and also formally charged,
and made no further reply.
-- WILLIS (Detective Sergeant). I went on June 20th to Wain's
address at 39, Hogarth Road, Earl's Court, and inquired for him, and in
consequence of what I was told I sent for his doctor-I saw Mr. Wain
and told him the charge--he said, “This is, I suppose, the outcome of
touching pitch and becoming defiled "-I conveyed him to the Old Jewry,
and read the warrant to him.
Cross-examined by Mr. ISAACs. During the course of these
proceedings I went to Messrs. Brandon and Nicholson's office-I saw Mr.
Meadows, a clerk in their employ-I said I wanted to speak to him in
connection with the affairs of the London and Scottish Bank-he said
something to the effect that he knew nothing at all about them; that it
was his principal, Mr. Jocelyn Brandon, who knew all about that, and I
then saw Mr Jocelyn Brandon-Mr. Brandon is alive now, as far as I
know.
Re-examined I do not remember that Meadows told me anything about
Mr. Brandon ; as to whether he had at that time been subpoenaed or not.
JAMES MURPHY (Inspector, City), About 2 o'clock on June 20th I went

was a police officer, and had a warrant for his arrest-I read it-he made
no reply; he was taken to Cloak Lane Police-station and charged, and
made no reply there.
