WILLIAM FARLEY. I am one of the letter-carriers in the Post-office.
was on duty in the Letter-carrier's-office on the 23rd of June last, durine
the period that the letters by the Indian mail were being sorted, and
Ringrose come from the Inland-office with a handful of letters in his hand-
he had nothing else that I am aware of-be passed from the Inland-office inte
the Letter-carrier's office, and passed into the tenth division-I followed him
and saw that they were what we termed "good letters"-I saw the letters in
his hand-good letters are unpaid letters, with the postage marked on then
for which money is to be received I observed a newspaper in his hand-it
was a 3d. charged newspaper, that created some suspicion in my mind, and
I mentioned it to my superiors.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Did you go round the Post-office
to get signatures to a petition to the Postmaster-General, to keep the
Directory in the office ? A. Yes, that was not by the request of Mt
Kelly-it was the request of the Committee formed to draw it up-there wa
no inspector among the Committee--I was employed on the Directory many
years.
Mr. Clarkson. Q. You thought it was a good thing for you and other
letter-carriers, and you and others presented a memorial to the Postmaster
General ? A. We did.

Lower Thames-street walk, No. 106, and entered the amount of the charge
on the docket now in my hand.
