GEORGE KING. I am in the employ of Edward Wallis, a horse-slaugh-
terer, in Garratt-lane, Wandsworth. On the 16th of June the prisoner
came to me with a black horse belonging to Mr. Thomas Miller, of Croydon,
who is a sand-man, and keeps a beer-shop. I know his master very well-
his Christian name is Thomas--I lent him another horse to go home with
as be said he had left his own in our field-he left Miller's black horse in
our field, and said to me, “What can you give me for the other horse ?"
meaning Miller's black one-- I told him to tell his master I would allow him
558. for it--next morning he came and brought my horse back, saying he
would take the money I had bid him I sealed the money up in a letter,
put a note inside it, and gave him 18. for himself-he went down towards
Wandsworth, and tied his horse, which he had brought with mine, up at a
sign-post, and went away with the money-I gave him the money to de-
liver to his master, believing he had authority to sell the horse-it was
the black horse which had been turned out into the field-my master's
daughter wrote a note with the money.
- THOMAS MILLER. I keep a beer-shop, and am a broom-maker; I live
at Croydon. On the 17th of June the prisoner left my service I was not
aware of his bargaining for the sale of one of my horses-I never autho-
rized him to sell the black horse he never brought me the money for it
-I had sent him to Wandsworth on the 16th with a cart and two horses,
and he brought Wallis's horse home, saying the black horse was knocked
up, and appeared ill, and Mr. Wallis had lent him a horse to bring home,
which he must return about ten or eleven next morning I said, “Well,
be punctual, and get there in time"-I tied Wallis's horse behind my
cart, and expected him home at night, but Wallis's man came over, and
said he had bought the horse-I did not see the prisoner again till he was
in custody.
WILLIAM LEE. In consequence of information I received, I went to the
shop of Townsend, a clothes-dealer, in High-street, Wandsworth --I saw a
note taken out of the pocket of a pair of trowsers there, by the shopman,
which I produce I cannot identify the trowsers-the shopman bas left.
WILLIAM REED. I apprehended the prisoner at Brixton-hill-I made
him no promise, but cautioned him if he said anything, I must state it
before the Magistrate-he acknowledged that he had absconded with the
money, and hoped if he got over this, he should not do anything more
wrong.
GEORGE KING re-examined. The note produced by Lee is what I sent
by the prisoner with the money-(read)—"Sir, I have sent the money
for the horse, as agreed on yesterday, 21. 158., which you will find
enclosed."
Prisoner's Defence. The policeman told me I had better tell what I had
done with the money, or I should be sorry for it: this was an old horse,
which master bought at a sale; it fell down four times, when the eart was
empty; when I came to Wallis's with a load of bricks, it fell again ; people
said I should never get home with it; King said, “As I know your master,
I will lend you a horse to go home, and will turn yours into my field;" he
told me to tell my master what he would give for the horse; I told my mis-
