696. HENRY COOPER (28), and FREDERICK HENRY DRAKE
(29), Feloniously forging and uttering & request for the delivery of goods
with intent to defraud ; to which Drake PLEADED GUILTY.
MESSRS. SLEIGH, POLAND, and TAYLOR conducted the Prosecution.
FREDERICK HENRY DRAKE (the prisoner). I was formerly a clerk, in
the employment of Messrs. Bryant and Hodges, sugar refiners of Fieldgate
street- I went into their service in March, about three years ago -I know
Cooper, he had a grocer's shop in Aldgate-I first became acquainted with
him in the latter end of 1858, but did not become intimate with him at that
time-he was aware that I was a clerk in Messrs. Bryant and Hodge
service-he has called there two or three times on week-days, and once
twice on Sundays-he called twice on a single Sunday- I lived on the
premises-he did not come in when he called on week-days, I saw him out-
side the door, but he did on Sundays-Messrs. Henderson and Constable,
Great Tower-street, are customers of Bryant and Hodges before the writ
ing was on this document (produced), I took the blank form from the rack in
the sale-room, at Messrs. Henderson and Constable's when I was there on
bnsiness for my employer-I knew at that time that Henderson and
Constable had purchased some sugar of my employers, which was not
delivered-I took the blank form afew days before the sugars were delivered,
a few days before the 24th May-after I had taken it I saw Cooper ats
public-house in Whitechapel—I do not think it was by appointment, I cannot
say—I do not know the sign of the house, it is opposite Whitechapel church,
and is kept by a man named Morton--it is not quite half a mile from
Cooper's premises I had met Cooper there several times before person
was with him on this occasion, whom he introduced as his brother-in-law-

day-pen and ink was called for and brought-we were in a small back-
