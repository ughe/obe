2485. CHARLES CROMWELL HICKS was again indicted for unlaw.
folly obtaining 2 sovereigns and 1 half-sovereign, from William Selwood,
by false pretences.
Mr. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM SELWOOD. I am a grocer, and live in Belgrave-place, Wands.
worth-road--the prisoner dealt with me some short time before the 28th of
Aug. for grocery-he came into my shop on Saturday, the 28th, about
four o'clock, produced this check, and said it was too late to go to
Charing-cross that night to get it cashed, if I could cash it he would
be very much obliged to me, and I could pay it away on Monday morn-
ing, if I liked-(read--"London, Aug. 28th, 1847.-Gentlemen, pay to
myself or order the sum of 21. 10s., as per advice from Lieut.-Col. Car.
ruthers. C. Hicks. To Messrs. Cox and Greenwood, army agent s, Craig's-
court, Charing-cross.") I asked him who it was drawn on--he said on Col.
Carruthers, payable at Cox and Greenwood's, Charing-cross-he said Col.
Carruthers owed him 151., and he could only get leave to draw on him for
that small amount-he said that the colonel was an old acquaintance of his
I gave him the money for the check, 21. 10s., and in the course of a few days
I paid it away to Mr. Underwood, the tea-dealer--the prisoner called on me
again on the Monday evening, and asked me if I had paid the check away
I said I had not, I should pay it away in the course of a day or two-he
said, “Very well"-he did not say anything farther then, but I think there
was some talk about his knowing Col. Carruthers many years in the army-
I do not remember what he said-I saw him next on the Saturday morning
- he asked me if I had paid the check away-I told him I had he said very
well, he expected it would be paid-he came again the same day, and asked
me if I had heard anything about the check-I told him I had not, and he said
he did not want me to be a loser by it, and there was 21. of the money, and
threw down two sovereigns--I told him I could not take the two sovereigns,
as I had not got the check-he took the sovereigns up, and went off -
Teceived a letter the same day from Mr. Underwood-I have it here I sent
for the prisoner about six o'clock the same evening, and showed him the
letter-he read it-(letter read_"11, James-street, Covent-garden. Dear

convenient. Yours respectfully, J. PEARCE, for Mr. Underwood. Sept. 4,
