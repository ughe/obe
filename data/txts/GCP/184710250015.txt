WILLIAM MATTHEWS. I remember going over the premises in Wellelose-
square, on the 9th of Aug.--I afterwards spoke to Mr. Adams, the convey-
ancer, about them; he told me that everything was right, that he had perused
the deeds for a whole day; that they were a yard high, that there was no
objection to pulling down the place; that it had been let originally for
garden ground-it was not fit for anything but to pull down.
Mr. WuLDE. Q. What are you? A. A carpenter, foreman to Mr. Rom
binson-I went to view the premises on the 9th of Aug.--I saw Mr.
Adams, and I raised an objection that I thought the building could not be
sold to be pulled down, and then he stated that he had been perusing the
deeds all the morning, that there was no objection to pull them down--that
was when I met him just opposite the Kent and Essex tavern, in White
chapel ; and he said, " I have been at work for you all the morning."
Charles Button. I am a dealer in hay and straw, and a carman. 1
remember the 101. being paid to Mr. Gardiner by Mr. Rowe-I think it was
on the 17th of Aug., about half-past six in the afternoon- I was to have
found part of the money, and the money was ready to be paid for the
building, and I told Mr. Gardiner so on the 21st-I heard Mr. Gardiner
tell Mr. Rowe, at the time the 101. was paid, that he should pull the back
part down first, that he had got a very good bargain, and he would want a
number of horses and carts to take down the premises, and he had got a
tenant at Hackney, and he should be very glad if he would give him a job,
