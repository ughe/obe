I live in Acton-place, Manor-street, Clapham. On 6th Feb. last I was married to the prisoner in Hammersmith Church—I had known him a few months previously—I had been married before—I accused the prisoner, six weeks before my marriage to him, before Elizabeth York, who had told me he had been married, and he said, "My wife has been dead three years; she died in childbed at Brussels"
   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose you said, "Are you married?" A.  I said, "Innes, my friend has told me that you are married;" and he said, before her, "My wife has been dead three years; she died in childbed at Brussels"—the prisoner was living in my neighbourhood, just below me, and his family—I think he had a situation in the Admiralty—I keep a beer-shop—I know the prisoner's family, but I had no intercourse with them, only by seeing them at Church—he had sisters and brothers—they were all living in the neighbourhood except one, who was living at Tewkesbury—we were married at Hammersmith Church; that was by mutual agreement—I after wards heard the prisoner was married, and I felt it my duty to institute this proceeding—I went to his first wife's mother on the 16th of this month—the fact of his previous marriage was not talked of before our marriage, only by my friend—after our marriage it was not talked of more than once, to my knowledge; that was in presence of my servant—it appears that every one. in the neighbourhood knew it but myself—I am not in the habit of going from home—I had not heard it.
   Q.  Did he not tell you that he had heard his wife was dead, that he had had information on which he could rely, that his wife was dead, from a person of the name of Waddilove? A.  He said she was dead; Waddilove's name was never mentioned till after the marriage—he said decidedly his wife was dead.
   MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  And on that you married him? A.  Yes; his brother's were in the habit of visiting at my house.
   COURT . Q.  What was your first husband? A.  A licensed-victualler—I keep the same house that he died in four years ago—I have what this brings me in, and some leasehold houses which are settled on me by will—I shall be thirty-nine years of age in December. ELIZABETH YORK. I live in Cross-street, Clapham, and am unmarried. I know the last witness—I made a communication to her about the prisoner, and she said to him, "I hear you have another wife," or "have got a wife," or something to that effect; and he said, "I bad a wife; she has been dead three years or three years and a half; she died in Brussels, in child-birth."
