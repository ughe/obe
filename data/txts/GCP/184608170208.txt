Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. You have made out many bills for
Wicks, at larger prices for his friends ? A. Yes—those accounts were entered
in the ledger-I entered the short price in some few instances--it is impor-
sible to say how many, perhaps half a dozen-there is an account of his ex-
tending over more than twelve months—that is not kept the same as my as
count-the parties to whom the coals were sent is entered in Wicks's account-
he is made debtor to his master in the ledger, but is different to mine-in some
instances the party who had the coals is stated in the margin-that is after a
certain time--in the earlier part of his account the parties are not named-
that is thesame as my account--the accounts are balanced from July, 1848
to the present time-Mr. Pegg could refer to these accounts at any time.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. There is a debtor and creditor account to the pri-
soner in the ledger; what is the reason of that? A. My first inducement to
put his name was, there was a dispute with him about an order that came
through some friend in some way which remained unpaid some time the
prisoner disputed as to having any knowledge of it--this account enables us to
refer to parties he recommended there is no other object in it-I had no
authority to make any abatement to him-he never said he was entitled to
abatement-he asked me to alter the bills as a matter of favour-Watts's name
does not appear in the prisoner's account—if I do not receive an order myself,
I ask the prisoner what price he sold coals at-I enter the price he tells me in
the journal--be made no claim to have them at a lower price--this 238. in
the wharf-book must have been put there since I copied the entry into the
journal-if he was entitled to the deduction there would be no object in
making the alteration-no such arrangement existed with the prisoner.
COURT. Q. How do you enter when you are allowed 58. profit by your
master? A. The price I am to pay myself, not that which is charged to the
customer.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You had a right to charge the customer anything
you pleased ? A. Certainly.
MR. Clarkson. Q. Do you know of any dealings with Watts except the
three items named ? A. They are the only transactions.
MR. CLARKSON to Mr. PEGG. Q. Did you say to the prisoner, about
the time Moore was married, that Moore was indebted to you 571. : that he
was going to be married with your money, and you should close the account
both with Moore and himself? A. Certainly not, nor anything like it.
