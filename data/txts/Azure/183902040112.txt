666. JOHN PAYNE was indicted for embezzlement.
LENY DEIGUTON SMITH. I am a crape manufacturer, and live in Lor-
nine-place, Holloway. The prisoner was an occasional gardener in my
employment—I was in the habit of entrusting him with money to purchase
trees, shrubs, and so on—my wife has had cheques from me, which
through our servants have been entrusted to the prisoner—on the 1st of
February I drew this cheque (looking a' it) on my banker for 101.—1 gave
it to my wife, who is an invalid.
SUSANNAH BAILEY. lam in the service of the prosecutor. I know
this cheque—l got it from my mistress on the 1st of February, and gave
it to the prisoner to get changed, and pay a bread bill of 9s. 9d. to Mr.
Greig, the baker—he did not return—I generally used to see bim two or
three times a day—I did not see the prisoner again till Monday last at
Hatton-garden—he used to live very near my master's—l never went to
his house to inquire after him.
MARY ANN I live with my father, who is a baker. I know the
prisoner—I have frequently seen him. On the 1st of February, he brought
this cheque to change, about eleven o'clock in the morning—I changed it,
and took our bill out of it—I gave him 91. IOS. 3d., and he went away.
THOMAS WALKER (police-constable N 88.) On the 3rd of February I
apprehended the prisoner going into the Cock, at Holloway, about nit*
o'clock in the morning—he was sober—I told him it was for a cheque
belonging to Mr. Smith—he said he knew he had committed a crime and
he must suffer for it—I said I must take him to the station-house—he said
he expected as much—he had 48. 1 Rd. on him—I asked what bad become
of the rest of the money—he said he had lost it.
GUILTY. Aged 52.—Recommended to merey.—Confined One Mouth.
