   MR. PHILLIPS  to MR. BROWN . Q.  How long have you known Ellis Cas par? A.  Since 1818—he is a watchmaker—I purchased a watch of him, for which I paid him 51 l .—I recommended one person to him—in his dealing with me, I found him fair and honourable—I believe him to be an honest tradesman.

I am an assayer of metals, and carry on busi ness in Maiden-lane, Wood-street. On the 2nd of April, some assays for Henry Solomon were made at our house—there were three assays tried for gold and silver both, and one of silver only—on the 3rd of April, there were two assays of gold made for the same party—in consequence of some mes sage I afterwards received, I sent the two last assays to Mr. Bult, in Cheapside, by the desire of John Dean, Solomon's man—the writing on these two assay papers is mine—I believe they are what were sent to Mr. Bult.
