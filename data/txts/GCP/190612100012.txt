STANTON, Henry James (36, jeweller) and JONES, Harry (30,
bricklayer); breaking and entering the warehouse of Frederick
Bradley and others and stealing therein 683 skins, their property, and
feloniously receiving same.
Mr. Burnie and Mr. J. R. C. Lyons prosecuted; Mr. Purcell de
fended Jones.
JAMES MARSHALL. I am in the employ of Messrs. Thomas Bailer
and Company, Limited, of Aldersgate Buildings, leather merchana
and fellmongers, of which firm Mr. Frederick Bradley is a member
The prisoner occupies the ground floor and basement of No. 5. I
superintend the looking up of the premises, and saw the place locked
up on Tuesday, November 6, at six o'clock.
FRANCIS HENRY FORD, manager of Thomas Bailey and Co. On Tue
day, November 6, in consequence of a message, I went to the warehouse
at about half-past 10 in the evening. I found that the padlock had beer
broken off the inner door, the bar taken off, and the door forced in. The
outer door had been left open, as the people upstairs were working late.
Seven or eight shelves in the warehouse had been cleared. The skiss
produced are the property of the firm. They are sheepskins
or skivers, as we call them, and had been prepared for binding and
for making various fancy leather goods. The value of them is about
£129. At the Guildhall I was shown two other skins, which I identi-
fie as the property of the firm.
Cross-examined by Mr. Purcell. The warehouse is some distance
down the passage from the street. The door is seoured by a bar
fitting over a staple, and a padlock, but there is a mortioe lock ss
well. A jemmy would soon effect an entry. The skins were not rolled
up but simply packed upon the shelves.
