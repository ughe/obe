ALFRED Brown (Detective E). At 3 o'clock p.m., March 6th, I went
with Inspector Fletcher to No. 11, Fitzroy Square-I found some rope hang-
ing in the yard on some iron railings on the top of a wall—I found marks
upon the wall as though some person had been getting over--I went into
the back yard of No 12 and found footprints on the garden mould by the
side of the wall the footmarks were of some person without shoes and as
if wearing ribbed socks-I then examined the other wall leading to No. 13
and found similar footprints there and slight marks on the wall--the yard
close up to Mr. Plumbe's house is paved with stones.
Cross-examined. There were marks in the footprints showing hollows
between the knit of the socks as though the foot had been ribbed, but not
So wide as the leg of the stocking would be.
Re-examined. I noticed the small lines between the ribs—I have seen
the socks produced and have no doubt they would have made the im-
pressions.
BRUCE JOHN CAPELL I am clerk to Mr. Plumbe and have been with
him 4 years--I knew the prisoner-- on the afternoon of Thursday, 6th March,
I assisted in examining the premises-1 noticed that the dust had been
brushed off from part of the railings round the back window as if by some-
one sitting on them--there is a flight of stone steps to the yard- I saw the
footmarks—they were all in the direction going from No. 11 towards No.
13-I at first thought they were marks of a naked foot, but on examination
they appeared to be marks of one wearing knitted soeks-I assisted in
taking this plaster cast (produced).
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I should say it was a knitted sock-when
I said a ribbed sock at Bow Street, I meant a knitted sock-I do not know
the distinction—there are certain ribs on it but I did not mean the big ribs
which I have seen on socks-it is just such a sock as I or anyone might
wear.
JANE LUDGATE. I am a domestic servant in Mr. Plumbe's employ-upon
the Thursday morning, March 6th, about 7.15, I was the first to come down
stairs- I noticed the chain of the front door was down and the bolt drawn
back--the door was locked-it has two locks-ono was undone and one
not the door would lock if pulled to from the outside.
CAROLINE HODGSON. I am housekeeper at 94, Hercules Buildings, Lam,
beth--the prisoner lodged there with a man named Richard Edwards—they
occupied the same room-on the night of Wednesday, 5th March, the
prisoner asked me to call him at 1, as he had to go into Fleet Street to some
friends- I called him about 2 o'clock, and let him out-he came back at
6.15 the same morning- I did not see him; my son let him in- I saw him
afterwards, about 9 o'clock, in the kitchen-he said he had a cheque to cash
at the Bank, and was waiting till the Bank opened at half-past 9-he left
about 9,30, and returned about 11-he then went upstairs, and on coming
down again he paid me two sovereigns and 6d., being 188 6d. for lodgings and
28. I had lent him.
