512. JOHN ROGERS JOHNSTON (40), Feloniously forging and
uttering an order for the payment of money with intent to defraud.
MR. BRINDLEY onducted the Prosecution; and MR. DALTON tha Defence.
John Smith. I live at 73, Southampton Street, Camberwell, and am
a timber merchant-a man named Mitchell brought me this bill of ex-
change. (Read : "14th September, 1877. Three months after date
puy to my order the sum of 251. 68. Od. for value received. John R.
Johnston. To Edwin 8. Mogford, 19, Cecil Court, St. Martin's Lane,
Strand. Accepted-E. 8. Mogford. Payable at 19, Cecil Court, St.
Martin's Lane, Strand." Endorsed "J. M. Johnston, 8, Albert Terrace,
Star Road, Fulham. G. W. Mitchell.”) I discounted it for George
Mitchell and presented it in due course it was returned and pronounced
by Mogford to be a forgery I had no return whatever for it.
Cross-examined. It was marked "Not within, no orders lett," not as
a furgery I do not know any of those handwritings on the bill, except
that of George Mitchell," which I saw him endorse-I do not know
the prisoner's handwriting my clerk presented it he is not here it
was sent through a notary as well--finding that it was not taken up
it was sent to a publie notary-it was presented at Mogford's promises.
(The bill bore the name of " Donnison, Notary, 71, Cornhill.")
GEORGE MITCHELL. I live at 3, Gladstone Terrace, and am an
accountant-I took a bill to John Smith, but whether this is the one
I do not know I had two-one I received from the prisoner and the
other from a man named Trimm-I know the prisoner by sight and
have had business with him-I believe both the bills were for the same
amount-I have no memorandum-I did not receive them both the
same day-the first bill was given me by Trimm-I think it was
September or October last year that I received the bill from the prisoner
outside a public-house, opposite Aldridge's Horse Repository, St. Martin's
Lane - I took it to Mr. Smith the next day, I think the prisoner told
me he was in some difficulty about some rates he had to pay and he
wanted 41. 148. Od., I think-he said he had received the bill from Mogo
ford, Cecil Court, St. Martin's Lane-there had been a previous bill, and
he said that Mogford offered to pay 11. if the first bill could be retired
and a fresh one given-I left the bill with Mr. Smith as collateral secu-
rity, not endorsed by mehe then paid me 41. 148. Od., I think it was,
by cheque, to enable the prisoner to pay his taxes, and he let me have a
5l. cheque for myself upon my giving him a written undertaking to
obtain, free of cost to him, a certain deed or bond in the hands of a
person named Cooper-I gave him this memorandum when he gave me
the two cheques on the 20th October-Frederick Trimm brought me the
