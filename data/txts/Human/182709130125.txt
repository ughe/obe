 1864.  JOHN GRACE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 2 books, value 6s., the goods of John Brooks, his master.
   DAVID HERRING. I went, in company with Mr. Brooks, and found the prisoner in a room in St. Giles'; these two books were in the room; I asked where he got them; his mother said they were bought in Holborn, but she could not tell the shop; but at the office the prisoner said he took them to read and to return - he was servant to Mr. Brooks; I do not know Mr. Brooks' Christain name.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1865.  JOHN DUDLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , 1 spoon, value 12s., the goods of Richard Wharton.
   ISABELLA VASEY. I am servant to Richard Wharton, who lives in Grafton-street, Bond-street. On the 11th of September, the prisoner was there, at work with others, white-washing the house - I lost a spoon off the shelf in the kitchen.
   GEORGE ELIAS LAZARUS. I am a silversmith, and live at No. 10, Broad-street, Bloomsbury. On Tuesday, the 11th of September, the prisoner brought this spoon to sell, between two and three o'clock - I asked where he got it; he said he found it among some rubbish - I said that could not be, he had better go and get the owner; he went and brought Winsland, who said it was Mr. Wharton's.
   NICHOLAS WINSLAND. I am a painter. I met the prisoner, who had worked for me; he came into my house, and confessed that he had stolen the spoon - I went with him to Lazarus, and told what he had said.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.
 1866.  DENNIS GALLAKER was indicted for feloniously assaulting John Pluck , with intent to rob him.
 JOHN PLUCK. I am a  carpenter, and live at Ponder's-end. On Saturday night, the 4th of August, about half-past eleven o'clock, I was coming towards Ponder's-end from Waltham-cross, in the road - I saw the prisoner in the footpath, reeling about, as if intoxicated; he was going in the same direction as me - I walked on to get out of his way; he came into the road, and asked how far I was going - I said, not far - I walked on at a good pace; he followed me, and went into the foot-path - I found he got nearer to me - I ran, and he ran, for about half a mile - I then got up to some houses; he came within about a yard of me, off the footpath into the road; he appeared to pull something out of his pocket, and said if I resisted the least, he would blow my brains out in a minute - I saw something black in his hands - I supposed I should be shot; he did not put any thing up to me - I ran up to some houses, about twenty yards off; he followed and asked where I was going; I said to speak to a friend; he went over the bridge - I went a back way to a constable's house, and as I was calling him up, the prisoner came by the door - I do not know whether he saw me; the officer and I followed him, and took him - I did not know him before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What was in his hand? A. I do not know; he was searched, and a razor was found on him in a sheath.
   JOHN WILSON. I am a constable. Pluck called me up and seemed much frightened - I went about three hundred yards and took the prisoner; he said he had only stood in his own defence, and he told the Magistrate that Pluck had shoved him in a ditch; he had nothing but this razor in a case - a pistol was found by somebody, in a ditch near the spot; the man who found it is not here.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking pretty fresh; this man shoved me over into the hedge; I saw him running away - I ran after him, and said he ought to be shot.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1867.  WILLIAM BROWNUTT was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 bonnet, value 10s.; 3 caps, value 3s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s.; 2 collars, value 1s.; 1 pair of bracelets, value 6d.; 4 shillings, 4 sixpences, and 6 halfpence, the property of Richard Wells.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I am an officer. On the 8th of September I saw the prisoner, with three others, in the New North-road, trying several area-gates; they stopped, and one of them disappeared for a short time; in a few minutes I saw the prisoner with this box under his arm - I sent Berry after the other two, but he mistook me; he went round and met the prisoner with the box, just as I got up to him - I had seen him at Mr. Well's house in Bridport-place, New-road - I went there, and they claimed the box - Mrs. Wells did not attend the office without a summons; and when she came, she would not swear to the property.
   JOHN BERRY. I am a butcher. I saw the prisoner with three others in Bridport-place - I saw another go into the house, and bring this box out; he gave it to the prisoner - I ran and took him.
   HANNAH WELLS. I am wife of Richard Wells, and live in Bridport-place. I had a cap like this, and have no doubt it is mine, and the bonnet is mine; they were in this bonnet-box; the bracelets are mine; the box was taken of a chair in the parlour - I do not know whether the door was left open.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the other lads, who asked me to carry the box, and told me to follow them; they brought it out of the house, and told me to go to the City-road.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
