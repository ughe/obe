1363. HENRY WHITE, was again  indicted for stealing, on the   1st of April , 3 shifts, value 3 s  .; 1 table-cloth, 1 s  .; 1 shawl, 1 s  .; 6 petticoats, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 5 shawls, 1 s  .; 5 towels, 1 s  .; 2 caps, 6 d  .; 2 handkerchiefs, 6 d  .; and 2 bed gowns, 1 s  .; the goods of John Ruston . ELIZABETH RUSTON. I am the wife of John Ruston. I left these articles out in my garden on the night before the 1st of April—they were safe at eleven o'clock—I got up at five o'clock the next morning, and they were gone—these are mine. CHARLES GILL( police-constable K  59.) These four towels, two night caps, and two handkerchiefs I found on the prisoner at three o'clock in the morning, and these other things were found in the bag where Spencer's things were.
   Prisoner's Defence.  At eleven o'clock at night I saw a lad who was intoxicated; he said if I would see him to Stratford he would pay me, and in going back I saw these things lying down; I put them into my pocket, and the officer stopped me; I told him where I had been. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Four Months .
 1364. MARY ANN WILLIAMS, was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of Feb., 1 cloak, value 5 s  .; 1 pinafore, 1 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, 1 s  .; the goods of Mary Matilda Ebdon , from her person. SARAH LANE. I live in Bride-lane, Fleet-street. On the 2nd of Feb. the prisoner came to me for a lodging, as I had a bill in my window—in a few minutes the person came in who lives in my first floor, and it was agreed that the prisoner should lodge with her, and pay 10 s . a week for board and lodg ing—she then asked me to recommend a man to fetch her boxes from the Commercial Club-house at the back of the Bank—I called a man—she agreed to give him 4 s ., and he was to fetch them at six o'clock in the even ing—he came, and asked if she was ready—she said, "Yes," and she asked me to let my little niece go with her—I said she might, and she did—her name is Mary Matilda Ebdon—she had a cloak, a pinafore, and a handker chief on, which were her own property—I bought them and gave them to her—her father is dead, and her mother is 160 miles off—I gave her these things for herself—the man came back at eight o'clock and said he could not find them—the child could not find her way back to Bride-lane—she got to her aunt's at Clerkenwell at one o'clock in the morning, and had not these things then. MARY MATILDA EBDON. I recollect going with the prisoner that day. I am sure she is the person—when she got to the top of the lane she took hold of my arm, and said she wanted to make a call or two before the man came—she took me towards Temple-bar, and to Piccadilly—she took me to five public-houses, and at the last one she forced me to drink—she took me up some court, took off my cloak, pinafore, and handkerchief—she then took me further on to the corner of a street, left me, and said she would re turn in two or three minutes. FRANCIS MANBY DICKER. I am a pawnbroker. This pinafore and handkerchief were pawned by the prisoner.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I never took the child's things off; the policeman has a spite against me. GUILTY.
