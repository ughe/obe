JOHH MASON. I am the father of Mr. William Mason, and live at New castle. I remember the last election in July very well—I remember the nomination day on Saturday—the nomination took place about eight o'clock in the morning, or soon after-when the nomination and speeches were over, I went about canvassing with Mr. Miller and Mr. DeHorsey—I continued canvassing till dinner time, five o'clock—Mr. Miller and a number of people dined together at the Castle—after dinner, I and Mr. Miller left the Castle together-to the best of my recollection it was six o'clock or soon after—it was from six to half past six o'clock—we then went to a few houses, to voters who had been out when we went can vassing—I accompanied Mr. Miller during that after-dinner canvass—the canvass continued till eight o'clock, or soon after—Mr. Miller then went down to my son's—I walked with him down to there—I did not leave Mr. Miller after quitting the Castle after dinner, till I went with him to my son's—he did not return to the Castle at all in the meantime.
   Q.  As well as you can remember, what time should you say you ar rived at your son's with Mr. Miller, that evening? A.  Eight o'clock, or from that to half-past eight o'clock, at the outside—I did not look at any clock, but I believe it was at that time—it was quite light—I went into the lobby with Mr. Miller—I saw Mrs. Mason there—I did not stay any time there—I bid Mr. Miller good night—I left him there—I did not return to the Castle that night—I did not see Mr. Miller at all that night after leaving him at my son's house—after that I went up the London-road and through the market—I went by the Roebuck home—I went from my house to the Roebuck, and Mr. Badnall was addressing the crowd—I suppose it was not nine o'clock when he was speaking-l sup pose it was nine o'clock or after when he finished-here was no speaking from the Castle this evening-down to the time of my leaving, there was no speaking from the Castle.
