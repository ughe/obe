EMILY HUxTABLE I was married to the prisoner on 5th May, 1858,
at Dr. Laing S house-I don't know what they constitute a chureh-th is
no congregation held, I think, at the place where I was married is Dr.
Laing 8 private residence-I have heard of people being married at private
residences-Dr. Laing, his sister, and another person were present
the form of service gone through was not exactly like the Church of England
service, but similar a long ceremony, rather longer than the Churoh of
England service-I have never before heard the msrriage service in the Scotch
Church performedth prisoner put a ring on my finger in the course of
the service, and the minister then said, 46 Those whom God hath joined
together let no man put asunder," and long prayers-no one gave me away
-I do not think the prisoner was asked whether he would take me to be his
wife-I do not remember whether the prisoner said anything when he put the
ring on my finger-I lived with him after that for about six weeks in the
colony and then I went to England alone, and he joined me here some months
fterwards-that was by agreement with m-when he came home the first
time I was very ill, I had just got out of my confinementah stayed with
me two days, and then went a voyage then he came back, and I joined him
in London, and he came with me to my father's house, living with me as my
husband-he represented himself as steward of a mail steamerthis
(produced) is the certificate that I received, at the time of the marriage, from
De. Laing, immediately after the ceremony--the prisoner represented himself
as a single man.
