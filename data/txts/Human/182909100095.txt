 1739.  REBECCA CHITTY was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 23rd of July , of a certain evil-disposed person, 2 pairs of seales, value 3ls. and 9 weights, value 4s., the goods of William Gayton , which had then been lately before stolen, well knowing the same to have been stolen.
 WILLIAM GAYTON. I am a  surgeon. I lost a pair of seales from my shop in Church-street, Bethnal-green, on the 23rd of July, at twelve o'clock; I saw them at the prisoner's house; these are them; I value them at a guinea and a half - they cost me near 3l.
   THOMAS WATERS. I went with a search-warrant to the prisoner's house; I asked if she was the mistress of the house; she said Yes - I asked if she had any doctor's scales; she paused, and said, "Yes, up stairs;" I went up, and found them under the bed, covered with some dirty linen; she said she hoped we would be merciful to her, she was a poor widow woman - I asked for her book; she said she could not write herself; she gave me a book, which had not got them in; I took her to the office, and afterwards a little girl, whom she called her daughter, came and brought a book, which had got the scales down at 2s., which she before said she gave for them - she did not say from whom she purchased them, nor did she say that she had not them for sale, as they were intended for a friend; I did not hear her say so at the office.
 Cross-examined by MR. MILLER. Q. Was not a boy examined at the office? A. Yes, but he was put in the workhouse to be kept, and ran away; he was here on Saturday, but I could not find him to-day; he said he saw two boys go in with them, and they gave him 2d. to say nothing - it was through him the search-warrant was granted; his name is Driscoll.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The officer came to my house, and asked if I had a pair of scales; I said, "What sort of scales?" he said, "Doctor's scales;" my reason for asking was, I had bought several pairs for the shop - I said, "I hope I have not done wrong in buying them;" I did not know where my little girl had put them, as she had put them away up stairs, as she had been cleaning - I stood for some minutes; my little girl came up, went to the box, and took them out; the officer did not take them; the lad I bought them of was by himself; he had a very decent appearance: I questioned him, and he said they were his father's, who was ill - I had bought two or three things the week before of him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1740.  REBECCA CHITTY was again indicted for receiving, on the 23rd of July , of a certain evil-disposed person, 4 milk-pails, value 11s., the goods of Ebenezer Blayney , she knowing the same to have been stolen.
   EBENEZER BLAYNEY. I am a milkman. On the 23rd of July I lost four milk-pails from my garden, in Queen-street, Hackney; I had seen them on the garden pails the night before - these are mine; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   THOMAS WATERS. I am a Police-officer. On the 25th of July I went to the prisoner's house with a search-warrant; she is a kind of marine-store dealer, in Hackney-road; I found these pails up stairs; she said she bought them - I asked her for her book of the entry; she gave me one book, and I said, "It is not in this;" she said, "I have another, which my brother puts down things in, as I can't write myself:" I took her to the office, and soon after another book was brought, which had them entered; she said she had bought them some time, but she did not say of whom; she did not tell me the price, nor whether she bought them of a man, woman, or child.
 Prisoner's Defence. I told the officer I bought them in an honourable way, gave 7s. for them, and exposed them at my door; one of the witnesses came and bought them, and gave me a deposit of a shilling, which induced me to take them in.
   JOHN BAILEY. I am a chair-bottomer. I went into the prisoner's house to ask if she had any chairs to mend - I cannot tell the month, but it was on a Tuesday; I saw her give 7s. for these pails - she bought them of a tall man, in a drab coat; she gave him two half-crowns and two shillings - she called me to do some chairs, and offered me ninepence a piece to do them; I said I could not do them under a shilling.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1741.  ESTHER WELLS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 1 bed, value 10s.; 1 blanket, value 2s.; 1 half blanket, value 1s.; 1 pillow, value 1s., and 2 rugs. value 2s., the goods of Thomas Brown.
   THOMAS BROWN. I lost this property from a backroom, on the ground floor, No. 3, Angel-gardens, Shadwell; the prisoner did not lodge in my house; I got up that day a little before twelve o'clock, as I am a patrol I left a little girl asleep, and went out for an errand; as I was coming back a woman came running round the corner to me, and said, "That woman has come out of your house with a bed, there she goes;" I went, and took her with these articles.
   ELIZABETH TAYLOR. I am a neighbour of the prosecutor's. I saw the prisoner come out of the house with the bed on her shoulder.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I got into conversation with a person, who said her name was Brown, but I had no knowledge of her; I said I was very destitute - she said if I would go to this person's house, and get the things out, she would give me a shilling, and she would wait for me in King David-lane.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. Confined Fourteen Days.
