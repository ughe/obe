   Cross-examined. Q.  What did you give for them? A.  5 s . for the two—I had seen the prisoner two or three times before—he had sold me trifling things on each occasion—I think the time before that he sold me an old box, and some old newspapers—I gave him 1 s . or 1 s . 6 d . for the box—he sold me an old shot-belt and powder-flask for 1 s . together—I do not recollect what I bought of him besides—I do not keep an account of what I buy—he was dressed as he is now—he brought me the bottle-jack about two o'clock, and the lamp at the same time—I know these knives were offered to me, between five and six o'clock—I said I did not want them, and offered 5 s . for them. JOHN DOWLING. I am a clothes-salesman, in Cleveland-street. I bought the knives of the prisoner, for 1 s . they were quite new.

   MESSRS. JONES and BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. ELIZABETH THORNTON. I am single, and live with my mother, in a cottage in Pocock's-fields, Islington—MR. Templeman lived in a cottage facing us. On Tuesday morning, the 17th of March, I went to Mr. Tem pleman's cottage, at half-past eight o'clock, with two sheets of writing-paper—I knocked at the door, but received no answer—MR. Templeman used to sleep in the further room from a cottage inhabited by Mrs. Jarvis, and the further room from the door—the door opens into one room, and then you go into the other—there is a window to the bed-room—when I received no answer I went to the bed-room window, and found it shut down as usual—there was a short curtain to it inside—I was too short to see over the cur tain—I then went to the sitting-room window, and found it open—I then returned home, and told my mother what I had observed.
