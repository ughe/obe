939. JAMES M'CARTHY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
February, 2 muffs, value 8a., the goods of Charles Lutge; and JOHN
M'CARTHY, for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing then to
have been stolen against the Statute, &c. also, as an aceessory after the fact.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH LUTGs. I am the wife of Charles Lutge, a furrier, in
Newgate-street. On the 5th of February, a man (neither of the pri-
soners) came to our shop to see some furs-ihe selected two muffs, which
were to be sent to No. 14, Qaeenhithe, on approbation] sent them by
Barber, our porter, and told him not to leave them without the money
I never intended to part with the goods except on the receipt of the
money.
Cross-examined by Mr. JoNes. Q. Did you also send an invoice and
receipt A. I did-the porter returned in about half an hour withotit
the goods-next morning I scolded him for leaving them without the
money- did not send him back again that evening-Mr Lutge sent him
next morning.
CHARLES LUTGE. I was present when my wife sent the muffs--I told
the porter not to leave them without the money-he was to bring back
the goods or the money- have since made repeated applications for the
guods or money-I have sent at least every day, three or four times.
Cross-exramined. Q. Did you send the porter back again that evening ?
A. No, as he said Mr. M'Carthy was gone into the country- sent him
next morning- was not present when the muffs were ordered-- under-
stood they were to be sent for the inspection of the daughters have
seen the prisoner's daughters-I afterwards sent some shawls on the 9th,
and sent an invoice with them, including the muffs in the receipt- never
saw either of the muffs in possession of any female afterwards.
