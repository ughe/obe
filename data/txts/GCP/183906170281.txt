HENRY LEUTCHFORD police-constable L 87.) On Saturday evening,
the 25th of May, I was at the Marsh-I heard the disturbance, and saw a
mob of people collected in Coleman's-place-I went to it, and saw Mr.
Godfrey coming out of the mob, bleeding from the head, and at the same
time saw the prisoner in custody of my brother constable-he said, “Take
this man to the station-house, for he has struck Mr. Godfrey with a mal-
let," producing it at the same time, I took hold of the prisoner's arm, and
brought him out of the mob he said he would not go I said he must go
- he was a little violent, and I held him pretty tight-I said I thought it
a pretty weapon to strike a man with-he said he meant to do it, for he
went and fetched it for the purpose.
John FENN (police-constable L 154.) On the night in question,
I heard the cry of police and murder in Coleman's-buildings-Iran
there and saw Mr. Godfrey in the mob, bleeding from wounds re-
ceived in the head-I did not see the prisoner at the moment, he had the
mallet in his hands, and parties standing there said, “For God's sake, po-
liceman, see what this man has got in his hand "--- I turned round, and saw
him with his arm up, attempting to make another stroke, I suppose at Mr.
Godfrey, with the mallet in his hand-I then closed on him, and took the
mallet from him--he had a handkerchief round the handle of the mallet,
but the upper part of the mallet was quite clear--his wife then closed on
me, and endeavoured to pull me away from him-he at the same time gave
his shoulders a bit of a shift, and endeavoured to slip his coat off-he got
it off as far as the wrists-I had a hard struggle to take him, but I did, I
brought him out of the crowd, and delivered him to my brother con-
stable.
John Peter Bury. I am a surgeon. I was called on between ten and
eleven o'clock, and saw the prosecutor in his own parlour I found two
severe lacerated wounds on the left side of the temple, made by some blunt
instrument--they were of a dangerous character-one branch of the tem-
poral artery was divided he was a week before he recovered entirely-the
wounds were nearly healed then, but he was very nervous and ill after
that.
MR. PHILLIPS called
John PATTERSON. I am a printer. On the Saturday night in question
I went to the prisoner's house, about ten o'clock, to call for Mr. Harwood,
who lodges there, to go to an Odd Fellows' lodge with me I went in at
the shop-door (there is no private entrance,) and saw Mr. Daley at work
at the stone, or imposing-stone, as we call it-he is a master printer-he
had a mallet in his hand, which is obliged to be used in the business-he
requested me to remain at his shop-door, whilst he went to a neighbour's
next door-it is Mr. Daley's own house, but Mr. Shears, a lodger, occupies
the back part-he went out, taking the mallet in his hand, forgetting it-
it was not wrapped in a handkerchief, but bare-I saw him knock at
Shears's door-Mr. Godfrey came by me as I stood at the prisoner's door
--he rushed past me in a state of fury-I heard him exclaim something
about Irish as he went down the court, but what it was I cannot say-he
