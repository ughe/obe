I went to Ireland to register the Waterford Copper Mines. Hoge

Barber was introduced by C. W. and 8. E. Brown; they had been try-

gested that an intermediary or third party might succeed where the
company could not, and mentioned Clarke Barber to me; then I
mentioned him to the prisoner Hogg. the chairman of the company.
Our instruotions were, “ Get what you can." I am not quite sure I
mentioned Clarke Barber's name at the time; both Gartside and the
prisoner Hoge knew that an intermediary was being employed; that
would be January or February, 1909. It is referred to in the
minutes of March 30, 1909; undoubtedly it was fully reported at that
time. I agreed with Clarke Barber that he was to effect no setole-
ment with anybody until Me. Hogg had agreed what amount we could
acoept. Gartside and Hogg both agreed to accept £550 from Ashurst,
Morris, and Co., and that whatever Clarke Barber could get over that
ne could keep for himself. In Hicks, Arnold, and Mozley's case the
same arrangement was made to accept £200. The Orders of Court
on which those settlements were made were brought up to the office
and I corrected the register in accordance with the settlement; the
orders were then filed at Somerset House by the solicitors; the settle-
ment was reported to the board and the amount entered in the cash
book, which Hogg had access to. I should not mention the amount
received to the board. (To the Court.) Vot a word was said about
£259 12s. being deducted for commission and costs. I received the
cheque from Ashurst and Co. for £809 12s., cashed it, handed Clarke
Barber £259 12s., and paid £550 into the company's account, enter-
ing the whole transaction in the cash book. Hogg knew that £259 128,
had gone to the intermediary; he saw the cheque; I told him it had
been oashed and converted into notes and gold; he told me to pay the
balance into the London and Westminster secret account; the same
answer applies to the £200 in the case of Hicks, Arnold, and Moseley.
