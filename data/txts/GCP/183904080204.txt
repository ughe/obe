Q. What is the proportion between what a pawnbroker lends, and the
real value? A. Two thirds, if I think a thing would fetch 58., I lend 4.
on it-I am not a judge of flannel--fine flannel would be very chean at
1s. 3d. a yard, but this is very thin-flannels may run from 3s. to 4.
58. a yard-Williams had pawned with me five or six times before, but
Baynton had not-Williams has left flannel a week or a fortnight before
he redeemed it-on all the other occasions it was flannel he pawned. I had
not had disputes with him about the quantity before, as I always made them
within ten or a dozen yards of what he stated.
Q. How happened it, as you had all this chaffering, you did not put the
number of yards on the duplicate ? A. I supposed I was dealing with an
honest man-if I had measured it, I should have done it--we are answer
able for the number of yards we put on-I believe it is the custom
of manufacturers who have articles by them, to pawn them for the purpose
of getting ready money, and then redeem them--that is the reason I took
the prisoner's word.
Court. Q. Did you measure either of the pieces that he redeemed !
A. No, I did not-he had not left any of them as long as three weeksi
was sometimes only three days.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Hand in the duplicates which relate to these two
pawnings ? A. Here they are-here is eighty yards on one, and eighty.
eight on the other—that was put on about a fortnight after I took
the flannel in-at the time I measured this, it was placed in the
usual place where we keep pledges--when the discovery took place, they
were done up just the same as when I took them in-they appeared to have
been done up by a machine—there was no possibility of their having been
undone and done up again.
