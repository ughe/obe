it was very quiet—I sent for something for it—its mouth was very sore—I asked for the baby's bottle, which was given to me in a very dirty state in deed—I sent it down to Mrs. Payne to wash it, and asked for some warm water—I then gave the baby the milk from the bottle, which it took very ravenously—it had no diapers on, and its clothes were very much soiled—its flesh was very much wrinkled—we then went to the Islington Work-house and saw the matron and afterwards the relieving officer, who went with us back to the prisoner's house—we found her there—I had told the little girl that we should go for a policeman—the baby had then been changed into clean clothes and the prisoner was feeding it with brandy and egg—I told her I was very shocked to find the child in that way, and it was very unkind of her to treat it so badly and in that dirty neglected state—she said that she had not neglected it—she had left it with her little girl to look after it—the relieving officer went with me and we took it to the union and left it with the night nurse—I had never seen a child in such a state before—it was very scantily clothed—I touched it, it was cold; it had no socks on and nothing to keep it warm.
   Cross-examined.  My husband was an earthenware dealer—I have seven children and have considerable experience in the care of young children—when I took the child to the prisoner I did not say that it was for six weeks—I said that it had no socks, and that its feet were cold, but its feet were covered over—I am not in the habit of paying visits to very poor people this was the 26th of August—it was remarkably fine weather—I should load clothes on a sick child at that time of year—I have had a child of mine suffering from diarrhoea, and I lost one nine months old—I do not think it was ill twenty-four hours—when I left on the second occasion to go to the workhouse, I did not tell the prisoner when I should return—I had given no suggestion or directions and yet I found her when I returned feeding it with egg and brandy—the girl was fifteen years old—there is nothing remarkable in a girl of that age attending to a baby—when the child was first left with the prisoner I paid for some milk and for a bottle, out of my own pocket.
