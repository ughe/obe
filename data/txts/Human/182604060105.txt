 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not your sister change her name? A. Yes; while she was in service she took the name of Groves: Mr. Groves was a tailor- he is dead I believe; they lived together as man and wife about seven years.
   ELIZABETH ANN ROGERS. The prisoner married me on the 20th of October, 1823, at St. Matthew's, Bethnalgreen. I had been in service, and left it to marry him - I had known him from the July before; I had no fortune; he behaved very far from well to me - I was never happy - we had no children; no one told me to bring forward this case, but I heard he had another wife.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is your mother here? A. Yes; I recollect the prisoner going to the police-office with my mother - it was to inquire whether his wife was dead; he said he had not seen her for nine years; I had no suspicion that he was married when I married him.
   JAMES LEA. I am an officer, of Lambeth-street. I produce the certificates of the marriages and a burial.
 These certificates were here read, the first was of a marriage between  John Wheatley and Elizabeth Felsted, on the 17th of May, 1809, at St. Olave, Southwark - and the second of John Wheatley and Ann Rogers, on the 20th of October, 1823; also a certificate of the burial of Elizabeth Groves, at St. Mary-le-bone, on the 1st of August, 1825.
 - ROGERS. I went with the prisoner to Worship-street, before he married my daughter Ann; he came to my house as a single man - but he was accused of being married - he said he had been, but his wife was dead, he believed, and he would go and inquire whether he might marry again; there was no Magistrate there, and the clerk would not decide the question, but said he might advertise his wife.
 GUILTY. Aged 35. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined Six Months.
 844.  JAMES BLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 1 saw, value 5s. the goods of Nathaniel Hudson.
 NATHANIAL HUDSON. I am a  carpenter. On the 16th of February I left my saw in Black-horse-fields, on the carcass of a house, at twelve o'clock, and missed it at one.
   JOSPEH HARROW. I am a bricklayer. About ten minutes past twelve o'clock I was on the top of a building, and saw the prisoner coming along with the saw wrapped up in a dirty cloth, about a quarter of a mile from where it was taken - I called to him, and asked him how he got it - he said he had found it; I said, "If you will go back, and can show the place, you shall be let go;" he went back, put it against a heap of bricks, and said he had found it there.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met a boy who asked me to take care of this saw for him.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. Transported for Seven Years.
 845.  JOHN BARRATT was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of March , 60lbs. weight of lead, value 20s., the goods of John Smith and William Hill Stamp , and fixed to a certain building.
   ROBERT BOYD. I am a builder. I know where this lead was taken from - I afterwards saw it fitted - it tallied exactly; I have no doubt it came from there - the building belongs to John Smith and William Hill Stamp; on the 22d of March, about a quarter-past eight o'clock, I saw the prisoner and another person drawing a sack from the area of this carcass, and when they got it to the wall joining the side of the bank, I saw another youth heaving it on the prisoner's back; I pursued, and met him with the lead - the other escaped; the lead was gone from off the gutters of four houses - there were seven or eight hundred weight in all.
   HUGH CRAIG. I am an upholsterer. I saw the prisoner draw the bag from the area - I was not aware what was in it; I called Boyd, who followed him; I turned the bag out, which contained the lead.
   ROBERT TEASDALE. I am watch-house keeper. The prisoner and the lead were brought to me; I saw it fitted on the building, and it matched exactly.
   JOHN SMITH. I am part owner of this property, in trust for Mr. Joseph Biddulph.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 846.  JONATHAN BATTY was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , 1 cast-iron plate, value 5s.; 1 door-scraper, value 2s.; 1 dung-fork, value 2s.; 1 pewter plate, value 1s., and 1 brass cock, value 1s., the goods of Thomas Heapley.
 THOMAS HEAPLEY. I live in  St. John's-wood-road. About nine o'clock in the morning of the 3d of March, I was in the first floor of my house - the child came to say two boys had got over the wall - I ran down stairs with my son and desired him to get over the wall and see what they were about - he got over and called me - I jumped over and saw the prisoner and another, who had got these things, which had been taken from the back of my garden, into the field - I had seen them safe forty-eight hours before, but they were not on the spot where I found them twenty-four hours before - the prisoner fell on his knees, and hoped I would forgive him - he said it was the first time he ever was found taking any thing.
   JOHN HEAPLEY. I saw the prisoner and another boy breaking up a cast-iron gutter - I gave the alarm, and went down and found the property over the wall - the prisoner and the other were carrying it - I heard him beg forgiveness.
   ROBERT WILLIAMS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Recommended to Mercy. -    Judgment Respited.
