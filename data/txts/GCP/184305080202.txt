MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE WILLIAM DEANE. I am in partnership with Thomas Winkley-
we live in High-street, Borough, and are watchmakers. In Jan., 1842, I took
the prisoner into our service in consequence of an advertisement, and fully
approving of him, I raised his wages to 188. a-week-he boarded and loded
in the house exactly as one of my family-on Sunday evening, the 20th of
Nov., he was taken ill, and on the Monday morning he was not able to go
up-about a fortnight before that, or between a fortnight and three weeks, 1
had put into his hands a French gold hunting-watch to have the upper case
raised, that the works might be free of the glass-the cases should have been
taken off the works-when I gave it to him, I said, “I have determined on
having the case raised so that it may free the glass, I have determined not to
go to further expense," and I think I said, " It shall go to Fielder, in 8t
John-street-road," but whether I did or not, he knew that, because he wa
the only person I employed for that purpose--he was to have sent it by a
lad I keep for that purpose, and I should have expected it would have been
ready in two or three days, for the boy to have called again for it-it was not
necessary that the works should have gone with the case, the prisoner knee
that, decidedly-he was taken ill on the 20th of Nov., and a day or two al.
terwards I missed the movement of this watch he was then very ill-I said
nothing to him about it-he resumed his work on the 3rd of Jan., and on the
following evening I brought him the whole of his work, which I had taken
