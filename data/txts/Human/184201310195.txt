   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Do you know how long they lived together? A.  No—I saw Emma Fox at Manchester—she was living with her husband, whose name is Bell—she is married now—I went to see whether she was alive—Mr. Barnes sent me—he is an architect—she said she was very comfortable.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know whether she is married to Bell? A.  No. WILLIAM BARNES. I produce a copy of the register of marriage from St. Chad's, Shrewsbury—I examined it myself with the register of mar riages—I am related by marriage to Margaret Turner Thompson—I pro secute this case—I took this certificate down to Shrewsbury by order of Mr. Traill, and found the parish clerk—the register was open—I compared this copy with the book, and Mr. Webster did the same, and signed it—I will swear it is a true copy—( reads )—"Richard Light, of this parish, bachelor, and Emma Fox, of this parish, spinster, were married in this church by banns, this 16th of July, 1823, by me, Thomas Landford, minister. " WILLIAM SPEAK ,"
 In the presence of "  ANN DAVIS . "
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Before you had obtained this, had you seen the prisoner? A.  Yes, on the 3rd of August, at my house—he made a state ment to me, which I took down in writing at the time—he said he was married at St. Chad's on the 16th of July, 1823; that he married Emma Fox, a dress-maker, by whom he had three children, and one was dead; that he left his wife and two children in Manchester, that be had agreed to leave them, and never see them again—he stated that William Speak and Ann Davis were witnesses to the marriage, and that the clergyman was Mr. Landford.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had you made him any promise that he should not be prosecuted? A.  No—I swear he made the state ment without my giving him any inducement—I told him I should make use of the evidence against him—his third wife, Butler, was present. GEORGE YARROW.
