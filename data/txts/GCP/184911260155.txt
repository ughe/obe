Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Were you not very drunk? A. No,
perfectly sober-I usually take my coat off when I go into a house--I did
not take my other coat off as well—I did not take off my great-coat for the
purpose of fighting; I had no intention of fighting--there was a disturbance
-a man insulted me, and I took my cane, and told him if he repeated the
words again I would put it across his back-I struck him, but he did not
strike me-when I first went in I had a glass of soda-water, and a little
drop of brandy in it-1 was not tipsy; I had not above five wine-glasses of
punch while I was there--I did not drink half-and-half-I swear I bad not
ten glasses of punch; it was rum-and-brandy punch, and small wine-
glasses--I bad just come off a journey, which made me call, there-
the prisoner did not say he had found these things of mine till he was at
the police-station-he at first refused to be searched.
John LOOKER ( policeman, R 295). On 7th Nov., at an early hour in the
morning, I was on duty in London-street, Greenwich, and heard a ery of
"Police !"-I found the prosecutor with his face, coat, and hat, smothered
in flour-he gave the prisoner in charge-I took him to the station, searched
him, and found three pairs of gloves, a six-foot measure, and a silk hand-
kerchief-he said he found the measure in the White Hart-he did not say
anything about the other articles—they were found in a different pocket.
. Cross-examined. Q. What time was it? 4. A quarter to two o'clock-
I do not know whether there had been a disturbance-I did not hear any-
thing about the prosecutor refusing to pay for the bowl of punch-he
was sober.
Witnesses for the Defence.
CHARLES MILLER. I am the conducter of an omnibus, and have been in
Mr. Wheatley's service fifteen years--I have known the prisoner twenty
years; he is a driver. On this evening, I went into the parlour of the White
Hart, and there were two or three gentlemen lying just inside the door on the
top of each other, I suppose they had been fighting-Mr. Parry was stand-
ing at the further corner of the room, and I saw him cane Mr. Moore, a very
respectable old tradesman of Greenwich, with his stick-I think Mr. Parry
was in his shirt-sleeves when I went in; I will not be sure; I know he was,
some part of the evening-Walker went in with me, and be picked up a
gentleman named M'Killan, a wine-merchant; the potman helped-after we
came out of the parlour, and the scuffle had been quieted, Mr. French, the
landlord, got Mr. Wilcox, another old gentleman, out, and put him in the
bar-parlour, and the prisoner said, “I have picked up something: look here."
and he rolled it along the counter: it was a tailor's measure, a round
thing that wound up, like that produced-the prosecutor wanted to fight
again two or three times, and wanted to get to Mr. Wilcox and Mr. Moore,
and we would not let him-there had been some disturbance with them about
some punch.
