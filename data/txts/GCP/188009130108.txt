WILLIAM NEary. I am an accountant of No. 2, Grant Road, South
wark Park-I have been engaged in bankruptcy matters for twenty
years, and am trusteo for several estates-I was appointed chairman of
the mooting of creditors-Challender's proof was not sworn or signed
there was no oppositiou to Bremerkamp being trustee, and I waited
three-quarters of an hour at his request for sufficient creditors to form a
quorum to enable them to prove.
Cross-examined. Bremerkamp asked the prisoner what he could pay
and he said 28. in the pound-De Martelaar said "I would not accept
158."-I discharged the meeting-I produced no proof, no proxy-I
waited for Mr. Challender for whom I was instructed to appear had he
arrived-I told the meeting I only signed as chairman-there was no
evidence before the meeting of the debt of 1501.--I went as a stranger
and took the chair -it was resolved that the prisoner's affairs should be
liquidated by arrangement, and I signed that resolution as chairman.
RICHARD CHALLENDER (Re-examined by the Court). A gentleman came
to me about a proof-I should not know him if I saw him-he came to
know what I owed Pateman-I told him what I owed him and that he
had money of me several times-& printed paper was shown to me, and
I showed it to Pateman-it did not say anything about 1501.- I do not
know anything about it-I should not know a proof in bankruptcy-I
am no scholar-I did not arrange to swear to 1501.; I arranged
to swear to my 45l. ; I sent my receipt I do not know who to-I did
not arrange to come to London, nor promise I did not know about the
meeting-I did not say I would come nor that I would not; I never
Baid anything about it-when the gentleman asked me if anything was
due from Pateman, I said he owed me 451.-Pateman promised ines
horse and van-I proceeded to come-the van was sold-I never made
a proof in bankruptcy-I do not know what bankruptcy is I don't
know anything-two gentlemen came to see me one brought me an
old debt he owed me, 31. I had lent him--the prisoner's father came and
got 251., and then he got 207. for his son to go on with the business,
and gave me on account his horse and van, and he does not owe me any
more-I understood the prisoner had had a fire-I agreed to come to
London and I am come.
GUILTY on the first count. Recommended to mercy, by the Jury on account
of the fireand the troubles which came upon him.-Six Months' Imprisonnant.
