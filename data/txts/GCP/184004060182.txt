CATHERINE SCANLAN. I am a widow, and keep a clothes shop in
Austin-street, Bethnal-green. On the 20th of March I hung some boots
out for sale--six or seven hours after I saw the prisoner in custody of
Kemp, with three pairs of boots, which I had seen safe about half-an-bour
before.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Are you quite sure about their
being yours? A. Yes I had had them some days these are them-
(looking at them)- I know them by particular marks-they hung by a
rope on a nail at the upper side of the door-post. ad
GEORGE KEMP (police-constable N 82.) On the 20th of March I was
on duty in Old street-road a little before six o'clock in the evening, and
I saw the prisoner and his companion in company larking together-
watched them for half-an-hour-I saw them go up to the prosecutrix's shop,
look at some shoes at the door, and pass by-the prisoner then sat down
on the next door step his companion passed by the step several times,
and each time came and talked to the prisoner-the prisoner got up, and
looked into the window-his companion then took the 'shoes down, and
both ran off together-I pursued and took the prisoner-his companion
dropped the shoes--the prisoner said he saw the other take the shoes, and
he ran after him to stop him—they had both run away together-I fol.
lowed them better than a quarter of a mile they both ran off at one time
and in the same direction—the boots were dropped in a street a quarter of
a mile from the prosecutrix's--the prisoner was running in the same direo
tion as the other, but he never called "Stop thief" or any thing till I stop-
ped him—the other passed the prisoner in running, and the prisoner joined,
and both ran together--the other was about ten or twelye yards beyond
the prisoner when I took him.
