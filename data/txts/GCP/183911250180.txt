I had not the least suspicion of the prisoner till the 7th of this month-I
then accompanied the officer to her lodging at Lewisham-I had had their
address from her sister-I saw her there, and saw a box which she had had
when in my house--the prisoner produced a key, and opened it herself,
and in it I found a crape handkerchief, which I had lost from a drawer, but
I did not miss it, and could not tell how long it had been lost-I am quite
certain it was mine-nothing more was taken out of the box then--next
day we went again, and found some more things in the box, which must have
been placed there since the first search, as they were not there then-I also
found my thimble in a work-basket on the table, and an apron of mine in
a drawer down stairsthe prisoner's sister lived in the same house, and she
was frequently in the habit of coming to my premises--a pair of shoes
were found on the prisoner's feet at the station-house, which was one of the
foreign pair.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. Had the sister worked from time
to time at your house? A. Yes, and went home to sleep-I went to their
house because I found property on the sister, and had frequently missed
property before she is several years older than the prisoner--the prisoner
always conducted herself properly while in my service the sister continued
with me up to the 7th of November—the prisoner lived with a surgeon in
Commercial-road for two years and five months before she came to me.
Court. Q. Where did you keep the scarf ? A. It was packed away
in the bottom of a very deep drawer in my bed-room-the sister could
not have access to it-she was not a servant-I generally kept my drawer
locked, but one Sunday in June I found my drawer upset, and a lavender-
bottle broken-her sister was not in the house that Sunday I know.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Have you any mark on the scarf? A. Not in
particular-I did not miss it till the 23rd of September-I had not seen
it after June.
THOMAS HICKEY. I am a policeman. I went to Lewisham, and found
some things in a box in the house-I produce a thimble and a muslin
apron- I found this pair of shoes on the prisoner's feet-she said she had
bought them of Mr. Gilham-I found two pairs of boots, and two pairs of
shoes in a basket in the house.
Cross-examined. Q. What did she say about the handkerchief? A.
She said it belonged to her sister, that a young man had given it to her at
Croydon fair.
ELIZABETH NICHOLSON. I know the prisoner-I saw her wearing this
muslin apron on the 1st of November on the same afternoon I saw her
with a silver thimble like this I said, “Priscilla, what a pretty thimble
you have got, where did you buy it, what did you give for it?"-she said,
“That's tellings," and said she was found in thimbles.
Cross-examined. Q. That is the way young girls talk? A. Yes-I
do not swear to the thimble, but I do to the apron- I took it up in my
hand-I swear to both the ribbon and apron-I am a servant out of
place I was living at Deptford at the time, and saw the prisoner at
Lewisham-she came to our house to tea on the 1st of November-I have
not known her long, but I was living with her first cousin.
WILLIAM GILLHAM. I am a shoemaker at Greenwich. I never sold
these shoes to the prisoner-I deal with the prosecutor-I never bought
such shoes of him.
