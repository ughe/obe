   Re-examined.  Mr. Warne said he had been obliged to cancel the prisoner's indentures two years ago and to discharge him, and he said to the prisoner's father, "You know, Mr. Temple, he has been keeping very indifferent company lately, I have seen him with" so-and-so, mentioning different names—the father said he would wash his hands of him.
   Witnesses for the Defence. MARGARET DOUGHERTY. I am single, and the landlady's daughter—I was standing with Mrs. Calloway at the door on this night, and saw a man come down the street running—I do not see that man here; the prisoner is not the man I am satisfied—he was by himself—he had a dark moustache and no whiskers—I have known the prisoner for three years by standing outside my door, but I have never spoken to him—I know nothing about the prisoner's character.
   By the COURT . He was in the volunteers, and they used to assemble outside my window.
   Cross-examined.  Charles Street is badly lighted; there is one lamp, and one lamp over the baker's door—the lamp was next door to where I was standing, about a dozen yards off—I went to the corner with Mrs. Calloway; we all followed to the corner after the man ran away—he had got into the Waterloo Road by the time we got to the corner, and we did not see him, he had gone. MARGARET TEMPLE. I am the prisoner's mother—the prisoner lived with us until quite recently—the last time he slept in our house was the night before Bank Holiday—he had a conductor's licence—his father in at work and not able to come here—I never heard his father say he would wash his hands of him—he shed bitter tears when we heard of it—I believe he was always an honest lad—after the statement of the sergeant that he was dismissed from his apprenticeship I saw Mr. Warne's manager, and Mr. Warne wrote this memorandum—he was in Mr. Deard's employ, and he entrusted him with 60 l . or 70 l .—he is out of town.
   The Jury here intimated that they had made up their minds, and returned a verdict of NOT GUILTY.
