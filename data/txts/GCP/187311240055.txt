Cross-examined. The bulk was on the floor when the prisoner told me to
fill the gunny bags--the men were there at work at the time-the directions
were given to me on the same floor, but the men did not hear them--the
men put the shellac in the bags by my directions there was no secresy
that I am aware of I have never seen shellao put into bags for the pur
pose of being filled into the cases that require raising-the case is brought
to the bulk and filled with a shovel - I did not see the bags again until
they were pulled out by Mr. Stephens and Mr. Hadow, that was 3 or 3
yards from where I had originally placed them-I have known the prisoner
a great many years, and have been in other employment with him - I have
not made use of threats to him since we have been in Mr. Stephens' tem-
ploy-I swear that I never had a word with the man in my life.

EDWARD BOWRING STEPHENS (re-called). I found one of these bags of
shellae on the 11th September, the day the prisoner was charged I had
been in communication with Mr. Hadow before I went to make the search
I found it on the top floor, a few yards from the loop-bole, amongst broken
cases, or what I might call a lot of rubbish - the loop-hole is on the side of the
water, the water which runs up at the side of the whare there is a crane
by which goods are lowered-Mr. Hadow showed me the bag he had found
about a quarter or half-an-hour before.
Cross-examined. My impression is that the loop-hole was open, but I
can't say for certain ; it was not being used at that time--the rubbish was
broken cases; lots of cases come in very bad condition, and we can do
nothing with them but break them up I know the two bags were found
about the same place.
