 520. HENRY TRUEMAN and BENJAMIN HOUGH , stealing 10 cwt. of coals, value 12 s  .; the goods of Thomas Freeman , their masters.
   MR. ORRIDGE . conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES CHANNON. I am clerk to Mr. Thomas Freeman, of Blackfrian wharf, Blackfriars—the prisoners were in his employ. On 3rd May I sent them with ten tons of coals to Mr. Wheeler, in Hyde-park-gardens, seven tons first and then three tons—Trueman, the carman, and Hough, his trouncer , left the yard with a waggon, with three tons, about a quarter past 9 o'clock—I saw them go—there were thirty sacks in the waggon, con taining each two cwt, or 224lbs. of coals—from what I heard afterwards the prisoners were given into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE . Q.  Did you see the coals put into the waggon? A.  I saw them in the waggon when they went out, and I counted the sacks in the waggon—there were ten sacks down the side, and three across, which made thirty—I gave the ticket for that number—Trueman had been with us about two years—during that time he has borne a good character. ALFRED JAMES GURR. I am butler to Mrs. Wheeler, of Hyde-paik-gardens. On 3rd May some coals were brought to our house—my mistress opened the window, and asked Trueman, the carman, if he had three tons of coals, he said, "Yes"—she asked if he had thirty sacks, he said, "Yes"—my mistress and I watched them, and on shooting two or three sacks, an empty sack turned up—it was taken out by Trueman, and put on the others—in the course of shooting a few more, four more empty sacks turned up, and were put on the others—twenty-five full sacks were shot, and five empty sacks were placed by the side of them—Mrs. Wheeler opened the window, and said, "You have not brought thirty sacks of coals"—Trueman said, "Yes, I have"—she said, "You have not, twenty-five sacks were full, and five were empty"—Hough came into the house afterwards—I told him it was a serious affair, and Mrs. Wheeler would have to go to Mr. Freeman
