I took the letters out of the box when I went in at half-past 8—there had been a robbery from the same box about four or five months ago—the prisoner was standing on the step of the door when he threatened to do for me. HARRY PORTER( City Policeman  121). I was at the corner of Alder manbury on the morning of 2nd April, and saw the last witness struggling with the prisoner about four or five doors from the warehouse—I ran up and took hold of him—Oram said, "I give this man in custody for break ing open the letter-box of 49, Aldermanbury"—I took the prisoner there, and saw the letter-box—they were coming back towards the door—at the station I searched him and found this chisel in his right-hand coat pocket, and also two patent keys and one common padlock key.
   Cross-examined.  The keys have since been given up to his wife by order of the Magistrate—I didn't discover that they were the keys of his coffee-stall. JOHN WISE( City Policeman ). I examined 49, Aldermanbury—there were marks on the outer door of its having been forced, which corre sponded with this chisel, and there were marks on the letter-box which exactly corresponded with the chisel—the prisoner could not tell if there were letters inside the box until he had broke it open because it is a wooden one. ARTHUR ELMAR. I am employed by Jones, Longman, and Co., house agents—they have the management of this warehouse for Mr. Hope Johnstone. GUILTY.— Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
