9112. JOIN HARDAWAY and JOHN BROWN were indicted for
feloniously assaulting Frederick Temberman, on the 9th of September.
with intent to rob him, and his goods and monies, from his person, and
against his will, feloniously to steal..
FREDERICK TEMBERMAN. I am a cooper. On the 9th of September I
had been drinking at a public-house with a friend--I got drunk, and in
going home I lost my way, and met the prisoners, I cannot say where they
Tripped me up and put something before my mouth like a handkerchief,
and they were about my pockets-one got his hand into my pocket-I was
on the ground--a policeman came up-I had never seen them before.
WILLIAM CUDMORE. I am a policeman. At one o'clock in the morning
of the 9th of September I was at the bottom of Pearl-row, South-street,
Borough-road-I observed the prosecutor and two others—the prosecutor
appeared drunk-they were all three together on the pavement-I knew
the two prisoners, and as I went towards them I saw the prosecutor in the
act of getting off the ground—he had been on the ground the prisoners
were standing there-he charged them with throwing him down, putting a
handkerchief before his mouth, and putting their hands into his pocket-
they said it was false, they had done no such thing, for he had fallen down-
I directly took them both into custody, and after I had gone about three
hundred yards I met Piper, and he assisted me the prosecutor collected
himself in the interval, and laid hold of one of the prisoners by the neck of
his coat-I found on Hardaway 1s. 1 d., a handkerchief, and an old
apron-and on Brown was found some money, and a false nose for the
purpose of disguising his countenance.
Cross-examined by Mr. Jones. Q. I believe they went very quietly
with you? A. Brown did, Hardaway seemed to resist, but Brown per-
suaded him to go quietly-the prosecutor was drunk-he said he lived at
No. 25, Pearl-row-I took him there, and found he had left there a month
- I could do nothing but take him back to the station for safety.
Hardaway. When he took hold of me he tore the front of my shirt right
out-I told him to take my collar, which he did, and I then went quietly.
Witness. If I tore his shirt, it was in the struggle-I am not aware of it.
JAMES PIPER (police-constable M 83.) I searched Brown at the station,
and found 94d. and this false nose on him, and some mustachios.
Cross-examined. Q. You found it in his pocket? 4. No, in his hat.
FREDERICK TEMBERMAN cross-examined. Q. Are you sober now?
Yes - I cannot say when I was drunk last, but it might be last Wednesday-
I have not been drinking since I attended the Grand Jury-I was fined last
Wednesday evening, in going home, for being drunk-I cannot say where I
had been the night before this happened I had not been drinking all
night-I cannot say how long I had been drinking--I cannot say when I
began—I had not been so tipsy for a good while as I was that night-I
began drinking about ten o'clock in the morning-I cannot undertake to
swear I had not quarrelled with one of the men before I was knocked
down-I did not tumble down, for I was chucked down.
NOT GUILTY.
