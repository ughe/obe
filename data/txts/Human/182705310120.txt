 1333.  WILLIAM MARSHALL and JOHN OULDS were indicted for embezzlement.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   JOSHUA HEARN. I am a coach and waggon proprietor, and keep the King's Arms Inn, Snow-hill; when I receive parcels, I deliver them to the persons to whom they are directed - Marshall was clerk, in what we call the upoffice; he was to keep an account of the goods received by waggon, and to deliver them to the porters for delivery; he had to make out tickets, with the charge on the parcels to be delivered, and to keep an account of what was received for them. Oulds was my carman for the city; it was his duty to receive money for the parcels he delivered; he was his duty to receive money for the clerk named Jessopp; Oulds could not receive that money without the printed ticket delivered to him by Marshall; it was Marshall's duty to enter in a book marked B what parcels he received - I have a weigh-bill, which was made in the country, dated the 30th of March; the carriers make the weigh-bills, and Marshall keeps them.
   STEPHEN JESSOPP. I am in the employ of Mr. Hearn, and know Marshall's hand-writing; this ticket (looking at it) is his hand-writing. except the printed part of it - I have no doubt of it. (Read.)
 "King's Arms, Snow-hill. 30th March. - 5 sacks, carriage 4s. To be delivered to Brooks and Co. J. Oulds, porter."
 On the 30th of March Oulds did not account to me for any money received from Brooks; here is the book marked B., which Marshall kept; it is in his hand-writing; he has not entered 4s. as received by Oulds from Brooks.
   ISAAC BUTCHER. I am in the employ of Mr. Brooks, a merchant, of Sambrook-court; this bill was delivered to me on the 30th of March, by Oulds, to the best of my recollection; he often delivered goods; I paid him 4s. according to the ticket; to the best of my belief it was two shillings and a half-crown piece; he said he had no change; I told him he might take the man who had assisted him with the goods, and spend the 6d. over the way; he had delivered five sacks of potatoes and some bacon.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. Can you, with certainty, say you paid him two shillings and a half-crown? A. To the best of my belief it was - I recollect giving him the 6d. over - I had received parcels from him before, and am certain he brought these - I am sure I gave him one shilling.
   GEORGE HAZLEWOOD WORRALL. I am beadle of St. Sepulchre. I took Marshall into custody about a month ago - I neither threatened nor promised him any thing - I told him I took him for making bad entries in Mr. Hearn's book - he told me, at the top of Newgate-street, that he was drawn into this case by the two porters, Oulds and Satchell.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. What case? A. This case of felony - he said, "I was drawn into it by the two porters" - this was as he was going to Guildhall, to be examined on this charge.
 COURT. Q. When he was first taken did he say any thing? A. He fell down on his knees to Mr. Hearn, who said, "I do not want any of that nonsense."
 MR. ANDREWS to S. JESSOPP. Q. Do you keep the accounts of the office? A. I receive all monies from the porters - this would be entered in the book B.
 Q. How do you receive it? A. Oulds comes to me and accounts for monies he receives for different parcels - Marshall enters it in the book B. I check it off from that book into the cash-book - when Oulds brings the money he pays it to me only - he ought to do it every night; but never less than every other night - he might bring me 3l. or 4l. I have an account of the payment made on the 31st of March, be paid me 4l. 14l. 8d., - I entered that in my cash book, it is not here; it should appear in this book B - the items are entered here and the different sums. I and Oulds cheque them off from the delivery book B. Marshall had nothing to do with the money - he was only to enter the parcels and deliver the tickets to Oulds.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Then, when goods arrive from the country. does Marshall take an account of them and make out the tickets for the porters to deliver? A. Yes; he enters on the ticket the amount charged - and in the evening the porter gives me what he has received, and I tick it off in this book. I enter the gross sum in the cash-book - the fraud could not be effected but by the joint operation of Marshall and Oulds - if Marshall had entered the 4s. in the book, Oulds must have accounted to me for it; as it would have drawn my attention to it, and I should have had to demand the 4s. of Oulds.
 MARSHALL's Defence. (written.) I have just to say, that if I made out the tickets it was with proper charges, and if anything happened afterwards the account was made to Jessopp; I knew nothing about it. I have a wife and four children to support.
 OULDS' Defence. The way of settling was sometimes twice a week or oftener. As soon as we settle the book we cut the leaves out. I had no opportunity of knowing whether any money was deficient - if any was short I had to make it up.
 JURY to  ISAAC BUTCHER. Q. At the time you received these bags did you sign a delivery-book? A. No; I asked if he had one, and he said No.
