GEORGE SPIGGET. I am a carpenter. I have been at work for Mr. Cooper
-I know this rule-I first saw it last April twelvemonth-I was working for
a plasterer then, and fetched the rule for the plasterer from David Reeve's
have seen it once since-I am able to swear positively to it-it is the same
rule.
Cross-examined. Q. You know it by the flaw, I suppose? 1. Yes.
HENRY COOPER. I am the prosecutor's son. I remember, on the 7th of
August, Haines came to my father's house, and brought this rule-he asked
my father whether he meant to say that that role was his--my father said ho
should know it amongst a thousand-Haines said it was his, and he would
tell him where he got it; when he was at work at a house in Streatham Para-
gon, the rule was there, and it belonged to the Asphalte Company, and they
left it at the house, and the people of the house gave it up to him; and he
said, if it was cleaned, the Asphalte Company's name was on it then, and he
could show it bim in a minute-he then left with the rule.

WILLIAM ALLEN. I am a bricklayer. I know the two prisoners - I knew
Byfield while at work at Brixton-hill, two years ago last March-while be
was going off the scaffold I saw his floating-rule fall-he was in the act of
shoving the tools on one side of the scaffold - the rule slipped off the scaffold,
and I saw this nail driven in it on that occasion-this is the rule-(looking at
it)-I say so on account of this mark-it fell on the ground, and this end of
it fell against & cill, and made this mark on it-I saw the rule split, and I saw
him drive this nail into it-I told him the rule was not injured a bit-I could
swear to it among ten thousand I was examined before the Magistrate-I
saw the rule chip by the "E. B.," and I saw Byfield drive the nail into it;
and seeing it now with this indenture, and this nail, I have not the least
doubt it is the same I have known Byfield four years-his character has
been very honourable-I never knew him charged with anything.
MR. CARTEEN. Q. By what do you take on yourself to swear to this rule?
4. I have explained it by this being chipped off, touching the "E. B." which
is on it-he said when it fell, “D-- you, this is all the good you have
done me"-I said, "It has not hurt your rule nor you, but very near knocked
your name out"-this was in the middle of last March two years--I had
never seen the rule before, to my knowledge-I have seen it since, at Union
hall-I see it now, and I have seen it at Brixton-I do not know that I can
say candidly that I have seen it any other time-I never examined it before
the day the accident happened-I examined it at Union-hall, and I have
examined it now-this accident happened to it at Brixton-bill, at Mr. Mugge-
ridge's house.
JURY. Q. Did you drive the nail ? A. No-Byfield did, and I believe I
can tell you what the nail is-about two years elapsed between my first seeing
the rule and my seeing it again-Byfield called on me, and said, "It is the
same rule that you saw fall from the scaffold," and I remembered that I said,
" It has not hurt you, but nearly knocked your name off.".
James WAY. I served my time with Mr Haines. He has lived in Brixton
twenty-five years, and was an honest, upright man as ever lived-I never
heard him charged with any offence.
MR. CARTEEN. Q. Have you ever worked for Mr. Cooper? A. Yes,
have, when Haines has been there, I never heard Mr. Cooper accuse him of
anything--I have brought this rule from Haines's place to Mr. Cooper's house.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. When Haines was at work, and Cooper saw it there?
A. Yes-he saw me use it there-it was the latter end of April, or the begin-
ning of May that I commenced there, I cannot say which-Haines was there.
