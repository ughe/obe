MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM GORDON. I am in the service of Mr. John Thompson; I am the gate-keeper - he is proprietor of the cattle-layers, in Liverpool-road, Islington; they are used for the reception of cattle going to Smithfield-market. On Sunday, the 2nd of August, I received a flock of sheep on account of Mr. Neal - the flock consisted of seventy-one sheep of the Leicestershire breed; there was one black one among them - I saw them last about half-past twelve o'clock at night; I them moved them out of one pen into another and counted them twice; there were seventy-one - I fastened the pen and left it; I went again next morning, about half-past seven o'clock, they were all gone then, they had been fetched away; thirty sheep were worth between 50l. and 60l.; I have since had twenty-seven sheep skins from Howell, on the 5th of August, from the watch-house by the Regent's-park; I delivered twenty-one to Mr. Griffith, a skin sales-man, and have the other six here; I gave them to Limbrick, the officer - I received the sheep into my possession on account of my master, Mr. Thompson.
   RICHARD LEE. I am in the employ of Mr. Robinson. On Sunday afternoon I saw these seventy-one sheep in Thompson's layer; on Monday, the 3rd of August, I was directed by Swingler, Mr. Neal's man, to go up to the layer at five o'clock; I found forty-one sheep there - thirty were gone; I had noticed a black one there on the Sunday - that was gone among the thirty.
   JOHN HOLLOWAY. I am a butcher, and live at No. 6, Tower-street, Newport-market; I know the prisoner Newitt. On Monday morning, the 3rd of August, about five minutes before six o'clock, he came and knocked at my door; I was not up - my wife let him in; he asked me if I could work - I told him my feet were very bad, I did not know that I could; but he wished me to go very much, and said he would satisfy me for my trouble if I would go - he told me he had sent some things to Mr. Johnson, No. 92, Edgware-road.
 Q. Did he explain what he meant by things? A. Whether he said sheep I do not know, I imagine he must; I was to go and dress them - he gave me some gamble-hooks, tied in a blue apron; they are to stick in the legs of the sheep to hang them up; I went from there to a gin-shop with him, and he gave me a drop of gin; and from there I went to No. 92, Edgware-road - he did not go with me, I understood him that he was going to Smithfield - he told me to go on to Mr. Johnson's, and there was somebody there to help me kill these things; I parted from him when he told me he was going to Smithfield, and got to Johnson's about half-past seven o'clock - I was very lame, and not able to walk fast; when I got near Johnson's I saw the door was open, and some sheep in the road at a distance - I suppose about thirty, but did not count them; I saw two men at a distance with the sheep; I went into Johnson's shop - there was no person in the shop then; I called Johnson - the sheep were driven into the shop; I assisted to drive them in - the other two men, Mr. Johnson, and other people who were passing, also assisted; they might be ten minutes or a quarter of an hour bringing the sheep in; I could not swear to the two other men who were with the sheep - I never saw them before to my knowledge - I never saw the other prisoners to my knowledge till they were at Hatton-garden - I cannot swear at all to them; I never saw them before - if they are the men, they were dressed different at the office, and I considered that one was taller and the other shorter than the prisoners - to the best of my knowledge they were; I then went to get somebody to help me kill these sheep; I returned about half-past ten or a quarter to eleven o'clock;  Henry Wincott and a man called Poultice, were the men who returned with me to assist in killing them - when I returned with them I saw Newitt forty or fifty yards on this side Johnson's house; I told him there was nobody there to help me, and I had been to fetch these two men; he asked if we would have some gin, to the best of my knowledge; he said he had heard in Holborn that there was nobody to help - he told us to go and get to work, to get some sheep dead; the men who was with me went and stuck the sheep, and I was stripping ready to dress them - I went in with them; Newitt came in a little time, and brought some ale into the yard in a pot while we were engaged with the sheep - I did not count them and cannot say how many were killed - we killed the whole in the yard, which was about thirty; I did not take notice what breed they were: there was no black one; there was a kind of brown one.
 Q. Come, Sir, was there a black sheep? A. There was a kind of blackish cast sheep; I cannot say it was a black one - I believe Newitt stuck one or two of them himself; but I was engaged in dressing them and took no notice particularly. Newitt said he wished to get them dead soon that they might get stiff, as he wanted to send them to market; we got done between five and six o'clock I think, but cannot exactly say the time: Newitt did not stay till we had done; he left in the morning: I did not see him again till about six, when I saw him at the public-house next door to Johnson's - Wincott and Poultice were there, and Newitt, I believe, paid them for their work; he paid them 3s. or 3s. 6d., I believe, but I cannot exactly say, as I did not count the money. I saw Newitt again that evening, in a cart near the Hop-pole public-house, Edgware-road; it might be between six and seven - a person named Clark was in the cart with him and a little girl; I got into the cart: there was a sack in it containing some substance, but I did not look inside it - I believe it was fat, but cannot say; I believe there were some skins in the cart; they were sheep or lamb skins - we went down to Clare-market in the cart, to Mr. Sadler's; the sack was taken out and delivered into Sadler's - I cannot exactly say whether Clark or Newitt took it out - next day, Tuesday, about six or seven o'clock in the evening, I was at the Green Man public-house, Coal-yard, Drury-lane, and saw Newitt there; other people were in the house - I cannot exactly say who; Newitt then paid me for my trouble.
