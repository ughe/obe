JAMES MARTIN, Mill-lane, Deptford.  I have known Redding about eight years, and Harman three or four months—they both lived in my house, Harman in a ready-furnished room, and Redding was a nightly lodger, and slept along with my brother—he never slept with Harman to my knowledge—mine is a lodging-house—I was with Redding when he bought the pair of second-hand boots—it was on a Tuesday, I believe, about seven weeks ago, about Croydon fair time—I had heard him say a long time before that he would buy a pair of Wellington boots—I was with him the same day when he bought the second-hand coat of Clothier for 13 s  —I gave Maxwell, who lives next door to me, a coat which Redding had given me to take care of the night he was taken—I did not know there was any thing in it then—it was not the coat I had been with him to buy—I have lent him money from time to time, 4 s ., 5 s ., or 6 s ., and so on—he got his living by collecting old clothes, which he sold in Petticoat-lane—he borrowed 3 s . of me to pay for the boots, and he paid me again the same day.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILKINS . Q.  Has he not generally paid you the same day when you have lent him money at other times? A.  Yes—the clothes busines is sometimes very profitable.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  When did he go to the Clock House to live as pot-boy? A.  Before last Christmas—he was in the clothes line before that, ever since I have known him—he continued pot-boy till he hurt his hand about four months ago—he then went collecting old clothes again. THOMAS MAXWELL, Mill-lane, Deptford.  On the 10th of Oct. I received from Martin this coat and a gown-piece wrapped in it—I afterwards gave them to Lovell. BENJAMIN LOVELL( police-sergeant R  15.)
