MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES ANTOINE PAUPIERRE (through an interpreter.) I live in Smith
place, Wapping. About half-past eleven o'clock at night, on the 3rd di
March, I met the prisoners between Hermitage-bridge and Wapping-ther
asked me to pay for a pint of beer-I said I had no money, but if the
would come to my room I would give them some out of a barrel-ther
both took my arm, and went home with me-on arriving there the table was
laid, and the soup was all 'ready-they supped, and drank some gin out of
a tea-pot, as much as they liked-after supper I said I was going to bed, I felt
tired, and if they liked to come to bed they might-M'Carthy went to bed
Sweeny sat by the fire-I went to sleep directly, as I was a little heady-
awoke about three in the morning-I looked right and left, and felt right
and left, and felt nobody there-I jumped out of bed, and went to look for
my snuff-box-I missed it, and three shirts-I gave information to the
police-I went to a public-house in the neighbourhood with the police, and
saw Sweeny there-I saw Murray find my shirts and snuff-box in a house.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. What business are you? A. I am
foreman to a company for making flour-I have lived in this neighbourhood
six months-it was cold gin in the teapot, not hot whiskey.
WILLIAM NORMOYLE (police-constable H 133.) At four o'clock in the
morning of this day, I saw the prosecutor with sergeant Brook, who could
not understand him-in consequence of what he told me in his own language,
I went to his house-- he gave a description of two women--I knew them!
found Sweeny at Skipper's public-house about six in the morning-the pro-
secutor recognized her directly as one who had robbed him-I told her he
accused her of stealing bis snuff-box and shirts-she said she knew nothing
about it-she said she had been in the room and supped there, and he made
them drunk- I took her to No. 6, Blue Anchor-yard to see after M'Carthy
-I stood at the door with Sweeny while the other officer and the prosecutor
went in- he found the shirts and snuff-box-the prosecutor said, “They are
my shirts," and on unfolding one of them, the snuff-box was in it-I went to
the watch-house with Sweeny and the property-I went back to look for
the other prisoner, between seven and eight o'clock-Murray knocked at the
door, and M'Carthy came out of the back part of the house, partly undressed
-- I took her as she was going into an opposite house--she said she was
drunk, and had no recollection of any thing of the kind.
Cross-examined. Q. Who is Mrs. Stewart ? A. She tents the house-
is a house of ill-fame- I have heard Stewart has been convicted.
Thomas MURRAY ( police-constable H 134.) I produce the things. Stewart
gave me the key of a box after I took Sweeny-I found these three shirts in
a box in her room.
JANE Stewart. I keep this house in Blue Anchor-yard. It is a house
where M'Carthy lived for five weeks—Sweeny. I have known for five or
six years-sbe knocked at my door about four this morning--my husband
let her in-she said she had had a few words with her husband, and asked to
leave a bundle with me, as she was afraid he would tear them-I told her to
put them on the table-she said it would be safer to put them in the box-
the box was unlocked-she put them in, locked it, and gave me the key
a quarter past six the policeman came with her-I gave them the key of the
box-I said she had been there--M'Carthy was out all that night till she wa
taken.
