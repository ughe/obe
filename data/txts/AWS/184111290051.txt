THOMAS YBOMAN. I am a constable of the Thames Police. On the
morning of the 3Ist of October I saw the three prisoners come out of
Messrs. Mastermans' premises, in Broad-street, Ratcliff-] live about 200
yards from there, in Harris'-court-the prisoners passed me at my own
door-Brown said to me, 44 Good morning, we are just going home"
Brailey was carrying a basketI stopped him, and found six bottles of
stout in this basket, four bottles underneath his apron, and one in each
coat-pocket-the other prisoners went away-I detained Brailey--I after-
wards went to White's house--he came home while I was there-I told
him what I came for, and asked where he had left Brown-he said he had
not seen him since Saturday night asked him what time he got up in
the morninghe said, At four o'clock, for the purpose of going mush-
rooming" then went to Brown's house, but did not find bim at home
-I searched the house, and found one bottle, with a portion of porter in

night, to drink-I then went to Brailey's house, and there found nine

-I asked him at the station how he accounted for the bottles I had found
