 1274. CHARLES MADELL. and HENRY EDWARDS were again indicted for conspiracy.
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. LEWIS COLLINS. I in Green's-terrace, Clerkenwell—I am a goldsmith—I manage the business of my father, Francis Collins—I have known Ed wards many years as a watch-gilder and colourer. On the 10th of April he came to my shop, between six and seven o'clock in the evening—he asked if I had any gentlemen's short guards to show to a gentleman that he thought he could sell them to—I showed him some—he selected two, and said he would take them to show the gentleman—he came again, and said the gen tleman had kept one, and he returned the other—he came again, and said the gentleman would prefer the one he returned to the one he kept—I let him have the other—he came again, and said the gentleman would keep the two at the price I had named—they were worth 4 l . 15 s .—I have seen them since at the pawnbroker's—when I saw Edwards at the station I asked him how he came to serve me so—he said he had not been able to see the gentle man.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You have known Edwards many years? A.  Yes—I do not know that he was once in a very large way of business—I have known him ever since I can remember, doing little odd jobs—he was engaged in the watch gilding trade—when he came to me on that Saturday evening, he asked if I had any gentleman's short guards—he se lected two of different patterns—he said he would show them to the gentle man and if the gentleman chose one he would bring me the other back, and bring the money for the one he kept. JAMES SWAINE. I am shopman to Mr. Reeves, a pawnbroker, in Red cross-street—Madell pawned these two chains with me, in the name of John Cook, one on the 12th of April, for 1 l . 5 s ., and the other on the 14th of April for 1 l . 1 s .—he came again on the 16th of April, and I advanced him 4 s . more.
