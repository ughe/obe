I live at 164, Church Street, Stoke Newington—I was manager to Mr. Ablett, an auctioneer—early in September last we had premises to let, 39, Green Lanes; a Mr. Fiddea had taken the place, but never opened it and paid no rent—Robinson agreed to take these premises at 100 l . a year upon a three years' agree ment—he gave his address, 3, Scarsdale Terrace, Dulwich, and wrote down these two names as references: Mr. Suter, carpenter, 5, Long's Court, Leicester Square, and Mr. Coxwell, solicitor, 12, Lawrence Pountney Lane, Cannon Street—I applied to these two persons—Suter told me that he had been employed by Robinson to fit up some premises before—on 5th September Robinson signed this agreement, and took possession of the premises, but he never opened the shop or fitted it up—I saw him in and out of the shop from time to time—I saw Johnson the day Robinson took possession of the shop; he was in at the Royal Oak, two or three doors off, the day Robinson signed the agreement—Robin son said that Johnson was a friend of his—I saw a cart outside on that day—there were no goods in it—Mrs. Johnson was in it—Johnson said the cart was his—no rent was paid.
