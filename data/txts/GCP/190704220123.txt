to work to keep her baby. I remember something occurring on Easter
Monday with reference to prisoner and another man having offered
refreshment to a couple of girls. I was at home on Friday, April 5.
Prisoner came in about half past seven, and brought in some vaccina
tion papers and talked them over with his wife in the front room. I
was in and out till quarter past 10, when I had an appointment to
keep. They seemed excellent friends. When I left my daughter was
sitting in a chair by the side of the fire with her baby on her lap.
prisoner being close to her. On the table were some cups and saucers,
bread and butter, and the knife produced, As I went out I heard my
daughter say to prisoner, “Jim, fill the kettle for me," and he was
going to do it as I went out of the house. He would have to go into
the kitchen to do that. At a quarter past 11, my daughter, Mrs.
Misselbrook, came to me at the "Star and Windmill," at the corner of
Long Lane, where I was talking to a friend, and in consequence of
what she said I hurried home. On passing from the front room to
the back I found my other daughter lying dead at the foot of the
staircase with her head upon the bottom step.
Cross-examined. There was a pool of blood where my daughter
had been sitting and there were traces of blood through the door to
the back kitchen. There was blood on the handle of the door and
on the bottom panel. The lamp was in the front room, and there was
no light in the kitchen but that reflected from the front room.
Prisoner came to stay in my house about three months before the
marriage. I did not know my daughter was th trouble till Novem
ber. I allowed prisoner to sleep at my house because he said he
was very uncomfortable at home. It was between October and De-
cember that he was vaken to the hospital with typhoid fever. It is
not true that I am to be found in the "Star and Windmill " every
night. I cannot have much time, as I am at work from nine in the
morning till two past nine at night. It is not true that I have the
reputation of constantly being drunk. I have never been in difficulty
at the police-court through being drunk. My daughter, Mrs. Missel
brook, had two rooms above mine. She has a husband and four
children. While prisoner was there they all slept in the ono room,
and he had the other. He paid so much a week for his lodgings.
