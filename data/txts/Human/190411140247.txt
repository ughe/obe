   Saturday, December  3 rd , 1904. HERBERT GEORGE THOMAS( examination continued ). Lawson's agreement of. I think, July 18th, was that he was to have a commission on all business he introduced to the company—100 per cent, were the original terms, I believe—on a deal of the magnitude of the Blackpool Tramway Company, £100,000. he would alter the terms, as we did eventually—on £100,000 he would have to get £100,000, according to the agreement—if Lawson brought in such a deal as would produce £10(1,000 profit lie would be en titled to half the profit that the company made—that would be 100 per cent—that is the best way you can work such an agreement out: it is always open to a little alteration as time goes on—the directors would de termine the alteration, with Lawson's consent, I presume—possibly he would take the whole of the profit, I do not see why lie should not in his own company—the Construction Company was his property pure and simple, and that of the Syndicate, they were running it to gether—there was no public money—Paine and Hooley were going to turn it into a public company—Hooley was interested with Mrs. Hooley—Lawson put the company into Hooley's hands with the eventual idea of promoting, it at such time as we did good business—the company was floated as a public company when shares were issued to the public—Lawson. I believe, put a large block of shares into Hooley's hands, and Paine joined Hooley with the idea that the three of us should be interested in the eventual promotion of the company as a company when it was in a fit state—the agreement of November 26th, 1000, is what I have in my mind—probably I knew all about it at the time, but it is impossible to say four years afterwards—you have the minutes, you can answer that question better than I—the £30,000 introduced applied to various schemes—I do not think the company ever got that sum for the schemes—Lawson's letter of November 15th shows that the company was in such a condition that Lawson took whatever he thought advisable—I think the books show about £20,000 advanced to Lawson at or before that time—I do not remember the deed of release, but the figures—would most certainly be put on the table at the time—the deed of release would not be signed without we knew about the terms and went into the matter—you have the minute there and it purports, I presume, to be correct—I am not saying it is not correct—the "services rendered" refer to the fact that Mr. Lawson was finding and buying Tramway Orders and things of that sort—"could always reckon that he was entitled to a fair remuneration for the work he did, independently of his engineer's fees, and his commission for introducing business on the terms of his agreement—he never pushed the point of his 100 per cent.—he dealt fairly and honestly with us—I believe there was an agreement to share the profits when the company would eventually float the Tramway Orders—I cannot say if the agree ment was reduced to writing—we had so many agreements with one party and another, it seems unreasonable to expect me to remember it
