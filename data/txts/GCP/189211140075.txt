Evidence for the defence.
ALBERT WEATHERHEAD (Detective Sorgeant). I was bound over as &
witness for the prosecution; I had the warrant on the morning of
August 29th the prisoner oame to the station, Tottenham Court Road,
and said his name was Maro Friedlander-I told him I had & warrant
for his arrest-he said he had been to Paris, and while he was there he
heard of the warrant and came over to meet the charge.
EDWARD ARTHUR RUSHER. I am in the employ of the Prudential
Assurance Company-gome money was lent to Friedlander on a bill of
sale on furniture at 29, Alfred Place-on August 15th the prosecutor
came and told me that Friedlander had been discovered, and had been
deceiving and defrauding him in money matters, and he understood that
the bill of sale which I had on his furniture was not scourate,
that it was fraudulent, that he was in Paris, and it was his opinion that
he would not return, that he was about to take out a warrant against him,
and asked me if I took any proceedings under the bill of sale if I would join
him and employ the same solicitor-he said he was under the impression
that the business plant was included in the bill of sale, and asked to see
it-I showed it to him, and the plant was not inoluded--I think he said
about Friedlander," He will never return "-I don't think he used the
word "absconded "-he asked me before I took any steps to wait for &
week--I said that I could not see my way clear to it, but ultimately it
was arranged that I would wait til Thursday,he inspected the bill of
sale on Tuesday, June 16th, and came again next day, Wednesday,
and said that Mr. Friedlander had been taking some of the
goods in the house which were included in the bill of sale, and
pawning them; that he had seen him go out with & large
bundle of goods and pawn them, and unless I put a man in possession
