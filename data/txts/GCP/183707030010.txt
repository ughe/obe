WILLIAM FEAVER (police-constable T 126.) I was called into the
prosecutor's house on Sunday morning, the 18th of June--I went into the
drawing-room, and found the prisoner in conversation with Mr. and MA
De Menron-the prosecutor told me he had given information before
our station-house to Feltham, of his being robbed of 601.-I asked the
prisoner what he had to say-he said if he was allowed half an hour, be
would see what he could do towards restoring the money
Cross-examined by MR. JONES. Q. Was the room door locked when
you first went to it? A. No—I did not see any pistols in the room—the
prosecutor took one from his pocket-he said he had been keeping the
prisoner in custody till a policeman came-he said he always kept pistols
in his house-he said he had locked the room door till he heard me coming
up stairs, and then he unlocked it-he did not say he had been keeping
him in custody with the pistol-I saw it drawn from his pocket after I
entered the room-the prisoner did not complain of having the door
locked upon him.
Louisa Maria Hunnam. I am married, and live at No. 10, London-
street. On Friday evening, the 16th of June, the prosecutor came home
between eight and nine o'clock-he was perfectly sober, but appeared very
much excited-he complained of losing something.
Cross-examined by Mr. Jones. Q. How long had he lived in this
house ? A. I think, about five weeks-he occupies apartments there-
never saw him the worse for liquor, I think he is a gentleman who can
drink a good deal, but I never saw him tipsy, nor rather the worse for
liquor-he can take his rum and water and his porter-he does not drink
more than many other gentlemen-he appeared excited that evening when
he came home, from the fall, as he told me he did not appear at all under
the influence of liquor I could not perceive that he had been drinking-
I remember the prisoner coming to the house on Sunday-I was at the
door part of the time-I did not see the prosecutor take out any pistols--
after the prisoner was gone I saw him take them from his trowsers pocket
- they were small pocket pistols-- he showed me how he had put them into
his pocket-he said he took them, and put them into his pocket, to secure
them, that the prisoner might not use them-he did not say it was to secure
the prisoner-nobody came with the prisoner.
