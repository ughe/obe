MICHAEL DOHERTY. I live in Bell-alley. I had the articles stated, in

JOSEPH JOHN Goode. I am a a pawnbroker. I have two coats and
a pair of trowsers, which I took in of the prisoner on the 6th of April,
which he said were his own property-it was between four and six o'clock.
Prisoner's Defence. I pawned them; I went out that morning at six o'clock
to work; the prosecutor took my wife and made her drunk; and when
I came home I found him and my wife in the act of adultery. I went to the
fire, took up the poker, and gave him a pretty good share of it. I went
out, and when I returned I asked my wife for some money; the prosecutor
said I might take some clothes, and make money, which I did; and in the
evening he got sorry about letting me have them; he asked me for the
money, and the money was scattered between them.

alias Smith, were indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of February, 1 work-
box, value 12., the goods of Mary Ann Parker: and 2 pillow-cases, value
58. ; 4 yards of muslin, value 14s. ; and 1 napkin, value 18.; the goods
of William Henry Parker.
ANN PARKER. I am the wife of William Henry Parker, and live at Plais-
tow with my brother, Thomas Gethin. I have a daughter named Mary
Ann, who is fifteen years old. On Tuesday, the 25th of February, 1 delivered
a work-box belonging to my daughter, and various parcels, containing
linen, books, and other things, to James Wood, in. Lothbury, to take to
Plaistow-they were the property of my brother-I am not living with my
husband, he is in Wales—this is the box and other articles which I deliver-
ed to Wood (looking at them.)
JAMES Wood. I am a labouring man. Mrs. Parker delivered the
work-box and other things to me, to take to Plaistow, on a Tuesday
night-I cannot tell the day of the month-I saw the prisoners on the
road, conversing together-I drew my cart up by the side of the road,

house, Ruffett had disappeared-Walker was still there--I had been gone
