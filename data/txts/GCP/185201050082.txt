199. CHARLOTTE FIELDER, stealing 1 purse, value 2d., and 15
shillings and 5 pence; the property of William Bennet, from the person of
Ellen Bennet.
ELLEN Bennet. My husband's name is William Bennet. On 1st Jan.,
about 8 o'clock in the evening, I met a young man in Deptford, and went
with him to a public-house, and had a glass of rum--the prisoner was at the
side of the bar, and could see me I took a shilling out of my purse, where
I had 168. 7d., to pay for the rum- put the purse back into my dress-
pocket-the prisoner was about three yards off, and could see what I did
- when I left the house she followed me, and asked me to give her 6d.
-I did not give it ber-she said she was both hungry and dry, and asked
me to give her a pint of balf-and-hall-we went to another public-house-
she took me, saying it was a quiet house; I did not take her - we sat down
together, and had a pint of half-and-half-I took twopence out of my purse
to pay for it, and put the purse back into a pocket under my dress, not the
same pocket-after I had drunk the half-and-half, I became insensible for
about half an hour-when I came to I missed my purse--the prisoner was
brought into the room, and said, “Will you give me in charge ?”-l said,
" Yes, you have taken my money"-I was sober; I bad had nothing but
the rum and half-and-half-I have been ill fifteen months - this is the purse
(produced).
Prisoner. Q. Did not you take me to the public-house, give me the half-
and-balf, and ask me to mind your purse for a few minutes ? A. No.
JAMES GREEN. On 1st Jan. I was at the King William public-house,
with two of my mates, and saw the prosecutrix and prisoner in the tap-room
--they sat down together, and had a pint of half-and-hall-we sat at the
same table-after they had the half-and-half, the prisoner put her arm round
Mrs. Bennet, and took off her bonnet-Mrs. Bennet did not appear to have
any senses—I saw the prisoner put a shawl over Mrs. Bennet's dress, and
take a purse out of her pocket, and put it into her own bosom-I told my
mates of it, and the prisoner made quite a laugh of it-she afterwards came
up to us, and asked if there was a back yard, where no one would interfere
with her-I thought she was going to run away with the money, and me and
another man went to the street-door to prevent her coming out-she went to
the back yard, a policeman came, and she was given in custody.
Prisoner. Q. Did I attempt to escape ? A. You could not, if you bad
-you were in the tap-room when I went in again.
HENRY BURNS (policeman, 261 R). I was called to the tap-room, the
prisoner was there, and was charged with robbing Mrs. Bennet-I asked her
to show me her money-she said she only had 1s. and two or three half-
pence-she put her hand into her pocket, shut the money up in her hand
and said she would not give it me, but to the inspector-I held her hand
till we got to the station, and she then laid 113. and 5d. before the inspector
-the prosecutrix was insensible.
Prisoner. Q. Did I not ask Bennet if I had robbed her? A. No; she
did not say you had not-she was not able to speak.
HENRY GEORGE RALPH (policeman, 367 R). I searched the yard at the
public-house, and a man with me picked up this purse under the bottle-rack,
and gave it to me it was empty.
Prisoner's Defence. I asked the prosecutrix to stand some gin, and she
said, “Come along with me," and took me to this public-house--she looked
at the men there and asked me to mind her purse a few minutes-I asked
where it was, she said, “In my pocket" and I took it from her to mind it.
