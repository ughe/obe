I went with my husband once into Mr. Maudsley's place. We went to see the models. Patterson showed us over the premises. We had some ale there—I know some keys were made, two lots, and I believe that Baker had them with him when he was taken at Slough As the job did not come off you can do nothing with it. You can do nothing with Patterson—Patterson and Baker did come to my house several times—I intended to wait until the job was done, and if a reward was offered, to give informa  tion—lid you expect to find the keys when you reached Patterson's house?" I said "Yes, I suppose they did. I was not there myself." She said, "Ah! clever men never keep those things in their own houses"—I said, well! if the affair had taken place she would not have got so much as she thought, perhaps. She said, "If it had been done at the right time, there would have been a good lot there, and they did not mean to do it except when there was plenty there. Sometimes it's more than at others. I will think the matter over, and perhaps I'll tell you more about it. I'll wait and see what Arthur does."
   Cross-examined by Arthur Workman.  This conversation was on the evening of the 23rd May, the day preceding that on which I saw you at Wandsworth.
   Cross-examined by Emma Workman.  I did not ask you to go to Mr. Wontner—as a fact he would not be engaged in the case then—when I saw you at the Orange Tree I asked you if you had reconsidered your determination, and whether you would become a witness, and you declined—I was then unaware what steps would be taken—you asked me if the keys were in the bag found on Baker at Slough—I said, "No, there were no keys of that description"—I said, "There is a sovereign in my pocket for you"—that had no reference to this case—you said, "Those keys, if we could find them, would be worth a thousand pounds," and asked me how much I would give for them—I did not say, "I will give you 3 l . for them." WILLIAM CHAMBERLAIN( Inspector L ). I have been present and have heard Hunt's statements as to these conversations, and they are sub stantially correct—I took Patterson in custody on the 22nd May, in Kennington Road—I said, "I am a police officer, and hold a warrant for your apprehension for being concerned with others in conspiring to break into your master's premises, Maudsley's"—he said, "I know nothing about it; who are the others?"—I said, "A man named Davis or Jones, and another named Harding, who were taken in custody at Slough some weeks ago for robbery"—Mrs. Workman made a statement to me after she was in custody—she said, "Mr. Chamberlain, this is a very bad job"—I said, "Yes, it is"—she said, "I wish I had told you and Mr. Hunt the truth the other night when I saw you at the Orange Tree"—I said, "Why didn't you?"—she said, "I hardly knew what to say about it then, I have been in the place ( meaning Maudsley's ) with my husband. Patterson took him and me in. They didn't allow me to go over the place with them. They shut me in a room whilst they went and done what they wanted"—I said, "Why did they do that?"—she said, "Patterson was afraid that the persons in the next house might see a woman in the house, which would cause some suspicion."
