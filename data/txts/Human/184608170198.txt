 1702. THOMAS CHURCH and JOHN BROWN were indicted for steal ing 1 watch, value 1 l  ., the goods of Sarah Smith . SARAH SMITH. I am a widow, and live at Leytonstone. On the 11th of July I left my watch in the parlour about one or two o'clock—I was absent from the room about five minutes—when I came back the watch was gone—the window was open—this is my watch. WILLIAM FRAZER. On the 11th of July, between one and two o'clock, I saw the two prisoners near the chapel pump, at Leytonstone—I saw Brown come out of Mrs. Smith's gate—Church was on the other side of the road—he could see Mrs. Smith's house—Brown took something out of his pocket, and showed it to Church, and then they went away—I described the boys.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Had Brown any poultry with him? A.  No—he had no basket—you go through a gate to the house. SAMUEL BUTLER BAKER. I am an engineer. I received information of this loss, and a description of the boys from Frazer—I went across the forest, and saw the prisoners—I followed them a short distance—I went and found this watch in a spot near to where I saw the prisoners. RICHARD LAMB. I saw the two prisoners together after this loss—they were sitting down at a corner near where the watch was found—I saw then about an hour and a half after the robbery, and the watch was found within two hours.
   CHURCH  — GUILTY. Aged 17.
   BROWN  — GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Three Months,
