   COURT to THOMAS WOOD . Q.  Had you two farthings and one halfpenny in your pocket that evening? A.  I cannot tell; I had them in the morning, when I was sober.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  The pot-boy says you told him that you thought it was him that came and asked for your watch? A.  Yes; I thought so—I had heard him speak before many a time—I thought it was his voice, and I asked him the next day where he had put it—the expression he made use of was, "Wood, let me take your watch, and take care of it"—I am sure he used my name of Wood—the pot-boy knew my name—the prisoner might know my name. HENRY STIREY. I manage the business at the Three Mariner's. I saw the prisoner there on the night that the prosecutor was there—after the persons had all been turned out, the prisoner came to the bar again—I went in to arouse the prosecutor—he used to sleep in the house occasionally—he afterwards said he had lost his watch. JOHN MILES( police-constable K  178.) I took the prisoner when he was brought by wood and the pot-boy—I said "You are charged with stealing this young man's watch"—he positively denied it, and pulled out these two farthings and this halfpenny. NOT GUILTY.
