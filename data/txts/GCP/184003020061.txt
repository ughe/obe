Mr. Bodkin. Q. Was the bill for the coals brought there that evening
by Mr. Morris? A. I do not know who brought it-it was produced that
evening-I do not know when it was delivered I saw nothing of it before

JAMES TORRINGTON. I am in the employ of Capel and Slaughter, coal-
merchants, London--they lighter coals for Mr. Morris. On the 10th of
December I superintended the lightering of some coals into the Mouse
barge--this is the book in which I make a memorandum of my daily
transactions-producing it)--the entry on the 10th of December is, "Mouse,
21 tons, Evenwood, Sun-wharf, from the Witham " the number of the barge
is not put the meaning of that entry is, that the coals went to Sun-
wharf that day by the Mouse-there was, no doubt, a certificate given for
them in the course of business-that certificate would be sent to Mr.
Morris--I did not see the ship's certificate -I did not see any Belmont
coals in the river on the 10th.
Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You do not know whether there
were any or not? 4. No-I have known Mr. Morris about three years, or
three years and a half-he has been doing business in a respectable way
as a merchant, as far as I know-I never heard any thing against him -I
have known him transact a great deal of business-I believe he always
bore the character of an honest, respectable man-Mr. Capel has been em-
ployed by him a good deal-I have not had any promise made me with
respect to my evidence- this book is my own private memorandum
Mr. Morris knew nothing about it.
- ALFRED HANCOCK. I am one of the meters appointed by the Coal
Committee. On the 10th of December last the Mouse and Edward
barges were laden with Evenwood Wallsend coals out of the Witham-1
gave certificates to that effect to the lighterman, on being applied to I
gave one certificate with each barge—these are them-(looking at them)
I gave them to the lighterman who took the craft.
WILLIAM Vale. I am a clerk in the Coal-meter's office, Coal Ex-
change---a committee of coal-factors and merchants have the management
of the office--Mr. John Bentley is one of the committee-- there are seven-
teen others--they provide books, with engraved forms of certificates, to be
given to the meters whom they appoint.
Cross-examined by Mr. ADOLPHUS. Q. Has Mr. Bentley been one of
the committee long? A. Ever since the committee was established-he
has not given any directions about this prosecution that I am aware of I
understand the prosecution is conducted by our office, but who gave the
instructions I do not know there have been meetings of the committee,
but I have not been present, and do not know what they did I ain not
aware that these certificates are given by other sellers-each book contain-
ing ninety-three of these certificates costs 88. 4d.
GEORGE LOCKETT. I am clerk to Edward Wood, a coal-merchant at
Kentish-town-wharf. On the 9th of December Evenwood coals were
218. 3d. in the market that is the price I bought them at-I tried to get
them as cheap as I could-Belmont coals were not in the market that day
-Evenwood are about 2s.6d. a ton inferior to Belmont.
