WILLIAM HENRY PALMER. I am a bricklayer, living in John-street-
I knew the deceased. On the Saturday night before she died, about half-past
nine o'clock, I was standing at my own door, and heard a noise from the
court where the prisoner lived—I went to the house, and found the prisoner
and his wife quarrelling--the wife said to me, “For God's sake, take
him away; for I am afraid he will kill me," or something to that effect-I
went in on two occasions--the first time I went in by the door, nobody was
thare then but the prisoner and his wife-I remained about five minutes that
time-I said to the prisoner, “What is all this noise about?"-he said, "Oh,
it is all right, old fellow !"-I said, “Well, put on your jacket, and come
out with me; what is the use of quarrelling ?--he said, "No, I will go up
stairs to bed"-I then came out-I went again from a quarter to half-an-
hour afterwards, because there was a second disturbance the door was shut
then, and I got in by the window-I then saw the prisoner, the deceased,
and the last witness in the room I was there scarcely a minute that time
the son came in, and directly he came in I came out the deceased seemed
in a dreadfully agitated state, the first time I went in; and so did the
prisoner,

no further than saying, "Take him away"--he appeared to have been drink-
ing too much, and was in a very excited state,
