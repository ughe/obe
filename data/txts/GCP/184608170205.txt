Messrs. BALLANTINE and CHARNOCK conducted the Prosecution.
1715. JOHN WICKS was indicted for embezzlement.
SARAH WATTS. I am the wife of Tbomas Watts, of the Yorkshire Grey
public-house, Park-street, Islington-I ordered some coals of the prisoner,
- I cannot say whether I was present on 13th Jan. when an account was
paid-I paid this bill of 41. 68. on 14th April to the prisoner, and be
wrote this receipt, also this bill of 31. 88., on 15th May-both these receipts
were signed by the prisoner in my presence.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You did not order the coals of
Mr. Pegg? A. No, of the prisoner-I knew nothing of Mr. Pegg in the
transaction on either of the occasions-I have dealt with the prisoner since
he is now in the service of Mr. Wright.
Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you take the first quantity of coals on his own
application ? A. Yes, to my husband - I do not recollect hearing it made
-I thought I was dealing with him--the bills are in Mr. Pegg's name
I thought it was on commission.

NATHANIEL PEGg. I am a coal-merchant at Phoenix-wharf, Southwark.
the prisoner has been in my employ several years-it was his duty to deliver
bills and to collect money-he did that in his capacity as my servant--he
had to keep a petty cash book--this is it-it was his duty to enter in this
book any sums of money he received on my account he was to pay them
over at the end of the day, and balance the gross receipts at the end of the
week--the book was the cheque I had on him in balancing-I paid him 30s.
a week-he had no claim for commission-he was in the habit of making &
charge for beer and other disbursements--they are in this petty weekly dis-
bursement book-at the end of the week I looked at the disbursements,
seeing the sums he received, and he accounted to me-I know his hand-
writing I have looked at this bill of 13th Jan. 1846—the receipt is in his
handwriting-here is entered in the petty cash-book-"On 13th Jan. 21. 68.
as received from Watts"-the bill was 21. 10s.--in another petty cash-book
here is entered, "On 14th April, Watts, Park-street, 41."-the bill is 41.68.
- he has not accounted for anything in the cash-book on 15th of May-it ap-
pears, by the cash book, that there was a settlement at the end of these re-
spective weeks--the book is balanced-here is the entry of 13th Jan.-there
are disbursements that week--they are carried out of the little petty disburse-
ment book into the gross amount at the end of the week-that is all settled,
according to the entries in the book-the 15th of May is the last time he
was in my employ-he also kept a wharf-book-the clerk could not make out
the bill without the price-he would have to inquire of the prisoner the price
of the coals if it was not entered-when he put the price it should be the
correct price at which they were sold—there is a price in this instance--the
entry in Jan. states, two tons at 238.-there is the same entry in the petty
cash-book--that is not the price at which the bill is made out-the clerk
would make out the bill according to the price in the book-by holding the
book up, it plainly appears in the book that he has written one price over the
original price, in which the darker ink predominates.

discovered it the very day I obtained a warrant against him for a previous
case about three weeks after he left-he left on the 16th of May-1 applied
for the warrant on the 13th of June--there was more than one examination
-he was held to bail-I did not discover the erasure till all the examina-
