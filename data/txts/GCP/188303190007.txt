WILLIAM MILLER. I am a portmanteau maker, at 49, Waterloo Road
-the prisoner came into my shop on the afternoon of 13th February
and bought a portmanteau for 308.-he said he had a post-office order,
but it was too late to get it cashed, and would I take it this is the
order, it purported to be for 41. 18. 9d. -he also wanted a little cash to
go with till the morning-I let him have 10.-he signed this "Alfred
O'Leara" in my presence-he said I was to leave the balance at 66,
York Road; he did not mention any name; and if he was not at home
he would call on me for it-o such person was known there he did
not call for the balance-I went to the post-office at Westminster Bridge
Road and found it was a forgery, and then communicated with the
police-I had done business with the prisoner before I saw him again
in about three weeks, about 2nd or 3rd March-he sent a man to my
shop to say a gentleman wanted to see me I went and saw the prisoner
on the pavement-he told me that he was very sorry for what he had
done, and he offered me the 10s., and also to return the portmanteau
ho understood there was a warrant out for him, and asked me if that
Vi 80-in the meantime I communicated with the police and gave him
in charge the police have since brought the portmanteau to me.
RICHARD MACEY (Detective Sergeant L). I received information from
Mr. Miller on 14th February, and on 2nd March I saw the prisoner in
custody at the police-station at Kennington Road-I told him he would
be charged with forging and uttering a post-office order for 41. 10. 9d.
to Mr. Miller, Waterloo Road, on 13th of last month he made a state-
ment which I took down in writing-when charged he said. "I did not
Sorge the order, but when the order came to me by post it was for
41. 11. 9d. I received it from a friend named St. James " (that is the
name of the sender on the requisition) " for a gold watoh he had from
me. I do not know where he lives "-he told me that he was a barman in
Bmithfield—I searched him and found on him a number of letters, and
upon those letters an address 6, Tennyson Street, York Road-I went
there and found he was living there in the top floor front room in the
name of Monté-I found a portmanteau there, which I showed to Mr.
Miller, who identified it-it is about a half or three-quarters of a milo
from the West Strand Post-office to Westminster Bridge Road.
