265. ANN BURLEY was indicted for stealing 6 spoons, value 21. ;
2 forks, 11. 38. ; 2 petticoats, 35. ; 2 handkerchiefs, 58.; 1 sheet, 103. ; 1 frock,
10s. ; 1 apron, 18. ; and 1 table-cloth, 6s. ; the goods of Ann Oakey, her mis-
tress.
Ann OAKEY. I live in Lisson-grove, and am a widow. The prisoner lived
servant with me from the 4th of August last-I missed a silver tea-spoon, and
after that a silk gown, and several other things she left me on the 11th of
Nov., without giving me notice-I then made a search, and missed my table-
spoons, forks, and several other things-1 gave information to the police.
CHARLES BENNETT (police-sergeant D 18.) I went to Newnham-street to
look for the prisoner-I did not find her there, but I searched a box there &
person, named Cook, keeps the house-Cook is not here I found there two
duplica 98-I afterwards found the prisoner at Cherrington, in Warwickshire,
at her mother's-1 told her I wanted her for robbing her mistress, Mrs.
Oakey, of Lisson-grove—she sat down, and said, “Well, I know I am guilty"
- she afterwards gave me up twenty-four duplicates, and said if Mrs. Oakey
had paid her the money she ought to have done, it would not have taken place
- I told her I had found some of the property-I brought her to London.
WILLIAM DAGLEISH. I live with Mr. Smith, 4 pawnbroker, in Edgware-
road. I produce a dessert-spoon, a table-spoon, a table-fork, a dessert-fork,
and three tea-spoons—the dessert-fork and tea-spoons were pawned on the
11th of Nov.- the prisoner is very much like the person who pawned them,
but I should not like to swear to her—these are the duplicates of them.
CHARLES BENNETT re-examined. The duplicates of these articles were
found in the lodging in Newnbam-street- I told the prisoner I found two
duplicates in Newnham-street, and I had been and seen the spoons, and her
mistress identified them- she did not take any notice.
GEORGE YOUNG. I live with Mr. Tomlinson, a pawnbroker. I have a
handkerchief pawned on the 13th of Aug., in the name of Ann Brown-I
have also a sheet, a handkerchief, a gown, and petticoat--I took in the gown
and petticoat of the prisoner.
CHARLES BENNETT re-examined. I have five duplicates corresponding with
this property-they were amongst the twenty-four the prisoner gave me.
JAMES MILLS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a tea-spoon pawned on the 22nd
of Sept., in the name of Ann Chapman.
SAMUEL HENRY BEZANT. I am a pawnbroker. I have a table-fork, an
apron, and petticoat-these are the duplicates of them.
CHARLES BENNETT re-eramined. I found the duplicates of these articles
amongst the twenty-four.
ANN OAKBY re-examined. I know the whole of these things to be mine-I
am not aware that the prisoner was short of money when she was with me
she received 11. 58. for rent from one of my lodgers, unknown to me.
GUILTY. Aged 82.-Confined Nine Months.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
