blanket, the goods of Richard Grimshaw,; in incurring debts of
£1 109. to Lucy Sargent, £1 17s. 6d. to Emily Grimshaw, £3 5s. 9d.
to Margaretta Éager, and 189. to Marion Constance Blake, obtaining

Mr. Wimpfheimer prosecuted.
MARION CONSTANCK BLAKE, 22, Queensdale Road, Holland Park,
spinster, boarding-house keeper. In June, 1908, prisoner took a
room at my house, agreeing to pay 30s. & week for board and lodging.
She gave me a name which I have forgotten, which was not Dennis.
After staying four days I found her room empty at 6.30 a.m., and
that she had gone, taking her two bags. She loft a letter, which I
have lost, saying she was going to the City. She said she was ill,
and had had a fire and her meals in her room.
EXILY GRIMSHAW, 4, Elgin Crescent, Bayswater, wife of Richard
Grimshaw. In July, 1908, I lived at 98, Vineyard Hill, Wimbledon
Park, and had put an advertisement in the Daily Telegraph " for a
paying guest. Prisoner oame in the name of " Mrs. O'Connor," and
agreed to pay 258. a week. She said she was the widow of an Irish
doctor, that she had estates in Ireland, that her money was paid to
her by trustees, that she had been travelling, had just come from
Bournemouth, so could not give references. After staying a week I
gave her her bill for 20s., and she said she could not pay me until
she had received her cheque from the trustees, which would be the
following Friday. On the following Friday we were disturbed about
3am. by hearing a door shut, and at 7.30 a.m. I found she had left.
She brought a black bag and a straw hamper, which were also gone.
I next saw her at West London Police Court. She owes me 37s.6d.
I am sure it was the prisoner.
LUCY BARGENT, 185, Holland Road, Kensington, boarding-house
keeper. On Sunday, February 22, 1909, in answer to my advertise-
ment, prisoner, in the name of Mrs. Kelly, took lodgings and board
with me at 30s. & week. She came in the next day and remained till
the following Sunday, when at 7 a.m. she left with her luggage. She
said she was ill, and had meals and fire in her room.

wait until Tuesday, as my allowance will be paid to me then." She
said it was paid to her by a solicitor in the City. On the following
Priday, at about 5 a.m., she had left owing me £3 5s. 9d. She had
