   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do persons who steal horses know something about persons who receive them? A.  Yes—I found the pony on the pri soner's premises—he said the cart came from a grocer's at Bushy—I took it to the Ram Inn, and then to Gray's-inn-lane—I bought the carts from what had passed between me and Pye, for the purpose of bringing the prisoner to justice—Douglas, Showell, and the prisoner were all ac quainted. JOSEPH RINGER. On the 8th of January, or on the morning of the 9th, I lost a cart, horse, and harness—I have seen the cart and harness since. JOHN ALWEN. On the night of the 6th, or the morning of the 7th of January, a cart was stolen from my shed, at Cudham, in Kent—I have seen the body of the cart since.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The place in White's-row I let to a man named Mitchell, who said he kept a cab, and was a horse-dealer—I did not go to the premises after I let them to him—I took the premises in Brown-lane, and this property was there—I kept a stable, to take horses, carts, and such things, to stand; and this pony was brought to stand there. WILLIAM HUGHES. I am a paper-stainer. I carry on business in part of Mr. Stratford's premises—I know that a painter hat part of the same premises—I have heard that others have part—I have seen people leave carts, and fruit, and vegetables there, and horses on market days—I have seen two or three brought there, and some remaining.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Are you a master? A.  Yes; I and my brother in-law, and two sons of mine work there—I live in Greenfield-street, which is about five minutes walk from there—I do not know Showell by name—I was never in Court before.
 (James Marshall and Thomas Richard Moore gave the prisoner a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 33.— Transported for Fourteen Years.
