WILLIAM ADDINGTON. I live at 1, Spring Gardens, Vauxhall-I was
in the employ of Mr. Beckett, a confectioner, who has stables in Cardington
Street, opposite the prosecutor on the Saturday evening, before I went
to the police-court, between 6 and 7 o'clock, I was up in the loft at Mr.
Beckett's--I saw the prisoner in the street, he asked if I would jump
over and open the gate for him I went down, he gave me a lift over,
and I opened the gate-he walked inside and said "Don't go away for a
while, if I see you about I will give you the price of a pot when I come
out"-I saw Carlisle about ten or fifteen minutes afterwards, and spoke to
hin-I believe the prisoner was then in the place-I did not see him after
I let him in. 33
John CARLISLE. I am in the prosecutor's employ as stable-boy-about 7
d'clock on Saturday evening, 6th January, I was in Cardington Street,
Addington spoke to me, I went down the street to Young's man, and waited
in the street till Bucban Sands came he went and fetched bis brother
Alfred—I did not go into the promises then-about 9.15 I was just inside
the gates ; I had the key-I was lighting a match to take the pony into the
stable, when the prisoner camo up-- he said “Don't go away, I shan't be a
minute"-I said " All right”-he went in the direction of the shed where
the ink was -- 1 put the pony in as quickly as I could, came out and locked
the gates-I went to Buchan Sands, and then to Alfred Sands and returned
with him, and the gates were then wide open, not the gates in Cardington
Street, that I had locked, but the other gates in Kennington Lane ; they are
corner premises-those gates were safe at 2 o'clock-I did not notice them
at the time I locked the other gates; I can't say whether they were closed
of not-I went all over the place with my master and found all the ink
casks broken and the ink spilt.
Cross-examined. I had been turned away from this employment by the
prisoner a month before this happened, and was afterwards taken on again
by the prosecutor and his brother before this happened I am sure I saw
the prisoner there at 9.15-a dog was always kept in the yard, it was
Tetched away that night, I can't say by whom, it was gone at 9.30--I had
not seen it since 2 o'clock that afternoon-I heard my master say it was safe
at 9 o'clock, it was a watch dog.
GEORGE BALDOCK (Detective Officer). I apprehended the prisoner on 11th
Manuary, at Liverpool, he was detained by the police there I read the
warrant to him-he said “They will have to prove that I did the damage"
I received this pistol and a box of cartridges from the police in his presence.
