 1861.  JOHN JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 necklace, value 5s., the goods of Thomas Garnish , from the person of Sarah Garnish.
   MARY GARNISH. I am the wife of Thomas Garnishwe live in West-street, Soho; he is a painter and glazier. My daughter, Sarah, is only three years old; she was taken out by Mary Ball, with a coral necklace on, about half-past eleven o'clock - she was brought home about one without it; we found it at Bow-street - the prisoner is a stranger.
   MARY ANN BALL. I am ten years old. On the 17th of July I took out the prosecutrix's child; it had hold of my hand, and just before we came to Denmark-street, the prisoner came and took the necklace off; I cried Stop thief! and ran up the street - he turned towards Churchpassage, and then the Policeman took him; I lost sight of him for about a minute, while he turned the corner, but am sure of his person - there were two others with him, who ran another away.
   THOMAS CANNON. I am a Policeman, I heard a cry of Stop thief! and saw Ball pursue the prisoner up Denmark-street; I kept him in sight - he turned round Lloyd's-court, and there I saw him throw something into an area; I ran, and secured him - Lish stopped to pick up what was in the area; the necklace was produced, and Bill claimed it.
   CHARLES LISH. I am a Policeman. I heard the girl cry Stop thief! and saw the prisoner throw the necklace down the area; I got it up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in Crown-street, and saw three children walking together - two young men passed them at the same time as I; there was a cry of Stop thief! a young man ran by me; I ran after him the same as the rest.
   THOMAS CANNON. There was nobody between the girl and the prisoner.
 CHARLES LISH. Nobody else was running in that direction.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Life.

 1862.  WILLIAM JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 2 pairs of shoes, value 18s., the goods of William Marsh.
   JOHN BOTSFORD. I am master of the Infant-school, Baldwin's-gardens. On the 13th of August I saw the prisoner coming from Mr. Marsh's house opposite, with something under his apron; I informed Mr. Marsh, who I had seen go into a neighbour's house a minute before.
   WILLIAM MARSH. I keep a shoemaker's shop, in Baldwin's-gardens. Eastford gave me information; I saw the prisoner about twenty yards off - he dropped something, and when I came up he said, "There are your shoes, I hope you will not prosecute me;" I took them up, and took him to the office.
   ANDREW LLOYD. I am an officer. I asked the prisoner why he took the shoes; he said it was wholly through distress.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1863.  ELIZA JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 1 watch, value 20s., the goods of Robert Snell , from his person.
 ROBERT SNELL. I live in Red Lion-street, Holborn. On the 18th of August, about one o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner about  Holborn-bars; she followed me from there to the end of Bedford-row, and immediately after she left me, I missed my watch - she had been talking to me, but I went no where with her; I am certain she is the woman - I had spent the evening with a relation in the City.
 ROBERT WOOD. I am an officer of St. Martin, Lndgate. On the 18th of August, about three o'clock in the morning, the prisoner was brought into my custody on another charge, and the watch was found on her; I asked who it belonged to; she said herself; that she had had it about three years and a-half, and it had belonged to her brother, who was dead.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put it in a written Defence, stating that a young man who had gone with her into a stable, had left his watch with her till the morning.
   ROBERT SNELL. Upon my oath, I went to no stable with her, nor did I give her the watch for any purpose.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Life.
 1864.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 sovereign, 9 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of John Willson , from his person.
 JOHN WILLSON. I am a  hair-dresser, and live at South Ockenden, Essex. On the 29th of August I came from Purfleet, in a steam-boat; the prisoner was on board with another man and woman - after we landed we went to the Black Horse, near Tower-Hill; the prisoner proposed to play a game; thimbles were placed on the table; I changed half a sovereign there - I then had a sovereign and 9s. 6d.; I left the room to go into the yard, and as I came back, I met the prisoner and the other man - the other man seized my throat - the prisoner put his hand into my waistcoat pocket, and the other hand in my trousers pocket, and they took from me all my money and a key; two soldiers came in, and the prisoner was secured - the other man ran away; I missed the sovereign from my waistcoat pocket, and the 9s. 6d. from my trousers.
 Prisoner. Q. Do you remember ringing the bell for the waiter asking him to fetch a constable, and charging me with stealing half a sovereign? A. After taking a pint of ale, I was dozing, and found the prisoner about my person, but not, at the moment, recollecting changing the half-sovereign, I said he had taken it - they talked of sending for a constable, not me.
