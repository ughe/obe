EDWIN BOWLEY. I am a cashier in the Liverpool London and Globe In-
surance Company, Cornhill-I have a policy dated 9th April, 1868, for
5001., effected in the name of Samuel Cohen & Son--I also produce a claim
for 5951. 108. 6d., made by Samuel Cohen alone--I do not observe any
date on it; but it must have been about 27th June, 1869--there was
some correspondence on the subject, and ultimately, on 15th June, the
claim was settled by this cheque for 2701., payable to Messrs. Samuel
Cohen & Sons, or order, with the salvage--the cheque is endorsed
"Samuel Cohen & Son" and "George Davis "--the son endorsed it,
“Samuel Cohen & Son," in my presence-I produce a receipt for the
cheque signed “Samuel Cohen & Son," dated 16th July, 1869--that was
signed by the father first-the son wrote the lower one.
FREDERIOK BAKER KIRBY. I am assistant manager of the City Bank,
Tottenham Court Road branch on 16th July, 1869, a person called at
the bank, representing himself to be George Davis--I produce the signa-
ture book of the bank he wrote this "George Davis," spelling the name
Davis without an "e"-he opened an account with this cheque for 2704
--the name “George Davis" was endorsed on it at the time it was placed
in our hands-I afterwards saw that person in the Lord Mayor's Court,
in London, and heard him sworn.
EDWARD WILLIAM Pass. I am a cashier at the Tottenham Court Road
branch of the City Bank-I produce a cheque for 501., signed, "George
Davies "--that was presented on 17th July by the prisoner--I refused
to pay it, the name of Davis being spelt wrong-I asked the prisoner
where he got the cheque from-he said he got it from Mr. Davis-I
told him I should very much like to see Mr. Davis-he went away, and
returned in about a quarter of an hour with the person who called
himself Mr. Davis--- it was the prisoner's son-I was asked why I refused
to pay the cheque-I said, “ Because he did not appear to know his own
name"-he then said he would draw his account out of the bank altogether
-I supplied him with a form, and he drew it out I have an entry in my
cash-book of what I paid him-I made the entry-I gave him two 1007.
notes, Nos. 40,435 and 28,929, a 501. note, No. 57,608, and a 201. note,
No. 16,264, all Bank of England notes of various dates.
Thomas Dry. I am assistant to Mr. Harrison, a pawnbroker, of Wardour
Street, Soho-I know the prisoner and his son, Harris Samuel Cohen-on
17th July last the son came and took & watch out which was in pawn for
151.- I can't swear to this 501. note being the note he gave me; I can
only swear to its being paid away by myself - I believe he did give me a
501. note-I am not sure of that even-I can't remember what change I
gave him-I can't say whether I received this 501. note on that day or not
-it bears my indorsement-I put that on it afterwards--I circulated it on
Tuesday, 20th July, I do not remember taking a 501. note from anybody
else--I have tried to remember.
