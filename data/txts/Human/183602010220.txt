EDWARD BELL. I lodge in the prisoner's house. I believe she is a married woman—her husband's name is Henry Fish he was living with her at this time as her husband I do not know how long he had lived with her—he slept in the same room with her—I believe he was gone out that day to get some things for the shop—I went out between eight and nine o'clock that morning—I do not know whether he had been in the room that morning—I came home about half-past eleven o'clock, and in coming through the alley heard the alarm of fire—I ran home, and met the prisoner on the stair-case, she said, "Oh! my God, the house is on fire"—she was on the middle of the stair-case—I asked her where the fire was—she said she did not know—she was hallooing out"I am ruined! I am ruined!"—I said, "Never mind, come along with me "—she went up into the garret with me, and into the back room, but could see nothing of it—I then came down into the street, and saw the window-curtains on fire—I ran up again—she was standing at the bottom of the stairs—I asked her where there was a hammer—she said she had not one—I went up-stairs, and tried to force the door open two or three times—I then went down into the shop and got a spade—I came up, and the policeman had just burst the door open—I had asked her for the key, and she said, "Henry has got it, "mean ing her husband—I asked her for a pail; she said she had not one—the policeman burst the door open—I did not go into the room—I just went to the door and saw the bed-curtains all on fire—I ran down stairs after pail—I asked her for some water—she said she had not—I looked into the butt and there was none—I ran to a neighbour's, got a pail of water, and brought it up—the policeman was doubling the curtains up and I three the water on them—the wainscoating was on fire quite red hot—it was not blazing—the wainscoating and window-ledge were red-hot for about three quarters of an inch deep but not blazing—I kicked as much as I could off it with my feet—the wainscoating had been on fire—it was red-hot when I saw it, but not in a blaze—the door of a cupboard close to the fire was open, and there was a piece of burnt rag in the cupboard—there were a great many rags in that cupboard, but not many of them on fire—it was burning tinder—"Henry Fish"was written over the door—I am sure of that—not "I Fisk"—anybody who could read must see that. HANNAH WOOD. I heard the alarm of fire about half-past eleven—I ran into the house to help a young woman out had been lately confined—I saw the prisoner—I have known her these two years—she is married—they go by the name of Mr. and Mrs. Fisk—I saw her at the bottom of the stairs—she asked where I was going—I said I was going up to help her lodger out, as her place was on fire—the prisoner was screaming on the stairs terribly—she gave me the key of a door, but it was the wrong key—she said her husband had the key—I went into the room, but the fire was all put out then—the cupboard was open a little—I pulled out a little rubbish—there was something mothered in the cupboard.
