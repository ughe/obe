THOMAS HALES. I held the horse. I saw the coat on it—the pri soner came out and asked me to let him have a ride—I told him he had better not, but he rode it five or six minutes—I ran by the side, and picked up a stick, and gave it to him—when I saw he was returning, I came back, and he said, "There is the horse," but the coat was gone then.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you see how far he rode? A.  About half a quarter of a mile—I did not go and look for the coat on the road—I am sure I had seen a coat there, it was tied with two pieces of rope—the horse was outside the door two or three minutes, without any one with it. DANIEL KINGSNORTH. On the 29th of October, Read sent for me to take the prisoner—there was the rope, and the loop in front of the saddle had been cut—I took the prisoner—he swore he would not go—he was with several navigators in the house—I had a desperate struggle to get him out—I was thrown down, and was obliged to call other people.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you say one word before the Magistrate about his swearing be would not go, and about the navigators? A.  Yes—I told the Magistrates all the particulars—I believe they took them down—he laid the coat was safe when be brought the horse back. THOMAS HALES re-examined.
