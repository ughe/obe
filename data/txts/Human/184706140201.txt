   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  At that time you had your money? A.  I had 191 l .—I went before the Magistrate with Jenner; I saw the clerk writing—I cannot tell whether he wrote down what I said—I forget whether what I said was read over to me—I first saw Leary on the 26th of April—Wyatt was not with him then—Leary said nothing to me on the 26th—I saw him alone at various times in the course of the 27th of April—I first saw him with Wyatt on that evening—I met them in the Borough—we went to a public-house, where the money was returned—I did not see Leary after wards till he was given into custody.
   COURT . Q.  Were both the prisoners present when that expression was used, "If you go in the country in the morning you shall have every shil ling?"— A.  Yes, both.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  Were you absent from any examination? A.  Yes; on the following day—I live with my father-in-law, a publican, he farms a little land—it was his check—it did not belong to me at all—I am twenty-three years old.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Where was the money returned? A.  At a public-house in the Borough—I do not know what house—the money was returned in a bag, which was tied with a string—Wyatt went out to get it, and brought it tied—after I had got the money, he said I had better go away as soon as I could, for fear I should lose it again—I did not see Wyatt again while a I was in town—he came down to my house in the country—he was not in the public-house where I was robbed—I did not see him till I met him at the time he brought back the money—I knew that Jenner's attorney was a person named Roberts—I had seen Roberts attending for Jenner at the Police-court—when Wyatt-came down in the country and mentioned Jenner's attorney, I knew to whom he alluded—Wyatt told me, that Jenner's attorney had told him, that if the letter which contained the summons was not directed right, I need not attend to it, but might re-seal it and send it back. HENRY BARRY( police-constable M  171.) I took Leary into custody in Martin-street, Union-street, Blackfriars-road, which is where he lives—I told him he was charged with being concerned with Jenner and Wyatt in com promising a felony—he said he done  it to get a few shillings for himself—he did not say with whom he went.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Is Mr. Woodhouse a sergeant of your division? A.  Yes—Mr. Cottingham, the Magistrate, directed me to take Leary—I know Mr. Robert's by seeing him attend the Police-court, that is all—I never had any communication with him about this matter—Mr. Cottingham ordered me to get a summons, and write a letter into the country, which I did—that was given out in open Court, Roberts might have heard it—I and Sergeant Woodhouse were at the station when Wyatt was brought there—I did not see Roberts there—I did not see him in company with Wyatt.
   MR. PARNELL . Q.  When was this given out in Court? A.  On the 28th of April—Jenner was before the Magistrate, the prosecutor did not attend, and the Magistrate said, "You had better write a letter, and enclose a sum mons," which I did.
