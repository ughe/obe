 1635.  MATTHEW BRYAN and ROBERT ISAACS were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 5 lbs. of cheese, value 2s. 6d.; - lb. of snuff, value 2s. 6d., and I jar, value 1s., the goods of Elizabeth White.
   ELIZABETH WHITE. I am a widow, and keep a shop in Joseph-street, St. Pancras. On the 10th of June I lost these things.
   THOMAS GOODYER. I am an officer. On the 10th of June, about ten o'clock in the evening, I was going up the back of Cromer-street, and saw the prisoners; Isaacs had this cheese under his arm - I said "What have you there?" Bryan ran off - Isaacs threw the cheese over some rails, but I made him fetch it back again - he then said "It is Mrs. White's - Bryan got it out, and then went and got the snuff" - I then went and took Bryan.
 BRYAN -    GUILTY. Aged 12.
 ISAACS -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharged.
 1636.  WILLIAM BUSHER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of June , 1 jacket, value 5s., the goods of Thomas Day.
 THOMAS DAY. I lost my jacket from on board the  brig Argo, in the West India-docks, on the 16th of June - I had seen it safe that day, on the round-house.
   HENRY KING. I am a labourer in the dock. On the 16th of June I was working on board the Argo; at twelve o'clock I went to dinner: I returned in twenty minutes, and saw the jacket on the round-house - I saw the prisoner lurking about the ship; I went a short distance, and then saw him with a jacket under his arm: I went on board, and saw the prosecutor, who said he had lost his jacket - I went and took the prisoner with it.
   ROBERT TAYLOR. I am a constable, at the West India-docks. I took the prisoner; he was a stranger.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met a shipmate, who told me of a free-trader in the docks - I went to inquire about it, but she had got her hands; I then walked through the dock, and being warm I pulled off my jacket - I saw a lad on board this ship, and asked him for some water; I laid down my jacket, and got the water, and while doing that my jacket was taken, and another put in its place.
 ROBERT TAYLOR. I found no other jacket on board the ship.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1637.  WILLIAM HOWLISTON was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM PRYKE. I am a baker. The prisoner was in my service from the 25th of March till the 9th of June, when I discharged him; he took out bread and was authorised to receive the money - I gave him the bills on a Monday morning; it was his duty to pay me the money he had received in the afternoon or evening, when he booked his bread.
   RACHAEL SEARLES. I took bread of the prosecutor. The prisoner brought it; I paid him 1l. 5s. 6d., on the 7th or 8th of June: I have his receipt.
   JEFFERY FRIEND. I dealt with the prosecutor, and paid the prisoner 7s. 2d., on the 5th of June; he gave me this receipt.
   SARAH FARNAN. I paid the prisoner 16s. 7 1/2d., on the 7th or 8th of June; I have this receipt for it.
   THOMAS VANN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner.
 WILLIAM PRYKE. He never gave me either of these sums of money.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 29.
 Recommended to Mercy. -    Judgment Respited
 1638.  GEORGE BURSIL was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of July , 1 coat, value 1l., 1 handkerchief, value 2s., and 1 card-case, value 1s., the goods of John Pearce Prangley.
   JOHN PEARCE PRANGLEY. I live in Bedford-row, and am a solicitor. On the 4th of July I was at Chalk-farm; I was shooting at some pigeons and sparrows: I took off my coat and gave it to Mr. Greenwood - in the pockets of my coat were my handkerchief, card-case, and gloves: when I had done shooting, my coat and other things were missing - I ran in quest of the man; I found the prisoner in custody: I had not noticed him on the ground.
   JOSEPH GREENWOOD. I was at the shooting-ground. I saw the prisoner there; I gave him the coat to hold, and he walked off with it: I saw him speak to a friend, who was on the ground.
 Cross-examined by MR. THOMAS. Q. Were you aware that the prisoner was a friend of that person's? A. Yes, he has know him many years.
   GEORGE WALKER. I was minding horses at the farm. I saw the prisoner, whom I did not know, coming towards the gate, with this coat - I took him.
   EDWARD GRAY(Police-constable S 92). I took the prisoner from Walker; the prisoner had the coat on his arm: the prosecutor has the coat on.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he intoxicated? A. He had been drinking.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he was intoxicated, and that the coat was put into his hands by a person, who the prosecutor had given it to, to hold.
     NOT GUILTY.
