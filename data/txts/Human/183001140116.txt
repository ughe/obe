 487.  JAMES CLARK was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MR. ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
 GEORGE GODFREY. I am the son of the late  George Godfrey, who has died since this happened: we live in Charterhouse-street, and keep a coal-shed. On the 2nd of January the prisoner came and asked for a peck of coals -I served him; they came to 4 1/2d. - he offered me a crown-piece: I knew it to be bad, and said I had not got change- my father came forward; I said, "This man has tendered me a bad crown-piece" - he shut the door and asked where he got it; he refused to tell, but after some time he said, he took it in change from a woman whom he had slept with the night previous - I said if he wanted the coals to burn he must live in the neighbourhood: he repeatedly refused to say where he lived: and then said he had come from a place close by Edmonton, had just come to town, and had borrowed an apron to fetch the coals, but would not tell where they were to be taken - we sent for Dell, who searched him; I marked the crown, gave it to my father, who marked it, and I saw him give it to the officer; it was not out of my sight.
 JOHN DELL. I am a constable. I apprehended the prisoner, and found two good shillings on him: Godfrey, senior, marked the crown-piece in my presence and gave it to me - I have had it ever since.
   ELIZA SAYER. I am Godfrey's grand-daughter. The prisoner came into the shop on the 1st of January, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, for four bundles of wood and gave me half a crown; I gave him change, and put the half-crown in front of the drawer, which is about three feet long - there were then two sixpence and two shillings in the drawer; there was one half-crown besides, but that was at the further end of the drawer - nobody but me had access to the drawer; my aunt sat by me, and before I shut the drawer, in consequence of what she said, I took the half-crown out and gave it her, immediatley after the prisoner was gone; it was quite at a distance from the other half-crown, and I am sure it was the one he gave me - I made a mark on it with my teeth; it was given to my grandfather, who is dead - I saw him wrap it in paper, and put it into a drawer in the bureau, which is up stairs, the bottom drawer on the right-hand - there was no other money there.
 Prisoner. Q.You told the officer I was not the person, and that it was a man of a dark complexion? A. When I first looked at him I thought he was not the person, as he was dressed quite different, and I was not close to him; I thought it was him afterwards - it was five days after when I said he was the person.
 ELIZABETH VICKRESS. I am the witness' aunt. I was in the room close to the shop, and saw the prisoner give her the half-crown: she put it in front of the drawer - I was sitting close to the drawer and thought it looked suspicious; she took it out and gave it to me - I know it to be the same as he gave her; it had a black mark on it - I told her to wrap it up and give it to her father when he came in; she put it in front of the drawer, away from the other.
   JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of counterfeit coin; this crown-piece is counterfeit, and made of white metal- no part of it is silver.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took it in change of half a sovereign the night previous. GUILTY of the single uttering only. Confined 6 Months, and to find sureties.

 491.  WILLIAM HARLETT was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of December , 30 yards of flannel, value 1l., the goods of John Goodinge.
 ANN GOODINGE. I am the wife of John Goodinge, a  linendraper, of Brick-lane, Old-street. On the 7th of December, I saw the prisoner go out of our shop with this roll of flannel, at a quarter-past six o'clock in the evening; I followed him; he dropped it when he had run about a dozen doors; I had seen his back as he went out of the shop, and as he turned by the shop window I saw his face - I did not see him stopped - a gentleman picked up the flannel and gave it me; he was brought to the shop in two or three minutes by the watchman.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Have you ever seen him before? A. I think I had the night before; I had but a short sight of him, but I followed him for three or four doors - he turned, and I saw his face distinctly by a gas-light.
   JOSEPH ALDRIDGE. I am a watchman; I was going my round at a quarter-past six o'clock, and heard the witness call Stop thief! I turned and heard her call again - in a very short time I saw the prisoner coming across John-street, with something white under his arm; he was the person she appeared to be calling after - he dropped the flannel and ran very fast - I ran across; he ran down Clarendon-street, and I followed him, calling Stop thief! all the way, and a person who keeps a little shop, ran out just in time to stop him - I was very close behind him, and never lost sight of him.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEALEY. Q.How far were you from the prosecutor's door, when you first saw the prisoner? A. It might be one hundred yards; he ran in a straight direction to the end of John-street, where he turned - that was two or three hundred yards from where he was stopped; he only turned twice, but I did not lose sight of him -I was a very short distance from him all the way.
   WILLIAM CHILD. I am a green-grocer; I heard a cry of Stop thief! I ran out and stopped the prisoner - I gave him in charge of the watchman; I did not see any thing on his person.
 Cross-examined. Q.Was the watchman near him? A. Yes.
   WILLIAM VANN. I am a broker. I was standing at the corner of Brick-lane - I saw the prisoner running with the bundle of flannel; I pursued him till he dropped it - I took it up and gave it to Mr. Goodinge.
 Cross-examined. Q.How far from the house did he drop it? A. Perhaps one hundred yards; it was dark, the gas was lighted - that part is very well lighted, it is near the gas-house; I did not see the prisoner stopped; I had not seen him before, but I met him then, and can swear to him.
 JOHN BROWN. I am the officer. The prisoner was brought to me with this flannel; I searched, and found on him, this phosphorus-box, and a duplicate of a coat, which he said he had picked up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been to Mr. Lawrence, in Clarendon-street, to fetch a box for Mr. Henderson, in Howland-street; I had but just got down stairs, when I heard Stop thief! called - I turned in the direction they were running, when I knocked against the man who took me - the watchman came up and said, I had a long coat on, and I was the thief - Mr. Brown would not allow me to send to Clarendon-street.
 JOSEPH ALDRIDGE. Clarendon-street was not mentioned then; he asked if he could send to his friend - I did not hear the name of Lawrence.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 492.  THOMAS CRAWFORD was indicted for stealing on the 9th of January , 4 lbs. weight of candles, value 2s. 4d. the goods of Joseph Little.
   JAMES NICHOLS. I am shopman to Joseph Little, of Norton-falgate, an oilman. On the 9th of January the prisoner took this paper of candles off the counter in our shop, while I turned round behind the other counter; he dropped them in the shop near the door - he had come in for a halfpenny worth of saitpetre, and I served him - two other boys then came in, and the prisoner stopped while they were served; they then made way to go out, and the prisoner turned and dropped the candles; a watchman came in, and asked it I had lost any thing - I said he had dropped this paper of candles - the prisoner ran across the road and up a court; the watchman pursued and brought him back - he said the boys who came in after him had dropped the candles, but I am certain he dropped them; the other were close to him.
   DANIEL HANK. I am a watchman. On the evening of the 9th of January I had been watching the prisoner and two others for some time - I missed them, and thought they had gone up White Lion-street; I turned, and met my partner - a person touched me on the shoulder and said,"Do you see what is going forward here?" I looked into the prosecutor's shop, and saw the three lads there - the prisoner and another went to the further end of the shop, and then two of them went to the counter - the shopman served them; I saw a brown paper bundle on the counter - the other two lads then went to the door, and the prisoner dropped this bundle from under his coat; I came round, and the other person had let the prisoner slip by him - I told my partner the prisoner had gone across the road; he went and brought him instantly - I had seen him go across the road and run down an alley; I am quite sure he is the person the parcel fell from.
   JOHN BURNESS. I am a watchman. I saw the prisoner in Mr. Little's shop, but did not see him do any thing - I saw him come out, and run across the road; I kept my eye upon him - he ran up a court, and up two pairs of stairs in a house; I followed, and brought him back - I knew him before.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went in to buy something - there were two others in the shop; a boy took the candles, and dropped them by my side.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Seven Years.
