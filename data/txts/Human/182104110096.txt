 694.  JAMES HIPP was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of March , 17 s. 6 d. the property of William Mansfield.
   WILLIAM MANSFIELD. I live in Union-street, Shadwell. The prisoner was a stranger to me. On Shrove Tuesday I was employed by Mr. Elliot, a wine merchant, to take some wine. I sent the prisoner to Poplar with some goods. He was to have 17 s. back. He brought it and delivered it to my wife. I never saw the money. My wife is not here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 695.  JOSEPH BOLTON was indicted for stealing on the 6th of June, 1819 , twelve pounds of lead, value 2 s. and ten pounds of copper, value 12 s. the goods of Thomas Richardson , (since deceased), and fixed to a building of his.
   JAMES SALTER. I am a gardener, at Fulham. I knew Mr. Thomas Richardson when he was alive. This lead was on the outside of the privy, - the copper joined it, and covered an other building. I was called up about twelve in the night of the 5th of June, 1819, to go to market, and I saw two persons drop from Mr. Richardson's back wall. I went after James Dormas whom I took, - he was tried and convicted. The other I did not know. The prisoner lived in the neighbourhood about a fortnight after the robbery, and then left.
   SARAH ROWLAND. I was servant to Mr. Richardson, he has been dead two years on the 5th of July; he was alive at the time of the robbery, his name was Thomas. Some part of the privy was robbed before. After Dormas was taken, I found it completely stripped.
   JAMES SHARP. I drive Salter's horse. I was up on that morning, and saw the two men come over the wall. I cannot say the prisoner was one.
 R. ELLINGHAM. I apprehended the prisoner, and we told him the charges. He said he was willing to go with me.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 696.  JOHN WELLS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of March , one saddle, value 2 s.; one halter, value 6 d.; and one bridle value 2 s.; the goods of Samuel Dalton, Barnaby Sanders, and William Wood.
   SAMUEL DALTON. I am a cornchandler. Barnaby Sanders and William Wood are joint executors with me to the estate of Eliza Dalton. The property was in a stable in Pratt's Mews, Camden-town.
   WILLIAM HALES. I keep a horse and cart. About five or six weeks ago the prisoner worked for me, - we were out selling sand, - he asked if I wanted to buy a saddle and halter, as he knew a person who had one to sell. I said, I do not want them, - he afterwards said they were very good, - I said I would look at them on Sunday. I saw them in the stable. I do not know who put them there. When I saw him, I said I was informed he had been and got 4 s. from my wife. He now denied selling them to my wife. I told him I should have nothing to do with them, and to bring the young man, or I would kick him and the harness out together. He absconded.
 Cross-examined by MR. HONE. Q. What trade are you? - A. I carry out sand and bricks.
   ELIZA HALES. I am the wife of the last witness. I never saw the harness. The prisoner brought me some money his master had sent him for, and he said,
 "Mistress you are to give me 4 s. 6 d.; I am going to buy a saddle and halter for 4 s." I did not like to give him the money, but I did.
   REBECCA PRICE. I was with Mrs. Hales, and heard the prisoner ask for 4 s. to buy a saddle and halter; she gave it to him at last. I never saw the things.
   JOHN DAVIS. I am a Bow-street patrole. I apprehended the prisoner last Friday. I told him what I took him for, he said he was not the only one to blame, and said Hales sent him for it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury before Mr. Common Sergeant.
