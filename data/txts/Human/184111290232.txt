   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner was in custody, did you ask her for the key of the caddy? A.  No, she was in Horsemonger-lane, I had no opportunity—I enquired about it, and I was told not—Busain, my inspector, told me I could not see her—I asked him if I was to apply to the Magis trate, and he said it was best not—only my wife was present when I broke it open—I took the niece in charge—I have not brought the drawer here—the inspector did not tell me not to bring it—the marks were very visible—when the drawer was open you could see where the chisel had been—there are no marks outside—I have never seen the key the pri soner produced—the chisels are Mrs. Alsager's—I have known the prisoner and her husband about the place for four years myself—I cannot say whether they are much respected, nor whether it was neces sary to bespeak the prisoner's services three weeks before they could he had.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  How long had she been in custody when you found the caddy? A.  One day—she was searched when she was taken—I was not in the room. HANNAH WILLIS. I am the prisoner's niece, and was with her at Mrs. Alsager's, when the family left town. The tea-caddy belongs to me—I lent it to the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you got the key now? A.  No, my aunt has it—she has lived eighteen years where she does now—my uncle is about fifty years old—I know he lent some money to a gentleman named Brand—I was taken into custody on this charge by the officer—I was not in the house when my aunt was apprehended—I had been in the house with her nearly all the time—she never had the charge of a house before—she went out to work for gentlemen and ladies in the neighbourhood—they have been obliged to wait a long time for her at times, she was so en gaged.
   COURT . Q.  Did you know any thing of two chisels in the house? A.  I never saw them—I saw the key, which was left in a drawer for my aunt to put her things into—when the cook came home, she went to her drawer next morning, then called my aunt up, and said she had left fifteen sover eigns in her drawer, which were gone, and asked if there was any key in the house which would open it—my aunt gave her what keys she had, and among them was the key of the caddy—the keys were all returned to my aunt.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know which key opened the drawer in question? A.  The one that had been left in the drawer when they left—the footman was at the house for three months after Mrs. Alsagar left—he had one gentleman come once and slept in the same bed with him—there was a Jew came twice—he did not sleep there. DAVID M'LEAN. I was footman to Mrs. Alsager, and remained in the house up to the 30th of June. After she left, my uncle, who is seventy-three years old, came to see me one Saturday night, slept there, and went away on Sunday—he lives at Poplar, and is a ship-carpenter—a Jew came twice I believe—he was in the habit of buying old liveries of the servants, and he did buy some—I slept in the front room, the best room, to protect the house.
