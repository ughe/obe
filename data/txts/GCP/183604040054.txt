FREDERICK COBB, M.D. I am a physician, and have been practising as such
for fourteen years in London. I am physician to the London Hospital. 1
was called in to see Captain M Kenzie on Friday night, to make an
pointment to see him on Saturday morning, the 30th of January; and
consequence of that appointment, I met Mr. Cumming there about
o'clock - I found Captain M'Kenzie in a state of extreme depression and
exhaustion ; and, in my opinion, labouring under a fatal discase-
informed by Mr. Cumming, on Friday evening, of the particulars of
illness, and the mode in which be had been treated-the statement made
was, that he had taken a large quantity of Morison's pills--an evacuation
was shown to me on the Saturday-it contained mucus, mixed with a good
deal of fluid, and slight spots of blood-I cannot swear to the fact of slim
spots of blood being mixed with it at that moment--the mucus had issued

mucus was the secretion from the inner lining of the bowels- orden

ment was merely to sustain life-I did not see him again that day-IM
