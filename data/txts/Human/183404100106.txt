On Saturday, Willard asked if I had spoken to any body? I said, "Yes, and they would look at it"- he brought the silks in his hand to the pay table, in Windmill-street, about half-past six o'clock. I went with him to a public-house - he asked the bar-maid to give him a pen and ink - he wrote me the direction where I was to call. I was not to ask any price for it, but to go, and let him know if it suited the party, and he would call himself, and arrange the price. I went to the shop about the silk, and directly I opened it, I thought it was not his own. The Jew said, if it was gown silk, he would buy it - I was bringing it away, and when I got to Marylebone, (Proctor, my shopmate, was with me,) a Jew caught hold of my arm, and asked if I wanted to buy any thing? - then the policeman caught hold of me, and asked what I had? I said, "Silk." I said it was not my own. I said I was a polisher - I did not say I was a porter - at the station-house I said, "I am drawn into this, and dare say I shall suffer for it, although innocent."
   BROOK PAYNE. I am the prisoner's uncle. He is in my employ - I am a French-polisher - Willard is in Mr. Broadwood's employ, and is called under-foreman, I believe - I remember seeing him on Saturday, the 5th of April - he had a paper parcel in his hand - a light-brown parcel, from ten to eighteen inches - I spoke to him - Willard was in the habit of going into my shop to fetch young men out, to go on errands - the prisoner has been in my employ five years, and always attended to my business.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Willard was employed at Broadwood's? A. Yes; and my nephew was employed on their premises by me - I was employed there by contract - I know they use silk in their business, but I cannot say it was silk - it was a paper parcel that I saw - I never knew a mercer give away silk as a Christmas-box - it might be so for all I know.
 THOMAS HENRY THOMPSON re-examined. I produce the paper the prisoner gave me.
 JURY. Q. When you took the prisoner was the piece of silk rolled up in the same manner as it is now? A. Exactly the same.
 JURY to T. AYLING. Q. Could a person employed under another have access to the silk, he being a French-polisher? A. Not without my consent or Willard's, who had access to it.

 726.  WILLIAM STEWARD was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of March , 1 ham, value 6s., the goods of Frederick Pamphilon.
 FREDERICK PAMPHILON. I  keep a cheese-shop in Skinner-street, Somers Town. On the 27th of March, while I was standing behind the counter, I saw the glimpse of two men pass the window, and heard a voice say, "Tom, here's a ham" - my man ran out, and the ham was gone - one of the policemen took the prisoner.
   THOMAS MITCHELL. I am a policeman. The prisoner passed by me with the ham under his coat - he went into a dark court, and my brother officer took him - I found the ham in a dark passage where he went down, and dropped it - we secured him as he came out.
   HENRY MARTIN. I am a policeman. I was standing at the corner of Cromer-street - the prisoner passed with a ham partly concealed under his coat - I followed and took him into custody - the ham was found two yards from him.
 Prisoner. I hope for mercy.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
 727.  RICHARD SMITH was indicted was stealing, on the 20th of March , 40lbs. of lead, value 5s.; the goods of Robert Briggs and another, and fixed to a building, against the Statute, &c.
   JOHN SHEPHERD. I am a policeman. On the 20th of March, about ten minutes after nine o'clock in the evening I was in Cock-alley, Norton-falgate- I saw a boy standing at the back of Briggs's premises - I suspected him - another joined him soon after, and when they saw us, they made off- neither of them was the prisoner - I sent the constable, and took the two boys into custody - I found a piece of lead where the boys stood - I went up stairs in a house in Cock-alley, in half an hour - I found the prisoner in bed, and this piece of lead lying about two yards from him; another boy was in bed with him - we took them both into custody - they said they knew nothing of the lead - it was concealed under the cinders in the garret - we took five persons who were in the house into custody - next morning I fitted the lead to Briggs's premises, and it tallied exactly; also the piece which I found where the boys stood - I took them all five before the magistrate - they were all discharged except the prisoner - I was present at the examination - I saw the magistrate sign the deposition which was read over to the prisoner - no threat or promise was held out to him - this is Mr. Broughton's handwriting (read) -"At the second examination, the prisoner said, the other persons knew nothing about the lead, for that he found it, and carried it there."
   ALEXANDER SCOTT. I am a policeman. Shepherd's evidence is correct.
   THOMAS BREESE. I am foreman to Robert Briggs and another - the back of their premises opens into Cock-alley - I had seen the lead secure on the top of the manufactory before this happened - the party had got on the roof from the outside - it is a great height - they must have had a ladder to get up - the lead appeared quite bright and fresh - there is a soot shed adjoining the premises - they must have got on the top of the shed; the lead was cut in a very unworkmanlike manner - I have seen the prisoner about the neighbourhood.
 Prisoner's Defence. About eight o'clock at night, I went to bed - I went outside to do what I wanted, and saw the lead lying on the ruins - I took it up stairs and went to bed.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
