1281. WILLIAM MASON, stealing 1 purse, value 1s. ; 5 sovereigns,
4 balf-sovereigns, 5 ball-crowns, and 5 shillings; the property of William
Buckmaster, in the dwelling-house of William Edgar and another.
WILLIAM BUCKMASTER. I am porter to Messrs. Swan and Edgar, of
Regent-street. The prisoner used to come to see his brother, who was porter
there, and slept in the house-on 4th April I missed a purse, with 71. 178. 64.
in it, from my box, which was in a room on the ground floor-1 had left it
locked and found it locked-we always saw the prisoner in that room-this
is my purse (produced).
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Is there any mark on it? 4. One
division is rather worn-I could swear to it from a hundred-1 gave 1s. for
it-four sovereigns were in it when found-the box was not sound, the
purse could be taken out at the bottom-I saw it safe on the 2nd..
Thomas BIRD (policeman). The prisoner told me where he lived-I went
there and found a box-he gave me a key which opened it-I found this
purse, containing four sovereigns and two shillings.
Guilty. Aged 22. - Confined Six Months--(See page 41.)
1282. JAMES MASON, stealing 12 knives and 12 forks, value 38.; the
goods of William Edgar and another, his masters—(See page 41.)
WILLIAM BUCKMASTER. The prisoner was employed by Messrs. Swan and
Edgar to clean knives and do the house-work-these knives and forks
(produced) exactly match ours- we did not miss any.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Who lives in the house? 4.
Neither of the partners, they have a housekeeper.
ROBERT CHEEKLEY. I took the prisoner in charge on 8th April-I asked
where he lived-he said, "6, King-street," that he had four boxes-he
gave me the keys- I went to a back room second floor-he told me to be
careful of a box which had some pictures in it, and in that I found these
knives.
Cross-examined. Q. How many keys did he give you ? 4. Six or seven
on a bunch.
Not GUILTY.
