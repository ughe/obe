THOMAS DURHAM (Sergeant P 5). On the afternoon of 2nd Nov. the
younger Stevens spoke to me and pointed out the prisoner-I went to
the Montpelier, where I received this shilling- I took the prisoner and
said "I shall take you into custody for uttering a counterfeit coin at
the Alliance publice-house and also at the Montpelier public-house,
Queen's Road, Peckham”-he said "I know nothing of it" I took him
to the station, searched him, and found on him a pocket-book con-
taining 257 penny stamps in sheets of 36, 18, 15, 12, 8, 6, 4, 3, and 2;
half a sovereign in gold, 188. in silver, and 67d. in bonze, all good-
when I searched him he said "I do not know where I got it from,"
pointing to the shilling, "I think it was at Rochester, where I sold
some clothes. I tried to pass it the second time because I thought it was
good. The stamps I had sent me by friends"-I said "Where do your
friends live ?"-he said "I don't want them to know anything about it,
I shall not tell you"-with regard to his address he said "I don't
want my friends to know; I shall refuse to give you my address."
