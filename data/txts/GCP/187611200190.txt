Cross-examined. My husband and the prisoner had a good many dealings
together at times- I believe on this day Pearson wanted him to sell him
some straw, and my husband said he had no more than than he should want
during the winter for his own cattle--they sometimes had a drop--- I don't
know whether they had had drop together that day-Pearson was in our
house very little that day, he was in and out a good deal most days-our
garden and his join by a quick set hedge, and he used to go down our
garden and make a backway through our hedge--occasionally they used to
have words, but they made it up-the dispute was a good deal because he
would come in the backway, he cut his fence down, and my husband was
very particular about the cattle going into his premises, he put up posts
and rails, and then Pearson made a way through-when my husband came
in I said "Lord bless me, what is the matter?" and he said that Pearson
had hit him with a pole; that was the second time he came in-after I had
washed his head and sent for Hammond, he went out again and was driving
the calf, and said to him “Come in," and it was then he said that Pearson
had hit him with a pole-he was quite conscious up to the last-the house
was quite quiet during his illuess-the room he was in was over the tap-
room, and we had customers coming in and out all the evening ; I kept him
as quiet as I could.

«The deposition of Charles Upsdell, taken in the presence of George Pearson.
I live here, I have lived twenty years in this house; the prisoner George
Pearson lives a few doors from me; I was in Pearson's garden about 4
o'clock this evening; I went after my calf which had got into Pearson's
garden. He knocked me down with a great stick about four times, he hit
me. The stick was about 10 feet long, it was a clothes prop. He knocked
my head all to pieces, I was 10 feet from him. He did not say & word to
me, nor I to him. I fell on the ground from the first blow. He hit me
three times afterwards. After the first blow was struck, while I was on the
ground he said I was not a going to take the calf out of his garden. I got
up again and drove the calf off the premises. Another man, my lodger,
Joseph Hammond, came to my assistance, I was covered with blood-when I
went into the garden, Pearson came from the granary with the pole, in about
an hour or more. We had some conversation about some straw, he wanted
a barrow load of my straw for his pig. He wheeled it out of the barn in
my wheelbarrow. We had no words about the straw, I sold it to him for
3d. I have never had any quarrel with him. After he had struck me he
put the prop behind the granary. He did not come to my assistance,
Joseph Hammond came to me. I called out for Joe to help me take the
oalf out of the garden. I was able to walk into the house afterwards ; I
sent for the doctor soon after I came in. Cross-examinal by the Prisoner.
You brought me in 4 lbs. of butter, about two hours or more before you
struck me; I did not see you after I got into the garden before you struck
me. I did not say when you drove the calf out Don't disturb the b-
you did not leave me and go indoors till after you had struck me; you
had a little straw in my barrow for your pig in the sty."
