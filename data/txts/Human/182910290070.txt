Although I was thus kept so long under examination, yet no other evidence than that adduced on my first examination was brought forward, except that of the witness, John Rossiter- every word of which is a tissue of the most glaring falsehoods; and I am certain that you will attach but little credit to the testimony of such a man. I need not say more on this subject, as I am certain your verdict will not be governed by such evidence; therefore, under the full confidence of an innocent man, and one who has made it his study to go through life in an irreproachable manner, and act with the strictest integrity, as the witnesses who do me the favour to come forward and speak to my character, will testify. Gentlemen of the Jury, - I cheerfully entrust my fate in your hands, under the firm conviction that your verdict will restore me to liberty, and to the bosom of my family, and enable me to resume that respectable occupation in life, which I have hither to enjoyed.

   JOHN TYNDALE. I live in Market-street - the prisoner Lesslie was in my employ; I was ill, and could not do my business; he was in my employ on Tuesday, the 30th of September, and went to the Police-office on the Friday following - he went there from my employ, and was in my employ for three days preceeding that time; I have known him six or seven years, up to the present time - I never knew any thing of him but as an honest man, as far as I know; all I know of him is, that I am a tailor and he worked for me.
   THOMAS ELDRIDGE. I am a painter, and live in Grangecourt. I have known Lesslie eight months - he bore a very honest character, as far as I know.

 Before Mr.  Baron Garrow.
 2032.  STEPHEN SANDFORD was indicted for that he, on the 26th of September , at St. James, Westminster , in and upon Joseph Bannister , a watchman , feloniously, unlawfully, and maliciously did make an assault, and by then and there drawing the trigger of a certain pistol, loaded with gunpowder and certain pieces of iron, which he had, and held in his right-hand, did then and there feloniously, unlawfully, and maliciously attempt to discharge the said pistol at and against the said Joseph Bannister , with intent feloniously, wilfully, and of his malice aforethought, to kill and murder him; against the Statute, &c.
 2nd COUNT, stating his intent to be to maim.
 3rd COUNT, stating his intent to be to disfigure.
 4th COUNT, stating his intent to be to disable.
 5th COUNT, stating his intent to be to do the said  Joseph Bannister some grevious bodily harm.
 6th COUNT, with intent to resist and prevent his lawful apprehension and detainer, for a certain offence for which he was liable by law to be apprehended and detained - (that is to say) for feloniously and burglariously breaking and entering a certain dwelling-house, about eleven o'clock at night, with intent to steal.
 7th COUNT, with intent to resist and prevent his lawful apprehension and detainer, for a certain offence, for which he was liable by law to be apprehended and detained -(that is to say) for the offence of burglary.
 8th COUNT, like the last, omitting the words printed in Italics.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   JOSEPH BANNISTER. I am a watchman of St. James', Westminster, and was so on the 26th of September, when, on hearing an alarm at Mr. Stulzs' premises, I went to the spot, saw the prisoner there, and seized him, in order to take him into custody - I collared him; he presented a pistol at me, immediately pulled the trigger, and it burnt the priming in the pan; he ran up Boyle-street and New Burlington-street, pursued by Clark and another.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you seen him before? A. Not to my knowledge - he snapped the pistol at me momentarily.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did he say any thing? A. Not a word.
 COURT. Q. In how short a time did you see the prisoner in custody? A. Not three minutes - I had not the least doubt of his being the man who pulled the trigger, and have no doubt of him now.
   JOHN CLARK. I saw Bannister knocked down, and saw the pistol flash - I ran after the prisoner, and was present when he was taken; I afterwards returned to the spot where Bannister had been knocked down, and found a pistol on the very spot Bannister had been fired at, and gave it to Schofield in the same state and condition as I found it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had it ever been out of your sight? A. No, not till I delivered it to him.
 COURT. Q. In what state was the lock of the pistol? A. The steel was down, as if the trigger had been pulled from the full cock - I did not observe whether there was a mark of any burnt powder in the pan.
   BENJAMIN SCHOFIELD. I have the pistol which Clark gave me on the 26th of September, the morning after the robbery; it had the appearance of having been snapped - there was powder burnt in the pan; I drew the charge - there were two small pieces of iron, and five large ones, two pieces of wadding, and powder.
 Q. Were those pieces of iron, if discharged against a man, capable of killing him? A. I should think so, at least of doing him grievous bodily harm.
 Cross-examined. Q. What did you do with the pistol? A. I locked it up in a cupboard, which I kept the key of; it was not out of my sight from the time I got it from Clark till I drew the charge - I was before the Magistrate at the time Clark put it on the table; I received it from him before the Magistrate at the office, on Saturday, the 26th - I know nothing about the robbery.
   JOHN CLARK. I picked up the pistol in the street, and gave it to Schofield at the office, on the Saturday morning - the robbery was between two and three o'clock in the morning; it had not been out of my possession all that time - it was in my pocket all the time till I gave it to Schofield; no alteration was made in the state of it before I delivered it to him.
