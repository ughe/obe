On Wednesday morning, the 26th of January, I was at the stable at Northfleet—I found the stable-door broken into three pieces, and the mare gone, which I had locked up over night—the pickaxe was moved from over the corn-bin, where I had left it over night—there is a pig-pound adjoining the stable, and the outside boarding of that pound was pulled away—the mare was brought again to Northfleet on Saturday night—I know the prisoner—he was in Mr. Ed wards's service, and when he left I succeeded to his situation. STEPHEN SIMPSON re-examined.  I have not seen the mare at North fleet since—the man is not here who brought her back. JOSEPH CLARKE re-examined.  I sent her to Northfleet by a very re spectable man who usually takes horses for Mr. Edwards. GUILTY. Aged 33.— Transported for Ten Years.
 832. FREDERICK CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of January , 214lbs. weight of coals, value 2 s  ., the goods of Charles Hoppe and another, in a barge on the navigable river Thames. HENRY JOHN KING( Thames police-constable, No  30.) On the morn ing of the 10th of January I saw the prisoner in a boat alongside the Bull  coal-barge, belonging to Mr. Hoppe, which was lying in the Thames by the Surrey Canal—I saw him take both his arms and sweep a quantity of coals from the bulk of the barge, into his boat—he then rowed away some distance down the river, then rowed back again, and threw his drag over board—I rowed after him, took him into custody, and found the coals in his boat—he said he had dragged them up—there were 214lbs. weight.
   Prisoner.  I got the coals by the Surrey-canal, where there were plenty of men by. I did not take them out of the barge. Witness.  I saw him take them. JOSEPH PARRY. I am lighterman to Mr. Charles Hoppe and another. The officer told me of this—I went to the barge, and perceived that a few coals bad been taken out—those produced by the officer correspond with the coals in the barge. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months.
   Before Mr. Baron Alderson.
