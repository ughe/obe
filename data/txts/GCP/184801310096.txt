EDWARD WATKINS. I live at 10, Mason's place, which commands a view
of the back of the prosecutor's house. There is a small row of houses be
tween, which we can overlook-we can see the upper part of the prosecutor's
house from the top room window on the Sunday evening of this fire I was
in the court on the ground, and heard an alarm of fire-I went round o
Rahere-street, and gave an alarm-I saw the prisoner at the door of Mr.
Hanaford's house-he opened it I do not know whether I had knocked-he
said the fire was there, and took me down stairs into the back kitchen he
asked if any one would lend him a little assistance with a few pails of water,
to put the fire out-I and about six others, I should say, went down stains
with him I cannot say whether he went down-he took us to a tap in the
baek kitchen which supplies the kitchen from the yard- I found one pail
down stairs, and that was used to take up water to the upper part of the
house-I asked if he had not got a water-butt in the yard, and some more
pails-he then took us to the water-butt, and we found some more pails in the
yard, and commenced dipping the water-I did not take any water up myself
-I then went up to the top of the house, to see if I could render any assist
ance-I did not go into any room-I saw a smoke coming from the door of
the back room which was on fire-the door was open there were other
people there when I went up-I did not stay a mioute-I immediately ra
down stairs to fetch the engine.
Cross-examined. Q. You rendered every assistance you could ? A. Yes,
and so did the prisoner, as far as I know there were two people at the room
door, but not in the room-when the prisoner opened the door I did not smell
him of turpentine at all-as near as I can tell, it was half-past six o'clock
when I went into the house.
