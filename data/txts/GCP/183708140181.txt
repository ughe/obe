Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where the woman was run over,
I believe there was a line of carriages in the middle of the road? A. They
had not reached the line of carriages-I will swear that they were nearly
the length of this court short of them-the cabs were more in the centre
of the road at first, but as they approached towards the stand they both
verged to the right--they did not divide, in my opinion; but in consequence
of the woman being knocked down, I lost sight of the cabs-as my attention
was taken about the woman I did not see them at the stand-she was ten
or twelve feet from the pavement.
Joux GRAVESTOCK (police-constable N 163.) I was on duty near Suffolk-
street, and saw a cab with a grey horse turn from Wellington-street into
Suffolk-street, at a very rapid rate, and heard a cry of “Stop the cab with the
grey horse" - I went in pursuit, and found it stopped by a brother constable
-We took the cab and the parties to the station-house-I saw Johnson in
the cab, besides Serjeant- I went to the woman, in the doctor's shop-she
was still alive-I assisted in taking her to the hospital, and she died before
we got there.
TOBIAS CHAPMAN (police-constable N 129.) I stopped the cab with the
grey horse, and took Serjeant to the station-I saw two men in the cab
besides—Johnson was one of them.
DAVID CORSTORPHAN. I am a police-inspector. I was on duty that
night-about twenty minutes after ten o'clock Serjeant was brought to the
station-house, and Johnson and Nicholls followed-Serjeant gave his name
--the constable stated in his hearing that the woman had been run over
by his cab—I asked him what he had got to say to it-he said it was true
-Johnson immediately said he was in the cab along with him, and he did
all he could to stop the horse : and Nicholls said afterwards that the horse
got the better of Serjeant.
Cross-examined by Mr. Phillips. Q. Were not Johnson and Nicholls
decidedly tipsy? A. They were.
