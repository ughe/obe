CENTRAL CRIMINAL COURT
TRELOAR, MAYOR.
THIRD SESSION.
LONDON AND MIDDLESEX CASES.
OLD COURT; Tuesday, January 8.
(Before the Recorder.)
COLE, Harry (50, warehouseman); pleaded guilty to stealing &
leather bag, the goods of Eugene Posen, and feloniously receiving
same; he also confessed to a conviction on June 16, 1903, at Guild-
hall Justice-room, of obtaining goods by false pretences; three other
conviotions were proved.
Sentence, Nine months' hard labour.
HUGHES, James (26, labourer); robbery with violence on James
Lynch and stealing from him the sum of 14s.
Mr. G. L. Hardy prosecuted.
JAMES LYNCH, labourer, Poole Villas, The Grove, Hackney. On
December 11 I was with a friend, Hidgens, in the "Bell" public-house,
Osborne Street, Whitechapel; prisoner was in the bar. Hidgens and
I left, prisoner being then in the "Bell." On parting with Hidgens
I returned to Osborne Street and went into the "Compasses"; prisoner
came in there soon after. On my leaving there I was foMowed by
prisoner and two other men, and they got hold of me behind at the
corner of a street and took between 14s. and 15s. from my trousers
pocket; prisoner got his arm round my neck; I could see his face
and am certain he is the man. The three men ran away. I met a
constable, and later in the night I went to the station, where I picked
ou: prisoner from ten or eleven others. At the “Compasses "'I had
changed a half-sovereign to pay for drinks; prisoner sew me do this.
To Prisoner. I first saw you in the "Bell" abou seven o'clook.
Two men came in and asked me and Hidgens to bu:""& ring; you
treated one of those men. The description I gav I you to the
policeman was, a tall, fair man, with dark features; I did not mention
clothes. I cannot give a description of the two men who were with
you when I was robbed.
Thomas HIDGENS, labourer, 64, Commercial Road. I was with
prosecutor in the "Bell” and I remember seeing prisoner there; I
saw him treat the man who came in to sell a ring.
