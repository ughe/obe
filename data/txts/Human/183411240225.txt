   Q.  Did not he ask you how it was, if you did not intend, nor make any bet, that you put your money on the table? A.  No—I did not make any bet with this man—I put the money on the table to show I had it.
   Q.  Do you mean to state that you (under all the circumstances that you have now explained) was induced to put the money merely to satisfy an almost entire stranger that you had got money? A.  That was my motive—that was the first game we played—we did not play it out—I did not play twenty games afterwards with him—decidedly not—I did not bet ten to one on the odd trick—I did not lose any money after I had lost the bag.
   Q.  When you were first before the Magistrate, did you state that the prisoner went out of the house alone? A.  He went out frequently—he went out alone—I found Grinham was coming to give evidence—I had him taken by the Magistrate's direction—Mr. Minshull was the Magistrate—I did not state, when Grinham was before the Magistrate, that he and the prisoner went away together.
   Q.  Will you swear that you did not, at the first examination, state that the prisoner went away alone, and afterwards say that he and Grinham went away together? A.  No; I could not—I do not believe I could say it, I don't know.
   Q.  Did not the Magistrate and his clerk both fern to your de position, and tell you that you had sworn that the prisoner went away alone, and discharge Grinham instantly? A.  They discharged him be cause none of the money was found on him—there was some remark made by one of them—he was not discharged because I at first swore that the prisoner went away alone, and afterwards that Grinham went him—I have seen Grinham about—he was one who was playing at whist, and Shillitoe was the other—I did not have Grinham taken up to prevent his coming forward—I have known him four or five yean—he was not a very intimate acquaintance, but was frequently calling in—I did not lend Grin ham money to play, that I will swear—I did not lend him any money that night, that I know of.
   Q.  Will you swear you did not lend him 30 s .? A.  I did not lend him 30 s ., or any money whatever—he had no money of mine at til that night, to my knowledge—I do not think I could lend him any money, and have forgotten it—I swear positively I did not.
   Q.  Did you ask the prisoner whether he would bet twenty to one upon the odd trick? A.  No; I did not ask him alter that if he would bet ten to one—I did not tell him he was no sporting man—it was between ten and eleven o'clock when I lost my money—I did not con tinue to play till between one and two o'clock—I did not play for stakes varying from 2 s . 6 d . to 1 l .
   Q.  How was Mr. Humphreys to suppose that this money had been deposited by you in the prisoner's hands, if you had not told him? A.  He was not at home—his son was—this letter is not stating what I told him—I did not tell Mr.. Humphreys, Senr., that his son had written the letter according to my direction—I did not know whether it was his son or his clerk—I said the letter was according to my di rection, but I never said the money was deposited—it was laid on the table.
