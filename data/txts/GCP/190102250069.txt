WILLIAM COLE (22 R.R). On February 4th I was on duty near the
Point, and heard four or five shots, and a woman's scream-I ran towards
the sound, and saw the woman some distance down the road; her back
was on fire-I asked her what was the mattershe said something, and I
went to the Point with another constable—the prisoner ran towards us,
put up his hands, and said, "Hands up "-I butted him in the stomach
and threw him down-he was very violent, and I struck him with an
umbrella which was on the ground-I asked him for the revolver; he
said, "It is over there. Is she dead ?"-I said, “No”—he said, "I wish
I had killed her, as I could easily have got away, as I have signed articles
to go away to-morrow"-on the way to the station I took this letter from
him: "January 28th. My dear Sister, -No doubt the bad news will
cause you a great start, but it is better thus, as this woman is too wicked
to live. I am also a very wicked man myself, and two such people are
better out of the world than in it. Good-bye; farewell. God forgive us
both.-Your loving brother, Luke"-it is torn; he tore it when I took
