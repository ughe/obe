 1773.  ALEXANDER FAIRBAIRN was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 1 file, value 5s., the goods of James Edwards , his master.
   JAMES EDWARDS. I am a coach-spring maker, and live in High-street, Bloomsbury. The prisoner had been in my employ, but left me about four months before the 19th of July; I missed a great number of files, and have only found this one, which has my mark on it.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. He was not in your employ at this time? A. No; he used to look in as a  shopmate; he has been in my service off and on this seven years - he says he took this off my bench: it is usual for men, when they go out to work, to take rubbers and files; they might leave them, but I never knew it done.
   THOMAS BURROUGHS. I bought a file for 6d. or 7d., but I cannot say it was of the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. Can you swear that is the file? A. No; I sold the one I bought to Welkes - my wife never purchased one of them, to my knowledge.
   GEORGE WELKES. On the 30th of April I saw this file in Grub-street, and bought it of this man for 9d.
   ROBERT JAMES. On the 17th or 18th of July this rubber was brought to the shop, and I detained it, knowing it to be stolen.
   FRANCIS FAGAN. I took the prisoner on the 21st of July; he said he had taken it in a fit of drunkenness, and was very sorry for what he had done; the prosecutor asked if he had taken any more, and he said No.
 GUILTY. Aged 45. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1774.  WILLIAM ELSBURY was indicted for embezzlement.
 MR. SMITH conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM DANBY. I live at Lambeth; my wife is in a lunatic asylum at Peckham, for which I pay the parish of St. Andrew, Holborn, 9s. a week. On the 7th of July I received a note from Mr. Wilks, saying he was desired to apply to me for the arrears of my money: and on the 10th of July, after tea, from half-past five to six o'clock, I sent by my nephew two sovereigns, to the best of my recollection - it was 2l.; I said to him, "Bill, take this over to the workhouse, and make haste back;" he returned between seven and eight o'clock, and said he had delivered the two sovereigns at the gate.
   WILLIAM BANTING. I am nephew to the witness. On the 10th of July he gave me two sovereigns, which I took to Gray's Inn-lane workhouse, and gave to the prisoner, who was at the gate - he took a book down, as they generally do, and seemed to write something; he said, "That will do," I went home and told my uncle.
 Prisoner. Q. Was it not three sovereigns? A. No - it was two.
   JOHN WOOD WILKS. I am assistant overseer of the parish of St. Andrew, Holborn. The prisoner was the gateman, and was formerly a messenger; it was his duty to put down in a book any payments that were made, and to carry them to account. On the 10th of July I had a settlement with him, between ten and two o'clock - I received 2l. of him, which he had received from four or five persons, and gave him a receipt for it; I wrote several times to William Danby for the arrears due from him, but I do not recollect the day; the prisoner continued in his place us gate-man on Saturday, the 11th of July; and on Monday morning, when I went, the first thing I heard was that Elsbury was off - I sent down to the lodge for the book, in which I found this entry made."Danby, 2l.;" I never saw the prisoner write, but I have little doubt it is his writing; this 2l. has never been paid to me, nor to any one else, to my knowledge: the prisoner was paid so much per week for his attendance, and had an extra allowance of provision.
   GEORGE STRIPLING. I am a watch-house-keeper. On the 17th of July the prisoner came to me, and said he came to give himself up, and he supposed I had heard what he had done - I said I had heard he had done wrong, but I did not know to what amount; he said to the extent of 40s. - he said he was sorry for what he had done, and he was a fool for it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I leave myself to the consideration of the Court and Jury; it was a great error, but not done intentionally - I have had 20l. or 30l. to get change, and if I had intended to defraud, I could have gone off with it.
 GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to Mercy on behalf of the Parish. Confined Two Months.
 1715.  WILLIAM VERRYARD was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September , 1 bevil, value 2s.; and 1 plane, value 1s., the goods of George Stockdale.
   GEORGE STOCKDALE. I am a carpenter, and was at work at Eaton-square on the 5th of September. I lost a bevil and a plane from a house I had the key of; the prisoner was foreman in the employ.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. I believe you and the prisoner are old friends? A. No; he was foreman to Mr. Smith; he has not lent me his tools - he has none; he lent me a bead plane once - I never lent him any: I did go with him to Bartholomew fair, but not on the night of the robbery - I did not lend him these tools, nor go to my master to get him turned out: I have not said, "I have got master Verryard now, and I am d-d if I don't transport him." - I said, if I could find who had the tools, I would.
   JOHN GRINDLEY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a plane and a bevil pawned by the prisoner; I have known him twelve months - he said his name was Verryard, and he lived in Chester-street.
