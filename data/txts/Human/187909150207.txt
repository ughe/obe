874. JAMES WALTER BROWN  (40) , Feloniously forging and uttering an order for 3 l  . 10 s  ., with intend to defraud.
   MR. GEOGHEGAN Prosecuted ; MR. FULTON Defended. RICHARD COXHEAD. I am a zinc-worker, of 73, Jamaica Road—on 24th May the prisoner came and asked me to cash a cheque for 3 l . 10 s .—he said somebody at Islington had given it to him—I told him I had not the money—I took it to Mr. Evans, a neighbour, and got it cashed—Mr. Evans sent it to Mr. Johnson, and it was returned four days afterwards marked "No account.
   Cross-examined.  I have known the prisoner some little time, and have cashed cheques for him before, one for 25 l .—this cheque purports to be drawn by a man named Jay—the prisoner wrote his name on the back; I did not ask him to do so—I have no banker.
   Re-examined.  The prisoner said "The party I had it from is all right, and he has had a banking account for some years. GEORGE ALBERT HILDT. I am manager to Mr. Wilkinson, of the Surrey Canal Saw Mills—I have received letters from the prisoner and have seen him afterwards and referred to them—I am acquainted with his hand writing—I believe the signature "William Jay" to this cheque to be his writing.
   Cross-examined.  I said so at the police-court when he was charged by Mr. Wilkinson with obtaining money by false pretences upon this cheque; he was admitted to bail.
   Re-examined.  He brought me a bill for 20 l . 10 s ., which was dishonoured. FREDERICK DEAKIN. I received this letter ( produced ) from the prisoner—he told me that he had written it himself; I did not see him write it—I believe all those three documents to be in his handwriting—I gave them all to Mr. Chabot—I was at the police-court on all the occasions—no charge of forgery was definitely preferred till the last occasion, after the cheque was shown to Mr. Chabot—I had mislaid it.
   Cross-examined.  The prisoner was committed for trial for false pretences and admitted to bail after opposition by me—I did not know that I could prefer a bill without the permission of the Magistrate—I have no ill feeling against the prisoner.
   Re-examined.  I opposed his going out on bail purely in my professional capacity as a solicitor. CHARLES CHABOT. I am an expert in handwriting—I have compared all the documents produced, and some others with this cheque—to the best of my belief the whole of the writing on the front of the cheque except the words "No account" is in the same handwriting as these documents—it is an assumed hand, not in the prisoner's natural handwriting—the endorse ment "J. W. Brown" is in his natural handwriting.
   Cross-examined.  My evidence is based on my past experience—it has been occasionally differed from—Mr. Nethercliff had been opposed to me about seven or eight times. CHARLES EDWIN SCARISBROOK. I am a clerk at the Holborn Branch of the Union Bank of London—this yellow cheque was issued in a book of 100 cheques to a customer of the bank, Mr. C. J. Moger, on 22nd January, 1877—the last transaction they had with us was nine or ten months ago—they have presented no cheque since—we have no customer named Jay. RICHARD KIMBER( Detective ).
