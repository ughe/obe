WILLIAM HENRY STROUD. On Wednesday, 27th of Jan., Watts came
to my master's shop in a horse and cart, with some books there was a
person named Love with him-Watts was in and out of the shop from time
to time while Love was bargaining for the books—nine sovereigns were paid
for them, I remember Mr. Petherham making an observation about the books
being imperfect, and Love said they had been lying about, and the children
might have got at them, as he had been years collecting them, and his father
before him-Watts said he did not think any that he looked over were imper-
fect, calling the other man “George"-amongst the books there was this one,
which is "Poems by I. D.," and this other book.
MR. HARMAN. These are two of my books.
JOSEPH MELLISH. I am inspector of the N division of Police. I found
Mr. Harman's place broken open-I took Watts into custody on the 18th of
Feb.--I said he must consider himself in custody, for being connected with
others in stealing the books from Mr. Harman's-he said he knew nothing
about them-I left him in custody of Sergeant Watkins- afterwards
took Box-he said he got the books from Watts, a lady's coachman, in
Lansdown-mews-he first said he was to get 61. for them-then he
said 5l., and then he said all he got above 41. he was to have for
himself-he said he called round at Watts' to know if he had got
any waste paper, and Watts said he had got no waste paper, but he had got
some books, and he said Watts had borrowed his horse and cart on several
occasions - I know Charles Love I have not had any description given to
me of him by Mr. Lilly-I have tried to find Charles Love, but have not
been able.
