4. NATH ANIEL HAWES, was condemned, for Affaulting Richard
Hall, in the Evening, on Finchly-Corymon, and robbing him of 4. s. biding
him Diſmount, that he might have his Horſe, as well as that he might
fearch his Pockets.
He ſaid, he was not 20 Years old'; was Born in Norfolk, but was
inoftly brought up in Hartfordſhire. That his Father, was a wealthy
Grazier, dying before lie was a Year old, he had much leſs of his Por-
tion than was left him. That he was put thereupon to an Upholſterer,
ſerved about 4 Years before he got into expenſive Company, which
put. hiin upon Robbing his Maſter, but that he injured him feveral
times before he was diſcover'd.
Wlien firſt Condemn'd, he ſhow'd a great Levity of Behaviour,
inſenſible of the wretched State he was in. He then ſaid, that his
Behaviour at the Seffionis Houſe, was as became a Man of Courage
and bold Spirit, and if the Court was fo Uncivil as to deny him his
own Cloths, lie had no buſineſs to oblige the Court, in Pleading, That
was he to act agen, he would ſay, (as before) That it uſed to be a
Court of Juſtice, but was now a Place of Injuſtice; and that
be doubted not but as ſevere a Judgment would light upon thein, as
they had paſs’d upon him : He ſaid alſo, that he doubted not but he
ſhould be able to leave the World as much like a Man, as he had liv'd
in it.
But afterwards, being convinc'd, that Fool-hardineſs was not Cou-
rage, nor Audaciouſneſs any braveneſs of Spirit ; he began to alter
his Sentiments, and own, that to be Unconcern'd at his Diſaſters, was
not a laudable Bravery, but a ſtupid Inſenſibility ; acknowledging,
that he bore on his Breaff the 250 Pound weight, for 7 Minuts, not
becauſe he would have better Cloths to be hang d in, than he appear'd
with on his Trial, (as he ſaid in the Court.) but to evince his Bold-
neſs, and to gain Applauſe among the Gentlemen of the Highway, as
he faid, for being ſo brave a Fellow; for he muſt allow the Clemency
of the Court, in not letting him die, when before Condemned, for Rob-
bing the Upholſterer.
He farther ſaid, that one fames and he, committed at leaſt 18 Rob-
ries in a Fortnights time. But that he eſpecially repented of ſome
Cruelties he acted towards thoſe he robb’d; as particularly a Waggon,
near a Park-Wall, going to Oxford ; a Quaker's Coach near Uxbridge;
in which and other Inſtances he pilliaged and ſpoil'd, without thewing
any Mercy, that he hoped for Mercy from God.
He added, that he ſhould not have broke out of New-Priſon, if a Wo-
man had not induc'd him thereto, who inſtructed him and another in
the Means, and furniſhed them with Requiſites; to whom he ſaid they
were ſo Grateful, that they firſt helped her over the Walls, and after-
wards eſcaped themſelves.
Sometime before he was Executed, he was more Grave and Serious
in his Deportinent ; defirous to receive the Sacrament; and poſitive
that thro' the Merits of Chriſt, he had duly prepared himſelf for it.
