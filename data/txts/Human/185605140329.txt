   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Is there any mark on the pans? A.  Yes—they have been in my shop—I cannot say they have not been sold—I have supplied tea sets to a bazaar—these are common patterns—all the crockery was taken at Perrin's house.
   MR. RIBTON . Q.  Had Smart any authority to sell any goods of yours? A.  Never—I strictly forbade him.
   COURT . Q.  What is the value of the whole of the property? A.  I put them down at 3 l . or 4 l . altogether—I never sold any of these articles to Smart—my daughter and two men and a boy all serve in my shop. MARY SEMON. I am the daughter of George Semon, a rag merchant, in Powis-street, Woolwich—Smart came to me on 10th April about 1 o'clock, with a bag of white rags with him—I gave him 5 s . 11 d . for 28lbs.—I laid them down by the side of the door of the shed by themselves—these are them—shortly afterwards the prosecutor and the policeman came, I showed them the rags and they were taken away.
   Cross-examined by MR. DENNEY . Q.  How do you recollect it was on the 10th of April? A.  I went to the Court the same day—I had no other rags in the same place where these were—this is the bag they were brought in. JOHN NEWELL. ( policemen, R  59.) I went to Perrin's house when Smart was in custody—I put some questions to Perrin—she at first denied all knowledge of these articles, and then she said she would tell all, and she said Smart brought them there—I searched Smart at the station and found on him 8 s . 10 d . and a key of Mr. Andrews' street door, which I gave to Mr. Andrews. THOMAS CHARLES ANDREWS. I received this key and tried it to my street door—it was the key of that door—we had missed it for six weeks.
   Cross-examined by MR. DENNEY . Q.  Did you give Smart the key to get in and out? A.  No; I never did—I missed this key—it was a patent lock on the door—I spoke to the prisoner and the other men, they all said they knew nothing about it.
   SMART  — GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Twelve Months .
   PERRIN  — GUILTY.— Confined Four Months .
