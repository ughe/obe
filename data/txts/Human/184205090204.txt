WILLIAM TWYMAN( police-constable R  127.) I went to Ackman's house and found the violin—the elder prisoner told me on the way to the office that his son had bought the violin of Mr. Pilkington, in High-street, for 7 s ., which he had given him to pay some rent to his landlord. CAROLINE WHITE re-examined.  I saw the fiddle safe about three weeks ago, and missed it out of the shop on the 22nd—it might have been gone before that—I am quite sure this is it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  When I offered it for sale I thought I was justified in making the most of any article that I considered my money had bought; I gave my boy the money to pay my landlord, and he promised he would; when I came home he said he was induced to buy a violin; I took it from him, and locked it up, but he got it again; he used to interrupt the peo ple of the house, and I offered it for a pair of trowsers for him, rather than he should annoy me with it in the house; he is a very bad boy.
   ROBERT BALDERY , Sen.— NOT GUILTY.
 1669. ROBERT BALDERY, JUN. was again  indicted for stealing, on the   19th of April , 1 pair of boots, value 6 s  . the goods of John Higgs . JOHN HIGGS. I am a shoe-maker, and live at Woolwich. On the even ing of the 19th of April I heard the door open, went and found the pri soner in my shop—he asked if Mr. Porter, the tailor, lived there—and a very few minutes after he was gone I missed the boots. WILLIAM LEECH. I am shopman to Mr. Booth, a pawnbroker, at Wool wich. On the 19th of April the prisoner offered these boots in pawn—he said he brought them from his father—I lent him 3 s . on them. ALFRED JOHN FINN( police-constable R  81.) I took the prisoner at his father's house—I told him it was for stealing boots from this shop—he said there was another boy with him, his brother, and that he pawned them at Booth's.
   Prisoner.  The other boy stole them and gave them to me to pawn; he waited at the door; he used to go to the same school as me. GUILTY. Aged 14.— Transported for Seven Years.—Convict Ship.
