RICHARD THOMAS Toms. I am a commission agent, in partnership with
James M'Gill, at No. 1, Bread-street, Cheapside, in the dwelling-house of
Mr. Thomas Nutting. On the 10th of June, about half past one o'clock, I saw
Runxtmann and Loenthall at our warehouse, which is on the first floor .
they inquired the price of various goods there, silk goods chiefly- Loerthall
spoke to me as well as he could in English, and said it was difficult to buy a
large parcel of goods, not understanding the English-he said he had got a
carpenter at home who spoke English and German well, but he was sick -
he laid his head on his hand and made all manner of motions-I showed him
the commoner sort of goods-he objected to them as not being good, and
required them to be weighed, and the boy was sent down into the shop to
weigh them-he then inquired the length and breadth of the goods—he gave
me his address, and I wrote it down, and required him to write his name
himself-he told me his name, but I could not understand what he meant-
he agreed about the discount, and kept me in conversation a long time, during
which Runxtmann went down stairs-when Loenthall went, I put on my hat
and followed him--when I got out I saw Loenthall going up Bread-street to
Cheapside-I then saw him cross and join Guttschalk, about seventy feet
from the warehouse-- they walked towards Lawrence-lane-Runxtmann
crossed over and joined the other two-they had some conversation at the
corner of Trump-street-by the time I got to the corner of Trump-street,
