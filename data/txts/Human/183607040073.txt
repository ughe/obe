   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Then the confession to which Mr. Gould referred, was a confession that he told you she had made when you were absent? A.  Yes—it was not made directly in my presence; but I had some further conversation wit her in Mr. Gould's presence—I am not sure whether the sureties were mentioned before she said, "I am ruined"—when the sureties were spoken of, Mr. Gould, Mr. Goodison, and the prisoner were present—I wrote that letter—my instructions were, that Mr. Gould did not, by any means wish to injure Miss Turner by prosecut ing her, and at the same time he did not wish to sustain any loss.
