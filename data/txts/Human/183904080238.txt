MARGARET MULCHANNAUGH. I am the wife of John Mulchannangh and lodged in the house. On the morning of the 15th of March, I heard the prosecutrix scream out—I went to her—she was standing in the pri soner's room—the prisoner had got hold of her hair by her left hand, and had a carving knife in her right hand—I saw her stab the prosecutrix in the left side of the head, before I had power to take the knife from her—I saw blood come from the prosecutrix—I took the knife from the prisoner and chucked  it inside the fender—I had heard the prisoner come to the foot of the stairs, and abuse the prosecutrix as she lay on her bed.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you been out that morning? A.  Yes, be tween seven and eight o'clock, for half-a-pint of gin, which we drank in the prosecutrix's room with her husband and the prisoner's husband—the prisoner was below in her own room—I did not hear the prosecutrix say any thing to the prisoner—the policeman came a few minutes after the deed was committed—I cannot say what space it was. JOHN LONG. I am a policeman. Between seven and eight o'clock in the morning of the 15th of March, I was called into the prisoner's house, and took her into custody—her husband gave me this knife—( producing it )—I asked the prisoner how she came to do it—she said both the witness and the prosecutrix rushed in upon her—that the knife laid on the table, and she laid hold of it to save herself.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you desire the prisoner to wipe her face before taking her to the station house? A.  No; but she was bleeding from the forehead—she has the mark now—I did not observe any poker in the room, or any pieces of wood—her face was streaming down with blood—my body must have seen it. WILLIAM MURDOCK. I am a surgeon, and live in Rotherhithe-street I was called in to see the prosecutrix on the morning of the 15th of March—I found her seated in a corner of the room, her face covered with blood, and her hair dishevelled, falling over her face, clotted with blood—she had fainted from loss of blood, and was then recovering—I found two cuts and a scratch on the left side of her head, a cat on her face, and a cut between the first and second finger of the left hand—they were simple cut wounds—I did not consider them dangerous—they might have been inflicted with this knife—I see a mark on the blade near the handle, which I believe to be blood—I have attended her ever since—she was confined to her bed nine or  ten days—she is very near recovery now—there is a wound not quite healed. MARGARET MULCHANNAUGH re-examined.  That is the knife I took out of the prisoner's hands. NOT GUILTY.

JAMES SOMERVTLLE was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of George Walmsley and others, on the   4 the of April , and stealing therein 1 snuff-box, value 36 s  ., their property. JOSEPH BRIDGE. I am shopman to George Walmsley and two other, in High-street, Borough, in the parish of St. George the Martyr, South-work. On the afternoon of the 4th of April, I was in the shop—I heard the window crack, turned towards it, and saw the prisoner's hand inside the window—he took a silver snuff-box out, and went by the window with it—I went out, secured him with it in his hand, and gave him in charge.
