The next thing I know is he is out in the road challenging me to fight with two pokers. He hit me across the shoulder and knocked me to my knees. I got up and got hold of him; we closed. As I was struggling with him for the pokers I might have hit him once or twice, but not more; then I walked indoors. The policeman's account is wrong. I did not say anything about the Sidney Street siege. I said, "Anyone would think this was Sidney Street to see so many people here and policemen." At the station I said, "This man came out after me with two pokers and he hit me once or twice; I took them away and tried to do the best I could." I did not hit him harder than was necessary. Prisoner was mad drunk. FREDERICK CHARLES HOLLOWAY spoke to seeing the fight between Holmes and another man in Prince's Lane; Holmes was drunk at that time.

   SURREY CASES . BEFORE THE RECORDER .
 (Wednesday, October 11.) WILSON  , Arthur (41, labourer) pleaded guilty , of breaking and entering the counting-house of the Ranelagh Club and stealing therein one clock, the goods of Alfred George Wagnell .
 Prisoner was stated to have refused to give any account of himself and to have been twice convicted of petty offences in this year.
 Sentence, Nine months' hard labour. BARNARD  , William (43, stoker) pleaded guilty , of burglary in the dwelling-house of Edward Collins and stealing therein two tea cloths and other articles, his goods.
 Prisoner confessed to having been convicted at South London Sessions on February 15, 1910, receiving 18 months for burglary. About 20 other convictions, commencing in 1885, for stealing, etc., were proved.
 Sentence, Three years' penal servitude.
