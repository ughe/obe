FANNY JANE OBORNE. I live with my aunt in Leader-place, Westminster. On the 23rd of March the prisoner came there at a quarter to eight o'clock in the morning—she said she had come from Windsor by the steam carriage, and wanted my aunt to recommend a person to go and fetch her boxes, which we did—the shawl was lying there, and she asked me to let her put it on her shoulders, as she was very cold travelling all night—she then borrowed a shilling and got some rum, and had some luncheon—then she got me to write a note to her aunt, at the Marquis of Lansdowne's—we went out, and she asked me to lend her another shilling, which I did—she went away with the man who was to fetch the boxes, and when I got home I missed my flannel petticoat, handkerchief, and gloves, which had been on the mangle where the shawl was. ELIZABETH HARMAN. I am a searcher at the station—I produce this flannel petticoat which I found on the prisoner. FANNY JANE OBORNE. This is my petticoat—there is no mark on it, but I have another like it, and it is my own making. JOHN BARKER( City police-constable, No.  466.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—(read)—the pri soner is the person who was then convicted. GUILTY. * Aged 23.— Transported for Ten Years .

 1366. JAMES LEWIS, was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of March , 1 coat, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Dove . MARY ANN DOVE. I am the wife of John Dove—we live in Homer-street, Marylebone. On the 23rd of March, I saw this coat, which is my husband's, hanging on the doorpost, just outside, between nine and ten o'clock—I am quite sure it is ours. GEORGE JOHN RESTIEAUX( police-constable E  49.) On the 23rd of March I stopped the prisoner with this coat, in High-street, St. Giles, about four o'clock in the afternoon.
   Prisoner.  A person brought it into a room where I was, and told me to go and sell it—I could not sell it, and was coming back. GUILTY.† Aged 20.— Confined Six Months .
 1367. FREDERICK ALLEN, was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of April , 168lbs. weight of iron, value 7 s  ., the goods of Thomas Murray . WILLIAM LOWE. I am in the employ of Thomas Murray of High-street, St. Giles—I took some of his iron from the Dutchy-wharf, on the 1st of April—I know this iron which is here—it is my master's, and was on that wharf. WILLIAM WEBSTER. I reside at Dutchy-wharf—I never gave the prisoner leave to take any iron away, nor lent him any sack to take it with.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you deny lending me the sack?—you went into your master's shed, and brought the sack out, and gave it into my hands. Wit  ness. No, I did not. PETER LOGAN. I am an inspector of police. On the 1st of April, about half-past eight o'clock, I stopped the prisoner coming up the Savoy-steps with this iron in a sack.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have been fifteen years employed there, and I very often pick up iron out of the mud and on the wharf. I put it on one side; Webster lent me the sack to take it in. GUILTY. Aged 36.— Confined Three Months .
