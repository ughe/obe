ELIZABETH TUCKETT. I conduct the National School in Little Guildford.
street, Bloomabury. About one o'clock on the Both of March, I was at the
school-the prisoner came to me and stated that she met two ladies in the
square, and one of them was Miss Powell, who was a teacher in the school-
she said that Miss Powell met another lady, who was a teacher in the school,
but she did not know her name, and described berdress-thaton one of these ladies
had bought a bottle-jack of her, and requested it to be brought to me, to
be left with me, till she should send her servant to fetch itshe said I was
to pay her 5s. for it, and this lady's servant should bring me the money
the bottle-jack was worth Iod. or 1s.-believing she came from the lady,
I gave her the knew Miss Powell very well-I wondered they had
not paid for it.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You had some doubt about
the truth of her story ? A. I cannot say that I doubted, but that remark
came into my nind-Little Guildford-street leads out of Great Guildford-
street-when she first entered the room, she asked for Miss TuckettI
said I was Miss Tuckett-she said she had met two ladies in the square, that
one was called Miss Powell, that she was a teacher in the school, that she
wore a merino dress with two flounces, a scarf, shawl, and a black velvet
bonnet, and that was her dress (Miss Powell had been with me not five
minutes before,) and that she had met another lady who wore a black shawl
and a black silk dress, and that they had purchased the jack of her.
Q. If she had come and used the name of Miss Powell, without describ-
ing her dress, should you have let her have the 5s.? A. I very likely
should, but the dress confirmed to me that she had evidently seen Miss
Powell.

I did not send the prisoner with the bottle-jack- never saw her till she
