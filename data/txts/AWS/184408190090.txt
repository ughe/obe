1956. JOHN WHITE was indicted for unlawfully obtaining 3 loaves of
bread, and other articles, from Thomas Munn, by false pretences.
THOMAs MUNn. I live with my father, Jonathan Munn, a grocer, at O1d
Brentford. On the I8th of June the prisoner came to our shop, and asked if
would allow him to have some things for Mr. Winter, as he was going out
in the barge-he said he wanted three loaves, a quarter of a pound of lard, half
a pound of bacon, and half an ounce of tobacco-] knew Mr. Winter had
dealings with my father-] knew the prisoner was in his service, but he was
not at that time, I believe-] let him have the things for Mr. Winter.
THOMAs WINTER. I am a lighterman and barge-master-the prisoner was
in my employ-he left about three months before this-he was not in my ser
vice on the 18th of June-] did not send or authorize him to go Mr. Munn's
shop for any goods for me, or at any time.
(The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he had not obtained the
things in Mr. Winter's name, but on credit, as he had done before.)
T. MUNN re-eaamined. He had not dealt with us on his own account-
we never gave him credit-I am sure he used Mr. Winter's name.
GUILTY. Aged 18.-Recommended to Confined Two Months.
