To Mr. Justice Darling. I went into the question in reference to
the proceedings instituted against Mr. Benson.
Examination continued. I cannot say that we had great trouble
with Mr. Peat on your behalf in obtaining access to the books. We
were a long time in their premises. There was a little tiny bit of
friction, but I cannot say there was an absolute endeavour on Mr.
Peat's behalf to keep us from the books. The books were in one room
und we were in another; there was a little difficulty in getting them;
a certain form had to be gone through each time before we could get
the books. I should be the last person to say that Mr. Peat delibe-
nitely delayed us from the books.
Cross-examined. I can find no trace in the books of any entry
relating to the cheque for £3,597 ; we simply have the counterfoil.

Guy STEPHENSON, Assistant Director of Public Prosecutions, was
ked by prisoner to produce a letter written by him to the Director.
Witaess objected, on the ground that the letter was written in con-
action with a public prosecution to the Director in his official
capacity.
Mr. Muir submitted that when, upon grounds of public policy, the head of
Government Department declines to produce a document, the same is privi-
Counsel cited Marks v. Beyfus, 25 Q.B.D., p. 494 ; and also referred to
korthand notes of a case of Latter v. Goolden-not in the books in which
Lord Esher, M.R., held that " when the head of a publie department says it
weld be contrary to the public interest to produce a document which is in
possession in virtue of his position as head of the department, it is for him
my so.")
Nr. Justice Darling ruled that the letter could not be produced.
