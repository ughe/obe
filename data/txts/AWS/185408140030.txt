907. WILLIAM CLARK, stealing 1 shirt, 7 shifts, and other articles,
value 14. 78. the goods of James Howe.
HARRIET Howe I am the wife of James Howe. I had been to Great
Stanmore, hay-making-on 26th June I left a bundle in the care of John
Saunders-i contained clothes of my own and my children's-Saunders was
to take care of the bundle while I was at work at Mr. Canard's-the
prisoner was working with me there-after leaving work on Monday, I went
to Mr. Blackwell's, to get other work-I left the prisoner against the gate,
as we went into the yard-I did not get back till between 10 and 11 o'clock
I saw Saunders, and he gave me some information-I did not give the
prisoner authority to go and get the bundle-it contained three gowns,
three pairs of stays, a dozen and a half of napkins, five shirts, petticoats,
and other things, and a Bible and Prayer-book.
JoHN SAUNDERS. I had a bundle given me by the last witness to take
care of on 26th June-I took care of it till the Monday night, when the
prisoner came to my house at balf past 9 o'clock-I got out of bed, and he
said his mate had sent him for the bundle of clothes-I said, 44 Where are
you going to sleep "he said, 46 At Mr. Blackwell's" "I gave him the
bundle, and thought he was going to his mate with it
JAMES Howe. I did not tell the prisoner he might go for the bundle I
did not say a word to him about it.
EDwIN LEIGHTON (policeman, 51). I received information, and looked
after the prisoner; I found him in a cook's shop on 29th July-I said, 44
Is
not your name Clark "-he said, 46 es"-I said he must consider himself
in oustody for stealing a bundle of clothes from that poor man at Stanmore
-he said, 46 I have robbed no poor man "I took him to the station-I
have not been able to find any of the property.
