JURY. Q. What induced you to watch the prisoner, and the other man
so long a time? A. There was a boy came out of the London and West-
minster Bank; he was counting some notes, preparing to put them into
his pocket; I saw the prisoner and the other man follow him, and go very
near him, I thought they were intending to rob him that induced me to
watch them.

GEORGE POTTER. I am a lime merchant, I have one partner; I reside
at Purfleet Wharf I remember my boy bringing me 41. 78.--I had sent
him for that sum—there were two sovereigns bad- I took them in my pos-
session, and kept them till the next day; I then gave them back to the lad.
Cross-examined. Q. Why did you give them back to the lad! A. Be
cause I received a letter which I have here (reado "4. Charles-street. St.
James's; Sir, your boy, the bearer of this, has been robbed, and in the man
ner he will describe to you; the servant here saw it done, and the clerk and
I saw the men and ran after them, but they escaped ; I have sent for a
detective, and given him full information. Yours faithfully, A. Elworthy.")
-in consequence of this, I gave the boy the money.
WILLIAM THOMAS HAYWARD. I am a cashier at the London and West-
minster Bank. On 17th Feb. I gave the lad 41. 78.; the sovereigns I gave
him were good.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you weigh them? A. Not in giving them out,
but I did in taking them in-no one has access to my till but me-my till
consists of gold and silver-I get it from the Bank of England, and supply
my own till with it I distinctly swear I did not pay the lad bad money.
COURT. Q. Did you ever give such things as these two bad sovereigns
out from the Bank? A. No.
