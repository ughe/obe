Ana KENNEDY. I am wet-nurse, at 17, Wimpole-street. The prisoners
came to sweep the attie chimney--they said they could not sweep it, for
there was a funnel on the top, and they wished to go out on the roof-I
opened the window, and let them go out-they went away--their master
came and swept it next morning.
THOMAS ARTER. I saw some one on the roof, whom I cannot identify.

CATHERINE DILLON, feloniously receiving the same.
JOSEPH John Goode. I assist in the business of Mary Goode, a pawn-
broker. Mary Dillon was in our service-we lost a ring, a gown, and a
brush-she left on 13th April, and I missed the ring-Catherine Dillon was
not in the habit of coming to our house.
Cross-examined by ME. PAYNE. Q. Had you any person in your employ
that you lost property by before ? A. Yes; about three years ago I had
this ring in my hand on the 11th, in a room adjoining the shop—we have a
great quantity of property there-I know this gown by the body being
detaebed from the skirt-I cannot state the day I saw it, but this furniture-
brush I know from the particular hardness of it; there is no mark on it-I
am positive it belongs to me.
RICHARD FAULKNER. This ring was pawned with me, on 20th April, by
Catherine Dillon-I knew her before-she said she pawned it for a person
named Smith,
Cross-examined. Q. How many times did she pawn it? 4. Twice-it
was redeemed in a few days, and pawned again on the 20th.
JAMES Brannan (police-sergeant, G 20). I went to Catherine Dillon's-1
told Mary Dillon I came to take her for stealing a ring from her late master,
in Goswell-street-she said she never lived there--I then said to Catherine,
"And you for pawning it"-she said, "I know nothing of it"-she took a
ap, and gave something to her daughter, and they began chewing some
duplicates ; I took some from her mouth-I found this gown in a drawer,
and this brush in a cupboard. MR. PAYNE called
JULIA KENNY, I was present when Catherine Dillon bought a green
dress- I cannot say this is it, but this is the colour of it-it was a short
body, and full sleeves.
MARY FEEnAy. I was at Mrs. Dillon's about the middle of April-she
was going to get me some work-a person named Smith came in, and said to
her, “Go get me a few shillings on this ring I"-Mrs. Dillon said, “You
are at it again ; you are on the spree"-she said, "No, I am not "-she
pulled a ring off her finger, but I could not swear to it-I was near the win-
dow, and this was near the fire.
