 2179.  BENJAMIN THORNEYGOLD and CHARLOTTE MONK were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 2 sheets, value 5s.; 2 pillow cases, value 1s., and 1 counterpane, value 6s., the goods of William Welch.
   CAROLINE WELCH. I am the wife of William Welch, a shoemaker, of Laystall-street. The prisoners took a furnished room at our house on the 11th of October; they passed as man and wife - these things were let with the room; they were out on the 17th - I got into their room, and missed these articles; they returned at night, and I gave them in charge; before the constable came the man ran away - I called out Stop thief! and he was brought back.
 WILLIAM WELCH. I charged the prisoners with stealing my property; the woman acknowledged it, and gave me three duplicates; I asked where the rest of the property was; she would not own to that.
   DANIEL PARKER. I stopped the man who was running.
   JOHN ADAMAN. The female prisoner pawned this sheet and pillow-case with me on the 16th of October.
   GEORGE UNDERWOOD. I have a counterpane pawned on the 16th of October.
   ANDREW LLOYD. I am a constable, and took the prisoners in charge; the man said if they would forgive him he would return the things next morning - and he told the Magistrate it was done through distress.
 The prisoners pleaded distress.
 THORNEYGOLD -    GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined Six Months.
 MONK -    GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined One Month.
 Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 2180.  LOUISA HUGALL was indicted for embezzlement.
   ANN DAVIS. I am the wife of William Davis, a journeyman baker, who lives in the City-road. I employed the prisoner as my servant, to take home my washing, and paid her weekly; she lived in the house - if any one paid her she was to receive it, and account to me directly she came home; she had been about four weeks with me - I washed for Gerald, who always paid when the linen went home.
   SARAH GERALD. On the 13th of September I received some things from Mrs. Davis by the prisoner - I paid her 2s.; she brought a bill, but I did not have it receipted.
 ANN DAVIS. When she returned on the 13th she said Mrs. Gerald had no change - I found it out three days after, when I found several things put aways - she absconded; she did not return after Wednesday evening, the 13th.
   JAMES GLIBBERY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - her father is a very respectable man, and a tallow-chandler.
     GUILTY. Aged 16.
 Judgement Respited.
 2281.  WILLIAM BILL was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of October , 4 pair of stockings, value 20s.; 1 pair of half stockings, value 1s., and 2 shillings, the property of John Austen, his master.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 JOHN AUSTEN. I am a  linen-draper, and live in Fair-street, Manchester-square. The prisoner was in my employ from the 2d or 3d of September - he was taken up on the 3d of October; his salary was 40l. and he lived in the house - I never gave him leave to take any of my goods; in consequence of suspicion I marked 9s. in shillings and sixpences, and delivered them to Mrs. Seaward.
   MARY SEAWARD. On the 2d of October I received some marked money from Mr. Austen, and delivered the same money to Prince.
   ELIZABETH PRINCE. I received 9s. in shillings and sixpences from Mrs. Seaward on the 2d of October; I was not aware that it was marked; I was sent for nine yards of linen, at 1s. a yard - the prisoner served me; I paid him the same money as I received from Mrs. Seward about seven o'clock.
 MR. AUSTEN. I was not at home when this took place; I waited at the witness' house, and went home immediately; I then sent the prisoner out to get sovereigns for silver which I took out of my cash-box; when he was gone I looked into the till and found 7s. of the marked money, and about 2s. or 3s. of other money, or possibly not that; I sent for an officer on the prisoner's return, and said in his presence that I wished to have him searched, and wished to know whether he had any silver in his pocket like this, shewing a private mark on a shilling; 2s. of the marked money was found on him; his box was searched in his presence - he said it was his; two pairs of white stockings, two pairs of half-stockings, and one pair of silk stockings, were found, which I believe to be mine; they had all been worn except the half-hose - the three pair of stockings had S, the maker's mark on them, but no mark of my own; he had one pair on which I found the same mark on; I said if he came down stairs I thought I should be able to produce papers of the same.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Had you a lad named Nash in your service? A. Yes; I do not know of the prisoner lending him 2s.; the prisoner told the Magistrate, if my apprentice was there he could state that he had lent him 2s.; I said he should come, and fetched him; all manufacturers mark their stockings; I missed one pair out of one paper.
   HENRY STOWELL. I am an officer. I have the stockings and the two shillings.
 The Prisoner's Counsel called -
   FREDERICK NASH. I am apprentice to Mr. Austen. The prisoner was in his service - he lent me two shillings on Mr. Austen's account, not for myself, and it was understood he was to pay himself; he never told me he had taken it out of the till.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When did he lend it? A. About a fortnight or three weeks before; he never asked me nor Mr. Austen for it.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. Did he never mention to you that he had taken the two shillings? A. No - he said the same morning before breakfast that the till owed him 2s.
