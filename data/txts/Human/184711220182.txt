I live at 19, Mount-street, Charlton, near Woolwich—Gardiner has lodged there since Aug., 1846—he occupied a back bed-room up stairs at 5 s . a week—he has paid me part of the rent, but there is about 8 l . in arrear—I saw the officer search his room and carry off the letters and papers—it was Gardiner's room. MARY ANN GRAVES. I am in the service of Mr. Deputy Corney, who is the owner of Pinner's-hall. I am housekeeper there—I know Holliday, and knew a person named Schulkhauser—Holliday and Company occupied No. 6 as an office—I always understood that the "Company" was Schulkhauser—I have seen him with Holiday at the office—I am not certain whether I know Cusell—Holliday and Schulkhauser came about 23rd Nov., and stayed till nearly the middle of Jan.—I knew of their having gone, soon after they left—several persons called too inquire about them afterwards—they did not pay for the office—they engaged to pay 40 l . a year for two offices—" Holiday and Co." was on the door—I have seen their cards—this is one of them—( looking at the one produced by Mr. Benson )—I have seen cards like that—I do not know where they went to after leaving the office—I cannot say that I know any of the prisoners perfectly but Holliday.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  Schulkhauser was pretty fre quently at Pinner's-ball till the middle of January, was he not? A.  Yes, very frequently, with Holiday; pretty well every day—Schulkhauser was a rather tall and good-looking man.
   COURT. Q.  Did you see any of these books there at any time? A.  No; I have seen so many similar books in the different counting-houses, that I cannot speak to their being there—I think Holliday and Company left about 7th or 8th Jan. JOHN TAYLOR. I am a furniture warehouseman, at Claremont-house, Islington. In Dec. last, Holiday and a foreign gentleman called on me and gave me an order for furniture amounting to 91 l . 10 s .—it was to be sent to 10, Theberton-street—the goods were sent there—I afterwards saw Holliday and the same foreign gentleman, at Pinner's-ball, when I applied for a settle ment of my account—Holiday was to pay 20 l . down and give a bill of two months—I never got the money or the bill—I called several times for it—on one occasion, when I went after my money, I found some person, I do not know whether it was Mr. Peachey, of Bishopsgate-street, taking away a piano forte from Theberton-street—from inquiries I made respecting that, I deter mined to take my furniture away with me, and by receiving a note from Mr. Holliday, I did so.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON Q.  When was it they called on you? A.  In the latter part of Dec.—the goods were entered in the day-book on 1st Jan. in the present year—I received the whole of the goods back again.
