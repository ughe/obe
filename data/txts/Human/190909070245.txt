   BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT . (Wednesday, September 15.) BRETT  , James (23 carman), and HEATH  , Arthur (38, catameat vendor) , both maliciously causing grievous bodily harm to Minnie samovitz .
 Mr. Allan Ramsay prosecuted; Mr. H. Warburton defended Brett, Mr. Gervais Rentoul defended Heath. ABRAHAM SLAMOVITZ, 349, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26, at 3 p.m., I was standing at my stall with my daughter Minnie, who was then 2✗ years old, when I heard a scream and saw her lying in the road on her face. I picked her up, when I saw prisoners driving away in a trap. My daughter's face was bleeding. After taking her to a doctor I took her to the London Hospital, where she was examined. There were no bones broken. She was in bed for three weeks and is still suffering from nervous attacks. There were a number of people in the road screaming.
 Cross-examined. I did not see the accident. The two prisoners were charged at the police court—Brett had a month's imprisonment and Heath was fined. I have taken civil proceedings through a society the solicitor has written demanding damages. There was not such traffic in the road.
 Re-examined. Prisoners were convicted at East Ham Police Court of being drunk in charge of a vehicle. JOHN PACKER, M. B., 336, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26, at 3 p.m., I examined the child Minnie. She was in a condition of shock and moaning; there were no broken bones or bruises—there was an abrasion across the face, apparently caused by the face rubbing against the road or the wheel. I attended her until August 4. She complained of pain in the back and was suffering from shock to the servous system. The injury to the face was quite superficial. I was informed that she starts up in her sleep. ANNIE BAUM, 341, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26 I saw the child lying in the road beneath the wheels of the cart. The two Prisoners were in the cart—they drove away. A great many people were shouting. I think Brett was driving.
