 2004. RICHARD GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 1 coat, value 3 l  . 10 s  ., the goods of Robert Barr , his master. ROBERT BARR. I am a tailor, and live in Slater-street. The prisoner was in my service—I went out on the 6th of June, and left him at work-when I returned, the prisoner and the coat were gone—I have never found it—he left me without notice, and I never saw him till he was in Worship. street. WILLIAM BURN. I am a weaver. Mr. Barr made me a coat—I tore it—I took it to the prosecutor's on Wednesday, and he was out—I went away, and met him—we went back to his house in about an hour and a half, and the prisoner and the coat were gone—the prisoner was left at home. WILLIAM HANKS. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner at Balls-pond, Islington, in August—I was looking for him the whole of June and July. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months.
 2005. WILLIAM COOK was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Joseph Jay , from his person. JOSEPH JAY. I am a plumber, and live in Upper Whitecross-street About half-past ten o'clock at night, on the 11th of July, I was standing in Upper Whitecross-street, having some oysters for my supper—Mr. Harm an ran by me, and said I had had my pocket picked—he ran up the court, and caught the prisoner—I have not seen my handker chief since—I had used it three minutes before, and put it into my pocket. CHARLES HARMAN. I Live in Whitecross-street. I was standing at my shop door, and saw the prisoner and another boy go by—I saw them pass by the prosecutor, and as the prisoner passed he touched his pocket, then turned back and picked the prosecutor's pocket—the other one stood by the prosecutor, and lighted his pipe by the gas—the prisoner then ran off—I do not know what became of the handkerchief, but I am sure I saw the prisoner take it.
   Prisoner . I was going home, and this man said I took the handkerchief. GUILTY. Aged 14.— Confined Three Months.
 2006. MARY BROOKS was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of August , 1 pewter pot, value 2 s  ., the goods of Thomas Smith . HENRY MILLS. I live with Mr. Thomas Smith, at the Coach and Horses, in High-street, Shadwell. About six o'clock, on the 17th of August, the prisoner came and called for half a pint of beer—I served her with it in a pint pot—she sat by the side of the bar, and there was a quart pot that some one had left-when I turned she and the pot were gone—I went and found her with the quart pot, and took her back—this is my master's pot. JAMES MARTIN. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and found the quart pot under her shawl, under her arm.
