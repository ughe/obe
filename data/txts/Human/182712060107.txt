 230.  JAMES GOLDSWORTH was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of May , 14 sovereigns, 1 sixpence, 4d. in copper monies, and one 10l. Bank-note, the property of Joseph Lee, his master.
 JOSEPH LEE. I am a  drover. The prisoner was in my service, and was in the habit of receiving and paying money for me. On the 25th of May I gave him a 10l. note, fourteen sovereigns, a shilling, a sixpence, and 4d. to go to Smithfield to pay to Mr. Hill, for eighteen sheep and four lambs - he was to take them to Mr. George Warner, in Bermondsey-street, who is my employer; the prisoner was to have met me at Romford fair on the following Wednesday, but I did not see him again till the 1st of September, when he was in Mile-end-road; I ran after him, but he escaped - he was taken again on the 5th of November.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Where did you give him the money? A. At the corner of Dean-street. Tooley-street, but I opened it again at the corner of Thames-street, to see that it was all right - he was to pay it for two different salesmen - it is entered in a book, which is not here; I went immediately to Romford, and staid there till Wednesday - whether he went to my house or not I cannot tell.
   CHARLES HILL. The prisoner did not pay me any money on the 22d of May, nor at any time afterwards; I expected the money.
   SARAH LEE. I am the wife of Joseph Lee. My husband came home to go to Romford; he told me he had sent the prisoner for the sheep - I saw him afterwards, three different times, in a different dress, and a different dog with him; I tried to get an officer, but could not till the third time, when I had him taken.
   THOMAS STIMPSON. I am headborough of Mile-end Old Town - I received the prisoner in charge - he told me he had received the money and lost it out of his pocket, but he could not tell how - I found nothing on him.
 GUILTY. Aged 19. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Two Months, and Whipped.
 231.  JOHN SMITH and HENRY BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the 2d of November , 5 pairs of boots, value 4l. 6s., and 2 boots, value 14s., the goods of James Purcell.
 JAMES PURCELL. I am a  boot-maker; I have a shop in John-street, Minories, but I do not live there, On the 2d of November, I went to New-square, to get my tea, leaving my son in the shop, and all my boots safe; in about six minutes, my son came and said the shop was robbed; I went there, and as I opened the door, I found one pair of boots under my feet. I went and gave information, and Smith was brought in at the time with these boots, which are mine.
   RICHARD BUSH SKILLERN. I have two odd boots, and one pair of top-boots, which I found on Smith, about six o'clock in the evening, on the 2d of November, in Wentworth-street, Whitechapel, about half a mile from the prosecutor's shop. I saw nothing of Brown at that time - I believe two Lambeth-street officers took him.
   RICHARD PURCELL. I am the son of James Purcell; I am nine years old; my father left me in the shop while he went to get his tea; the two prisoners came to the shop; Smith said he wanted a boot-lace; I stooped to get one, and on lifting my head up, I saw Smith take the boots off the peggs by the wall, and give them to Brown, who stood near the door; they then ran away by the window - I had not seen them before, but I am certain of their persons.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Have you not said that Brown was outside the shop? A. Yes; he went outside the door again, and then the other reached him the boots - it was about five o'clock - it was dark; they were there about ten minutes. I am sure I cannot be mistaken in them. I do not know how many pairs of boots Smith took.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 SMITH's Defence. I was going along Whitechapel, and a person gave me the boots to pawn - I did not know they were stolen; I was going into a pawnbroker's shop with them, and the officer took me.
 BROWN's Defence. I am quite innocent - I never saw Smith before.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 BROWN -    NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 232.  MARY ANN STOREY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , two pillows, value 3s., and two pillow-cases, value 1s., the goods of Mark Beahan.
 MARK BEAHAN. I live in  Brown's-buildings, Princes-street, and am a clothes-dealer. I let the prisoner a furnished lodging on the 24th of October, and among the furniture were these two pillows, and pillow-cases; the first week the prisoner was there, I gave her notice to quit, but she staid three days longer than the week. I did not miss these things till she had left; there were other articles in the room - she is a soldier's wife in a Scottish reginent.
   CHARLES STUBBING. I am shopman to Mr. Matthews, a pawnbroker, of the Minories. I took in these two pillows on the 8th of November, I believe, of the prisoner - this is the duplicate I gave for them.
