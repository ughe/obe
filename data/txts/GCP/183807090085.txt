FREDERICK MANSELL. I reside in Myddleton-square, and am an en-

Ton the 24th of June, I placed three £10 notes and three £5 notes in a
drawer, in my bed-room-I locked the drawer, and went out of town that
dav-my pupil, George Winter, had a duplicate key of the drawer I re-
turned the following day-the drawer had then been opened, and the notes
taken away.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had not Walker absconded? 4.
Yer-I returned about seven o'clock the following evening.
Grorox WINTER. I am a pupil of Mr. Mansell's. On the 24th of
Jane I went to the drawer, where the notes were kept-I took out a £5
note, and gave it to Walker to get change-he brought the note back, as he
could not get change, I replaced the note in the drawer, and locked it
--I placed the key on the chest of drawers, wben I went to bed at night,
that it might be handy in the night-in the morning, when I dressed my-
self, I missed the key-Walker had not left the house then, but he left
about three o'clock, and gave no notice at all-I procured the drawer to
be opened, and all the notes were gone.
MARY LAZARUS. I live at No. 112, East Smithfield, opposite the Lon-
don Docks. On the 25th of June, between one and two o'clock, Walker
and Fletcher came to my shop to purchase a waistcoat-Walker was in
livery-they bought the waistcoat, and Walker paid for it with a half-
sovereigu-there were two watches hung behind my counter-Walker said
be should like to purchase them, one for himself and one for his brother-
be bought the two for five guineas-he gave me a £10 note -I did not
give him change, as I suspected the note- I have not the note now-it
was passed two or three days after—the change I have here_Walker said he
wanted a coat, and my servant went out to get one, and in the interim,
Walker said he must go for his master, Mr. Savory, and he would return
in a quarter of an hour.
Cross-examined. Q. Did Fletcher speak at all? A. Not at all.
JOSEPR Hoppe. I live in the Minories. On the 25th of June, Walker
and Fletcher came to my shop-Walker bought a frock-coat for 158., and
a pair of trowsers for 6.-he left his livery, a coat, waistcoat, and trowsers
with me for 58.-they were very much worn.
William SUTTLE (police-constable R 96.) On the morning of the 26th

found on him a watch, and 58.-while I was searching Walker at the

and a half from Walker, at that time I was between them both--the notes
