296. JOHN MARNEY (20), Robbery on Arthur Sard, and stealings
rule and handkerchief, his property.
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS conducted the Prosecution; and MR. TUBES the
Defence.
ARTHUR SARD. I am a carpenter, at 3, Garden Row, London Road-
about 10 o'clock at night, on 3rd February, I was passing St. George's
Church, coming from my work-I felt a hand in my coat pocket, where I
had a handkerchief and rule- I put my hand in, seized the hand, and held it-
it was the prisoner's hand-he had the rule in his hand- the handkerchid
dropped on the pavement-he struck me in the eye to release himself, and
I gave him into the custody of a constable, who came up immediately-
was not the worse for drink-I had had two glasses of whiskey and water at
Hackney, but nothing since.
Cross-examined. . Had you just come from Hackney A. Yes-that
was all I had had all day, and that I had about 8 o'clock-I did not spank
to the prisoner at all—did not say I was a fenian-I did not jostle against
him or touch him, till I found his hand in my pocket.
ROBERT CRANSTON (Policeman M 77). I was on duty on this night in'quet
tion, near St. George's Church-I heard cries of Police!" went up and saw the
prisoner and the prosecutor struggling-I seized hold of the prisoner-be
had the rule in his right hand saw a pocket handkerchief on the ground
-the prosecutor gave him in charge-he did not say at that time that be
was not guilty; he did afterwards at the station.
Cross-examined. Q. Had the prosecutor been drinking 4. He had
had a drop to drink, but nothing to put him out-the street was tolerably
full of people at the time.
GUILTY.
He further PLEADED Guilty to having been before convicted in February
1868. — Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
