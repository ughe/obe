 1356.  DAVID EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Henry Hiblin ; from his person.
 HENRY HIBLIN. I was at  Bartholomew fair on the 3rd of September; an officer came and asked, if I had lost anything; I felt and missed my handkerchief - the officer had the prisoner, and took my handkerchief from him.
   PHILLIP PARISH(City police-constable No. 45). I was at the fair, and saw the prisoner and another lad following the prosecutor; I followed them, and saw something peep under the prisoner's coat, I laid hold of him, and called to the prosecutor - I took this handkerchief from under his coat.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1357.  HENRY LEWIS was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 waistcoat, value 5s., the goods of Henry Atkinson.
   HENRY ATKINSON. I live at No. 32, Barbican; I am a hosier and general dealer. On the 30th of July, I heard a sudden jerk; I looked at my door, and missed a waistcoat which had been inside - I saw the prisoner cross the street; I sent a boy after him, and saw them scuffling; I went and took the prisoner, and found this waistcoat about two yards from him, it is mine - my boy is gone away.
 Prisoner. I saw the waistcoat by a pump, I took it up, and his boy came and took me - he asked what I had got; I thought he was a strange boy and I hit him; he was going to hit me, and the waistcoat fell from my jacket.
     GUILTY. Aged 12. - Transported for Seven Years.

 1359.  ROBERT LINDSAY was indicted for a misdemeanour.
 HENRY JOHN SPEARS. I am the son of  John Spears; he lives at Nottingham, but he has a house in the parish of St. Michael, Wood-street- I sleep there as servant to my father - on the 5th of August, about a quarter before twelve o'clock at night, I found the prisoner concealed inside a large hamper in the cellar; he was a total stranger to me, he must have got in before the doors closed at eight o'clock, as no part of the house was broken; or have let himself in with this skeleton key, which was found in the cellar, near where he was - I usually go into the cellar every night to see that all is right, and that night when I went down, the prisoner spoke before I saw him, and said, "I will surrender"; I then saw him, and had him taken.
   HENRY PIPER. I am in the prosecutor's service. On the afternoon of the 5th of August, I saw a short man in the passage with his trousers down - I asked what he did there; he said, he did not know; I ordered him off the premises - I went to the door afterwards, and saw the same man talking to the prisoner - I found this key in the cellar the next morning.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Two Years.
 OLD COURT. TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 10TH.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1360.  MICHAEL SIMMONDS and JAMES SEYMOUR were indicted for feloniously assaulting Joseph Hayward , on the 4th of August ; putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 chain, value 2s.; and 1 seal, value 1l. his property.
 JOSEPH HAYWARD I live in Milton-street, Cripplegate. On the 4th of August, about half-past ten o'clock at night, I had been to Mile End-road, and was coming home - I had got through  Osborne-street, as far as Brick-lane, and there the prisoner Seymour came up to me; he took both his hands and drew my watch from my pocket; he said nothing; it flew over his head with the force he pulled it, and the glass flew out; the case came from it, and went against the shutters; he still had hold of the chain; the case dropped on the pavement, and he ran off with the watch - the other prisoner was standing just off the curb; I am certain he is the man - the moment he ran off with the watch; Simmonds followed him running; I ran after him, and hallooed out, "Stop thief"; they went down Wentworth-street, down an alley - the neighbours persuaded me not to follow, for if I did they would serve me out - Grant, the policeman, came up, and persuaded me to go home quietly, and say nothing about it; then some young men came and went after them - I have not seen the watch since; it was silver, and had a chain and seal - I am positive the prisoners are the two persons; Seymour is the man who took my watch; it was dark, but the gas-lights were very clear, both back and front, and I am positive of Simmonds - I did not see them speak together; they both ran down the same alley, and into the same house.
 Simmonds. Q. Are are sure it was me that was there, and ran away? A. I am positive it was you; on Monday morning, when the policeman fetched me to see him, he acknowledged to my face that it was him.
 JOHN MOUNCEY. I live at No. 17, Rose-lane, Spitalfields. I was coming down Osborne-street, near Brick-lane, about half-past ten o'clock - I went down Wentworth-street, and saw Simmonds running down the street, with his coat on, and as I went towards my residence, I saw him standing against Mr. Sheen's door, with his coat off - that was about five minutes after.
 Simmonds. Q. Was I not alone? A. I saw nobody with him; I did not see Seymour at all.
   JOSEPH HAYWARD re-examined. I saw them running in Wentworth-street; both ran together across Brick-lane, to Wentworth-street, and on to the alley, which is in Wentworth-street, and leads into Whitechapel - it was possible for a person to see one, and not the other; the one which had the watch got into the alley first.
