   Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY . Q.  At what time in the day did you first meet with the prisoner? A.  It might be between 2 and 3 o'clock in the afternoon—other persons were with me—I continued drinking with her for some time prior to this happening—we were drinking with one another, and we treated one another—it might be two or three hours after the note had been changed that she left—I dare say it was as much as between three and four hours—my attention was first called to this matter after 28th July, on the 3rd or 5th Aug., I will not be sure which—I continued in her company up till nearly 12 o'clock at night—I did not find out what her calling was—I did not discover that she was what is called an unfortunate girl—I swear that—after the theatre, we went to a public house, and had some gin, and I never saw her afterwards till I saw her at the station house a fortnight ago—I am a widower with four children—two of my children were with me all the time—one is nine and the other in seven. EDWARD SALISBURY. I keep the Tower public house, in Westminster Road. I remember changing a note for the last witness on 28th July last—I wrote my name on it two days afterwards when I paid it away—this is the note—Gray wrote his name on it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You saw the prisoner there, did you, on the same occasion that Gray was there, and had the note changed? A.  Yes; she was sitting in the bar in company with Gray, and two or three of his fellow workmen. EDWARD NEWTON. ( Policeman, L  76). I took the prisoner into custody on 12th Oct., in Webber Street. I told her that I wanted her for passing a forged 5 l . note, at the Tower public house—she said, "If I did, I did not know it was a bad one, as I had it from a gentleman"—she lives in Granby Street, Waterloo Road—that is about one of the worst streets in London, a regular den of brothels and thieves—a woman who was not a prostitute would not be likely to be seen there—there are but two decent women there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You had known her as an unfortunate girl, had you not? A.  Yes; some time—there are two or three respectable persons in Granby Street; there is the public house, and the greengrocer's shop. NOT GUILTY.
