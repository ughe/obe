CARROLL, Mayor.
1976. ANN HARRISS was indicted for stealing 1 51. Bank-note, the
monies of Thomas Whitewood, her master.
MARTHA WHITEWOOD. I am the wife of Thomas Whitewood, of South-street.
Greenwich--the prisoner was in my service. On the 9th of Aug., a gentleman
called to pay me some rent-he paid me a 5l. note and 178. in silver-I putit
all into a pocket-book, and placed it in a sideboard-drawer, in the parlour-the
prisoner knew of it-I said before her that I had received it-she had access to
the room--the drawer was left open-next day I placed the pocket-book under
lock and key, without examining it-I do not know whether the note was in
it then or not-next day, Wednesday the 11th, I went to the pocket-book, and
missed the 51. note and 35.-nobody but the prisoner and my daughter knew
anything about it-a constable was sent for, and the prisoner said, in my
presence, she had not seen it-on Friday, the 13th, she came into the kitchen
and asked me if I would forgive her if she paid me back the whole of the
money-I asked her if she took the money-she said, "No"--I said, "Can
I receive of you what you say you have never taken of me? No, I cannot
do it"-in about a minute she came again, begged me to forgive her, and
said she had taken the note and 38.-I had told her that day, that if she had
been tempted to take it, if she would replace it, I would forgive her-I said
nothing about her confessing, only about replacing the money-I sent for a
policeman in the evening, and gave her in charge for something else-she
is an orphan--I had no character with her-mine is the first place she has
been at.
George Goode. I took the prisoner on Friday evening at Mr. White-
wood's-I told her she was charged with stealing a 5l. note- I cautioned
her that I should tell the Magistrate what she said she said she was anxious
to tell the truth, and said she had taken the note and burnt it, and had taken
other things besides.
Prisoner. I am guilty.
GUILTY.

1977. ANN HARRISS was again indicted for stealing 1 pair of ear-rings,
value 58. ; 3 yards of satin, 58.; and 2 yards of silk, 5s.; the goods of
Thomas Whitewood, her master.
MARTHA WHITEWOOD. I missed a pair of ear-rings, and two pieces of
satin-1 knew nothing about these things when I said I would forgive the
prisoner if she replaced the note-she said she had taken the ear-rings, and
I should find them at Foy's, at Deptford, with a few more things I went there
and found them-I did not miss the satin until she acknowledged taking it.
GEORGE Goode. I produce a pair of ear-rings which I got at Mr. Foy's
in Collier-street, Deptford.
Mrs. WHITEWOOD re-examined. These are my ear-rings—they were lost
from an ornament on the fire-place in the parlour.
Guilty. Aged 19.--Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutrix.
Judgment respited.
