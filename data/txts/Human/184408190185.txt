Cross-examined. Q.  I believe your opinion, having seen the cart coming, is, that it was coming at a smart pace? A.  Yes—I was behind it—the pri soner did not fall till the collision had taken place—his fall was partly from the collision, and partly from attempting to stop the horse, immediately the accident had occurred.
   COURT . Q.  At what pace do you think it was going when it struck the woman? A.  Ten or twelve miles an hour, I should think—the prisoner was intoxicated, in my opinion. WILLIAM SMITH( police-constable. K  220.) I took the prisoner into cus tody on the 9th of July, about four o'clock—he was drunk—he said it was not his fault, it was theirs; he was pulling up his horse, and fell out of the cart.
   COURT . Q.  How long did he appear under the influence of intoxication? A.  I cannot say—I left him after half an hour or three quarters, and he was still drunk.
   MR. CLARKSON to ANN WILSON . Q.  Did you not say that when the de ceased sprang to your side, and the child took the curb, she had hardly taken a few steps before you saw the man pulling the reins taut ? A.  No, I never saw the reins in the man's hand at all—( looking at her deposition )—this is my name—( reading ) "I saw the man pulling the reins taut , and I think that turned the cart on her"—it seems my name is to this, but I did not see the reins in the man's hand.
