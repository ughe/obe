eleven months ago we began making free with my master's property-we
went on when the prisoner was there-when he found us out he began the
same himself-- I took three quires of writing-paper out of the drawer, and
gave it to him-he buttoned it up in his coat, and took it home-he knew it
to be master's-he had some ink--he put the bottles into his pocket, and
some stone-blue-I saw some books I was up in the loop-hole of the office,
cleaning it--I put the books out-the prisoner took them, and said they
would do for him-he put them into his coat pocket, and took them away
the same morning-I know these tools and letters-I did not know he had
them-when he took them, I cannot tell-we used to steal every Tuesday,
Thursday, and Saturday-he used to come in the morning and say, "I shall
want some more of that paper I had before"-I had a month's imprisonment
-my master took me back as I promised to be a good boy, and tell the
truth.
Cross-examined. Q. Was the writing-paper the first thing you saw him
take ? A. Yes-it is not here—the ink was the next thing I cannot say
whether he took anything more that morning the books were taken on quite
a different day- I cannot say whether this box was taken on a different day
to the ink and paper--there was ink, stone-blue, and paper taken-I think
the writing-paper and ink were taken at the same time, and the books were
all taken together he took paper at different times the prosecutor found out
that we were robbing, but did not complain to us. I do not think he stopped
me once when I was going to take something out-I cannot say whether he
did or not, I do not think I begged forgiveness of him-I cannot recollect
his stopping and talking to me about wasting my master's property-it may,
have happened—I used to threaten to tell Mr. Ford of this, and Jem used to
knock me about-I might have taken some books—I took a good many
things—I told Mr. Ford every thing I took-I have never taken any books
like these-I used to give all the paper and things that I took to Mr. Wright
- he used to promise us things, and used to give us money-when the pri-
soner heard that we were taken, he got from my master's wife where we were
taken to-he got home as soon as he could, and got all the things that were
my master's as soon as he could, out of his house, because he was frightened
that we should tell..
MR. DOANE. Q. When he stopped you thieving, did you go and tell your
master? A. No.
STEPHEN MANSFIELD (police-constable N 233.) I went to the prisoner's
lodging, in Gray's Inn-lane, last Monday evening-I found him there, and
told him I took him for robbing Mr. Ford-he said there was nothing there
belonging to Mr. Ford, and we were at liberty to search–I searched, and
found a basket by the side of his bed, and, in the basket, the property I have
produced to-night-he said, the small books he bought at stalls, and the rest
were Mr. Ford's.
Cross-examined. Q. Upon your oath, were not the words he said, that the
small books he bought, and none of the others belonged to Mr. Ford ? A. No.
Not Guilty.
