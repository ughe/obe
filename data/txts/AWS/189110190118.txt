THOMAs DOWDELL. I am a porter in the service of the Great Western
Railway Company at Paddington.-o 22nd July I was on duty at the
Lost Property Office about seven p.m.-any luggage on the platform
unelaimed is brought into the office-a tin box with a canvas cover was
brought into the office as unclaimed luggage I think the cover was
drawn together by a string-it was labelled 41 Paddington remained
in the office till about half-past eight; the prisoner then came to claim it
-she had her hand resting on the box as it stood apart-she said,
4 This is my box, I want a cab to take it said, 46 You must
sign your name and address if it is your property"- said yes, she
was quite willing to sign for it-I asked her what train the box had come
by-she said, The train that arrived at 0"-I asked her what train
she arrived by-she said she had only just arrived; there was another
train from the West of England in, within a few minutes' time-she
signed the book, which I produce; I read it as L. Mill, 9, Hunter
treet"-she took the box away-next morning Mr. Faulkner came to
claim bis luggage, and he then complained of the loss of the tin box-I
recalled the fact of the woman having been there the night before, and
the book was examined-] next saw her at Wandsworth Police-court;
I picked her out from a number of other women.
Cross-eramined. The person who claimed the box was dressed as the
person I saw at Wandsworth it was on 22nd July, the same date as is
in our book.

had a Gladstone bag this is it-on my arrival I went to the guard's van
for my bag, and it was not there-I got to the van before any luggage
was got out-the bag contained a quantity of clothing, and three 5

of the railway police-a large part of the contents were still in it; there
were a few things missing besides the notes and several papers
and documents and a few articles of clothing most of the things were
in the bag.
