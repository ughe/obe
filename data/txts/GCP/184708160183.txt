1918. SAMUEL CHARLES PRINCE was indicted for stealing 1 half-
eagle, value 288.; the monies of Eric Orr; in a vessel in a port of entry and
discharge.
ERIC ORR. I am second mate of the James Edwards, which is in the
St. Katharine's Docks; the prisoner was steward. On the 26th of July I
lost a half-eagle, which is worth about 1l. 88.-the prisoner had wanted to
borrow money of me the day before, and I told him I had not got any--he
had no business in my berth that I know of-I missed this piece of money
I took the prisoner to my room and spoke to him-he stated at first that he
had not taken it-at last he said he had taken the half-eagle piece, and told
me to say nothing about it, that he might not have disgrace-I told Sim there
was no necessity for it, he had been bad enough without-he had put the
money in a house, and had spent a cent of it.
Prisoner. I never took it at all; I went to speak to the mate, and this
witness called me into the state-room, and had a rope's-end; he said I had
taken the piece ; I said I had not, but sooner than have a piece of work I
would pay more than the value of it.
ROBERT GIPFORD (policeman.) I took the prisoner--he said he knew
nothing about it; but sooner than have any piece of work he would pay five
times the amount-the next morning he asked if he should plead guilty-1
told him I should give him no advice-he then said, "I took the money,
but it was through driuk and the persuasion of another party."
Ann Wilson. I live at No. 4, Neptune court. On Sunday night the
prisoner came to sleep in that house, and he left his coat for the price of the
room, he had no money-on Monday night, the 26th, he came again with a
gold piece.
Prisoner. I had twenty dollars which the captain paid me; it was my
own money.
Court to ERIC ORR. Q. Did he receive twenty dollars ? A. The cap.
tain would not give him a farthing-I swear he never had a farthing-his
pay was twenty dollars a month; but he acted in such a way that he was
not worth a farthing-he wasted more victuals, and spoiled more than he
was worth.
Prisoner. This witness told me the captain was a very bad captain, and
he said he would take his gold watch and throw it overboard; I am as innocent
as you are ; I did not take it, I assure you; I had my trunk full of clothes,
and 51. owing to me; the Magistrate said I might have them, but the ship
was gone to sea and I could not get them.
Guilty. Aged 18.-Confined Nine Months.
1919. GEORGE WAKEFIELD was indicted for stealing 3 bushels
of beans, value 168.; and 4 sacks, 48.; the goods of Walter Powell, his
master: and CHARLES GOBBY for feloniously receiving the same, well-
knowing them to have been stolen.
Henry Loades. I am in the employ of Mr. Walter Powell—I know the
prisoner Wakefield- he was in Mr. Powell's employ. On the 1st of July he
was ordered to take three sacks of oats and some straw to Mr. Fordham-I
helped him load - he told me to take a sack of oats into Gobby's- I took it
- hallooed out, no one answered, and I left it on the stairs- I saw Wake-
field again that night-he owed me a shilling, and I asked him if he could
pay me, and he gave me 38.- the sack of oats came from my master's loft,
where the other sacks came from.
