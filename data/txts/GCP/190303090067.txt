ARTHUR TRITTON (Detective Sergeant.) On February 5th I went with
another detective to a firm of drapers in Finsbury Road, Hampstead-I
there saw the prisoner- I told him I should take him into custody for
receiving a quantity of black silk which had been stolen from Edmonda,
Denham, and Goyder, of Wood Green-he said, "Yes, all right, I received
it from a man named Bell; I knew that he had stolen it; I met him
at Broad Street Station, I cannot say what evening it was ; he handed
the stuff to me, and I gave him £1 78. 6d. for it; I gave it to another man"
-in the train on the way to Wood Green he said, "I do not know where
Bell lives or works; what does it mean for me? I shall plead guilty "-
he was formally charged and said, "I did not steal it, I know : I received it."
The prisoner, in his defence, stated that on some of the goods there were
no marks at all, and any house in the trade had exactly similar gouds, and
it was just as likely they came from one house as they did from Wood Green.
Guilty. Nine months' hard labour.
