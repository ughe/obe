got out of the archway one of my traces broke—Walders assisted me to mend it, and during the time there was a cart waiting to come up—I pulled my van out into the main road to let the cart come by—when we mended the trace, we came on—this was about half-past seven or a quarter to eight, between seven and eight—I did not see the other two men—they did not join the van any more—Walders kept me company all the way over the bridge till we came to the Old Bailey—he told me to stop at a public-house there—I said, "No, I don't want to stop here, because it is up a hill for my horses"—he said, "Stop and have half a pot of beer"—I stood outside, and had one drink, and away I came—I saw the two men in that public-house, who were in the loft  chucking  the paper down—they drank after me, and Walders also—Walders and I then came on across Smithfield, up Thomas-street to Saffron-hill, to Cohen's house—the other two men did not come on with us—we got to Cohen's house at half-past eight or a quarter to nine—Cohen and Reardon were outside the door—I did not see Walders and the other two any more that night—Cohen, and Reardon, and a little boy, whose name I do not know, helped me unload the van—it might be some strange boy about there—the paper was unloaded, and put into the cellar with the scales, beam, and rope—after the van was unloaded, Cohen asked me how much—I looked at the time, and said, 3 s . 6 d ., which was 18 d . an hour—he changed a half-sovereign at the bar, and paid me—next day, Wednesday, between twelve and one, Reardon came to me, and said, "My master wants you"—he was going to wait till I put my horses to—I told him to go on—I overtook him facing St. Luke's church, in Old-street—he was along with one of the other men that had been along with me over night—that man was drunk—they got up into the van, and I drove to Cohen's house—when I came there, Cohen blowed  him up, and said he would kick his backside for stopping so long—some of the paper was put into the van—I should think sixty or seventy bundles, and the rest in bags—it had been put into bags since the over night—Cohen told me to take them down to a yard down the hill, and be and Reardon accompanied me to the house—when I got there it was an empty room in Pump-court, Saffron-hill—Reardon, Cohen, and a lot of boys about there unloaded the van into the room—I then went to Cohen's house again, and Reardon asked me what was to pay—I said 18 d ., and he paid me 2 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How do you know that the paper which you took in the van to Pump-court, was the same you had brought the day before, if the external appearance was altered? A.  They were tied up in the same bundles—some were in bundles and some in bags—that in the bags was of the same quality as what I had seen the day before—I saw the paper, because the bags were not tied close—it appeared to be the same sort.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You did not see Walders on the 26th? A.  No, I did not know him before—I might have seen him in the market, and not noticed him—I do not know that he is employed as a porter in the market—I did not observe anything remarkable in this employ ment.
