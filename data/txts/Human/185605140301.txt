   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Is it your habit to express the name of luggage? A.  We never give it any particular name. CHARLES POTTER. I am landing waiter and searcher at the Custom House, at Newhaven. I produce a book of the entry of two packages—this entry satisfies me that I examined two packages in the name of Lenden, on Sun day morning, 20th Jan.—the packages were opened by some one for me, and I examined them—I have the name of Lenden, because the person who claims the packages gives his name—as soon as the luggage is examined, I have nothing more to do with it—I make no distinction of registered or unregistered luggage—after I have done with it, it is delivered to the person from me—he may take it from the counter if he pleases—after that, persons may take registered luggage in the railway themselves.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have no recollection whatever of the person or of the bag? A.  No—I know nothing about the number of packages, except from this book—there were thirty-five packages delivered that day—a mark is placed on bags that have been examined—a ticket of this description, bearing the name of the boat, is placed on the bag, or whatever the luggage is, when it is brought up, and then the examining officer, which in this case was myself, stamps that ticket with a red mark, and after that it is counter stamped black—that ticket is fastened on with paste.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  What part is it put on? A.  On a bag it is put at the bottom—it is put on to remain till it gets to the door, and very fre quently they drop off.
