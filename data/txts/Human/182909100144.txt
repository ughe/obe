 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Where was this? A. In a yard up Harrow-alley, in Aldgate High-street - I have no doubt of Parker; I described his jacket - he was not in my presence half a minute, and I had never seen him before; I should not like to be too positive.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Is there any lamp in the passage? A. Yes, a gas-lamp just over where he was - I am positive I never lost sight of Turner; I was close at his heels - I knew him before.
   JOSEPH WHITNEY. I am a watchman. At two o'clock that morning I heard a cry of Stop thief! I saw Turner run across the road - I ran and knocked him down; I took him up, and found in his breast two bottles, in his waistcoat two more, and in the thigh of his trousers this other bottle, broken - there was no one else running.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. What distance was this? A. About forty yards from Harrow-alley, on the opposite side.
   WILLIAM MILLER. I saw Parker running down Petticoat-lane, about two hundred yards from Harrow-alley, just before two o'clock - I told him to stop, and crossed to meet him: I hit him with a stick - he whipped over the way, put his hand into his bosom, took out a bottle, and hove it at me; I ran and sprang my rattle - he made a stop, and threw another bottle at me; my partner came up and stopped him - a third bottle fell from him and broke; there was red wine in that - I took up the necks of the others, but there are no marks on the corks.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. How do you know it was not red mixture for colouring wine? A. I could smell it was wine - there was a gas-light just over our heads; the neck of that bottle was broken.
 JURY. Q. Had you time to notice whether there was any wine in the bottles which he threw away? A. No - it was a very wet night.
   DANIEL SHEPHERD. On Sunday morning, the 16th of August, I was on duty, and heard the rattle spring - I ran up and saw Parker running; I laid hold of him - he let a bottle of wine fall, which broke all to pieces.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. It was a very wet night? A. Not then - it was very fine; it had been raining - I have no doubt the other bottle was intended for me, had it not slipped out of his hand before he had the opportunity of throwing it; I found by the smell that it was port-wine - I swear it was not claret.
 Turner's Defence. I had been at a public-house, and had occasion to go up this passage - I saw the bottles; I was going to apprize the owner of it - there is some sheep dung up the alley.
 Parker's Defence. I work on board some barges, for Mr. Curry, of Russell-buildings - I was going home, up Petticoat-lane, and the first watchman struck me; I certainly had three bottles, but they were not wine - he broke one, and one fell down; I did not run, but was going along, and the other watchman took me.
 MR. HILL. I have now opened the bottles - they are port, and we missed bottles of this description.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Had you occasion to inspect the cellar? A. I had been there two or three days before - no bottle is sold out of that cellar; they come to our house for it - I swear it was not sold, because I keep the books; I could see by the saw-dust that some had been taken.
 COURT. Q. What was the wine kept there for? A. For stock - we never go there for small quantities; we use it as a warehouse - it is a large cellar or vault; I missed fifteen bottles. TURNER -    GUILTY. Aged 27.
 PARKER -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1814.  CHARLES WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , at St. Michael, Cornhill , 28 pieces of foreign gold coin called Napoleons, value 22l., the property of Adey Bellamy Savory , in his dwelling-house.
 SECOND COUNT, the same, only omitting the words in italics.
   ADEY BELLAMY SAVORY. I am a dealer in bullion, and live at No. 14, Cornhill. I had forty-five Napoleons on the 20th of July, and twenty-eight of them were taken; I had not noticed them very particularly; I deal in money generally - they were taken from my son; I was present, but did not see them taken; they were usually kept in a drawer, or in a bowl in the window.
   JOSEPH SAVORY. I am the prosecutor's son. On the 20th of July a person came into the shop and asked for 50l. worth of French money - I counted out forty-five Napoleons into my hand and laid them on the counter; I turned to my desk to reckon what they would come to, and the person who came for them said he would step into Birch's and take something while I made the account; the prisoner then came in, seized a handful of the Napoleons, and ran off - I called my father from the back shop, and jumped over the counter - I pursued the prisoner down Pope's Head-alley and Lombard-street to the Mansion-house; a person I knew, of the name of Finch, was coming by, he saw I could hardly catch him, he caught him, and brought him back - the prisoner was running: Finch is not here; I took the prisoner to our counting-house, and went back to Pope's Head-alley to look for one Napoleon he had dropped, but it was gone; he had the rest of the money on him.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Were they in your possession? A. They were on the counter close before me; I did lose sight of the person who took them for perhaps half a minute or a quarter of a minute - he ran two hundred or two hundred and fifty yards before he was taken; it was about five o'clock - there were a great number of persons about - our dwelling-house is in the parish of St. Michael, Cornhill; the Napoleons were within half a yard of me - they had not been paid for.
 COURT. Q. Had there been any bargain between you? A. No.
