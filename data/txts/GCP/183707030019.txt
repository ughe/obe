JAMES Wood. I live at Grove House, Islington, and am a timber-
merchant. On Saturday, the 27th of May, about six o'clock in the morn-
ing, I saw a cart loading with lead, at Fisher-house-I saw Fletcher
there will not swear positively that the prisoner Norris was there,
but I have no doubt of it--I knew it was wrong, and ran directly to fetch
an officer, and a man who I had on an estate adjoining to protect it-I was
not more than two minutes gone, and when I returned the prisoner and
cart were gone- I have heard Bickers say, but not directly to me, that
they would have the whole of the house down-both Bickers and Norris
said so.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you not understand that Fletcher claimed
Fisher-house and the property close about there as being part of the
homestead ? A. I never heard that he claimed Fisher-house till I heard
he had got into it-I never made any bargain with him for purchasing any
part of it, nor did any part of my family-I have property about there.
MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you hold part of some neighbouring pro-
perty of Miss Jobern? 4. Yes-she is alive I believe-I saw her nine

CHARLES HILTON. I am a dealer in building materials. I went to
Fisher-house on Monday morning, the 29th, and saw Bickers--I asked
him if there were some sashes to be sold at a price which I offered him
he said he could not give me an answer till two o'clock-he said there
were goods to be sold- I pointed out the sashes--they were fixed then in
the back of the house there might be twenty-five-I bid 121. for them
I did not go again at two o'clock, as I heard it was wrong-Bickers refused
to take 121. as it was too little.

RICHARD SWAINE. I am Mr. Pickering's agent in this property, and
have been so for some years, I have seen the premises since this outrage
has been committed—they are in a cruel state-a great quantity of things
have been removed wantonly-I think thirty doors are gone, old carved
oak doore--it would take 3001. to put the house in order.
