 331. GEORGE DUNN  (25) , Stealing a coat and waistcoat, the property of John Antony Rucker .
   MR. ST. AUBYN conducted the Prosecution. ALFRED HASLET. I am a tailor, of 142, Fenchurch Street—on 1st April, about 6 p.m., I was in Star Alley, leading from Mark Lane to Fenchurch Street, and saw the prisoner with several coats on, and a waistcoat tucked under one of them, he crossed over to the Lord Napier, and I gave him in custody; he said that he bought them. JOSEPH SARNES( City Policeman  747). I took the prisoner, and asked him where he got the coats—he said he bought them; I asked him who of, he made no answer, and I took him to the station. WILLIAM OSBORNE( City Policeman  711). I was at the station when the prisoner was brought in, I saw two coats taken from under the one he was wearing, and the waistcoat from under one of them—he made no reply to the charge, he gave his address at a friend's, who I found. JOHN ANTHONY RUTTER. I am clerk to Rugg & Co., of 7, Mark Lane—one of these coats and the waistcoat is mine—I left them on a peg in my office on 1st April, a little after a quarter to six o'clock. SAMUEL THOMAS WEST. I am housekeeper to Good & Rugg—I saw five coats and two waistcoats safe at ten minutes to six o'clock, and missed them next morning.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A man was hawking the coats and asked me to buy them; I bought two and a waistcoat, and the gentleman came up and asked me where I got them; I said that I bought them. You will see hawkers selling second-hand clothes in the streets at all times, and a man who buys them is not liable to know that they are got wrongly. NOT GUILTY.
 332. GEORGE DUNN was again  indicted for stealing two coats, the property of Joseph Sophus Levin .
   MR. ST. AUBYN conducted the Prosecution. JOSEPH SOPHUSLEVIN. I am a printer, of 59 A , Great Tower Street—on 26th March I went home and changed my coat—my errand boy afterwards said something to me, and I missed a great coat, a thin coat, and another coat. THOMAS DAY. I am twelve years old, and am errand boy to Mr. Levin—on 26th March I saw the prisoner in the middle of Red Cross Square, putting on two coats of my master's—I went back to the office and missed the coats, and told my master.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I am innocent. Nobody saw me near the place. GUILTY. He was further charged with a previous conviction in July , 1871, in the name of George Thompson, when lie was sentenced to eighteen months' imprisonment, to which he PLEADED GUILTY .**— Ten Years' Penal Servitude.
