DAVID GREEVES (Policeman K 308). On 2nd August, about 11.20 a.m.,
I took Dance at his mother's house-I told him I should take him in
custody on suspicion of stealing & number of tools from Mr. Rippe's,

of what I am accused of”-he was then taken to the station and charged,
and made no reply.
The prisoners in their statements before the Magistrate and in their defence
stated that they were not near the shed at the time; Dance stated that he was
at Covent Garden, and Wilson that he was employed carrying coffee to a
coffee-stall, and then went home.

953. ELIZABETH LOWE, Feloniously marrying Henry Bish during
the life of her husband.
MR. GREENFIELD Prosecuted; M. GEOGHEGAN Dofonded.
THOMAS EANES (Policeman KR 65). On 2nd August I was on duty at
Bow Bridge, and saw the prisoner—she said “Here you are, governor,
I give myself up for being married twice”-I said “What is your
name?" --she said "Bish” -I said "What is the other name?" she
said "Lowe"-I said " Where were you married ?"-she said "At
the Roman Catholic chapel up there," pointing towards Bow-I said
"Where did the second marriage take place ?" she said "At Stratford
Church"-going to the station she said that she had been told her first
husband had gone out to America and died there from some affection
of the brain, and that she had seen him the previous Saturday, and he
threatened to give her to Bish–I produce the two marriage certificates.
(The first of these certified the marriage of Elizabeth Vigors and Corneliris
Lowe on 18th August, 1883, at Poplar Roman Catholic Church, and the
second the marriage of Henry Bish and Elizabeth Lowe, widow, at St. John's
Church, Stratford, on 20th July, 1887.)
