Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Up to what time did your husband and Allen
have dealings together? A. Up to 17th Aug.- there were no dealings after
that, to the best of my knowledge, I will not say positively-I had told our
attorney all about this receipt.
WILLIAM STONE re-examined. My wife means ibat there was no account
uiter 17th Aug.-he brought me a few goods after the 17th, and I paid him

JOSEPH THOMAS EDRIDGE. I am the son of Mrs. Stone, and live with
ber and her husband. I know Allen, and that he had dealings with Mr.
Stone--there were disputes in the autumn about the acconnts-I recollect
Allen coming on 17th Aug.- I was present the whole of the time he was there
-there were six or seven receipted bills brought down stairs, and given into
Allen's bands-he read them over, and disputed one of them-before they
were fetched, Allen said he should like to see them; and when my mother
brought them down, she said she had brought all the bills and receipts; she
gave them to me, and I spread them out on the table, and handed them to
Allen, one at a time-after Allen left I missed those receipts; I have never
seen them since I was not present when he called afterwards- I can read
and write-I saw this receipt for 10s. and 11. on the back of one of these bills
at the County Court-I heard the clerk of the Court ask Allen if this was his
handwriting, and he said it was not—the 10s. was eventually allowed by the
clerk, Mr. Cheer-I believe it to be Allen's writing, and this other bill also
I have got a bill of his in my pocket-I have seen him write several times
-I gave him a pen and ink to write that I was present whilst this case was
heard at the County Court-I was not examined, or my mother; only Mr.
Stone I heard Allen examined, and he asked his son if he ever wrote a receipt
to a bill-be said, "No"-I have seen the son here to-day outside; he is
Jourteen or fifteen years old, I was at the Court, ready to be examined-I
cannot tell why I was not-the clerk of the Court hurried the case on all he
could-he asked Mr. Nuckey for the bills, to get on with it as quick as he
could-be said he should be kept there all day.
COURT. Q. What time of the day was this? 4. About 1 o'clock, I

taken by the Judge; the Judge did not have anything to do with it.
