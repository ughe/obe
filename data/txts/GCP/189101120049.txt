Mr. Fulton, in reply, contended that the particular jury which the prisoner
was charged with attempting to corrupt was named, and that no greater
particularity was necessary, as the attempt was made to influence the whole jury,
and it would be useless unless they were all influenced 80 as to aequit the
prisoner; as to the second point, he argued that it was not necessary to set
out with greater particularity the means employed (Q. 6. Fuller, Leach's Crown
Cares, 916).
The RECORDER ruled that as the language of 32 Henry VIII., of Black-
stone, of Mr. Justice Stephon, and of the Criminal Code Commissioners had been
singularly uniform, and had always referred definitely to individuals, this
indictment, which charged an attempt to embrace a jury, and did not set out
the names of the jurors on whom the attempt was made, was bad, and should be
quashed. That being his opinion, he did not consider it necessary to go into
the other points as to the sufficiency with which the means were set out.
