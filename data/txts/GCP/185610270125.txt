did he tell him he came to take him in custody on a charge of taking a
Watch from the Horns A. Yes, he asked me if that was the man that
took the watch—I said “Yes"-the prisoner said, at the police station, that
he was innocent, and it was a mistake I did not hear him say that in the
room-he might have, and I not heard it. I said to the mother, Give me
the watch that he took from my master's house I paid no attention to
what the prisoner said I do not know what he said; he was half asleep
I saw the policeman feel the prisoner's pocket- there was no watch found
I believe the watch belonged to a young lady, named Mary Mahoney,
who left it with with Mr. O'Sullivan—it had been hanging there from the
Monday week before it was taken—it had been there several days on this

minutes before they took the wateh- there was a great crowd, men and
women---they were not quiet a good many had been drinking, but they
had nothing to drink there—they kicked up a row, then they left, and in
ten minutes the prisoner and another man came back-no one came with
them--there was no one in the bar but myself—my master was at Dept
ford-the other man was a bricklayer's labourer--he came and had his din-
