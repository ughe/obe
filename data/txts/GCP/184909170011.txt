JOHN GARRETT re-examined. I think Cooper was a partner, but he left

1716. THOMAS WEST, unlawfully obtaining goods by false pretences,
of Robert Spence and others.--Other COUNTS, for obtaining goods of other
parties.
MR. BRIARLY conducted the Prosecution. To se
WILLIAM BROOKE. I am a salesman in the employ of Robert Spence and
Co., general warehousemen, of 5, Love-lane. On 30th Aug. the prisoner
brought a pattern of black satinet, and asked me for a length of 32 or 38
yards for two dresses, and said he came from Griffiths', in the Strand—they
are customers of our house - I believed he came from them, and gave the
satinet to Haile.
WILLIAM HAILE. I am warehouseman to Robert Spence, Baggally, and Co.
On 30th Aug. I received some satinet from Mr. Brooke, and gave it to the
prisoner-he went away with it.
James GrippiTHS. I am in partnership with my brother John, at 246,
Strand; we are linendrapers and hosiers, and deal with Spence and Co. I
never saw the defendant in my life, or the satinet.
GEORGE ARNISON HANSLER. I am warehouseman to William Carlisle and
others, of Bow-lane, Cheapside, wholesale baberdashers. On 30th Aug. the
prisoner came and asked for ilb. of sewing silk-I weighed it, and he asked
for 6lbs. of thread, and produced some to show what kind-he produced this
bill-head, from James Griffiths of the Strand—that would be understood as
an order in the trade-I asked permission, and then entered the goods, and
gave them to the prisoner-he signed this book as receiving them--they were
worth 11. 58. 6d.
Prisoner. Q. There is no signature to the order? 4. There was another
attached to it which was signed, but you told me to tear it off for you to take
to Spence's to get the satinet.
JAMES GRIFFITHS re-examined. I gave no order for this sewing silk and
thread-I never saw the prisoner.
