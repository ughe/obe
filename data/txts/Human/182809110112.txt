 1746.  MARTHA CLARE was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of August , 4 shirts, value 7s., the goods of William Gail.
   PHILIP GAIL. I live at No. 5, Thrawl-street, Spitalfields. On the Friday before the 28th of August; there were three shirts of my brother's and one of mine, hanging on a string in an empty room in that house; some were worth 2s. and some 2s. 6d.; I took the prisoner on the stairs with them under her arm, and she dropped them down; I called some people, and took them up: I took the prisoner to the office; my brother lived in the house with me.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have your shirts any marks? A. No; I did not see them lying about the stairs, but I saw her drop them; I had never seen her before; some English people lodged in the house.
 WILLIAM GAIL. I lost three shirts; I saw the prisoner with three of mine and one of my brother's - I took her to the office.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you at home? A. Yes; I never knew her before.
   MOSES SIMMONS. I am a constable. I was sent for to the watch-house, and found the prisoner there; these shirts were produced to me.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to see a friend, and these Germans ran after me as I came down the stairs, and seized me; they dragged me back into the room.
 COURT to PHILIP GAIL. Q. How many live in the room? A. Two of my sisters, three girls, and six men; they are relations of mine.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Transported for Seven Years.
 1747.  SAMUEL DREW was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 1 looking-glass, value 24s., the goods of Jacob Enticott.
   MARY ENTICOTT. I am the wife of Jacob Enticott; we live in Long-alley, and keep a broker's shop. On the morning of the 24th of July, about half-past eight o'clock, I was in my parlour, and saw the prisoner at my window - he leaned ever, and took this glass out of the shop; I went to the door, saw him put it on his shoulder, and walk away- I went after him, took hold of his sleeve, and asked where he was going to take the glass; he said it was no business of mine - I told him it was; I took hold of the glass with one hand, and him with the other; he tried to break it, and struck me two or three times; I called a girl to take the glass from me; I called Mr. Gardener, who took him into custody - I never lost sight of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. She is mistaken in the person; it was another man.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 1748.  WILLIAM EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 penknife, value 2s.; 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence, the property of Samuel Bonner.
   MARY BONNER. I am the wife of Samuel Bonner, who is a butcher; I have been separated from him for some time: I partly get my living by needle-work, and I receive the visits of gentlemen. On the Saturday night previous to the 17th of August, I saw the prisoner on the Pavement, near Finsbury-square - he was a stranger; he asked me where I was going, and whether I would take a glass of anything to drink; I went with him, and had a glass of gin- he then went home with me, and there he gave me 1s. for drink; he asked me if he could stay all night; I said he might if he thought proper - he asked what he must give me; I said a sovereign - he said he could not afford so much, he would give me half a sovereign; he gave me a sovereign, and I gave him half a sovereign in exchange - I put the sovereign into my pocket; I slept with him that night - we got up about seven o'clock in the morning, and walked out; he breakfasted with me - I provided the breakfast; we dined together, and after dinner I went up stairs - he came up after me; I took off my pocket, with my sovereign in it, and put it under the pillow; my brother called on me, and I went down to him - he gave me two half-crowns and two shillings; I changed one of the shillings, went up stairs, and put the remaining silver into my pocket; I laid down, and went to sleep - when I awoke the prisoner was still there; I heard a voice below, and came down, leaving him up stairs washing his hands - he came down in about two minutes; he had agreed to stay with me, and to go out walking, but he came down, and went straight to the door; I asked where he was going - he said he should be back in a few minutes, by the time I was dressed; I ran up stairs, found my pillow turned over, and the money and pocket all gone; I had a penknife in my pocket, with a white handle and two blades, and a bit of gold on one side of the handle; I looked out of the window, and saw him just turning the corner - I called my brother, and told him I had been robbed; I saw the prisoner again on the Friday following - I caught hold of him, and told him he was the man who had robbed me, but if he would give me what he took from me, I would do nothing to him; he said he would see me d - d first - I had put the money by towards my rent, which I pay monthly.
