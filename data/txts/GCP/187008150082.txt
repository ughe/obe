WILLIAM PROK (Detective Officer). On Thursday, 21st July, I went after
Mrs. Selfe, at No. 12, Bouverie Street, Paddington-she was at home with
Greene- I told her the charge—she said "I never had any of the jewellery,
and what wearing apparel you find has been given to me"- she said that
Greene was her laundress-I found a box in her room, searched it in her
presence, and found the articles produced-I found these ear-drops at the
bottom of the box-she jumped up and said " Take it and put it in your
pocket, and say nothing about it; I trust to your honour"-I found a
number of things had been pawned by Greene, at Smith's, in the Edgware
Road.

Cross-examined by MR. COLE. Q. What was Greene doing when you went

wrong."
WILLIAM GREENE. I am assistant to Mr. Smith, a pawnbroker of
the Edgware Road-Greene pawned those things on 14th July, in the
name of “ Sell, 5, Call Street, Paddington."
Cross-examined by MR. COLE. Q. Is it a very common thing for cus-
tomers to pawn goods in other names than their right ones? A. Yes, and
to give false addresses, too.
JOHN JAMES WILLIAM SIMMONS. I am the prosecutor-I identify this
jewellery-I put it into the drawer when my wife was ill-she never told

Cross-examined by MR. MOIR 8. Did you give Selfe anything? A. A
half-sovereign, because I thought she was a good nurse-I had two ladies
besides Selfe attending on my wife.
The prisoners received good characters.
