WILLIAM COLLINGS( police-sergeant R  18.) In consequence of informa tion from Cruton, I went with him into New-street, Deptford—I found Jones in Crown-court there—I asked her how long she had been in—she said she had not been out that evening—I asked if she had been to any hair-dresser's shop—she said she had not—at that time White and Turner came in—I asked White the same question—she denied all knowledge of having been in the shop—I sent for Morgan, who identified White and Jones—I afterwards found a piece of silk at their lodging—they said they lived there with Turner—I found the silk lying on the floor—I afterwards found in a room in that street, this watch, wrapped up in two shawls in the possession of Moor—she said something as I went into the room—it is four doors from where the other prisoners live—on my entering the room she snatched up a bundle which lay on the floor—I immediately took it from her, and in the middle of two shawls I found this watch—I asked how she came by it, and all she said was, "I took it." WILLIAM JAMES( police-constable R  83.) I received information, and went to No. 2, Crown-street, New-street—I found Jones there with the police-constable and sergeant—I said to Jones, "There are three females live here"—she said the other two were gone away to the privy—I directly turned out of the house, and saw White coming from the house four doors off, where Moor lives—she was pulling the door to with her right hand—she came into Jones's apartment—we searched the house, but could find nothing. WILLIAM COLLINGS re-examined . Neither of the prisoners were in the room I found the silk in—it was Turner whom we suspected—it was at White and Jones's room that they told me they rented the room—I asked each of them if they rented it, and they said the room belonged to them jointly. GEORGE MORGAN re-examined . I can identify this piece of silk by frequently seeing it—I know it by the colour and the shape—I had such a piece in my watch—I was about five minutes in the room after I came from the shop with Jones—the women wished to go home—I was in the shop with Jones eight or ten minutes—we were both in the room about twenty minutes before that. THOMAS MORGAN. I am the prosecutor's brother. I was in the room with White about ten minutes, or more—I asked George for some money, which he refused, and the women went away about five or ten minutes after—I was about five minutes in the shop with my brother—I had left White in the parlour, and directly I came into the shop Jones went into the parlour to White; they went away because my brother refused to give any money.
   White . Thomas Morgan gave me the watch to satisfy me, as his brother had no money. Witness . Nothing occurred between me and White—I had half-a-crown and sixpence in my pocket—I went to get change from my brother—White hardly spoke to me—we could not agree—I gave her no thing, and had nothing at all to do with her—I did not give her the watch.
   Jones's Defence.  I saw Thomas Morgan give White the watch when I came into the parlour, leaving George Morgan in the shop.
