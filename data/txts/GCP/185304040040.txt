Jury. Q. What did you say to him? 4. I told him it was no use to

Prisoner's Defence. The money found in my box was what was given to
me; some would give me farthings, and some halfpence.
Georgs WHEELER. I am a cow keeper at New Brentford Butts. I have
known the prisoner about twelve months-he worked for me I gave him
this bad halfpenny myself-he said, “This is not the first time you have
given me a bad halfpenny"-I said, "I can't help it; I took it and you
must take it"-I was not at the police office.
Court. Q. How came you to know about any halfpenny? 4. Because
the halfpence were taken out of his box in my house--the officer came to
my bed-room window, and counted them over, 1s. 5 d. I gave the prisoner
this halfpenny on Saturday afternoon, between 4 and 5 o'clock, with some
more-I did not pay him any wages-I used to give him a few halfpence out
of my pocket when he wanted a pint of beer, or a screw of tobaccp I gavo
him a few halfpence that afternoon to get some drink, between 4 and 5 in
the day--the halfpenny was an old George the 3rd., and had a hole through
it I have never found the least thing in the world against the prisoner I
have trusted him out with my goods, and to take money I cut wood, and
that is what he has been employed in.
Court to Mrs. STIMPSON. Q. You say that when the boy objected to the
halfpenny, you said, "I took it, and I don't wish you to take it?" 4. Yes:
I took it that day, but I do not know in what part of the day-the prisoner
was not in my place to my knowledge.
THOMAS STIMPSON re-examined. The prisoner had been in my house
two or three times; but I had not seen him there for a month.
SARAH ANN STIMPSON re-examined. I had not seen the prisoner there
that afternoon.
EDWARD FIELDER re-examined. Brannan saw the halfpenny before the
