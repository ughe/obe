FREDERICK DICKER (Detective Sergeant). I live at Plaistow-at 7.45
p.m. on 5th June I was in Lilliput Road, near the tidal basin railway
station, and saw the prisoner and Sevrin-the prosecutor banded a
piece of paper to a man passing by-the man pointed in the direction
of the docks-the prisoner said "Yes, that is right, come along"-they
went some little distance down the road to the corner of Frederick's
Road, where the prisoner said “This way"-they went down Frederick's
Road-Sevrin went into several shops, and showed the same piece of
paper--the shopkeepers came out and directed him-the prisoner went
in with him on each oocasion-the prosecutor would go towards the
docks, but the prisoner would cateh hold of him and take him in the
contrary direction--they went towards the station-Sevrin kept pushing
the prisoner, and making motions with his hand to go away--they went
to the railway station, where Sevrin showed the paper to some people
after remaining there a few minutes they went to the public-house
opposite-Sevrin again showed the piece of paper—they came out and
went to the station again, to the booking office, where Sevrin laid down the
money, and the prisoner said “Two," and showed the piece of paper to the
clerk -the train came in, but the prisoner prevented Sovrin going to it,
saying it was nearer to walk-they went to the Connaught Hotel-I
went in too—the prisoner asked for a glass of ale--Sevrin paid—they
came out and went to the dock-gates, spoke to the constable at the gates,
and returned to the hotel, had two glasses of ale, and went out-before
I followed out I heard cries-I went out and saw the prisoner with his
hand apparently round Sevrin's waist--the prisoner immediately ran
away some distance, and was leaping a fence at the bottom of the road
when I caught hold of his leg and threw him down, saying, “I am a
police officer"-he said "I will surrender”-I took him to the station-
when charged he said “He is only a foreigner; he don't know what he
has lost" I searched the ditch ; it is a large tidal ditch, 6 or 8 yards
wide, with several feet of water; it runs parallel to the fence about 18
yards off-just as the prisoner was making the jump I saw him throw
something away-it went in the direction of the water there is high
grass on both sides of the ditch-the moment it was daylight I searched
-I could not find anything-the ditch is never empty.
