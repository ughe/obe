William Norton. I am a tailor, and live in Cross-street, Hatte
garden, in the parish of St. Andrew, Holborn. The prisoner was
journeyman. On Sunday, the 27th of September, I left the house
seven o'clock in the evening, and returned at eleven o'clock-I had left the
prisoner and my two sons in the house, but nobody else—when I retured
the prisoner was gone-he lodged with me, but did not come home after
wards-he absconded--I had left my purse, with ten sovereigns and fou
teen half-crowns in it, and a small key; in a bottom drawer of a chest of
drawers, in my bed-room- the room door was not locked-I had seen the
property safe at four o'clock in the afternoon-when I came home I found
him gone-I examined, and missed the purse and money-I was at home
from four to seven o'clock-nobody had been in the house except a woman
who was not out of my sight.
JAMES NORTON. I am the prosecutors son. On the 27th of September,
I came home at about seven o'clock in the evening-my father went out
after that, and I remained at home, with my brother and the prisoner-
we were all three in the kitchen-I and my brother went out about hall.
past eight o'clock, leaving the prisoner at home-I had shut the door
after me I returned in ten minutes I knocked at the door, and be
opened it-there was nobody but him in the house-he proposed having
something to drink, and as he had told me he had no money of his own,
gave him 6d. to fetch a pot of beer-he went out to fetch it, and never T-
turned-I had been at home from seven o'clock, except for ten minutes.
Prisoner. Q. Can you swear I said I had no money? A. I do say so-
you did not show me any money-my brother did not tell me he had
given you some money.
WILLIAM VEARY. I live at West Wycomb, in Bucks. I took the pri
soner into custody about three quarters of a mile from West Wycomb,
Tuesday the 6th of October—I told him he was my prisoner, and that
there was so and so in the “Hue and Cry"-I told him it was for robbing
his master, Mr. Norton-he said he knew no such man as Norton-
searched him and found two sovereigns, one at the bottom of each stock
ing, on his feet; also one sovereign in a snuff-box, and he had one in a little
slip cut in his coat, one loose in his coat pocket, and 4s. 1 d. in bis
trowsers pocket.
(The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he had fractured his
skull when a child, ever since which, drink bad produced temporary in-
sanity, in which state he must have been when he committed the offence.
WILLIAM NORTON re-examined. I never knew him exhibit symptoms
of insanity-I have heard him say he met with an accident some years
ago-he has a habit of pawning his clothes, and every thing when in li-
quor-I always had a good opinion of him.
GUILTY. Aged 26.-Transported for Life.
