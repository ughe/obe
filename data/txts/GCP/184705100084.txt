1201. FRANCIS COCKERELL FREWIN was indicted for feloniously
breaking and entering a certain building, within the curtilage of the dwelling.
house of John James Messer and another, and occupied therewith, and stealing
14lbs. weight of copper, value 128.; and 20lbs. weight of brass, 108.; the
goods of John James Messer and another.
John JAMES MESSER. I bave premises at Nos. 19. and 20, King-street,
St. George's-in-the-East. On Saturday night, the 24th of April I was the last
person there I left the premises all secure and fastened the premises abut
on Grove-court-there is a sky-light to the foundry, and there is some
timber there-between twelve and one o'clock in the morning of Mon-
day, the 26th of April, I received information, and went to the foundry-1
found the inner door of the foundry open and the things disturbed-there were
foot-marks there---some person had removed the skylight and descended on
some loam that was there-I missed about 20lbs. weight of old brass, and
some copper-I afterwards saw some found in the court-it was mine- I had
seen it safe on the Saturday night--the premises belong to me and another
person.
Prisoner. I never did such a thing in my life before : I had a place to go to
on the following Monday; I did not know what to do to keep myself till
then.
Mary Swar. I live in Grove-street. On that Sunday night, about twelve
o'clock, I saw the prisoner coming off the prosecutor's foundry, at the top of
Grove court-I looked to see where he got down-he got down in Grove
court, and from there into Grove-street- I went down to the street-door to tell
the policeman---the prisoner passed while I was at the door, and his show
were in his hand-I told the policeman-he brought him to me at six o'clock
in the morning-I said he was the person.
EPHRAIM STOCKER (police-constable H 138.) Mrs. Swan gave me a de
scription of the prisoner, and I took him about six o'clock on the Monday
morning, in Grove-court-be had a bag in his hand-I asked him what he
was about-he said he was doing nothing there, he was going to his work-
he said the thing he had was his apron- I found it was a bag- I said I be-
lieved he was come for some copper which I had found in a privy there I took
him to Mrs. Swan, and she identified him-he then said, “I shall tell no more
lies about it: I did take the copper for 38. 6d. he owed me" -- this is the
copper.
MR. MESSER re-examined. I never owed him anything-he was never in
my employ—he had been employed on the premises a year ago.
Guilty. Aged 18.---Confined Three Months.

1202. WILLIAM HAYNES was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief
value 18., the goods of Charles Aylett, from his person.
CHARLES AYLETT. I live at Walworth. On the 28th of April, about
twelve o'clock, I was in Farringdon-street-I felt a tug at my pocket-I
turned and saw the prisoner- I took him, and dragged him out of a crowd,
and called, " Police!"- the prisoner dropped my handkerchief-some person
knocked my hat over my eyes--I should have lost the prisoner if two gentle-
men had not come and held him till the officer came--this is my handkerchief
and the one which the prisoner dropped.
Guilty. Aged 20.-Confined Three Months.
