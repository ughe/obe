JOHN SEARL. I am a coachman. This is my cape—I lost it from an omnibus in the stable-yard, in the Kent-road—I left it in the omnibus my self—the yard was not backed up—I know nothing of the prisoner.
   Prisoner's Defence . I work for Mr. Harris, who comes to Billingsgate market—on the 4th of February as I was going along Fleet-street it began to rain very heavy—I went with my master to the market; I took the cape off the horse and put it over my shoulder to keep them dry—I went home with it, and next morning the policeman stopped me with it. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Three Months.

 862. SAMUEL STEED was indicted for embezzlement.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution . FRANCES DREW. I am the wife of Thomas Drew, who sells ladies shoes in Newington-cause way. I know the prisoner—I have seen him come at different times to purchase shoes for his master—he never paid more than 4 s . 6 d . for satin shoes—I do not recollect selling him kid shoes myself—we charge 3 s . 6 d . for them—on the 30th of December he had four pairs and he wanted something allowed for himself—I told him I could not allow any thing—I made out the bill in the regular was—I cannot say whether he wanted it allowed on the bill, or for himself afterwards—on the 6th of Ja nuary he came to our shop, and bought some satin shoes—he paid 4 s . 8 d . for them—we have a fixed price, and never deviate from that—the prose cutor called on me a few days ago with the officer to make enquiries of me, and I gave him this account. PETER HUBURN. I am a boot and shoe-maker, and live at Denmark-hill. The prisoner was in my service some years—I had reason to com plain of him, and discharged him—I afterwards found there was something wrong about satin and kid shoes—I went to Mrs. Drew, and made inquiry of her, and she gave me an account about her satin and kid shoes—I had been in the habit of sending the prisoner to Drew's and other persons, to purchase kid and satin shoes, and when he returned he told me the price—on the 6th of January I sent him for satin shoes, and on his return he said he gave 5 s . for them and bought them at Mrs. Drew's—that was what I paid him—I always gave him the money before he went.
   COURT . This is not embezzlement. NOT GUILTY
