 404. WILLIAM HARWOOD , stealing 1 truss of hay, value 1 s  . the goods of Thomas Skennerton , his master. THOMAS SKENNERTON. I keep cows, at Wanstead. On 29th Dec., from some reason, I said to the prisoner, "Have you not sold a truss of hay from my stable?"—he said, "No, Sir"—I said, "Have you not sold one to Fraser?"—he said, "Yes;" and when he had finished his breakfast he walked out, and said no more about it—I never authorised him to sell hay, and he has never given me money for any he has sold. JOHN FRASER. My father sent me to Mr. Skennerton's—I saw the pri soner, and asked him whether he thought his master could let me have a truss of hay—he said he would go and see—he went to the house, came back, went to the loft, and gave it me out—I offered him 2 s . 2 d . for it, but he only took 1 s  —he did not say why he did not take the 2 s . 2 d . HENRY PARKER( policeman, K  272). I took the prisoner, and told him the charge—he said, "Are you going to collar me?"—I said, "Yes"—at the station, he said he sold the hay for 1 s .
   Prisoner's Defence.  I told Mr. Skennerton I had sold it, and I worked for him after that till three o'clock. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury.— Confined Two Months
   KENT CASES .
