in the Post Office, a letter containing two pairs of stockings, and an orde

Mr. CoWIE, Q.C., with MR. BAGGALLAY Prosecuted: MR. BURNIE Defended
EZRA BARNWELL MILES. I am the son of the postmaster at Lutten
worth, and assist him in the office-on 9th February I issued this postal
order for 108. to Miss Collins, of Bittiswell—the names of the payinz
office and the payee were left in blank-that was the only order we issued
that day-there is a sub-office at Bittiswell, close to Lutterworth in the
ordinary course a letter posted at Bittiswell between 6 and 7 p.m. would
come to Lutterworth the same evening in time for the 8.20 dispatch.
JULIA COLLINS. I am in service at Bittiswell Hall, Lutterworth-1
obtained this postal order for 108. at the office at Lutterworth on 9th Feb
ruary—the name of the paying office and the payee were then in blank-
I did not fill up those blanks--I kept it in my possession till 13th Feb
ruary, and then posted it with two pairs of child's stockings, one nany
blue and the other grey, and gave it to Margaret Hobson to post at Bitlis
well—I addressed it to Miss Carter, Crown Inn, Chotham, Pulborough,
Sussex—this pair of blue stockings are those I put in; they have beta
worn.
MARGARET HOBBON. I am a servant at Bittiswell Hall, Lutterworth
the evening of 13th February Collins gave me a letter to post-it was
addressed to Miss Carter, at Pulborough-it was properly fastened up-
I posted it at the Bittiswell sub-office between 6 and 7.
ELLEN CARTER. I live at the Crown Inn, Cootham, Pulborough-I did
not receive any packet from Collins containing stockings or a postal order
-the signature to this order is not mine, or by my authority.
JOHN ARTHUR DEVANY. I am employed at the branch post-office
Seething Lane-on 14th February, about 2 in the afternoon, the prisons
presented this order, and I paid him 108.--I think I asked him to fill in
the name of the office, Seething Lane, and his own namo-my initials ar
on the order.
Cross-examined. The prisoner lives with his mother close by our office
-I knew the prisoner very well.
JOSEPH FRAYER. I am overseer in the Inlard Branch of the General
Post Office-I know the prisoner-a letter packet leaving Lutterworth
at 20 minutes past 8 on 13th February would reach the General Post
Office in London by the 4.30 train a.m.—the prisoner was on duty
that time at the S. E. division-& letter directed to Pulborough would
pass through that division, and the prisoner would have access to i.-
I know his handwriting—the signature to this order is his writing.

extra sorters that morning, being Valentine's morning—there were four
or five other sorters besides the prisoner.
