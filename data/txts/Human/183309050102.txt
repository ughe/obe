   WILLIAM BROCK. I am a pawnbroker. On the 20th of July I took in this shirt from a man in the name of Wythe; I lent 3s. on it - I cannot say the prisoner is the man, but I gave him this duplicate.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Had you ever seen him before? A. No; he made no concealment of it; the shirt has a mark on it.
   RICHARD STEVENS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Brick-lane, Spitalfields. I have a shirt, pawned on the 29th of July; I cannot say by whom - this is the duplicate we gave.
 WILLIAM WOODS. The prisoner had been working for me on the 20th of July - these shirts were taken from a drawer in the room No. 5; they belonged to a gentleman who lodged there; they are marked T G G 8 32, and T G G 4 31.
 Cross-examined. Q. Who do they belong to? A. To Mr. Glover, who is out of town - all his shirts are marked in this way, I had looked at a great many of them; I don't know that I have seen the whole of them; I cannot swear that I had seen these two shirts - Mr. Glover is out of town, I don't know where - these shirts were not locked up, but kept in a drawer in his room - I have a number of servants, and they have access to that room - I certainly did not take an account of the number of shirts Mr. Glover left, but I have no doubt these are his; I don't know the number he had; I don't think he ever gave these away - there were a number of workmen in my house in June, and I believe the prisoner was one.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought these two shirts and a handkerchief of a man in the new Post-office for 10s. 6d.
   Samuel Bennet, bricklayer, Hackney-road; Edward Richards, a carpenter; William Marblay, baker, Brick-lane; Thomas Little, carpenter, Hackney-road; Mrs. Wilson; and Edward Tighe, gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1354.  WILLIAM WYTHE was again indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 1 cup, value 1s.; 1 saucer, value 1s.; 2 bottles, value 6d.; 3 pints of wine, value 6s.; and 1 towel, value 2s., the goods of William Woods.
   WILLIAM WOODS. I keep the Castle and Falcon. The prisoner was employed in June, July, and August, as a journeyman plasterer there - on the 2nd of August I found him too much intoxicated to go on with his work - I sent one of my servants for his basket, and in it I found this cup and saucer, and two bottles of wine, and a towel, which are mine; the towel has my name on it - the wine had been taken out of a binn in my cellar; I don't know whether the prisoner had followed my porter down, or whether he had opened the lock, but I know the wine was mine.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Is the wine here? A. No, but the bottles had a mark, which my servant put on in my presence; I knew them to be mine - there is nothing here but the towel, it is marked Woods, 1830 - the prisoner was so drunk as not to know what he did - the basket was in the room in which he had been working just before; I had been in the room, and ordered him to quit the house - I did not see the wine nor the towel in his basket then - his own labourer and a carpenter were at work in the same room - I don't know whether his own man could see the basket, for he was intoxicated also - I don't know whether the prisoner brought a towel there on the Tuesday before.
   LUCY WILSON. I am in the prosecutor's service. I brought the basket down from the room the prisoner had been in; the two bottles of wine, a cup and saucer, and a towel, were found in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is the wine, or cup, or saucer here? A. No, only the towel - I did not see the prisoner at that time, he had just gone down.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had not seen the basket after two o'clock; there were three other men in the room.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. Transported for Seven Years longer.
 1355.  SAMUEL BOULTER was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of August , 1 bag, value 3s.; 1 shirt, value 3s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 14s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s.; 1 cravat, value 2s.; 1 razor, value 1s.; 1 razor-strop, value 1s.; and 1 shaving-brush, value 1s., the goods of William Rhodes , being in a certain vessel on the navigable river Thames.
 WILLIAM RHODES. I am a  clerk in the Excise. On the 3rd of August I went on board the Kent steam-boat at Fresh-wharf, London-bridge - I had a carpet-bag, containing the articles stated, which I put into a locker over the paddle-box - when I got to Gravesend the bag was gone - I heard of it by an advertisement in about ten days after - I cannot say whether the prisoner was on board.
   JOSEPH GREEN. I belong to the Royal William steamboat. I went down into the cabin on the 15th of August, and saw the prisoner with a carpet-bag - I gave information, and he was taken into custody - that bag did not belong to this prosecutor.
   JAMES HENRY EGLINGTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a shirt, a pair of drawers, and two handkerchiefs - I don't know who pawned them - I gave this duplicate for them.
   JOHN BARLEY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pair of trousers - I don't know who pawned them - I gave this duplicate for them.
   JOHN RANGCROFT. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, on the 15th of August, on board the Royal William - I found on him a pocket-book containing fifteen duplicates, two of which relate to this property.
   WILLIAM RHODES. These are my property - I went on board at four o'clock, and they were pawned the same night - they were stolen on the river Thames.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). I have every reason to believe that the whole of the transaction is a gross conspiracy against me, for the express purpose of extorting money from me- first they prevailed on me to purchase the duplicates for seven shillings at the half-way house (the sign of the George) to the West India Docks, in which Docks I am employed. I purchased the duplicates of two Scotchmen hawkers. On my arrival opposite the Mansion-house, when I was going to fetch the tickets, my prosecutor stopped me, and said if I would give him the tickets, and a trifle of money, he would not prosecute me; but I objected, and then he informed me that three men was gone to my lodgings to apprehend me again.
