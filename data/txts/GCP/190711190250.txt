ELIZABETH FRANKLIN, 43, May Road, Plaistow. On October 1 last
I was living at 111, St. Mary's Road, Plaistow, where I occupied one
room on the third floor. I went to bed that night between 11 and
11.30. Hill lived there. He did not sleep in my room. Sometimes
he would pay me a visit in the night. I heard him come in that
night but did not see him. I think he came in between 1 and 2. He
did not come into my room. I was up at 10 in the morning. Stowe
came downstairs. He did not live there. I heard Mrs. Hood, who
lived in the house, say to him, "Have you got any beer 1" He
said, "Yes, send the jug up." I took a jug upstairs, where I saw
Hill and Hayes. Stowe had gone out into the yard. Hayes lodged
with Hill. Hill had been in arrears with his rent, but paid the
landlady 8s. that morning, and gave me 2s. He was hard up the
day before in fact the whole three never had nothing that I can
prove. On the morning of October 2 they all went out about 11.
Cross-examined by Stowe. I did not see you come in at night-time.
I saw you in the morning about 10.
Cross-examined by Hayes. I never saw you after pension morning
at all. I saw you out with Mrs. Hood and Tom. You came home
late. I was waiting up for you.
To Mr. Justice Ridley. "Pension night is October 1. They were
home on pension night. I do not live at 111, St. Mary's Road now.
Cross-examined by Hill. I remember your paying Mrs. Hood 8s.,
and you called me out of the kitchen to give me 2s. The detective
called and asked me if I had been pawning any stolen goods or
jewellery. I said, "No."
Cross-examined by Hayes. As far as I know you came in between
1 and 2. I had no clock.
Re-examined. I have been to Brixton Prison to see Hill. I have
not talked over anything. It is the truth what I have said. I
thought I heard Stowe laugh when he came in on pension night.
