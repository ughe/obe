John CABAN (Sergeant, H.) At ll p.m. on Thursday, April 28th, I went
to 47, Nelson Street, accompanied by a man named Carniver--I saw the
prisoner and said to him, “Mr. Carniver wishes to give you into oustody
for stealing two diamond rings "-he replied, "It is not true, I did not
have the rings yesterday, on Tuesday I took two rings to sell for Mr.
Carniver on credit; I was robbed; the rings were taken away "-I told
him he would be charged-he replied, "All right”-I took him to the
station-when charged he made no reply--the next day he was brought
before the Magistrate, and on that day I and Carniver were examined
as witnesses the prisoner was then remanded-on Tuesday, May 6th,
the other charges were made against him-he said, “Why did not they
say they gave them to me! I did not steal them "- I went to his premises
and examined the window which was pointed out to me- I found no
marks on it, or the bedroom door.
Cross-examined. The prisoner was the first person who reported to
the police.
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he had carried on business
in London as a jeweller for twenty-three years, that this soar the first accusa-
tion ever brought against him ; that he was not in any financial difficulty at
the time, that he always paid his rent, rates, and taxes ; that his business was
to get jewellery from other dealers and try and sell it, that he sometimes kept
jewellery a week, a fortnight, or three weeks ; that it would be useless taking
it for two hours, that he sometimes had as much as £600 or £700 worth ; that
on April 25th, 26th, and 27th he had many customers wanting jewellery, as
it was just before the Jewish holidays, that it was untrue that he went with
Carniver to a pasonbroker's; that he had never denied that he had the jewellery
from the prosecutors, that a man named Atlas was a good deal in his company
on the Monday and Tuesday, that he (Atlas) had often been to the prisoner's
house, that what he had said with regard to the burglary was true; that he
could not say who had stolen the goods , that he had offered to pay back part
of the value of the things, which offer had not been accepted, that he had never
said to Mrs. Bromberg that he had left the things at home while he went to the
music hall , that it was safer to carry the jewellery about with him than leave
it at home, that he could neither read nor write, and that he had dealt with
these dealers entirely on trust all these years, and had never robbed anybody
of a penny
