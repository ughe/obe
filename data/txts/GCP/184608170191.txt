Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. How far were you from them?
A. Within one house of them- they came out of No. 1, Rupert-street-I was
opposite No. 3-I followed them because they were going the same way as I
was--they went up the court, and I lived up the court-I went into my own
door, and saw no more of them--they were in the court when I entered it.
CHARLES DORKMAN. I live in Tollington-street, Whitechapel-road. On
Friday night, the 17th of July, between twelve and one o'clock, I saw
Webb, just by Whitechapel-pump, going towards Rupert-street, about two
minutes' walk from it-I knew him-he asked me to have a pint of beer, and
I refused-he and Gill lived at my mother's at the time.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did not he say, “Holloa, Charley,
how are you?" A. Yes, and asked me to have half a pint of beer.
AMELIA SCARLETT. I am single, and have been staying some time with
Mrs. Dorkman-I knew the prisoners Webb and Gill. On Saturday, the 18th
of July, between four and five o'clock in the morning, I saw them at the Blue
Anchor public house, Whitechapel-road, drinking half-and-half-that is not
more than a quarter of a mile from Rupert-street--I left them there--I had
been staying at Dorkman's about a fortnight. 9

ELIZABETH DORKMAN. I live in Thomas-street, and let apartments-the
prisoners Webb and Gill lived three weeks with me, as man and wife. On
Saturday morning, the 18th of July, about five o'clock, they both came home
together.
HENRY BOARD. I am a cow-keeper, and live in Lipman-street, Cambridge
road. I found this cash-box in my cow-yard, which is three or four minutes'
walk from Thomas-street- I do not know how far from Rupert-street-my
yard is in the high road-the box was open---there was an ear-drop in it.

-Gill said, "Is that the charge ?"-I said I could not exactly tell her the
