I saw the prisoners in Nine Elms-lane, walking together—Churchill had a bundle over his right arm—I followed, and asked Churchill what he had got there—he said he had a piece of lead—I asked where he got it—he said he had brought it from the Water-works, where he was at work, and was going to take it to Mr. Hemmings, his master—I asked him if I went to Mr. Hem  mings, whether he would know he was going to bring it—he said no, but the foreman had told him to take it—I asked Higgins if he had heard the foreman say so—he said yes, it was all right—I went to Mr. Morland, the foreman, but the prisoners were not present—I had left them with another constable—in consequence of what I heard at Mr. Morland's I went and took the prisoners to the station-house—they were about three-quarters of a mile from the Water-works, coming towards Westminster—I produce the lead—here are 43lbs. of it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What Churchill said was perfectly true, that it was lead, and that Mr. Hemmings was his master? A.  Yes, and he had been working at the Water-works—Regent-street leads out of Horseferry-road—they were going in a direction towards that—both the Prisoners were admitted to bail. JAMES MORLAND. I am foreman to Mr. Hemmings, of Regent-street—he is a stone-mason—the prisoners were employed by me on the premises of the Southwark and Vauxhall Water-works—I did not direct either of them to take away any lead from the premises—I was working there—I cannot say that I saw any of this lead on the premises—there was lead about, but in much larger sheets than this—this is my signature to this deposition—I think it was read over to me.
   Q.  Here you say, "There was a good deal of such lead as this produced, lying about on the premises." A.  I said there was a great quantity of lead, but I was not aware that there was any of this sort about—there was sheet lead about there. JOHN HANCOCK. I am engineer to the Southwark and Vauxhall Water works Company—I live on the works—there was lead similar to this on the Premises—it was the property of the Water-Works Company.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Can you venture to swear that this lead ever belonged to the Company? A.  No—there has been lead used similar to this—I could not go so far as to swear that this was the lead—these men were working at flooring, in stone-work—I suppose they might have to remove rubbish at times—they had a large job—it had been going on seven or eight months—there were a lot of things taken away, fresh things brought, and rubbish and other things moved.
   COURT . Q.  Would any person have a right to remove these sort of things by way of rubbish? A.  Not to my knowledge.
   MR. PAYNE to JAMES MORLAND . Q.  Would it be the duty if these men to carry away rubbish? A.  Yes—they have to clear away the rubbish after the work is complete. NOT GUILTY.
