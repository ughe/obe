709. THOMAS RIORDAN was indicted for that he, on the 9th of October, in the fortieth year of the reign of George the Third, at St. Peter, in the City of Cork, in that part of Great Britian, called Ireland, was married to Catherine Roberts , spinster , and her had for his wife, and afterwards, and during the life of his said wife, on the 17th of May, in the forty-second year of the reign of the said King George the Third, at St. Paul, Covent-garden, in the name of Thomas Reading , feloniously did marry Ann Barton , and her did take as his wife; against the Statute, &c.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution
   CHARLES SHEEN. I produce a certificate, which is from the church of St. Peter's, at Cork; I received it from the hands of John Forsyth, the minister; I compared the names and dates with the register - it is a true copy; that enables me to swear that the names and dates are the same.
 Cross-examined by MR. P. ALLEY. Q. Where do you live? A. At Greenwich. I went from England to Cork to get the copy of the register; I did not know any one at Cork, nor the minister; I went to St. Peter's, and got this from the parson at the church, and then I brought it here; I swear I took the book, and compared the hand-writing - the signature, and that also: the parson was a very old man; I mean to say I had my hand on the book while I read it - the parson did not read it to me.
 The certificate was read, the purport of which was that Thomas Riorden was married to Catherine Roberts on the 9th of October, 1799, at St. Peter's Church, Cork, according to the ceremonies of the Established Church of the United Kingdom.
 MR. ADOLPHUS objected that there was a variation in the the date of the marriage according to the certificate, and the year of the King's reign set out in the indictment. And that they could not have been married according to the rights of the Church of the "United Kingdom" for, according to the certificate, it was before the union took place.
   ELLEN LE GRESSE. My name before marriage was Ellen Roberts. In the year 1799, the latter part of it, I was living in Cork; I was there, I believe, on the 9th of October - I had a sister named Catherine; I was married then. I know the prisoner - he was then living at Cork, as a carpenter; my sister was married twenty-nine years ago - it was in the month stated; I remember the day of the month - I went with them to the church door, and then returned to help my mother to prepare the wedding dinner - they returned in about three quarters of an hour; my sister went by the name of Catherine Roberts, and after marriage by the name of Catherine Riordan; they lived in my father's house, as man and wife; I heard Riordan say after the marriage, that Mr. Forsyth married them; they lived at my father's for four months, as man and wife; they then lived a short distance off: my father and mother are both dead. In about four months he left my sister in a state of pregnancy, without any notice. Five years ago I came over to England, and saw the prisoner; I had never before seen him since he left - an arrangement was come to; he said he would settle a house on my sister Catherine, and told me he would come to Greenwich on the Sunday following, to settle; he came on the Saturday evening, but so drunk that no business could be done. - About a month after that I met him on Blackfriars-bridge - he gave me 1l., and said that he would allow my sister 2s. 6d. per week - he continued to do so for about two years and a half, and I sent it to my sister at Cork. During this transaction he sometimes called my sister Kitty, and sometimes his wife. At the end of two years and a half, he said he could not live longer with his second wife, and if I would send for her over, he would either take her home, or set her up in business; he gave me 4l. to send over to bring her over; he had three children by his second wife: my sister is still living - my sister did not then come over; she came over last June or July; she was ill at the time the money was given - that was about two years(when she came over) after the money was given. The child by the first wife is dead. When my sister came over my husband wrote, and the prisoner came down to Greenwich he saw her, and said he would put her into business, but she objected to a school, as being too sedentary for her- he slept at my house that night; he said he was living in Exmouth-street, but he did not come down again. I next saw him in Exmouth-street; he was living with a young woman there, and had two children.
 Cross-examined. Q. This was in 1799? A. Yes; I do not know that he was apprenticed to his father; my sister is about fifty-six years old - she was then about twenty-seven: I had lived in Cork all my life - I never saw his father; I have heard since that he was an apprentice to his father: I did not then know that he was more than seventeen - I know he was then more than twenty. I remained in Cork all my life; until about five years ago- my sister was Protestant; he was a Catholic - I had heard him say so - I left her in Ireland.
 Q. Did she not keep a house herself before her marriage? A. She did; in Cold-lane - she kept it about six years; she was not keeping that house when she was married - she was living with my father. I have lately been on good terms with the prisoner; my husband did borrow a little money of him, and he paid it - it was lent before this charge was made, and it was paid before; I swear that my husband is not in debt to him - he would do nothing for her; I did not know the second wife until lately - I heard of it five years ago; we did not wish to make any charge if he would have provided for my sister.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. Have you brought the charge against him at all? A. No. I only know that he is a Catholic by what he said.
   JAMES HILL. I am clerk to Messrs, Selby, Bowden and Co., vestry-clerks of Clerkenwell. I produce an extract of the register of St. Paul Covent-garden; I received it from the Rev. Mr. Vickery; he is the curate of that parish; I received it in the vestry of that parish, and compared it with the register - it is a true copy.
 This cerificate was read, and stated, that Thomas Reading was married to Ann Barton, on the  17th of May, 1801, by Edward Embrey, curate.
 MR. ADOLPHUS took an objection to this certificate, on the same ground as the former one: there was a variance in the date of the certificate, and the year stated in the indictment.
 ANN BARTON. I live at No. 6, Bedford-court, New North-street, Red Lion-square. I became acquainted with the prisoner in 1800; I never knew he was married till about three months back; I was married to him in 1801 - I was never before married - I lived with him twenty-four years and two months; I have had as good as twenty children by that marriage - there were born alive six sons and three daughters; we separated three years ago, next July: I was obliged to leave him for his ill usage.
