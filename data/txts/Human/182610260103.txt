 Prisoner. Q. Did I agreed to meet you that night? A. Your daughter did; you had agreed to meet me on the 25th of September, but did not come; and your daughter agreed for you to meet me on the 30th.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer of Hatton-garden. In consequence of information, which I received about the prisoner, I gave directions to Dutton; Lee and Kirby were in company with me. On the 30th of September, about a quarter to nine o'clock at night, at the Green Man; Dutton came into the house, and said, "They are in sight" - Lee then searched him - he had nothing about him; Lee gave him four shillings, which I had marked - he went out, and soon after returned. saying, "It is all right;" I then saw the prisoner brought in - she was searched, and the four marked shillings, which had been given to Dutton, were found on her - I searched Dutton, and found these twelve counterfeit shillings on him (producing them) - he said in her presence, that he got them from her; she exclaimed, "Oh! you traitor! Oh! Mr. Limbrick, I am a dead woman."
   WILLIAM LEE. I was with Limbrick on this evening; he gave me four marked shillings, which I gave Dutton, after seeing that he had nothing about him - I went out shortly after him, and saw the prisoner near him - I took her, brought her in, and I found on her the four shillings, which I had given to Dutton; twelve shillings were found on him.
   WILLIAM KIRBY. I have heard the officer's evidence - it is correct.
   JOSEPH NICHOL. I am moneyer of the Mint. These shillings are counterfeit, and from one die - they do not appear to have been in circulation, but they have been prepared for circulation.
   JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of counterfeit coin. - The shillings are counterfeit - they have been rubbed down with scowering-paper, and then coloured with something, to make them look black, as if they had been in circulation.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have had twelve children - my husband is a painter and glazier, but neglected his business; I have been left in distress, and for a week together have been without a bit of meat; I have been brought to this through a person who owed me some rent, and gave me seven score of these - I kept them by me a long time, and wanted bread while I had them. I have often returned, after going out to pass them, and pawned something instead; it was nothing but distress led me to do it.
     GUILTY.
 The prisoner was called upon to receive judgment, and upon praying benefit of clergy, a counter-plea was put in, on behalf of the Crown, and a record of the conviction of  Martha Byatt read, for having put off, on the 25th of February, 1819, seven counterfeit shillings, to Cecilia Willis, for 2s. 71/2d. - the prisoner pleaded that she was not the same person, upon which a Jury were impannelled to try the issue.
   CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am assistant to the solicitor of the Mint. I produce a copy of the record of the conviction of Martha Byatt, at the April Session, 1819; I examined it with the original - it is correct - (read).
   BARNARD GLEED. I am an officer. I know the prisoner, and knew her in 1819 - I was present at the April Session that year; the prisoner is the person who was tried and convicted here - I was a witness on that occasion, and gave evidence against her.
   GEORGE BRAND. I was a turnkey at the House of Correction in 1819; the prisoner was in my custody twelve months from that time - I am certain of her person. The Jury found that she was the same person, and she accordingly received sentence of Death.

ELIZA BYATT was indicted for a like offence, in putting off, on the 27th of September , six counterfeit shillings; and the said MARTHA BYATT was indicted for that she, before the said felony was committed, unlawfully did counsel, aid, abet, and procure the said Eliza Byatt, to do and commit the said felony, in manner and form aforesaid, against the statute.
   GEORGE DUTTON. I had seen Martha Byatt on the 25th of September, and agreed to meet her on the 27th, at a person's house, near North-row; she promised she would have some little ones - I went to the place, but she was not there; the prisoner came in the evening, and sent for me; I went out, found her in Green-street, and said, "Have you got any little ones?" she said, No, but she had got six big ones, meaning counterfeit shillings, which I took of her, and gave her two goods shillings, which Limbrick had given to me, on the Tuesday before - I kept the bad shillings till I saw Limbrick, and gave them to him.
 Prisoner ELIZA BYATT. Q. Did I say I came from my mother? A. Yes; she did not say she brought them on her own account.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer. In consequence of information I gave directions to Dutton, and gave him two shillings, which were not marked; I afterwards received from him six counterfeit shillings, which I produce - they were wrapped in paper, as they are now; Kirby was with me when I apprehended Eliza.
   JOHN FIELD. These are counterfeit shillings, and have never been in circulation.
 ELIZA BYATT's Defence. My mother never brought me to this - it is I that has brought her to it.
     NOT GUILTY.
