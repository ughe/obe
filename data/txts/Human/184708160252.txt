Mary Ann Morrison came for a halfpenny-worth of sugar-candy; she gave me a shilling, I gave her the change—she turned from the counter, and the male prisoner came in, and asked me what time the train started—I told him, and he said, "We shall be just in time"—I put the shilling which the woman gave me into the till, but not into the bowl with the other silver, because I thought it was a bad one—I was in the act of biting it when Nicholls came in, and I gave it him—this is the shilling. ELIZABETH PADDY. I am the wife of Thomas Paddy, a baker—on the 12th of Aug. the male prisoner came into the shop, asked for a biscuit, and gave me shilling—I gave him a sixpence and 4 1/2 d . in coppers—I gave the shilling to my little girl to put in a box, where there was no money but two sixpences and one half-a-crown—I saw the box after the shilling was put into it—I saw that there was no other shilling in it—the male prisoner lelf the shop, and Nicholls came in—I bit the shilling and cut it, and gave it to Clark, the policeman. WILLIAM TRUNDLE. I keep the White Swan beer-shop, at Westow-hill, Norwood—the prisoners came on the evening of the 12th of Aug.—the male prisoner asked for a pint of ale—I served him, and he gave me half-crown—Nicholls came in afterwards, and I showed him the half-crown—while I was showing it, the male prisoner snatched it out of my hand, and said it was a good one—I did not see what became of it—the officer came in, I gave the prisoners in charge. RICHARD ROBERTS. I am a gardener—I was in at Mr. Trundle's when the prisoners were there—I saw the male prisoner cast his hand, and I heard something chink outside the door—I took a candle, and searched—I picked up a half-crown, and gave it to Threadwell.
   Morrish. Q.  How far from the door did you pick it up? A.  About three yards. STEPHEN THREADWELL( policeman. ) On Thursday night, the 12th of Aug., Nicholls came to me—I went to Mr. Trundle's—the prisoners were there—I saw Trundle return something towards Nicholls—the male prisoner caught at it—I caught his arm, and he tossed a half-crown towards the door—I heard something fall, but as the prisoners stood both together I have no idea from which it fell—I received from Nicholls two shillings, which he stated he had picked up at the counter—he did not tell me from which it fell—I produce them—Roberts gave me this half-crown—this is the shilling I re ceived from Nicholls, which came from Miss Cherry.
   Mary Ann Morrison.  It was me that threw it. JAMES CLARK( policeman P  108.) I produce one shilling, which I got from Mrs. Paddy—I found on the male prisoner two sixpences and ten shillings, all good; and on the woman, six sixpences, a fourpenny piece, and 2 1/4 d . in coppers, all good. CALES EDWARD POWELL. This half-crown is counterfeit—these four shillings are all counterfeit, and from the same mould.
