159. JOHN MATTHEWS was indicted for stealing 1 sack, value 2s., and
197lbs. of turnip-sced, 101., the goods of the South-Eastern Railway
Company.
Messrs. HUDDLESTON and Baldwin conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE BRATT. I am a licensed victualler-I know the prisoner-I saw
him on the 14th of Oct., between eight and nine in the evening - he came to
my house at New-cross, with two other men - he asked for a pot of porter-
he said he had no money, he should have some presently-I refused to credit
him-I saw him and the other two men whispering together, and they
went outside once or twice-I went after them-I saw the prisoner call my
pot-boy on one side, and whisper something to him, I went into the stable-1
sent an old man, named Hoy, who was acting as ostler there, for a light, and
I found a sack of turnip-seed-I asked if he knew how it came there-he
said, "No"-I knew it had no business there- I locked the stable, and went
for a policeman-I gave the seed to him.
JAMES WILKINS. I am in Mr. Bratt's service. On that evening I saw a
sack of seed taken into my master's stable by a man with a blue striped smock
frock on his back- I was going out with a pint of ale, and in coming back I
saw the same man in conversation with the prisoner and two others--the pri-
soner went to my fellow-servant first, and wanted him to go up stairs and
borrow a sixpence of Henry, the ostler, which he refused - the prisoner and
the man with the blue smock-frock were together the prisoner then spoke to
another boy- the man with the blue smock-frock wished me to go up stairs
and borrow a shilling-the prisoner said, " Borrow two," not telling me what
it was for; but he followed me to the passage, and said, "Go up stairs and
borrow 2s. of Henry for me on a sack, I have left in the stable, worth 2."
- Henry is the ostler-he was up stairs ill-I told my master.
Cross-examined by Mr. Parry. Q. There were three or four other men
the prisoner was talking with ? A. Yes-he asked Alexander Young first to
go up stairs and ask Henry for a shilling-Young is not here, nor Henry.
Court. Q. Was it the person with the blue-striped frock who carried the
sack into the stable ? A. Yes, from a wagon opposite our house--the pri-
soner, the man in the blue-striped frock, and Whitfield, came with that wagon
-they all three had the management of it-the man with the blue-striped frock
carried the sack into the stable, but the prisoner wanted to borrow 28. on a
sack which he said "I have left in the stable."
George Wilson (police-constable R 19.) On that night I went to Mr.
Bratt's house, at New Cross, and took possession of the sack of turnip-seed
which I found in the stable-I took a sample of it, and took the sack of seed
to the station- I went in search of the ostler, Hoy, and took him on sus-
picion-in consequence of further information, I took the prisoner on the fol-
lowing morning, the 15th of Oct., in Mill-lane, Deptford, in the employ of
Mr. Kingsford- I told him what I took him for, and to be cautious in what
he said-1 asked if he stopped at the New Cross inn last night-he hesitated
a bit, and said, “Yes, I did, and had a pint of beer"--I asked if he knew
anything of a sack of turnip-seed left there-- he said no, so help him God, he
did not-I said, "Be cautious what you say," and took him into custody.
Cross-examined. Q. You said nothing about "so help him God” before!
A. I do not know that I did, but I believe they were his words.
