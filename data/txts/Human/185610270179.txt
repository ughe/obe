COURT . Q.  The amount of the counterfoil of 22nd Aug. is 3 l . 4 s . 6 d ., and the paper you received 2 l . 5 s . 6 d .? A.  Yes—I am not quite certain when it was discovered that this variance existed—it was about 2nd or 3rd Sept—after this alleged fraud had been discovered, Mr. White, an accountant, came to me, and the prisoner was with him—Mr. White asked him what was the amount which had been rendered by him to the company—it was then found that the counterfoils and the accounts did not agree.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you sure as to the time that the prisoner has been in the service of the company, is it not as long as thirteen years? A.  I have been there twelve years—I think he was there when I came—during the last three months I have occasionally received gross sums on account—on 18th Aug. I did not receive a sum from him—he left it with another person—on 22nd Aug. I received 15 l ., and there was still a small balance left in his hands—I know that he received the money in small sums from the ticket collectors—the excess forms are always sent up in an envelope—I find them in the letter box, or they are brought in by some of the guards—I have known him some years—I always thought him an honest man.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Was that 15 l . on account of moneys he had received? A.  Yes—I had no open account with him. DAVID WHITE. I am an accountant in the employ of the Railway Com pany. I was sent down to Forest Hill station—I saw the prisoner there—I asked him to give me an account of the discrepancies there were between the counterfoils and the money paid—he said he did not understand it—I showed him the duplicates—he gave me no explanation—he came up to London with me—I took him, first of all, to the booking office, to see Mr. Cockerton, and after that to the general manager's office—the matter was then stated in presence of the prisoner—he was again asked for an expla nation—we could get nothing more out of him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What was the answer he gave? A. "I do not un derstand you"—I asked him for an explanation—he replied, "I do not un derstand it"—I pointed out to him the difference between the counterfoil and the returns, and that was what he said at both offices—he came readily with me, and brought the books with him—I went down on a special mission.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Did you show him this document of 18th Aug? A.  That was the one I called his attention to—the difference between that and the counterfoil—I asked him whose writing the duplicate was, he said he did not know—he did not say that was not the return he made. HENRY DUFF. ( policeman, M  219). On 3rd Sept, I took the prisoner into custody—I told him he was charged with embezzling 2 l . 17 s . 6 d ., on 18th Aug.—he made no reply to the charge—when at the station he gave me 15 l . in gold—one gold coin, 2 l . 0 s . 10 d . in silver, and 1 s . 2 1/2 d . in copper—while I was counting it, he said, "I dare say you think it strange that I have got so much money in my pocket, but some of that money belongs to the South Eastern Railway Company," mentioning the name of some person which I did not hear.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where did you take him? A.  At the London Bridge station, I think in one of the directors' offices—he did not say he wanted to pay this money over—he did not say he was ready to pay it over—he said, "I dare say you think it strange that I have got so much money, but some of it belongs to the South Eastern Railway."
 (  The prisoner received a good character. ) GUILTY.— Four Years Penal Servitude.
 (  The whole amount of the prisoner's embezzlement was stated to be about  200 l .)
