Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How long have you kept your
'beer-shop in Vauxhall-road ? A. Four years, and upwards-I play at
cards at times, but very seldom--many weeka slapse when I do not see a
card--we have other amusements when we do not play at cards--we play
at quoits and knock-em-down-we play for love, or a pot of beer, or 1s.-
we generally play so always-never for more.be
Q. Did you ever play at quoits for any money? 4. Yes, I have done
so, for more than a shilling or two on one occasion we played for 20.,
but not at the beer-shop-I was one who played- I won balf of the money-
I cannot say how long it is ago about two months, I think-we played
at Clapham--the persons we won the money of, were Magnus, a butler,
and Buxton, who, I believe, lives on some property-he is not a friend of
mine; not at all-I know but little of him-I have known him three or
four months-it may be five, not six-I can well swear it is not twelve
months-I never knew he was in the police--I heard he was one of the
Union-hall officers--I never heard he was tried for felony-before I kept
this beer-shop, I was a publican for three years I kept the Red Lion, at
Lambeth—this game of whist was played in my tap-room--there were only
the four persons present who played at it--when the prisoner came in,
there were several persons present; but my time expired at ten o'clock,
and others left-I had no appointment with the prisoner to come there that
afternoon, to play at quoits ; none whatever I do not know John Cotton
-I know this person, (a witness, but I understood his name was Colton.
Q. Now, did you never, in presence of Cotton, appoint the prisoner to
come to your house that evening, to play quoits ? 4. Never; but I dare
say I can explain it-Ryland came to my house a few days before this,
and had several pints of ale with a person at the bar-he then addressed
himself to me, and said, was not I a quoits player l-I said I could play,
but I was not the best player-he said there was a person at Chelsea,
whom he knew, who was anxious to back a man he had against any other,
for any sum of money; and if I thought I could play sufficiently, he would
get the match for me- I told him I had no objection to see the parties, and
if I thought I was able to compete with him, I would play-he told me he
would see him that night, and mention it to make an appoiotment to meet
me; and the same afternoon, I received a note from Ryland, (but I have
not got it here,) wishing me to meet him at the General Hill, at Chelsea-
