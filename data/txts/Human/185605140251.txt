A little before 4 o'clock on Sun day morning, 20th April, I was in Lower Thames-street—I heard the breaking of glass—I went to No. 71, I waited two or three minutes, and saw the prisoner come out of the court—I pursued him along Thames-street—I lost sight of him for about half a minute on Tower-hill, I sprang my rattle, and after I turned the corner of the rails round the Tower ditch, I saw him ia the hands of H  75—he was not half a minute out of my sight—I had seen him during the night, about 1 o'clock, in company with another man, standing very near the court; I watched them away, and they went towards London-bridge—I afterwards saw him again, about 3 o'clock, coming out of Harp-lane into Thames-street, that is about fifty yards from the prosecutor's premises.

On Sunday morning, 20th April, About a quarter to 4 o'clock, I was in Trinity-square, Tower-hill, and heard the springing of a rattle—I turned, and saw the prisoner running across Tower-hill, towards the Mint, in a direction from Lower Thames-street—I stopped him, he was out of breath—Tomkins came up in about half a minute, and recognised him—I searched him—before I did so, he gave me 6 d . and a lucifer match when I asked what he had got about him. CHARLES BUTTON. ( City policemen , 569). A little before 4 o'clock on Sunday morning I was on duty in Lower Thames-street, and heard a cry of "Stop thief!"—I went to No. 71, Lower Thames-street, and saw Carty—I picked up this looking glass on a bench under the window—two panes of the lower sash of that window were broken, near the fastening—I found a work box and a snuff box outside—I called Mrs. Tomlinson and her son up, and then examined the room—I found one of the drawers open, and some things that were taken from it, on the window ledge—I found this screw-driver outside in the street, and some lucifer matches about fifty yards from the passage in Thames-street, nearer to Tower-hill—this snuff box, brooch, and pair of tweezers were picked up in Thames-street—I also found a piece of candle in the court—the matches correspond with the one which the prisoner had on him. THOMAS TOMLINSON. I am a fishmonger, at No. 71, Lower Thames-street, in the parish of St. Dunstan in the East I was not at home on the night in question, my son and my wife were in the house—these articles ( produced ) are mine—I lost a silk handkerchief altogether, from a drawer that was not locked. THOMAS CHARLES TOMLIKSON. I am the son of the last witness. I went to bed at a quarter to 11 o'clock on 19th April—before doing so I examined the window of the ground floor room—it was fastened with two nails, quite secure—I saw some of these things on the sideboard, in front of the window, and some were in the drawers—the handkerchief that is lost was in a drawer—I saw it there about 8 o'clock on Saturday evening, and also a sixpence in the work box—the street door was not fastened—I locked the room door, and had the key—I found it still locked in the morning.
