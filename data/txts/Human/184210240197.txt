W. Beaumont, Maidstone." GEORGE FREDERICK MINTON. I am a wholesale hosier and warehouse man, in Cheapside. On the 18th of May my young man brought a card into my counting-house, with "Beaumont, Maidstone," on it—Shackleford was not there at that time—I desired my young man to make inquiries, and on the following day, in consequence of those inquiries, I permitted the Mr. Beaumont, as I supposed, to have the goods—that was on the 19th, and on the Monday afterwards, which was the 23rd, I went to Mr. Bachelard's, at the Cork Stuffing Company's—Bachelard stated that he had known Mr. Beaumont ten or twelve years, that he was a very respectable young man; he knew he had 900 l . capital; he had seen that; and he had married a young lady who had a whole house in Sloane-street, and 300 l . a year, and he had a friend who would help him with one or two thousand pounds, or more, if he required it—the communication from Bachelard to me was satisfactory—I believed it to be true, otherwise I should not let Shackleford have the goods—I at last began to have some misgivings—in consequence of that, I desired Shackleford to be shown in to me, and he was so, a few days after the 23rd of May—he then gave himself the name of Ford, and represented himself as buying for Mr. Beaumont—I did not inquire into the state of the account from my young man till early in June—I was not aware it was so heavy till I received a letter from Beaumont, and a further order—I then called on Bachelard again—he offered to be come surety for 50 l . if I would let him have more goods—I considered, and declined it—I went to Maidstone myself about the 21st or 22nd of June—I found the shop, with "Beaumont" over the door—I went into the shop, and saw two persons—one was Mr. Beaumont's father, as I was told, and the other was the prisoner Gosbell—I addressed myself to Gos bell, in consequence of his coming towards me—I asked him for Mr. Beaumont—he said he was in town, and would not return till Saturday—(this was Thursday)—I then asked for a Mr. Smith—Gosbell said he was gone to Chatham, and would return in the course of that afternoon—I waited in Maidstone the whole of that day, and went to the shop again after ten o'clock at night, when they were closing, in order to see Smith, but I could not, he had not returned—I then asked Gosbell if the buyer was down there—he said no, he never came to Maidstone—I asked him if he knew him—he said he did not, and did not know his name.
