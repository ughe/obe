Re-examined. I had known the woman about two years, and it
was in January of this year that she first came to live with me. With
regard to the postoard album I could not say whether I found it on
the sewing machine or the chair near by. I believe it was on the
machine, but I cannot say definitely. As I have said, the shutters
of the front room were partly open. I slept in Sheffield on the night
of September 11-12, and was seen there by a fellow employee. We
slept in the same street, in houses apposite. I heard Roberts's state
ment at the police station on the Thursday night (September 12).
The postcard was mentioned, and the letter signed "Bert." The
fragment of a letter produced is not in my handwriting. I do not
know the handwriting at all. Roberts's statement was that the frag
ments of the letter produced (mounted on glass) were found in the
fire grate in the back room, the bedroom.
Sir Charles Mathews asked if Mr. Marshall Hall desired to have
the alibi of the witness proved.
Mr. Marshall Hall accepted the statement that witness was in
Sheffield on this particular night.
Re-examination continued. I did not make a minuto examination
of the contents of the purse. I merely saw some silver and coppers
When I last saw her on the afternoon of September 11 she had on
brown velvet skirt, a light blouse, and her bir was in metal curlers.
She was attending to the washing that day and I left her so employed.
To Mr. Marshall Hall. She was in the habit of keeping her hair
in curlers in the day time.
To Mr. Justice Grantham. Her habit at night was to wear a night
dress. I have never known her sleep nude.
SARAH Ann STOCKS, wife of George William Stocks, 29, St. Paul's
Road, Camden Town. The man Shaw and the woman Emily
Dimmock lodged with us. We knew them as Mr. and Mrs. Shaw,
and previous to her death they had been there nine weeks, occupy.
ing two rooms on the ground floor and paying a rent of 89. & week.
I knew that Shaw was employed on the Midland Railway On Sep
tember 11 I was at home in the afternoon and evening. Shaw left
as usual about 4.30, and the woman was in the house then. She
was employed washing in the washhouse in the yard at the back.
After Shaw had gone she gathered up the linen and took it away.
She afterwards went into the washhouse several times to clear the
place up, and the last time she was there was naar eight o'clook.
