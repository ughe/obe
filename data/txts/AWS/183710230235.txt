RICHARD BARLOW. I keep the Rodney Arms New King-street, Dept-
ford. I did not know the deceased Allen before Sunday, the 8th of
October, when he came to my house, about twenty minutes before seven
o'clock in the evening, with a soldier and a girl-the girl went away, and
Allen and the soldier went into the tap-room-Allen was very much intox-
icated, and the soldier a little in liquor--there were about seven persons
in the tap-room-the soldier and Allen were in the same box with the pri-
soner, who is a sawyer-in a short time the prisoner and Allen quarrelled
- heard them making a noise, and ran into the tap-room thought they
were quarrelling, because the deceased said, 44 Never mind, Iam determined
to fight this man," and he pulled his coat off to fight said I would have
no fighting there-I took Evans out of the house, and told him to go home
-I then went to the deceased, and made him put on his coat, and asked
him to sit down for a few minutes, to govern his passion, which he did;
in about five minutes he went out of the house, and put his back against
the partition as he went out, and said, I wish I could meet with that man,
I would have a fight with him to-night"'-he bid me good night, and went
out, and in about two minutes, I saw him lying -the ground, about eight
feet from my door-the prisoner was intoxicated as well as the other.
