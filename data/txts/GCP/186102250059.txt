into custody on the Saturday- I had borrowed a sovereign of the landlord

Cross-examined. l. You have not given us the names of the persons who
kept the public houses where you bought the opium; did Mr. Morris keep
one, the Eastern Shades? A. Yes, that is where I paid the sovereign-the
Duke of Gloucester is kept by William Johnson, that is where I generally
dealt--the place where I first saw the prisoner is the Britannia-I only saw
him there once the landlord's name is White - I did not understand the
opium to be stolen-I understood it was smuggled-he told me that ships
came into the docks two or three per week, up to January, and that there
could be ten or twelve pounds smuggled—I only know that he told me it
was smuggled from the ships in the dooks—I had no occasion to make any
further inquiry at all, except as to the value and quality of the articles I
was going to purchase-I have dealt in sponges this forty years, and inquiry
has been made into my character by respectable gentlemen-my bills of
parcels will prove all my dealings in sponges to be fair-I buy the sponges
from different merchants ; Mr. Cantor is one, and Mr. Furminger another, and
Mr. Lewis, the wholesale dealer in the article, and several more-I never got
auy smuggled sponges I have dealt occasionally in other things ; I haye
dealt in pictures, but sponges is the manner in which I get my living-I
have dealt a little in jewellery at times, nothing else that I can think of I
carry a bag about when I travel—I do not hawk and go about from honae
to house I go and serve shop-keepers only-I do not always get an artiole
at 108. 6d. when it is worth 208. in the market ; I only sold it at 158. A
