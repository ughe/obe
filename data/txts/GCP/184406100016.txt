Ann RAYMENT. I am the wife of John Rayment, a labourer, and live in
Wellington-street North, Whitechapel-road. On this night I was at the top
of North-street, talking to a friend, and saw the prosecutor walking in a direc-
tion from the Grave Maurice, towards the Blind Beggar public-house, with his
hands in his pockets-the two prisoners were just behind bim-I knew them
before, and have seen them in Whitechapel --I saw them walking along to-
gether, a few yards behind the prosecutor-when he got to the butter-shop I
turned round my head, and saw him falling I said, “ Good God, it is Jack
Hatfield, fallen down !"- I then saw the prisoners-M Nally stood nearest to
bim—they both made their escape off the pavement into the road-the pri-
soners were the nearest persons to him at the time he fell-M'Nally had a
paper cap on, and the other a shiny hat-1 assisted in doing all I could to
help the prosecutor-he was bleeding very much indeed.
7 Cross-examined. Q. How do you get your living? A. I am a charwoman,

MR. DOANE. Q. Have you any doubt whether these are the two men?
A. I am sure they are.
