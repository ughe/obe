WHETHAM, Mayor.
Cross-examined. Two guineas and a “con” would be a proper fee to
mark when the senior has three and a “con"--you told me you had to
draw an indictment and present it to the Grand Jury yourself after Mr.
Metcalfe had drawn the indictment on your paying me for the brief I
said “There's the fee for drawing the indictment - you said, “Oh, I've
had to do it over again, and I cannot give you the fee"- you have given
me several briefs before--you saw Mr. Metcalfe personally.
WILLIAM CHAMBERLAIN (Police Inspector L). On 5th May I received a
warrant for May's apprehension, and went to his office, but could not find
him-I searched for him till Saturday night, 24th May, when I saw him
in Chippendale Road—I said, “I have a warrant for your apprehension
for obtaining money from Mr. Fullagar;" he said, "Oh dear, yes, Mr.
Chamberlaiu. It is a very unfortunate affair. I gave a friend of mine,
Mr. Lewis, a cheque twice to settle the matter, and I am afraid he has not
done so; if you had not taken me, I was thinking of giving myself up.
I have come over from the country on purpose to do so "-on taking bim
in custody he gave me two pieces of paper, saying, “These papers refer to
Lytton's case, and belong to Mr. Fullagar."
JOHN KERSTEN. I keep the White Swan, 225, High Street, Shad-
well-on July 20th I had a case on, and this claim was made for
201. 88. 6d., and I considered I owed only 61.-I saw May respecting it,
and showed him this letter I had received the same day from the plaintiff's
Solicitors. (Read: “32, Mark Lane, London, E.C., 24th July, 1878. Sir,
--We are instructed by Messrs. Murrow and Holt, of 18, City Road,
Finsbury, to apply to you for payment of 201. 12s., amount of account
rendered. Unless we receive this sum, with 68. 8d. for our charges, by
10 o'clock on Friday morning next, we shall at once proceed to recover
same. Yours obediently, Keene & Marsland. F. Harris." May said,
"It was foolish of you to have offered him the 61., because, as he did nto
fulfil the contract, you had nothing to pay; but now you have offered him the
67. you will have to pay it"- and so I gave him the 61. to pay it into
Court-he called on me about three weeks afterwards, and said, “We
can't settle the affair in any other way; we shall have to go to the Lord
Mayor's Court;" and he wanted 71., and I gave it him, as he said he
had engaged Mr. Besley to plead for me he gave me no receipt, he only
marked it on one of the papers in his pocket (Paper marked "C":
“71. paid on account.") I saw him subsequently in September at my
place, and I asked him how the affair was going on, and he said it was
put back for the Long Vacation-I did not see him again-he did not appear
for me, and the end of it was, I found the brokers in the house on the 31st
October for 371. 148., and I had to pay them out.
Cross-eramined. The 61. was paid into Court-it has not come to my
knowledge that it was paid into Court at the proper time, but that on
account of the plea not having been delivered by your clerk in proper time
the other side signed judgment--the 71. was advanced to pay Mr. Besley
-I recollect your telling him you were en leavo ring to settle the matter
with Keene and Marsland, and if you had settled it for 101. I should
have been satisfied-you did not tell me you had offered Keene and
Marsland 101. to settle, and I know nothing of it I gave you full dis-
cretion as Solicitor to act for me--I saw Mr. Beck, Solicitor, and told
him where to find you, and he said he would write you a letter, but I
do not know whether he has been in correspondence with you.
