 2226. CATHERINE HERION was indicted for stealing 1 pair of bellows, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 1 candlestick, 2 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 watch-guard, 4 d  .; the goods of Phillip Kreckeler . ESTHER KRECKELER. I am the wife of Phillip kreckeler—we keep a toy-shop, in London-street, Greenwich. On the 30th of Aug., between one and two o'clock, the prisoner came to me, and purchased a toilet-bottle—these was a pair of bellows, some china candlestick, and an India-rubber watch-guard on the counter—she was in the shop about half-an-hour—she only purchased the toilet-bottle—I served her—she gave me five sixpence—I did not miss anything after she was gone, but was sent for to Mrs. Smaill's, up the court by the side of my shop, and saw my property there—I had not sold it—nobody serves in the shop but me—these bellows, guard, and candle-sticks ( produced ) are mine.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you recollect what passed, when I purchased a chain of you? A.  There was a chain sent home for your daughter to look at, and if she did not like it I was to take it back—you did not say you would pay for these thing when you got the chain—I did not say, "Take what you please, the money is sure"—your servant was with you—I had not told you to out at my shop at any time, and I would give you a glass of porter—I did say to you that the bellows were not new—you did not take them by my leave—your servant asked me to allow you to have a pint of porter, as you were not well—I had never seen you before—I found the things between six and seven o'clock—you were there between one and two. CAROLINE KING. I live at No. 3, Burley-court, Greenwich. I have known the prisoner five months—on the 30th of Aug., between five and six o'clock, I went to Mrs. Smaill's, and saw the prisoner—she gave me a basket, and asked me to take care of it till she came home—I kept it till between six and seven, and gave it to Henry Chorley—this is it ( produced ). HENRY CHORLEY. I live at No. 3, Burley-court, Greenwich. King gave me a basket—I carried it down, and emptied the contents into Mrs. Smaill's apron—there was a china candlestick, a watch-guard, and a pair of bellows—these produced are them. ELIZABETH SMAILL. I am the wife of Thomas Smaill, of Burely-court, Greenwich. I have known the prisoner about six months—on the 30th of Aug., in the afternoon, the prisoner came to my house—shortly afterwards I received a basket from Chorely—these things produced were in it—the pri soner was in the house, but was not present. HENRY BURNS( policeman. ) On the afternoon of the 30th of Aug. I ac companied Mrs. Kreckeler to a house in Burley-court, saw the prisoner, and asked her where the bellows, candlestick, and watch-guard were—she said she had nothing in the world but what she had paid for—I asked where her basket was—she said, behind her—I found it, there was nothing in it—Chorley said in her presence, if I went down stairs he would bring me to where the property was—I went down, and received the articles from Mrs. Smaill—I went back to the prisoner, told her I had found the things, and took her to the station—she said she thought it was better for her to go—I think she had been drinking—she was not drunk then.
