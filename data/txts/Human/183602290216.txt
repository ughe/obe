Fifth Jury. before Mr. Sergeant Arabin .
 853. ANN KEENAN was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling—house of William Stewart , on the   11th of February , and stealing therein 1 clock, value 20 s  ., his goods, CHARLOTTE STEWART. I am the wife of William Stewart, and live in Bell—court, Woolwich. On the 11th of February I went out about ten o'clock, and returned about twelve—I left my own boy at home—he is twelve years old—I occupy two rooms on one side of the passage—the landlord lives in the next house to us—I left the room open which my boy was in, and my cloak was in the room—when I came back my cloak was gone—the prisoner lived in a room in the same passage. EDWARD WOOLWICH, I have o'clock pawned by the prisoner, I believe—in the name of Ann Scott—I did not swear to the prisoner positively when I was before the Magistrate—I said, to the best of my belief the prisoner was the person. GEORGE WHITEHEAD. My mother left me at home, but I did not stay—I do not know who took the cloak—when I went out, I locked the door and left the key in it.
   Prisoner's Defence . I hope you will have mercy on me and my child. I was in distress, and want led me to do it. NOT GUILTY

   First Jury, before Mr. Recorder .
 854. JOSEPH SHARP was indicted for feloniously assaulting Jerome McGurrin , on the   23rd of October , With intent to rob him, and his money goods, and chattels from his person and against his will, feloniously and violently to steal, take, and carry away. JEROME MCGURRIN. On the 23rd of September I took a house from Henry Clare, and bought the goods 5 l .—the prisoner pretended to be a broker, to let the house for Norris, the old landlord—on the 20th of Oc tober he put an execution into the house, and on the 23rd he took away the goods—after sending away the goods, he knocked me down, to get away the warrant from me—he represented himself to be the freeholder of the certain—I had a £5 note and some halfpence in my pocket—he said he wanted the warrant—I would not give it to him, and he knocked me dowa—the warrant shows he was not the freeholder of the house—it was a false execution that he put in—I have been tried for robbing this house, and was honourably acquitted—he put in an execution and took my goods away twice—he was in the habit of doing this, and has been convicted twice in this Court for taking goods by false distresses—I took the house at 20 l . a year—when he struck me he said, "What is the use of talking to me? I told the inspector of the police I was the freeholder, but I am nothing but an agent to him"—I kept the warrant from him—he knocked me down and ransacked my pocket; and my wife went for the policemen, but they would not ender into it—I went to Union Hall—they said I must go to a higher court—I was robbed twice by him, and my goods sold—here is the warrant—he did not say any thing about the money I had in my pocket—he said he wanted the warrant back—he collared me, he and Clare, and knocked me down to take it out of my breeches' pocket, but I kept it—he took nothing out—he put his hand to the pocket the note was in.
