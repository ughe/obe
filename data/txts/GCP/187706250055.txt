John BATTEN COOMBE. I am a licentiate of the Royal College of Surgeons
and resident medical officer of St. Mary's Hospital-the prosecutrix was
brought to me on Saturday night, 2nd June, about 10 p.m. she said she
felt sick and had great pain in her stomach I used the stomach pump,
after which she got rapidly better--the glass was brought to me on the Sun-
day evening and the jug two or three days afterwards--I analysed what re-
mained in the jug and glass, and also the vomit-I found in them all a large
quantity of ammonia of chloride of mercury, commonly called white preci-
pitate ; it is used for children; if it had been absorbed into the system most
likely the patient would die; it was sure to have produced intense salivation
--there was sufficient in the vomit to have caused death.
Crow-examined. It is insoluble in water and would sink to the bottom of
a vessel containing liquor, but would dissolve in time, say twenty-four hours
---you would easily see it, it is quite white like white lead-the froth of the
beer would hold it. SON
