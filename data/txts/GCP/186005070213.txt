JOHN HOLDAWAY (Policeman, P 219). On Thursday night, in conse,
quence of what Luscombe told me, I went into Pomeroy-street, and found
this knife lying in the carriage-road, about two yards from the footway, Dear
the spot where Luscombe described that he had been assaulted it was open
as it is now, and slightly wet with a little rain that had fallen-I took it to
the station and gave it to the sergeant.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you know the Victoria ? A. Yes, it is about
half way between the Victoria and the Queen's-road, about two hundred
yards from the Victoria—I found it about 4 in the morning-I am sure that
was about the time—while I was searching only one of the constables spoke
to me, the one who was on the beat in Luscombe's place-no civilian spoke
to me- I looked about the entrance to Kendall-street and Powis-place, which
leads from Pomeroy-street to Kendall-street.
MR. METCALPE. Q. Did you find it near the place pointed out to you by
Luscombe 4. Luscombe described that place.
ELIZABETH MATILDA COOMBES. I live at 45, Bedford-cottages, Peckham
-on 11th April, in consequence of something which had been done to
me, I gave the prisoner into custody, and went to the station—I had sent
for a policeman-As I followed to the station, I heard the prisoner say to
Luscombe, "I will do for you, I will watch you”-he used bad language,
but I could not say what words I never saw him to my knowledge before
that night.
Cross-examined. l. I believe you drank with him on that night! A. By
his pushing me into the place he went quietly to the station after the police-
man had hold of him-he was discharged there,
JOSIAH CRUTCHER. I am a dairyman in Pomeroy-street-on the night of
12th April, about a quarter to 12, I was at the Victoria public-house, and
saw the prisoner-he was not drunk, but he had been drinking-he went
out before me and was talking to the two constables—he was threatening
Luscombe that he would do for him, for some grievance that had happened
the night before he said, “This is the b_ that took me last night, he
shall never take me any more"-I got a pot of beer for my supper in a can,
and asked the prisoner to drink, saying, "Do not say any more, go home
- he drank, I believe, and Luscombe said, “I do not want to have anything
to say to you, as far as last night, that is forgotten, and if you do not say
anything to me I shall say nothing to you"-he walked on and I stopped
behind-Luscombe followed him I then followed and saw them some dis-
tance off struggling together-I stepped back and spoke to Cox-I after-
wards saw Cox knocked down-I assisted him up and stopped with him-
the prisoner ran away; Cox was insensible for five minutes.
