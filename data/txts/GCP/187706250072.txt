WHITE, Mayor.
ALFRED BRISTOWE, I have been twelve years in this office---it is my duty
to attend to the Greenwich Hospital busiuess; I had nothing to do with the
Lowestoft business in the office, but I used to help-I know nothing about
Maid's Acre-1 am not able to say from recollection whether I was at the
office on 29th or 31st January, but I was there every day that year until
the 26th, when we removed the office-1 did not hear of the forgery of this
cheque until I saw the report in the newspaper next inrrning of the pro
ceedings before the Magistrate-up to that time I knew nothing of the
missing conveyance; I had nothing to do with any of these forgeries of
with the receipt of any of the monies--I have known the prisoner some
time, and have been in the habit from time to time of lending him money-
we used to have a little account together of monies borrowed-he some
times lent me money, and sometimes I him-he did not lend me any money
about the end of last year; I think he was in my debt at that time, and be
has been so since-he is still slightly in my debt-borrowing money was
frequent, being in the same room together - I applied to him to pay we
the money he owed me, somewhere about March ; I will not say the date,
but it was after we left the office at Richmond Terrace-he then owed me
about 71.; I did not get it--this letter is my writing.
Cross-examined. I was on friendly terms with Fullagar, and we accom-
modated each other with small sums; I also borrowed of Swainson-there
was a sort of brotherly love existing in the office-Fullagar owed me money
at the end of the year, and very likely I borrowed money of him the next
month, but I cannot say; he owes me about 21. now-he told me that his
solicitor said that he had better not pay it while these proceedings were
going on-I cannot say that Mr. Swainson ever entered anything in the
journal which he had not done himself, but it was not very often that he
did it.
Re-examined. I think it was last Monday or Tuesday that we talked
about this 21.-I heard last year I think that the prisoner was connected
with the promotion of a company.
ALFRED DRUMMOND OSWALD. I am a cousia of Mr. Alfred Isaac Bristowe,
and have been in the Admiralty solicitor's office on and off since January,
1876-my salary runs about 1001. a year; I had no particular office work
-I acted as confidential clerk to Mr. Bristowe-I have expressed a doubt
whether I was at the office on the 27th or 29th January; I do not think I
was, but cannot say for certain-I know nothing of the Maid's Acre busi-
ness, or of a cheque, or of the bill of cost, or of the forgeries--the first I heard
of it was after the prisoner was arrested I have had to advance him money
professionally for work he has done for me, aeting as my solicitor, during
the time I was in the office, and occasionally he has borrowed small sums
of me-I should say he dues not owe me money now, and I do not think
he did in January or February, 1877-I really did not keep an account of
it-be may have borrowed a small sum one day and paid it the next; he
last borrowed of me some time last Autumn ; I really do not know how
much; might have borrowed a 11. at a time on more than one occasion, I
kept no acoount--the larger portion of this paper (produced) is in my writing,
but the heading is not.

or 201. about February, on account of work he had done for me, and which
