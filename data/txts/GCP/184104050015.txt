JANE LETTS. I live at No. 8, Wynyatt-street. I gave inspector
Campbell the duplicate of a Macintosh, whicb Mrs. Harding gave me.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Had you known Harding
any time? A. About fifteen years--Mr. Letts has worked for him-I
knew him in a respectable way of business, and he always bore a respect
able character-it was in consequence of his difficulties that he came to
lodge in this way.
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Do you know that Frenaby worked for him? 4. I
know nothing about Frenaby-it is fourteen years since my husband has
worked for Harding.
Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Do you mean, your husband has not worked
for him for the last four years ! 4. He has worked for him up to
within the last four or five weeks.
Cross-examined by Me. PHILLIPS. Q. Has not your husband worked
for Frenaby also ? A. Yes, for the last five or six years.

SAMSON DARKIN CAMPBELL. I am a police-inspector. On the night
of the 11th of February I took Harding and his wife into custody, at No. 5,
Goswell-terrace-I said I had a warrant against him and his wife he
asked, “What for ?"-I said for a transaction connected with some
pins which had been pledged at different pawnbrokers-he said he knew
nothing about it.
Mr. BALLANTINE, Q. Before he stated any thing to you, had you held
out any promise to him? A. None whatever his children were there
I did not tell him I was, like himself, a family man-I do not think such
an expression was used by me, or any thing to that effect-I certainly
sympathized with the apparent distress of his wife and family-I should
say it was extremely unlikely I should make a remark of that kind
am quite sure I did not say it would be better to tell the truth, on the
contrary, I cautioned him, and he thanked me for my caution.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you say any thing to him about the Wal-

within the last week or ten days ?" he said no, he had not--I said, "Do
you not know a man of the name of Frenaby?”
