115. JOHN SKELTON (20), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
Messrs. Ellis and Cook conducted the prosecution.
EMMA MADGIN. My father keeps the Prince Alfved, in the Caledonian-
road-on 5th December the prisoner came about 9 o'clock-he asked for
half a pint of beer-it came to a penny-he gave me a 28. piece—I took it,
and gave him 18. 11d. change-as he left the house I examined the 28. piece
and found it was bad-I sent the potman after the prisoner, and he was
brought back in about five minutes with a constable said that he had
given me a bad 28. piece-I gave it to the constable, and he kept the
prisoner-I know Mr. Sims shop-it is about two minntes walk from our
house.
WILLIAM BLENCO (Policeman, N 440). I took the prisoner— I received
this florin from Miss Madgin-on searching the prisoner I found no money
on him-there was a packet of salts- I went to Mr. Sims' shop and received
from him this half-crown, which is bad.
HENRY DEVONPORT JONES. I am assistant to Mr. Sims, the chemist-last
Monday week the prisoner came to the shop for two ounces of salts, which
came to 14d.-he gave me a bad half-crown-I put it in the till, and gave
him 28. 44d. change-there was no other half-crown in the till-I saw the
prisoner afterwards in the constable's hands, when he was brought to the
shop half an hour afterwards-the policeman brought the package of salts
that I had given to the prisoner.
JAMES FREDERICK SIMS, I am the master of the last witness—I looked
in the till about half-past 9 o'clock-I found but one half-crown in it; it
was bad-I gave it to the constable.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These are both bad.
Prisoner. I am quite innocent.
GUILTY.-Confined Nine Months.
