• HENRY BERESPORD. I am an inspector of the division. On this day
I went with a gentleman to the prosecutor's house, a few minutes before five
o'clock, in consequence of a communication I had with him a few days
before-I was in disguise--I had been in seven or eight minutes, when two
persons came to the wholesale counter-one called for a glass of spirits-a
quartern of liquor was drawn by Lonsdale-while he was serving them across
the counter, directly he drew it, he whispered to them--one of them put
down a shilling-he dropped it into the till the moment the money was
given--he gave a shilling back and some halfpence-Robins then came in-
they were all three together-Lonsdale, without asking a question, or any
thing being said on either side, took a small liquor-glass, drew it full, and set
it on the counter--they were all close together-Robins put down a sixpence
on the counter-Lonsdale took it up and dropped it into the till he then
put his left hand into the halfpence, shook them, and never looked at bis
hand at all, but put some halfpence down, and took out the sixpence, and
laid it down beside them-Robins took them up, and as he left the bar, put
them into his right hand trowsers pocket- went out at another door close
by, and seized him with bis hand in his pocket-I found 64. in copper, and
a sixpence on him-I then took Lonsdale-in going down Queen-street,
Robins asked him whether he thought it would hurt his character, or whether
he could get bail-he said, “Hold your tongue, you b y fool; whatever
you have to say, say it in another place."
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Where were you? A. In front of
the bar-I had something to drink-Mr. Aldhouse, by my directions, called
for it--I should say I was about three yards from where Robins was stand-
ing-it is a cross counter, and comes to an angle- I had my eye directed to
what was going on, I swear I saw this sixpence, given by Robins, put into the
till - Lonsdale took the halfpence out with his left hand, and took the sixpence
out with his right.
• HENRY ALDHOUSE. I was there by direction of Mr. Beresford - I have
heard what he has stated-it is correct.
Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. Did Lonsdale ring the six-
pence? A. No, he put it into the till directly-I believe it was laid on the
counter, and taken up--I know he dropped it into the till--I saw him pick
it up-it was laid on the counter, to the best of my knowledge.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Are you sure it was a sixpence that
was given ? A. Yes, I am sure it was not a shilling--I was three yards off.
NOT GUILTY.
