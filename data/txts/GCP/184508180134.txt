1720. WILLIAM SUGGELL was indicted for stealing 1 pewter-pot,
value 18. 6d., the goods of Edward Minton; 1 pewter-pot, 18. 2d., the
goods of William Walter Wade; and 1 pewter-pot, 18., the goods of
Thomas Whatman; and WILLIAM WARNE for feloniously receiving
the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
EDWARD MINTON. I live with my father, Edward Minton, who is a
publican-this is his pot.
WILLIAM WALTER WADE. I am a publican. This pot is mine-
consider it was stolen from my premises—it is a bar pot, which we never
send out.
WILLIAM THOMPSON (police-sergeant D 4.) I found these pots inside
Warne's fire-place at his house, No. 50, Devonshire-street, Lisson-grove,
I had seen Suggell go to the house with something bulky about him-1
followed him, and saw him hand this bent pot from bis hat to Warne-
there was a frying-pan on the fire, and a small pot-I found two more pots
in the cupboard, with the names erased-Suggell might have handed more
pots to Warne before I got to them, for I fell down in going down the
stairs, and was sometime before I got to them.
THOMAS WHATMAN. This bent pot is mine.
SUGGELL-GUILTY."* Aged 42.2
WARNE-Guilty.*Aged 32. Confined Eighteen Months.
1721. ANN DOWNES was indicted for stealing 1 watch, value 145.;
1 seal, 6d.; 1 watch-key, 3d.; and 1 chain, 3d. ; the goods of John White;
and that she had been before convicted of felony.
MARY WHITE. I am the wife of John White, No. 3, Clarence-passage,
St. Pancras. The prisoner called on the 18th of Aug., about twenty
minutes before twelve o'clock, and staid till half-past one o'clock-after
she was gone I missed my husband's watch-I went to her mother's
dwelling and said to the prisoner, “You have taken my husband's watch ;
if you will give it up I will say nothing further about it"- this is the
watch, seal, key, and chain.
ANN MANSER. I found in the prisoner's bosom, at the station, this
key, seal, and chain.
William Gay. I am a pawnbroker--this watch was pawned by the
prisoner.
Prisoner. I went to her place; I had spent my mother's money; I was
quite tipsy with White, and I had pawned a scarf of my mother's.
MARY WHITE re-examined. I went to the Clarence for some beer, and
the prisoner was there drinking rum-she asked me to have some, and
then a young man who keeps company with her, brought her to my house.
JOHN HARDY (police-constable S 205.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction-(read-Convicted on the 28th of Jan., 1845,
and imprisoned one month)--the prisoner is the person.
GUILTY.-Confined One Year.
