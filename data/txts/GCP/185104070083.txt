945. ALEXANDER WARDROBE and BENJAMIN LEWITT,
feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Adam Urinh
Bryant Meakin, and stealing 12 saucepans, and 225 tin plates, value
bl. 188.; his property.--2nd COUNT, for receiving the same: both having
been before convicted of felony.
MR. Mew conducted the Prosecution,
FREDERICK Dibon. I am an apprentice to Mr. Meakin, a tin-worker, he.
lives at 27, Tunbridge-place, New-road; his shop is in Lemon-tree-yard,
Bedfordbury. On Thursday night, 6th March, 1 locked up the shop-I
was the last there I came out with the men about eight or nine o'clock
-I cannot fix the time nearer than that seven is the usual time for shut-
ting the shop-it was later that evening- I returned to the shop between
seven and half-past next morning, and found it had been broken open
the side window was broken open, and the counting-house door also I
sent for an officer-I know the prisoners -- they have both been workmen
of my masters-Lewitt was at this time, but Wardrobe had left, a dos
ADAM URIAH BRYANT MEAKIN. On Friday morning, about a quarter
to eight o'clock, Dibon fetched me from my private residence to the shop
-I found the shop had been broken into, and the counting-house window
broken open-- searched and missed a dozen saucepans from the manu-
factory, and a quantity of tin plates from the warehouse, besides a great
quantity of tea-kettles, and other articles (looking at some saucepans
produced) these are my property--my figures are inside this one I put
them on myself-it is impossible to tell how much I have lost-I have
missed as much as bl.-worth-Lewitt was working with me at the time-
I had taken Wardrobe into my employ about six weeks or two months
previous to the robbery- he worked with me about a week, and was then
discharged.
