387. GEORGE BAILEY was indicted for a like offence.
FREDERICK WESTLEY. My father is a stationer, in the Strand. The
prisoner came to me on Saturday, 16th Dec., a little after six o'clock, for
half a quire of note-paper and two envelopes, which came to 6d.-he offered
me a half-crown-I told him it was bad, and marked it with a hammer-I
asked where he came from ; he did not seem able to answer me--I asked
where he worked-he said, "Down the Strand"-1 cut the half-crown with
the scissors and put it amongst the halfpence-I took it out on Monday
morning and kept it in my pocket till Wednesday, when I gave it to the
policeman.
GEORGE READ. I am a butcher, of the Strand, about 150 yards from
Mr. Westley's. The prisoner came to my shop about a quarter-past six
o'clock on Saturday evening week for half a pound of beef-steak-he offered
me a bad half-crown-I said, "Young man, it is a bad one ; have you got
any more of these ?"-he said he had not-I chopped it into two pieces-I
gave him into custody, and gave the two pieces to the policeman.
THOMAS WARD ( policeman, F 102). I took the prisoner into custody at
Mr. Read's, and received from him these two pieces of broken coin, and this
half-crown from Mr. Westley-I found on the prisoner two penny-pieces.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL. This half-crown and these two pieces are
counterfeit. (The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined
Four Months.
388. GEORGE MORTLOCK, unlawfully having counterfeit coin in his
possession, with intent to utter it.
John Gunn policeman, A 401). On 24th Dec. I was on duty, with two
other officers, at eleven o'clock at night, in Peter-street, Cow-cross- I saw
the prisoner in company with another man-I knew the prisoner well—I said
to him, "What have you got about you !"-he held his hand up my
brother officer tried to get hold of it, but he threw a white-paper parcel away
-it fell on the pavement, and burst, and a number of half-crowns flew away
from it-there was a violent struggle with the prisoner-he tried to put his
foot on the half-crowns-I picked up two of them-he was taken to the
station-a 6d. and 3d., in copper, was found on him, in good money-he said
we should find nothing on him.
WILLIAM HENRY WASHER (policeman, A 407). I picked up nine of the
half-crowns close by the prisoner-I had observed him throw the packet away.
John THOMPSON (policeman, A 414). I saw the prisoner throw the packet
away-I picked up one of the half-crowns.
