Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Was he quite sober then! A. Yes
I have attended the family, and always gratuitously, except on one
occasion in her confinement—the prisoner's mother was in my family, and
rendered us some assistance of course, in sending for me, he would be sure
he was sending for some one who would not charge him for attendance-I
had attended this unfortunate woman-she had not complained of anything
in her head except headaches, nothing more when I saw the prisoner he
was intoxicated-he said to me that his wife was drunk, but it was not true.
Q. Supposing the head had been violently thrown on the hearthstone, or

violence-I only observed one external injury—there would have been
other marks than I saw, if it had been with great violence - I think there

hemisphere of the brain if a person had received a push, and had fallen
from the banister, that would occasiou all that I saw, because there would

his wife was the worse for liquor, or the worse for drink that was an

might take up-his wife was not a particularly heavy, strong woman.

MR. Parry. Q. Would a fall without a push have occasioned it? 4.

Q. You have heard Mrs. Browning's statement about the treatment on the
hearth, do you believe that so much violence could have taken place
without some marks of injury A. I think the absence of marks might be
attributed to the hair being on the head.
