 486.  HANNAH SAVORY and JANE FERGUSON were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of November , 1 gown, value 5s., and 1 shawl, value 5s., the goods of Hannah Lake, widow.
   HANNAH LAKE. I am a widow, and live in Kensington poor-house. I lost a gown and shawl on the 16th of November, from a room on the two-pair of stairs: the prisoner was in the house; I had seen my articles in their place on the Saturday, and missed them on Monday; I stopped Ferguson on the stairs, and said I suspected she knew something about my shawl and gown - they had been in a box, which was corded.
 Prisoner FERGUSON. She seized me by the throat, and said, "I insist upon searching you;" I said she was not a proper person - she said if I would not be searched she would have my life: she then searched me, and took me to the watch-house; she said she would have many a pound out of me, or she would send me across the herring-pond. Witness. I did not - she told me where they were pawned.
   JOHN KILLINGWORTH. I am a pawnbroker. These articles were pawned with me on the 17th of November, by Ferguson, I believe.
 Prisoner FERGUSON. Q. Do not you remember telling the Magistrate it was not me? A. No; I told him I believed it was you; I had some doubts about the other - two persons came, and one stood near the door.
 WILLIAM BENNETT. The prosecutrix came to me, and said the two prisoners had confessed where the things were - I went and took them, but found no duplicate on them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 FERGUSON's Defence. The Magistrate asked if she could swear it was me - she said she could not; there were sixteen people in the same room; the pawnbroker said he thought it was a girl taller than me, and the Magistrate discharged me; I had not gone ten yards, when I was taken to Hammersmith, and locked up for fourteen days, and then the pawnbroker said, to the best of his knowledge, it was me.
 WILLIAM BENNETT. I generally take my prisoners to Bow-street: the Magistrate asked me if one of the overseers were come - I said, No; he said I might take them to Kensington, which I did, but they were never out of my charge.
 FERGUSON -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 SAVORY -    NOT GUILTY.

 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM WOODBERRY. In consequence of information, I went to Proud's lodging, in Augusta-court, Laurence-street, Chelsea, about eight or nine o'clock in the morning of the 10th December: he had been apprehended the day before - I was informed in the house that was his room, and I found some pieces of wood in a box, which his wife unlocked - I believe they are called beadings or mouldings; in the kitchen in which Cooper lived, I found some mahogany; in a cellar under the stairs I found a large quantity of wood chopped up for fire-wood - I found these three pieces in a closet in the parlour; Cooper was not at home, but I left word for him to come to Mr. Cubitt's - he came, I left word for him to come to Mr. Cuasked him about the property - he said he had the mahogany from a lodger of his, who owed him money; I think he mentioned the name, but I forget it - he said he had had it several months; I think he said nine months; he said he did not know how these pieces got in the closet, but there was a piece of oak cill there which he had from the same man.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q.Why did not you bring the cill here? A.Because we had a great deal to bring; we have not ascertained that it was not Mr. Cubitt's - I think Cooper came in about four hours after I left word; it is a single round house - there is but one kitchen.
 ABRAHAM  THOMPSON WOOD. I am in the service of Messrs.  Cross-examined. Q.What are you? A. A clerk - I have nothing to do with manufacturing any wood; we did not miss any, but supposed wood to be stolen at different times - I can match these pieces of mahogany with some on the premises; I am certain that these pieces formed part of another plank cut from the same log as this plank - the whole log was in Mr. Cubitt's possession, and has not been cut four months.
 WILLIAM TAPSON. I am in the employ of Messrs. Cubitt's - I make barrows. I know these pieces of ash they were in my possession; these form the leg of a navigator's barrow - these were one solid body at one time; this piece I have brought was taken out here - they match exactly.

 COURT. Q.When had you seen them on the premises? A.Never, till they were found at the prisoner's- I never saw them at my master's.
