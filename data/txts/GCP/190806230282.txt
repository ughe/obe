I beat him, and he threatened to put me away." I searched the
prisoner and found on him nine postal orders for varying amounts,
totalling £3 13s. 1d., £23 10s. in gold, £2 in silver, 10 betting slips.
and 11 stamped envelopes addressed to himself, 15 similar envelopes
with the Flushing address bearing the 24d. stamp, 14 books of rules,
10 betting circulars, 10 private cards, with his name and address,
quoting the telephone number, and a pocket book containing entries
relating to betting. I found that the back room on the ground floor
was fitted as an office and was used extensively in connection with
the betting business. It was furnished with four tables and one
clerk was sitting at one of the tables receiving telephone messages.
There were six receivers in the room, and the clerk was devoting the
whole of his attention to the messages which were being received and
writing them down on a scribbling pad. He was also answering ques.
tions about different horses. Between one p.m. and four p.m. or five
p.m. 93 messages were received. I did not interfere with him and he
did not interfere with me. He was attending solely to that part of
the betting business. There were also a tape machine recording
racing news, & copying press, these two large ledgers (produced),
which are fairly full and relate to the credit betting business, a
large quantity of betting circulars and betting slips and different
papers and books, all relating to betting. The room was about 15 ft.
square. The safe was locked, but defendant gave me the key. Inside
the safe I found two postal orders one for 5s. and the other for
2s.6d., £21 10s. 6d. in silver, and £1 8s. in bronze, There were also
bank books. Another room in the basement was devoted to the busi-
ness. That was not furnished except with a table and was a sort of
store room for betting papers. There was also a table which had
apparently just been vacated by a clerk, who subsequently came in
and, in the presence of the defendant, said he was the clerk. There
were about 2,000 envelopes addressed to different persons in the
provinces, whom it was intended to circularise. While I was there
some man came and dropped a note in the letter box. Hearing some-
one moving away from the door, I went to the door and called him
back again. The note in the letter box contained a betting slip relat-
ing to horses named Detection, Maryport, Belle of Mayfair, and Ben
Beg, which were running on that day. The man admitted in the
presence of the defendant that he had put the slip into the letter
box. I said, "How did you come to do it?" He said, "I understood
I had dealings with a man who was carrying on his business in Hol.
land. I pay him the money there." Prisoner said, “That is all
right." While I was there 19 letters were received by post all re-
lating to betting, seven of them from persons whose names appear
in the book register as persons doing business with the defendant at
Flushing. Sixteen express letters by messenger were also received,
containing betting slips. These defendant explained were sent by
men he employed to take bets from customers in the streets. I also
