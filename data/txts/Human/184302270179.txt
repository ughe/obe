   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Have you the lead here? A.  Yes—the nook was six or seven yards from the brewhouse. WILLIAM TEATLING( police-constable K  264.) I received information from Nicol, and took the prisoners, in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour after wards, at the corner of Fore-street, standing both together—I did not tell them what I took them for, I only said, "I want you two youths"—Hennessey said, "I don't know anything about the lead." THOMAS SHEEN. I am a policeman. I was called by Teatling to assist him—I went the next morning to where the prisoners were taken, and found 47lbs. more of lead—I took that to the brewhouse, compared that and the other lead—it matched exactly. HENRY WOOK. I am a policeman. I went on the roof of the store-room at the brewhouse with Brundell—I had observed the week before that half the lead was gone, and now found the remainder was gone—Brundell pointed out some lead in the cellar of an empty house just by—I compared that with the other pieces of lead—it corresponded with them and with the roof also. JOHN BRUNDELL. I am foreman carpenter in the employ of Mr. Isaac Walker And two others—I inspect their premises from time to time—I missed about half the lead from the top of this place the week before—when Wood came on the Saturday morning we put the lead on again, and it matched exactly—it had been in the gutter and on the roof.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  What is the height of the place? A.  From eight to nine feet.
   COURT . Q.  How could boys, like the prisoners, get there? A.  We had pulled down part of an old house next to it, and by that means they could get up.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Were there not a number of work men employed there? A.  Yes. NOT GUILTY.
