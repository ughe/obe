Prisoner's Defence. It is not my intention to follow the learned gentleman
through all the mazes of the law-first, because I conceive that the late
stringent enactments that have been passed for the purpose of crushing
those who have embraced the same political opinions which I this day stand
here to advocate, are no doubt sufficiently explicit in themselves, to give him
all the advantage which a prosecutor requires to enable him to procure a
conviction; and secondly, it would be utterly out of my power to do so
being immured in a gaol, I was of course obliged to submit to the discipline of
the place; and although I was supplied with materials for writing, and such
purposes, yet I was denied the use of those books which I considered
were essential for my defence: this may seem rather strange, nevertheles it is
the fact, and this restriction was carried to such an extent, that not only were
political pamphlets refused me, but even a sight of the Penny Cyclopedia,
containing a definition of the word "Sedition," had the chaplain's veto affixed
boit; and further, I could obtain no knowledge of the specific nature of the
charge against me until Saturday, when I heard it very imperfectly in this
court, when I was called up to plead; but whether this is an arrangement
for the distinction of political and criminal offenders, or whether it is an
established rule of all the prisons in the kingdom, I am not sufficiently con-
Tersant with criminal jurisprudence and prison discipline to determine ; but
trust you will at once perceive, that labouring under such disadvantages, it
places me in a position not at all calculated to meet the charge, particularly
Woen that charge has been preferred by a gentleman who stands at the top of
