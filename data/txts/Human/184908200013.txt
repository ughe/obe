   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you known either of the prisoners before? 4. No; they were brought to me—Haycraft brought them to me. WILLIAM HAYCRAFT. I am a fishmonger, at Cow-cross, Smithfield. Mrs. Cox asked me if I heard of a horse to let her know. On 6th July, I met the prisoners with this horse in St. John-street, about three o'clock, just before the market opened, coming towards it—I asked if they would sell it—they said, "Yes"—I knew them by sight in the market—I never knew any harm of them—they said they had been round the country, and had more horses on the road, that they bought it of a man who had an execution in his house—they asked me 12 l .—I said, "I don't think the person has got above 8 l . or 9 l .—West said, "Who is the person?"—I said, "An honest woman who keeps a caravan, and a cart"—he said, "If she has got the money I don't mind trusting her a little"—we went in, and called her down; she looked at the horse, and said it would suit her—he asked her 12 l .—she said it was too much—an ostler in the yard said, "Mrs. Cox, if you can buy this horse for 8 l . I don't think it will hurt you"—they agreed to take 10 l .; 7 l . down, and 3 l . to be paid on the Friday, if the horse answered—I saw a receipt, and saw 7 l . on the table. EBENEZER GREIG.
