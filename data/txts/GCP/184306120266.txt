2053. THOMAS EAGLESFIELD was indicted for obtaining moner
by false pretences.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES Callow. I am an appraiser, and live in St. Mark’s-road, Kenning.
ton. In September last year I saw the prisoner-he ascertained my name
from me, and delivered this card to me-we had some ale together--nothing
particular occurred then I saw him again on the 10th of October) met
him in the street-he asked if my name was James Callow-I said, "Yes"_
he asked if I hạd a brother abroad I said yes, and his name was John Callow
-he then said there was money in Chancery, left by a John Callow abroad.
to James Callow, and he had searched all about, but could not find James
Callow till he found me, and he was sure it was right; it was up in Chancery,
and by the side of the account of the money it was said, " Foreign"-he said,
“ You was advertised in Kingsland-road." which was where I had lived he said
he could get it for me I saw him again, I think, the next night-he then said
for 12s. 6d. he would file affidavits in the Court of Chancery, to make applica.
tion-I gave him five half-crowns, to the best of my memory, and he gave me
this receipt-produced)-I saw him write it-(read)—"Received of Mr. Cal.
low, Oct. 11,1842, the sum of 12s. 6d. for filing affidavits on application made
to the Lord Chancellor. T. EAGLESFIELD.")-1 gave him the 12s. 6d. because
he said he was sure he could get the money, and he must pay it in Chancery,
or else I should lose the cause in a few days—I saw him the next day-he
then said, “I have done your business, sir, the Lord Chancellor asked me if
that was your assignment, and it is all right”-he told me he had paid the
128. 6d. in the Court of Chancery, to file affidavits-two days after he came
and got a sovereign, half-a-crown, and a shilling from me-he said that
was to pay Mr. Montague, the Counsel, to move the Court of Chancery, and
half-a-crown for the clerk-I gave it him, believing that he had properly ap-
plied the 128. 6d.--this is the receipt he gave me for the money-produced)
- he wrote it directly I paid the money-(read)—“ Received of Mr. Callow,
Oct. 14, 1842, 1l. 38. 6d. for application made to the Court of Chancery by
me, T. EAGLESFIELD.”-I saw him again next day—he said he had done the
business, that Mr. Montague was Counsel, he had paid him a guinea, and
half-a-crown for the clerk -I met him on the last day of December, in Chan-
cery-lane-he said he was going to get the order, it was all right-I went
with him to a public-house, and had some ale-I did not go to any office-be
said he had been to the Registry-office and got the order--this is the one he
delivered to me-he said it was made by Mr. Blake-he did not say what
Judge made it-he said it was made by a Master in Chancery, Sir W. Horne
- I have thoroughly searched and inquired, with an attorney, at the Chancery
courts, and find no account of any proceedings, or any death of my brother
advertised, or anything of that sort - I went with the attorney to search—(The
paper purporting to be the order was here read; it was addressed by Blake to
the prisoner, and informed him that property to the amount of 4000l. was left by
John Callow, of Vermont, United States; that the applicant, James Callow, was
his rightful heir, and directing him to take out letters of administration, upon
which the Court would grant him an injunction.)
