1667. JONATHAN POINTING, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 158.;
the goods of Hugh Watts.
solishi
HUGH Watts. I am in the Marines at Woolwich. On Friday morning,
3rd Aug., I gave a pair of boots to Frederick Noble, the waiter at the Royal
Standard, to take care of for me-these are them produced)--they are mine.
FREDERICK NOBLE. I put these boots in a cupboard in my bed-room on
the top shelf-I missed them next day- saw the prisoner in the tap-room
while the boots were there.
ho
THOMAS Walsh. I am a marine-store dealer at Woolwich. On 3rd Aug.
I saw the prisoner on Market-hill, with a person who knew me; he offered
me these boots for sale-I bought them there were two marines with him.
CHARLES MAY (policeman, R 296). I took the prisoner- he said he
knew nothing about it.
Prisoner's Defence. There was a house full of marines from eight o'clock
in the morning till ten at night.
Not GUILTY.
1668. GEORGE TRUBY, stealing I watch, value 258.; the goods of
John Moore.
GEORGE TAYLOR. I am shopman to John Moore, pawnbroker of Wool-
wich. On 2nd July, the prisoner came and said he wanted a watch-I showed
him a silver one-he asked me to show bim another-I turned my back, and he
ran out of the shop-I ran after him, calling "Stop thief!"-he was stopped,
and I found the watch behind him--this is it produced).
George Gurney. I heard a cry of “Stop him!"-I saw the prisoner
stopped, and saw Taylor pick up a watch at the place.
WILLIAM HARDING (policeman, R 78). I took the prisoner--he said he
knew nothing about it-he said at the station he only stole it to get a blanker,
meaning a blank discharge from the régiment, which soldiers get when they
-are convicted of felony-he had been drinking, but knew what he was doing.
Guilty. Aged 27.-Confined Six Months.
