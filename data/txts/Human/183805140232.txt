WILLIAM RANDALL. I am a surgeon. I usually attend Mr. Bonner's family—I live next door to them—I was sent for to the deceased—he seemed quite in possession of himself—his head was bleeding—I took the hair off, clearly to see the state of the wound—the scalp was divided, not so deep as the bone—blood was flowing in considerable quantities from the wound, and it appeared to be bruised as well as divided—there were no marks of more than one blow—as there was but one wound, I think it must have been only one blow—I directed him to get his head poulticed—it did not strike me as at all dangerous at the time.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You did not consider the wound of any serious character then? A.  I did not—I was told to attend him the next day, and see him as often as was necessary, and Mr. Bonner would satisfy me for any expenses that would occur—I told him to come to me the next morning—he did not come—I went to him to Guy's Hospital, at the de sire of Mr. Bonner, but I could not interfere there—I did not see him lat terly—he was never let out of the hospital—I believe, for a considerable time, he was doing well, and was expected shortly to recover. JOHN COALES. I was an assistant to Mr. Key, a surgeon, at Guy's Hospital, at the time the accident happened. I remember the deceased coming there about the middle of the day on the 23rd, as an inmate—he remained there till his death—on the top and forepart of his head was a slight contused wound, about an inch and a half long and not very deep—I thought it of little consequence—he had, however, some suspicious symptoms which indicated that the brain had received some slight injury—no portions of the bone ever came away; a piece of the bone became dead, and was undergoing the process of excoriation—I consider the blow had produced a slight injury to the brain, which in about nine weeks pro duced inflammation of the membranes, and that produced death.
   Q.  Then you trace the death to the original blow? A.  Undoubtedly.
   Cross-examined. Q.  If he had not received the blow he would not have been exposed to this? A.  No, he underwent the operation of trepanning rather more than two months after the injury—for a considerable time Mr. Key and myself, and all the surgeons, had great hope of his recovery. GUILTY  . Aged 60.— Strongly recommended to mercy by the Jury. Fined Five Pounds.
