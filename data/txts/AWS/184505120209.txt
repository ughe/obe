1215. CATHERINE GREEN was indicted for stealing 1 gown, the
goods of Alice Barber.
ALIcg BARBER. I am a widow, and live at Rotherhithe. I left my
gown behind the door on the 5th of April, between three and four o'clock
in the afternoon, leaving the door locked-I returned on the Monday fol-
lowing-I found the door open, and the gown gone-the prisoner was not
at home-I lodge there, and her husband belongs to the house-when she
came home I asked her where my dark gown was-she said she took it
and pawned it, and would get it me again.
Prisoner. She lent me the gown. Witness. I did not.
JOHN WADHAM. I am a pawobroker. This gown was pawned on the
5th of April by the prisoner, in the name of Ann Davis, which is the name
she goes by.
Prisoner. She is my husband's own aunt.
GUITy. Aged 4.-Confined Three Months.
1216. THOMAS REDDY was indicted for burglariously breaking
and entering the dwelling-house of John Askew, about the hour of three
in the night of the 17th of April, in St. George the Martyr, Southwark,
with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 2 coats, value 2l. 108.; 1 half-
sovereign, 3 shillings, 9 groats, 36 pence, and 24 halfpepce; his property,
JOHN Asxew. I am a draper, and live at No. 110, Blackman-street,
in the parish of St. George the Martyr, Southwark. I left my coat hang-
ing in the counting-house at ten o'clock on Thursday night, the 17th of
April-there was a letter in the pocket-the coat and letter now produced
are what I left there-I was not the last up that night, nor the first up
the next morning-I looked at the skylight over the counting-house
the next morning, and part of the frame of it was broken-tl had first
broken a square, and opened a small light, and then opened the skylight,
and got in-I missed the coat which had the letter in it.
JOHN Brown. I am shopman to the prosecutor. I saw the skylight
safe on the night of the 17th of April at ten o'clock-I was the last person
in the shop-I locked the shop, and all was safe-there was a half-sove-
reign, three shillings nine groats, thirty-six pence, and twenty-four half-
pence in the till-I awoke in the morning about six o'clock, and went
down a few minutes after-I then found the skylight broken-they had
entered the shop, and taken the money from the till, and I missed two
coats from the counting-house-all the drawers in the counting-house had
been broken open, and the papers were all about the floor.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Were there many things
stolen A. Only the coats and money.
