   Sarah Dabbs.  You said you would not give me in charge. Witness.  There was no such word said—the policeman said, "I see, the girl has bounced you out of your watch, so, old woman, you had better go home;" and two days afterwards that policeman was discharged for being a bad character. GEORGE COCK. I had the watch—I would it up at a quarter before ten, and placed it on the hook again—it was perfectly safe—in a short time I saw Elisabeth Dabbs run out of the room with it—my wife ran after her, as I had got my boots off.
   Elizabeth Dabbs.  You know you were perfectly tipsy, you could not fol low me. witness.  I was perfectly sober.
   Elizabeth Dabbs' Defence.  I did not take it, nor did my mother; Mr. Cock asked me to make tea, as she was not able—I then went and told my mother to come; we wanted to have a pint of beer; my mother said, "I have got a penny in my bosom;" I was feeling for it; this women came, and said, "Did you see my husband's watch?" I said, "No;" my mother said, "I hope you do not accuse my daughter;" she said, "No;" the policeman and she came, searched the room, and it was not there; the policeman is not on duty now.
   Sarah Dabbs' Defence.  The policeman came, my wish; there was no watch; I said, "I hope you don't think my daughter has got it;" she said, "No; my husband is in the habit of putting it away, and he has done so;" I said, "Here is the policeman; "he came and searched, and said, "Shall I take them in charge?" she said, "No; my husband has mislaid it before, he may have done it again;" we went to two public-houses, and then to the Feathers; we had all had enough; Mrs. Cock swore the watch was on the table, and next time she it was on the hook. AMELIA COCK re-examined.  My husband never mislaid his watch—I never said so—we were all perfectly sober—Dabbs was at home in the dark when the prisoners went home—the door was open—they had no key.
   ELIZABETH DABBS  — GUILTY . Aged 17.— confined Three Months .
