WILLIAM GLADWIN (policeman, R 122.) On Wednesday, 2nd Feb., I wa
in the bar-parlour of the prosecutor's house, and saw Flowers standing in
front of the bar-I saw Johuson and Mayhew come in and go up stairs-1
afterwards heard a bell ring, went and took Flowers into custody as he stood
at the bar, and handed him over to Sergeant Parry-I went up stairs, and
saw Johnson struggling on the floor with Newell-I saw him put his hand
under the sofa-cover, I turned it up and found this key, and these two I took
from the fire (produced)-on the same evening I went with a search warrant
to Flowers' house, the Folly, at Bethnal-green-Duffield accompanied me
he pointed out a cupboard, in which I found these eighteen skeleton keys,
crowbar, a chisel, dark-lantero, and a canvas-bag-in a cupboard, in the bar
parlour, I found this piece of apron, which exactly corresponds with the piece
found on the fire at Mr. Mould's-I also found some dummies, or paper
parcels, one contains an old sboe--they are neatly tied up, intended to throw
off suspicion, as if for a gentleman or a clerk.
JAMES PARRY (police-sergeant, R 8.) On Wednesday, 2nd Feb., by the
inspector's direction, I went with other constables to Mr. Mould's house-
went into the bar-parlour-I saw Flowers standing in front of the bar, and
saw Johnson and Maybew go up stairs--I assisted in searching Flowers' hour
-I searched Mayhew at the station, and found on him a knife and a ball
peony-he said he had dropped a half-crown in the room, but I found non-
I only felt the outside of his pockets in the room, to see if he had any money
-Flowers denied knowing anything of the men up stairs.
WILLIAM JAMES COVILL. My house was robbed, on 10th Jan., of about
1651.-Duffield communicated with me on the subject on 30th Jan.
Cross-eramined by MR. THOMPSON. Q. I believe the fact of your robbery
was very well known throughout Woolwich ? A. It was, and the extent of
it-I lost four 51. pound notes, and a Government bill.
EDWARD JAMES Levy (police-inspector, R.) Duffield communicated with
me on this subject, on 30th Jan.- he had my sanction for going on in the
matter after that.
JOHN JENKINSON (policeman, G 53.) I know Flowers, and I know
Johnson, by the name of William Montrose-I have seen them in company
many times at different public-houses-I have frequently met them in differ
ent flash houses in the neighbourhood of Sboreditch, and in the street--they
were always to be found in the neighbourbood of Shoreditch and Bethnal
green, not at Woolwich.
WILLIAM BALL (policeman, N 365.) I know Flowers and Johnson-1
have seen them two or three times together-I have known Flowers about
six years-be kept a shop in the general line in Hoxton about three year
and since that he has lived in the neighbourhood of Bethnal-green.
