900. PHILLIP DILLON, stealing 28lbs. weight of rope, value 25.;
the goods of the East and West India Dock Company, from a dock
adjacent to the river.
JAMES FELL. I am a gate-keeper in the service of the East and West
India Dock Company. On 18th March, between eight and nine o'clock
at night, I saw the prisoner and three other lads about twenty yards from
house I was at, close to the Docks—I saw three of them go to a wall,
where there was some rope, the property of the Company, hanging up-I
am quite sure the prisoner was one of them - two of them lifted the rope
off, and the other some small line, took it on their backs, and ran away-
I ran after them; they dropped it, and I caught the prisoner.
John SUTTON. I am a constable, in the service of the East and West
India Dock Company. The prisoner was given into my custody, and
this rope (produced)—be said the other boys asked him to lend a hand to
carry it away, and he was taken.

902. GEORGE HUGHES, stealing 6 sovereigns, 4 half-sovereigns,
2 half-crowns, and other moneys; of Daniel Elliott, in his dwelling-house.
MR. PLATT conducted the Prosecution.
DAVID Smeg. I live with Mr. Elliott, a cheesemonger, of Portland-
place, Edgware-road. On 4th March, I was behind the counter, Mr.
Elliott was in the shop, and he called my attention to the prisoner who
was leaving it - pursued him into a coffee-shop, in Church-street, Pad-
dington, and found him in a corner very pale and agitated-I asked him
if he had stolen some eggs-he said, No"--I took him back; a lad
went with us; and Mrs. Elliott said, in the prisoner's presence, she had
missed a parcel of money I went back to the coffee-shop, and saw
parcel on the table, containing gold and silver-I took it back, Mr. Elliott
counted it; it contained six sovereigns, four balf-sovereigns, two half-
crowns, four shillings, six sixpences, three fourpenny-pieces, and one half-
penny.
