WILLIUM HAMMOND( police-constable P  90.) On Friday evening, the 15th of Nov., I was called by Mrs. Barnes into the shop—while speaking to her the prisoner came in—I heard Mrs. Barnes ask her why she took her box away—she said because she wanted to book her place to go by the eight o'clock buss  to Charing-cross—she ran out at the door, down to where she had taken her box, about fifty yards from Mr. Barnes's—I followed—she took her box from there—I took her into custody as she was turning the things out of her box into the chandler's shop—I stopped her from doing so, put the things back into the box, and took her and the box to Mr. Barnes's—on getting there the things now produced were found in her box—she said she had bought some of them at two or three shops above there—I found this silver spoon in her hand, a little knife, and this necklace. FREDERICK BARNES re-examined . I cannot swear to the spoon—it is such a one as I had in use—I know these beads—I missed some, and these match exactly with them.
   Prisoner . My mistress would not pay me my wages; she told me she should not pay me a farthing; I went up stairs in an obstinate humour, and did put the things into my box, I own; she took me more than 100 miles from home.
   MR. BARNES . I told her I did not think she ought to be leaving me as she did, but I was going to pay her just before this robbery was found out—she said she had no fault to find with my mistress or me, but she did not like Peckham—I did not turn her away—we should have been glad to have had her stop if she had done right.
   Prisoner . My mistress told me I might help myself, for I should not have a farthing of wages; I had no fault to find with my master or mistress, but I did not like Peckham.
   MR. BARNES . I have no reason to believe that my wife told her to help herself—my wife has money—I do not know that she always has money by her—I suppose she had sufficient, but I do not know how it was at that time—I understood the prisoner to bear a very good character when I took her—her friends are very respectable—I do not know what wages she was to have.
   Prisoner . My last mistress gave me 3 l ., but said she would give me 5 l . if I would stop; Mrs. Barnes said she would give me 6 l . a year if I would come up with them. GUILTY  . Aged 20.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor.  — Judgment Respited  .
