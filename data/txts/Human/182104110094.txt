 688.  ANN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , the sum of 9 s. in monies numbered, the property of William Hill , from his person.
   WILLIAM HILL. I am a watch-gilder, and live in Great Sutton-street, Clerkenwell. On the morning of the 8th of April, after two o'clock, I was returning from my master's house, in Duke's-street, Lincoln's-inn-fields. I had been to Trinity-square part of the way home with a friend. I had business with my master, which kept me late. I was quite sober. In passing through White-cross-street I met this woman; she wanted me to go home with her. I said I had a home of my own. I had got my hand in my right-hand pocket with my silver in my hand; I let go of it and took my hand out, in order to disengage myself from her, as she had hold of me; I did not perceive her hand in my pocket; but the instant she turned from me I missed my silver - there was 10 s. 6 d.; 1 s. and 6 d. were left; I lost 9 s. among which was a half crown. Nobody was near enough to do it but her. I took her to the watch-house, and gave her in charge, stating what money I had lost. She denied having it, and said she had no money. The officer found it on her - there was a half crown, 5 s. and three sixpences. She had no more money.
   WILLIAM PARTRIDGE. I am constable of the night. The prisoner was brought in by Hill; he said he had lost 9 s., among which was a half crown piece. I asked her if she had the money? She said she had none whatever about her. I searched her, and in her right-hand pocket I found a half crown piece, 2 s. and a 6 d., and down her bosom I found 3 s. and two sixpences. She said next morning that the prosecutor gave her part of it.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 689.  JOHN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of March , one spade, value 1 s., and one shovel, value 1 s., the goods of Robert Doe.
   ROBERT DOE. I am a cow-keeper, and live in the City-road. The prisoner is a stranger to me. The spade and shovel were in my cow yard, near Goswell-street. The prisoner was brought to me by an officer. I never gave him authority to take them.
   WILLIAM EDWARDS. I am a spring-maker. I was standing in Henry-street, Old-street-road, about a quarter of a mile from the cow-yard, and saw the prisoner and three more young lads coming from across some ruins in a direction from the cow-yard, they stopped against the corner of a stable which I knew had been robbed. I went over the way to see if they had any thing; they retreated when I went towards them. I got over the pailing after them, and the shovel and spade were thrown down by one of them, all four ran away. I overtook the prisoner, and asked how he came by them? he said, he had not stolen them, but the boys had, and he was going with them to White-cross-street to sell them. Doe's name was on them.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met these boys as I was going to work, they said they would come with me; I turned aside for something, they followed me. The man said,
 "What have you got?" they then threw them down, and ran away.
     GUILTY. Aged 10. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 690.  MICHAEL NEAL was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of March , one coat, value 3 l., the goods of Henry Arthur Broughton.
 MR. HONE conducted the Prosecution.
   ROBERT REYNOLDS. I am groom to Mr. Henry Arthur Broughton, of Great Marlborough-street, solicitor. On the 6th of March I was going with my master to Clarendon-place, Edgware-road, in a gig. On arriving there I got out of the gig and rang the bell; it was about half past six o'clock in the evening. When I returned from ringing the bell, I saw the wheel of a cart locked into the wheel of the buggy. It is a wide road. I had drawn up as close as possible to the foot way. There was room for four carriages to pass without touching us. There were at least six persons in the cart; it was full of men and one boy. Master's box coat was in the buggy over his knees; we had the head up, it being a wet night. I saw the prisoner in possession of the coat about ten minutes after I rang the bell. We were expostulating with them for coming in contact with the gig, and begging them to unlock the wheels. Master ordered me to pursue the prisoner; I overtook him about a hundred and twenty yards from the gig, and saw the coat on his arm; he dropped it, and put himself in a fighting posture. I drew back, and stooped to pick up the coat, and he gave me a very severe blow between the two eyes; my hat fell off. The blow knocked me down. Several of the party who had been in the cart were behind, and I received some kicks from them as I lay in the road. The prisoner picked up a silk handkerchief which flew out of my hat, and ran away with it; he came back in about a quarter of an hour, to enquire what was the matter, and was secured.
