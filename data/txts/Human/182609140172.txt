JOSEPH SCOTT. I am shopman to George Tickell, a linen-draper, of Leadenhall-street. On the 19th of August, about twelve o'clock, the prisoner came and looked at some ribbons; he was about an hour there, and selected about thirty pieces - I had observed him put one piece into his pocket; he said he was going to Houndsditch, and would call in half an hour; I followed him out, brought him back, and he produced two pieces, and said, "I suppose this is what you want, but it is not Mr. Tickell's;" I said I could swear to it - he was given in charge - he had paid nothing.
   JOHN THOMAS GRAY. I am an officer, and took him; he had no money whatever.
 The prisoner pleaded distress. GUILTY. Aged 67.
 Strongly recommended to Mercy, believing it was distress that induced him to commit the offence. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1785.  THOMAS JOSEPH BACON and THOMAS GARROWAY were indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , 1 pair of breeches, value 9s., the goods of Edward Adams and George Clamp.
   WILLIAM ADAMS. I live with Edward Adams, my father, who is in partnership with George Clamp; they live in Aldersgate-street. On the 11th of July these breeches hung about five inches within the door; I heard the cry of Stop thief! I ran to the door, missed some things, crossed over, and found the prisoners in custody, with the breeches, twenty doors off.
   CORNELIUS BIRDSEYE. My father lives in Old-street. I saw the prisoner, Bacon, coming from Adams' shop - I and another person met him; I had seen the prisoners in company about the neighbourhood for a quarter of an hour, and watched them, but did not see the breeches taken.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I took the prisoners on the 11th of July, in Aldersgate-street, near Adams' shop; we had been watching them; I saw Garroway cross the road - Bacon came to him with something under his coat - I seized him, and took him with the breeches; Birdseys ran and took Garroway.(Property produced and sworn to).
 BACON'S Defence. I bought them.
 BACON -    GUILTY. Aged 22.
 GARROWAY -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 1786.  THOMAS BROWN and WILLIAM PINK were indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of a certain person unknown , from his person.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I am a Bow-street patrol. On the 6th of September, about twelve o'clock, I had been watching the prisoners above an hour, in company; I saw them go round several gentlemen, and touch their pockets; when they came to Finch-lane, Pink took a handkerchief from a gentleman's pocket, and ran off - they were close together - I was thirty yards off; I called to the gentleman - he did not hear me; I then followed them, and took them both; Pink twisted from me and ran away, but was stopped; he threw this handkerchief down.
 PINK's Defence. I bought the handkerchief.
 Several witnesses gave the prisoners good characters.
 BROWN -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 PINK -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Whipped and Discharged.
 1787.  THOMAS EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 ring, value 20s., the goods of John Barfield.
 Mr. JOHN BARFIELD. I am a  jeweller, and live in Cheapside. The prisoner came to my shop on the 7th of September, at a little before eight o'clock in the morning - I was called down - he asked to see some mourning rings; I showed him a tray, and observed that he took up two, and returned but one to the tray - he said we had none to fit him, and was going out; I told him he had stolen a ring; I closed the door, and sent for a constable - he had then had the ring in his possession about five minutes - he was searched - his coat and waistcoat were taken off, and I saw a ring between his fingers, which I supposed to be mine, but it was not; we then ceased searching him - he was taken to the Compter; I swore to the ring before the Magistrate - it was a fac-simile of mine.
 Q. Did you miss any ring? A. No.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. And yet you swore to this? A. Yes - I believed it was mine then, but now know it was not; I told the Grand Jury so, and am surprised they have found the bill.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1788.  THOMAS EDWARDS was again indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 ring, value 20s., the goods of Samuel Jones.
   THOMAS JONES. My brother, Samuel Jones, lives at No. 78, Cheapside, and is a jeweller. The prisoner came about eight o'clock in the morning of the 7th of September, and looked at several mourning rings - none would fit him: he went away, saying he would call again in the evening if I would get one to fit him - in about half an hour Mr. Barfield sent to me; I went to his shop, and found the prisoner there; I understand the ring found on him is my brother's, but I missed none; I have sold several of this sort.
     NOT GUILTY.
