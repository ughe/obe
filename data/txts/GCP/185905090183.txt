handed the I. O. U. up to the Judge; he looked at the signature, and re-

Is that your signaturel" and he said “No”-the Judge asked him whether
he could write, he said “Yes"—the Judge requested me to give him a pen
and ink; I did so, and said " Now write your name;" that paper has been
mislaid, and I cannot find it; but he wrote “Wilham" or "Wiltam;" hows
ever, the Christian name was spelt wrong--I handed it up to the Judge,
who said, "Can you spell your name?” he said, "Yes; and spelt
" William " correctly—the Judge then cautioned him, and asked him again
whether it was his signature-- he said, “No "on that the prosecutor and
his brother were examined again, and they swore positively that they saw
him sign it; and an order was made that he should be committed.
Cross-examined by MR. BEST. Q. Is it part of your duty to examine the
cases instead of the Judge 4. Not instead of the Judge; but I do put
the questions, by his permission, to facilitate business. I have not got the
original copy; the prisoner has that he appeared as sober as he does now,
when he was examined; but in the after part of the day he was excited
when I insisted on his putting certain papers in his pocket-the Judge ad-
dressed him before his committal to prison-he kept perpetually denying
his signature he said " I owe the money," and commenced pulling a quan-
tity of papers out of his pocket-—I said "Do not excite yourself ; calmly
look at that piece of paper, and tell the Judge whether the signature, W.
Hogan,' is your writing ;" and he said “No; it is not;" he stuck to the
"No" all through-a man that was not sober might think he could deceive
by signing his name wrong; I think so now.
MR ROBINSON, Q. What was the interval before the first committal
4. When the Judge ordered him to be committed, it was half past 1, and
I think, before he was sent to prison, it was about 3 in the afternoon.
JOHN JONES. I am Usher at the County-court- administered the oath to
the prisoner--I saw him during the whole tinne he was in Court; I saw
nothing like intoxication about him.
Cross-examined. V. Have you never cases of drunken people at your
Court ? 4. Yes; and we put them on one side till they are sober-drunken
persons are not always excited,
