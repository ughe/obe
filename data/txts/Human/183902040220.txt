their friends, as they were engaged in these smuggling transac tions—I did not consent to wait—I said I would have nothing further to do with it, and left the house—I had got about half-way across Wellclose-square, when Christall came after me, and said it would be doing him a service if I would buy the butters, as he should only get his commission upon it—I said no, I would have nothing further to do with it—I called on Tripp at his lodgings within five minutes, on my way home, and com municated to him what had passed—in consequence of that, Shelswell and Taylor, the officers, were sent for from Lambeth-street—I went home, and Tripp came to my house soon after, and Shelswell and Taylor were placed outside my door—I and Tripp looked more par ticularly at the cask, and noticed the brand marks had been cut out—Christall then came to my house, and asked me to buy the one cask—I said no, I would not have any thing more to do with it—he said he had made a mistake, it was not Kiel butter, as he had told me, but it was Friezland butter—I told him no, it was not Friezland butter, it was Kiel, but I refused to buy it—he beckoned to Hopkins to come and take it away, and Hopkins came and put it on a truck in front of my door—I do not think there was any name on the truck, but I did not look parti cularly—I did not notice whether they went away together—I saw it put into the truck, Hopkins being with it, and Christall left my house—I did not notice whether he covered it over.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Christall and you went to gether from your house to this place in Wellclose-square? A.  Yes—we had some talk together on the road—he did not propose to have any other dealings with me, to my knowledge—he said I might have 100 gallons of brandy if I liked.
   Q.  Did he not profess his readiness to let you have any article in the smuggling line—not calling it so, but intimating it? A.  I recollect he mentioned brandy.
   Q.  That sort of article on which the duty was not paid? A.  Yes—I had asked for the head of the cask before we left my shop—I do not re collect Christall inquiring for it when we got to Wellclose-square—he might have done so, but I did not hear him—I said nothing about the head there—he said it would be of service if I would buy the butters, as he should get his commission—Hopkins acted merely as a porter, bringing the goods with the truck, and afterwards following to Wellclose-square with the truck—one cask being left at my house, there would be good reason for a porter to suppose he would have to bring more—he did not assist in any way but as a porter.
