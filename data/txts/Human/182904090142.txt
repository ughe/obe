 994.  ANN THEW was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 cloak, value 7s.; 1 gown, value 1s, 6d., and 1 pair of shoes, value 1s. 6d., the goods of William Martin , her master.
   WILLIAM MARTIN. I am a schoolmaster, and live in Bull and Mouth-street, The prisoner was in my service, about two months previous to the 13th of February, on which day she left me; we missed the articles stated, and found her about a fortnight afterwards, in Field-lane; the articles were found on her, except the shoes, which were in pawn.
   JOSEPH WILLIAMS. I am a constable. I went with the prosecutor to a house on Saffron-hill, where the prisoner was seen to go in; I inquired if there was a person of her name there - they said there was; I met her on the stairs, and asked her if her name was Ann Thew- she said No; I said I would not let her go till I got a light, as it was very dark: I found these articles in the room, not on her; I was told she lodged there - she said she had torn the lining out of the cloak, and threw it in the fire, and the gown was up stairs in the room; there was no threat or promise made to her.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 The prosecutor stated that he had missed other property which the prisoner acknowledged she had taken.
 Second Middlesex Jury.
 995.  GEORGE FRANKLIN, RICHARD HEMMINGS, and JOHN GRAY, were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Thomas Atkins , on the 29th of March , and stealing therein 2 pecks of onions, value 2s.; 2 casks, value 3s.; 4 gallons of shrub, value 30s.; 2 gallons of peppermint, value 1l.; 2 gallons of gin, value 18s.; 2 gallons of cloves, value 1l.; 3 gallons of Rum, value 30s., and 2 bottles, value 4d., the goods of the said Thomas Atkins.
 MR. BARRY conducted the prosecution.
   THOMAS ATKINS. I keep the  White Lion public-house, Dancer's-hill, South Mims. My house was safe on the night of the 29th of March, and the cellars quite secure - the next morning, which was Sunday, I went to draw some ale - I saw the cellar broken open; the boards had been broken exactly where the bolts were, and they had put their arm in and drawn the bolts back; I can swear that I missed about ten gallons from different casks, of cloves, peppermint, shrub, rum, and gin - some ale, onions, casks, and stone bottles - one bottle had a little shrub in it, one a little ale, and one was empty; I sent off to Barnet for an officer, and we both examined the premises; a person called on me, and I went with my ostler, cooper, to the field, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon; I found five men drinking in the field, four of whom I knew - the prisoners were three of them; they were drinking ale out of a stonebottle - they all drank in their turns - it was such a bottle as I had lost; Franklin was sitting on one of the casks which I knew - I ordered my ostler to take three of the casks up, and Gray said, "Master took it out;" I said they were my casks - they said if they were mine I might take them; Franklin said they found them in the hedge - I got on the coach to go to Barnet, leaving them with one bottle, pouring ale out; Cooper took the casks to my house - I got two officers, went to South Mims, and found Gray in a chandler's-shop; we went to a cottage near where they had been drinking and found Franklin - my ostler and I then got on a coach, and took Hemmings about a mile on this side of St. Alban's.
