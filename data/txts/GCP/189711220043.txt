Cro88-examined: MR, GEOGHEGAN. I had not known Aylmer before
I knew Morgan, or Moor; it was after-Morgan did not introduce me
to him-Aylmer speculated for me on the Stock Exchange-I know what
speculating for differences means; those were my dealings with Aylmer
-tbere have have been a great number of cheque transactions between
me and Morgan-I am a loser by £270_-I suppose some of these trans-
actions later on were accommodation bills ; I include those, and every-
thing, with the £270-after Morgan had told me he had burnt the bill
for £26 14s. 6d. I found it was in Cobbett's hands ; Morgan did not
tell me he had found it-he said he would hold me harmless as to it,
and I trusted to him - only one of those bills of £26 148. 6d.
has been presented-I wrote to Cobbett and nothing more was done
I do not include the second bill for £26 14s. 6d, in the expressions in my
letters-I met one of those bills and no proceedings have been taken
against me in respect of the other; it has not been presented-I suppose
it was in consequence of an arrangement between Cobbett and Morgan
that the second one was not presented—I understand that the eighth
Count refers to a bill for £25, dated June 26th, 1896--two bills were
given about that date, both for £25-Morgan told me in reference to the
first of these that Sachwell had the money-two of my bills-each for
£25, being out, Morgan and I came to the arrangement that he should
take up one and I the other, and that arrangement was carried out
-I noticed that Evans' cheque for £100 was not endorsed-that
cheque has never been presented so far as I know I called Morgan's
attention to the fact that it was not endorsed-I understood they
were endeavouring to raise £500 capital for the company-I kept
Evans' cheque for £100-I did not present it, it was not endorsed
and I hardly liked to pass it through my bank-Morgan said that
Evans would not allow it to be used unless the £500 capital were
raised-I understood from Morgan that the payment of that £100
cheque to the United Founders' Association was conditional on
the £500 being raised he said something about my getting a bonus
if the £500 was raised - I did not take much notice of it; I thought
more of getting the money for my bill - I don't think that he told me
that if the £500 capital were not raised he would have to return the
cheque to Evans, exactly-he said he was not at liberty to use it-he
said, "I have the cheque, but I am not at liberty to use it"-he did not
tell me who was going to find the other £400—after January, 1896, when
I first saw the advertisement, there have been a good many transactions
between me and Morgan, I don't think 100, or very nearly 100-
receiving cheques, paying cheques, giving bills, returning bills, and so
forth, it might have been something like that.
