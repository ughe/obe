1799. JOHN RUSSELL, was indicted for unlawfully uttering counter feit coin.
   MR. SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution. JAMES TAYLOR. I keep a shop. On the 4th of June, the prisoner came to my shop for half a pint of porter—he gave me a good half-crown, and I gave him 2 s . 6 d . change—I observed a cross on the head of each shilling—he took them off the counter, and the halfpence also, and said, "Master, you have given me a dark one," and gave me back 1 s ., which I saw was a bad one and had no cross on it—I took it and beckoned to a person to stop him—I gave the shilling to West, the officer. THOMAS WEST( police-constable M  249.) I received this shilling from Taylor. MR. JOHN FIELD. This is a counterfeit shilling.
   Prisoner's Defence.  He said he could not give me change, and then he found the shillings; as soon as he put them down I said this one looks rather dark; he took it up and went into the tap-room, and out came three men, who said it was a good one. JAMES TAYLOR re-examined.  About five minutes elapsed from the time of his passing the bad shilling and his being searched—there were two more behind him, near enough to touch him—I marked the shillings, because it was the fourth time I had been served so. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Six Months .
 1800. ALEXANDER REVELL was indicted for stealing 1 watch-guard, value 1 s  .; 1 cross, 1 s  .; 2 keys, 6 d  .; 1 reticule, 1 s  .; 2 half-crowns, 2 shil lings, 2 sixpences, 3 pence, and 4 halfpence, the property of Sarah Shirley ; from her person. SARAH SHIRLEY. I am servant to Mr. Maldon, in the City-road. I was returning down the Kent-road, about ten o'clock at night, on the 28th of May—there were three boys together—one of them made up to me, and took my reticule from my arm—it contained the property stated—this is it—I I do not know who the person was—he ran down a street. JOSEPH SKINNER. I live at Peckham. I heard a cry of "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner running, and saw him throw this bag down—I took it up with my right hand, and took him with my left—this is the bag.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What are you? A.  An auc tioneer and appraiser—I have no doubt about the prisoner.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months .
 1801. GEORGE WESTCOTT was indicted for embezzlement. CATHARINE SOAMES. I am a widow, and live in Dorset-street, Clapham-road. On the 5th of May I paid the prisoner 7 s . 7 d . for his master, Mr. Fox, for beer, and on the 7th of June 11 s . 1 d . MARY ANN ROWCLIFFE. I paid the prisoner 2 s . 3 d . on the 2nd of June for his master. CHARLES FOX. The prisoner was my servant—if he received money, it was his duty to pay it to me directly—if he received 7 s . 7 d . he has not paid it to me at any time—if he received 2 s . 3 d . from Mrs. Rowcliffe he has not paid me, nor 11 s . 1 d . from Soames—he has paid 10 s . on account of what he has had from me. JOHN MENNIE( police-constable V  70.) I took the prisoner, and found on him seven bills, which his master had given him to give to customers, but he kept them back, and has eceived the monies weekly from the customers.
