SIDNEY BEX (Detective Sergeant.) On January 26th, 1903, I arrested
the prisoner on a warrant at 87, Long Lane--I read it to him-he said.
"You have knocked me, I thought this had all been settled satisfactory"

Cross-examined. I know he has been trading in London for about
fifty years - he was at his business premises when I arrested him-I found
chegues on him amounting to £7 to Charles Cook, and £9 to Jane Cook-
his name was not on his premises--the name there was "The Original
Busy Bee Boot Company "-I believe he had always been a boot manu-
facturer-I searched his premises after I had arrested him-he never
put any obstacle in my way-- he did not say he thought the matter had
been settled by his solicitor.
I. The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said he had himself paid Mary Cook
the interest on the money in Mann, Crossman's when she wanted it, up to the
time of her death; that he paid in money of his own to the account, and made
it a sort of savings bank for himself ; that he had paid the funeral expenses and
for the maintenance of the children, which had exhausted the money; that
he did not know it had all been expended until he called in an accountant,
and that what did remain there belonged to him, as he had paid the interest
,out of his own pocket.
Guilty. Judgment respited.
