soner's house-it was about ten minutes after the robbery-1 knocked at the
door, enquired in which room the boy lived, and was admitted up stairs--the
boys went up with me, and said, “There he is"-be made no answer.

1149. GEORGE BUDDEN, stealing 16lbs. weight of lead, value 38.;
the goods of George Gordon Davis; fixed to a building.
CHARLES STINY (policeman.) On 22nd March, about half-past seven
o'clock in the morning, I followed the prisoner to a marine store shop, and
then to another-I asked what he had in his bag-he had some lead, which I
have examined-it exactly fits Mr. Honeyball's premises, nail boles and all.
John HONEYBALL. The prisoner worked with me--I missed some lead
from the house at Loundes-terrace, Knightsbridge--I have examined this
and believe it to be the same it is George Gordon Davis's, my master.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALYE. Q. When had you last seen it? A.
On the 9th-it weighs 16lbs.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 47.-Confined Three Months.

1150. JOSEPH WALSBY, stealing a sack and 6 pecks of a mixture of
oats and beans, value 6s; the goods of Thomas Cricuit, his master.
THOMAS CRIOUIT. I live at East Ham. The prisoner was in my service
I allow my carmen to take chaff and corn in their nose-bags--I saw some
corn in the corn-bin in a sack, which ought to have been shaken out of the
sack loose into the corn-bin-1 tied a piece of string outside the sack, to
know it again, and put some paper in it-the policeman stopped the wagon
after it got out of my yard.
Cross-eramined by Mr. Horry. Q. It was your wagon that the corn was
found in ? A. Yes; I have no doubt the corn was mine.
MICHAEL MAGUIRE (policeman.) I stopped Mr. Cricuit's wagon-the
prisoner was driving it-I found in it a quantity of oats and beans in a sack,
on the fore-ladder-I also found three nose bags full of horse's food.

nose bags as I allow them-it has been a rule of mine for years-I do not
