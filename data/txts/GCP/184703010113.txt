Q. Had you been in a course of intoxication from the time you had the
good fortune to receive this 2901. down to the time you say you were robbed
of this 1001.? A. I could not have been in a continual state of intoxication,
or I could not have looked after my business- I was not drunk four times,
nor twice a week, and for a fortnight I was not drunk at all, I never touched
drink--I had taken Mrs. Baxter's house just as it stood, except three glasses
and some old china-I had nobody to leave in charge of the house if I went
away myself, but I was perfectly well aware of everything there was in it,
except it was something very small--if anything large bad gone I should
have missed it-I had taken it just as it stood I did not go there for the
purpose of making an inventory, and I did not tell Hyde so-I said to my
wife that I thought I ought to have had an inventory-I did not go down to
the house and tell my wife I was going with Hyde to make the inventory-
did not tell my wife he was going with me-Mrs. Baxter said we were to
leave the place because I had not got to take possession till the Monday-it
was on the Tuesday I made the purchase-she never said that both I and the
prisoner should leave, for that I was not to have the place until the Monday,
as the money had not been paid she had had 5l. by way of deposit, and she
had acknowledged it-the words she said to him or me (I do not know which)
were, "Neither him nor you shall stop, if there is much more said, for you
have not to take to the place till Monday"-I Dever heard her add, " as the
money has not been paid"-I might have said, “as the money had not been
paid"—it had not been all paid-1 had been drinking--that is not my common
practice I was sober on the Wednesday-I had been drinking in bed on the
Sunday-Hyde was in his shop when I charged him with the robbery-it was on
the same evening—it was Mrs. Baxter's little boy who came and told me that
the money was gone-there are two men that work for me, living in the house
with me-they work on commission for me--they hawk things about when
I charged Hyde with the robbery he denied all knowledge of it, and laughed
-I saw him in the shop next day, and Mrs. Hyde in the parlour-he said,
"My lad, I am going away, and if you want me you can find me at any
time"-he said he had lived there ten years, and bad never before been charged
with a robbery in his life-he told me on Friday or Saturday morning that I
had borrowed 88. 6d. of him-I said, "You lent me 1s."-I might have said it
was more money than I knew of I told him the exact sum he had lent me
- there are other parties in the house that could prove it-he said he had
lent me first 18., afterwards half a crown, and then 58.-I had borrowed
money of him on one occasion before that-we bad been intimate he told
me he had got money, and I told him the same-he lent me 38. once-my
friends and lodgers, and all the persons near, knew that I bad money-my
lodgers could not help knowing it, because I sent them out for different things
-when I came home, on being sent for, I found one of the lodgers at home,
the old man, George White-I stated, when I got up in the morning, that I
had no thoughts of going to the public-house, till I had mentioned it to Mr.
Hyde-I mentioned that I had taken the place he did not know it till I
told him-it was not on his recommendation that I went-I mentioned it to
him, and he said, “I will go down with you"-I never said his offering to
go with me was the sole cause of my going--I told my wife I must go and
look after Mrs. Baxter's place, or I should lose my deposit--I said I had put
