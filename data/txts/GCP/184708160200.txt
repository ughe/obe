Humphreys. Q. Did you see me pay any fees? A. Yes; to the
clerk at the Court-I asked you to give me 18. or a trifle for my trouble,
and you said you had to pay 15s. at the Court-I did not state, before the
Grand Jury, that No. 16, Shepherd-street, was a common brothel-I said it
was an accommodation-house, which it is, but I do not know the persons that
live in it, or anything at all about it, I get my living by paying my way–I
am not in every court of justice bailing people--I was in Horsemonger-lane
for an unfortunate person who absconded from the country- I have had no
money for coming here I am to be paid for my trouble-I did not go to
Twig, the other day, and demand money to pay my taxes, nor to Mrs. Gale,
and have 11.--I never saw Mrs. Gale.
Court. Q. Did you know that any of the persons Humphreys suggested
had kept any of these houses ? A. I did not-I was completely led into this
-Pheasant went as a witness before the Grand Jury, before me.
Pheasant. Q. Did I ever have any conversation with you about the
indictment at Westminster before that evening? A. No-I never saw
you before I saw you at Westminster-bridge, when I saw Humphreys-
the trouble you told me you were in, was a Court of Requests job-I will
swear you told me about the indictment at Westminster-you said there was
an indictment against you at the Queen's Bench, of a case at Covent-garden,
and if I could go to Covent-garden, and see a party there, I could arrange the
thing, and get a little money by it, but you could not go, and Humphreys
could not go-I said I would have nothing at all to do with it, for I had had
no sort of remuneration for what little time I had spent.
