OLD COURT.-Wednesday, January 1st, 1876.
Before Mr. Baron Clearby
218. JAMES BEAL was charged on the Coroner's Inquisition only (the
Grand Jury having ignored the bill), with the manslaughter of Elizabeth
Serjeant. 9 5
MR. STRAIGHT, on behalf of the defendant, before plea, applied to THE COURT
to quash the inquisition on the ground that it did not contain the true finding
of the Coroner's Jury. What they had found was that the parties guilty of the
alleged manslaughter were the "oroner or owners" of certain property by tobie
negligence the deceased was said to have met her death. The Coroner, from
subsequent inguiries, had inserted the name of the defendant in the inquisition
as the supposed owner of the said property, and upon that state of facts me
contended that the inquisition was bad in the substance of the charge and onge
to be quashed. MR. BARON CLEA8BY was of opinion that he could not dispaa
of the matter in the preliminary mode suggested. The inquisition on the
face of it was regular, and there was nothing to show that it was not property
and intentionally signed by the Coroner and the Jury whose names were
appended to it.
The defendant PLEADED Not Guilty.
MR. COOPER, for the Prosecution, then offered no evidence, and the defendan
was acquitted.
