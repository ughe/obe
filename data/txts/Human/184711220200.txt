 203. HENRY KEYS , 3 bushels of oats, peas, and chaff, value' 6 s  ., the goods of John Maxwell and another, his master; and WILLIAM GRANBY feloniously receiving the same. JOHN MAXWELL. I live at East Ham, and am a farmer—Keys was my carman—it was his duty to feed my horses with a mixture of oats, chaff, and peas at home, and to take out three nose-bags full for his three horses—I have examined this mixture—( produced )—it is mine—Keys had no busi ness with this quantity from my house.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALF. Q.  How many days ought Keys is have been absent? A.  He ought to have returned home by ten o'clock the same morning—he started at three o'clock in the morning, but he ought not to have started till five—my brother is in partnership with me—he gave out the corn in the evening to Keys. JOSEPH BENTON( policeman, K  381.) On 2nd Nov. I was on duty by the Pigeons, in the Romford-road, about a mile from Mr. Maxwell's, and ✗ Keys drive the wagon into the Pigeons yard—it was drawn by three horses—Granby was standing by the tap-door—he and Keys had some conversation—they went into the house, and were there ten minutes, or a quarter of an hour—they came out, and conversed again for two or three minutes—Granby then took something from his pocket and gave it to Keys—they both went to the wagon—Keys got a sack off the fore-ladder, and Granby took it up—he saw me, and ran very fast into the public-house—I called him out, and told him I must take him into custody—the sack had chaff and peas in it—the prisoners asked me if I would look over it—I told them I could not.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  When did you first recollect that Granby took something out of his pocket? A.  I stated it before the Magi state—the deposition was read over to me—I did not notice whether that was in it or not—I have no doubt I stated it—I did see what Granby gave Keys—I was six or seven yards from him—I could see them take the sack off—persons feed their horses there when they come a long distance—I saw Granby go to the wagon, and Key handed him the sack—when Granby saw me he threw the sack down and ran into the house—I am sure he saw me—the Magistrate did not ask me that. HENRY MILES( policeman, K  235.) I took Keys into custody—he told me he drew his horses up to the Pigeons to give them water and get a cup of coffee, and Granby came and asked him if he had got anything that morning for him—he said he had got but a little, and it was the first time he did any thing of the kind. NOT GUILTY .
