   MR. GENT . Q.  When you saw him with the two men, did you pick him out? A.  Yes, directly; I knew him by his face; I did not look at the dress—he was behind the man on the stairs that struck me—there is a fan-light in the passage—the door was not open—I could see his face perfectly well—I saw him there and also in the garden—I have not the least doubt the prisoner is the man. HENRY HILTON. I live with my father at Albert-cottage, James-ground, Peckham. On Monday, 26th July, about 3 o'clock in the afternoon, I was in front of Mr. Kilburn's house—I could see the garden-wall of Mr. Kil burn's—I saw two men get over it; the prisoner is one of them—he used to come to Mr. Perry's house early of a morning and go out—Mr. Perry's house is next door to Mr. Kilburn's—I have seen him go to Mr. Perry's house about six or seven times—when I saw him get over the wall I ran round through my father's cottage, and I saw this man and another man run past me—I saw the prisoner throw something like this (a life-preserver) into the hedge—I afterwards looked in the hedge where it had been thrown; I could not find it, and I called a gardener's man and he knocked it out with his hoe—that was where I saw the prisoner throw it—it did not appear as if it had been long in the hedge when I got it out; it was perfectly dry—I have gene rally seen the prisoner come out of Perry's house about 3 or 4 o'clock in the morning—I have no doubt but that he is the man I saw getting over the wall on this Monday afternoon.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do you happen to be up at 3 or 4 in the morning? A.
