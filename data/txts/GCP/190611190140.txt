FREDERICK JOHN WESTCOTT. In my professional name of Fred Karno
I send out a number of companies to different music halls in London and
the provinces with pantomime sketches. Each company has an acting
manager; be draws from the music hall the payment for the troupe; he
keeps a weekly balance of £10 and pays salaries out of it. Prisoner was
acting manager of the Town Troupe. In Septeuber I went to Ameria
and returned on October 9 Mr. Bell, my cashier, made a statement to
me, in consequence of which I saw prisoner. I asked him how he had
got into such a muddle. He sid, "I can't explain it; I have paid some of
the boys and forgot to put it down; I will pay you £1 a week back." I
agreed to that; I did not then know of the Clegg matter, the subject of this
charge. On October 12 prisoner sent word that he was ill, and he left
my service. Subsequently I was shown prisoner's balance sheet contain-
ing an entry of £2 paid to Clegg and the receipt produced, which is in

in his writing. [l'hese letters contained admissions of guilt and appeals
