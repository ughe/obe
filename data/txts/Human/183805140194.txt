   MR. KELLY . Q.  Are you very well acquainted with the town of New castle? A.  Yes-during the after-dinner canvass, between half-pan six o'clock and half-past eight o'clock, we went down Friar-street, along the Lower-street, to the bottom of Church-street, up Church-street, through the square, then into the market-place and High-street, and Penkel-street, across the London-road, down to my son's—I do not know the Royal Express—I do not remember going into any public-house in Lower-street, or any where—we might go into a beer-shop.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Will you swear you did not go into that beer-shop and canvass a voter there? A.  I do not know the Royal Express. CHARLES EMERY. I am a feltmaker, and live at Newcastle. I re member the last election—I remember Mr. Miller and his friends going down to the Castle—I went off to dine somewhere else—I Game back after I had finished my dinner, to the Castle—it was about six o'clock when I came back, I believe—I went along with the gentlemen on their canvas, Mr. Miller was one of them—I remember the names of the streets we went through—we went down Penkel-street and High-street, called in Paradise-street, came up Penkel-street again, down Friar-street, Lower-street, and up Church-street, or Church-lane some people call it—we went into Red Lion-square, and into the butchers' market—I remember a voter of the name of Joseph Massey—I left Mr. Miller canvassing Massey, and went away—I went into the Castle, and had one glass of ale—after that, I went into the street again, near the weighing machine, opposite the Castle—somebody pointed out Mr. Miller and another person to me—that was my old master—he has a nickname—they call him "Old Jersey"—that was intended for Mr. Mason—I followed them down Penkel-street, and saw them go into Mr. Mason's front garden—Mr. Miller and the old gentleman were the persons who went in—I mean old Mr. Mason—I am not positive, hut I think it was a little after eight o'clock—I then went to the Castle—there was a a great row there, and I did not stay—I went home—my wife was suprised that I came home so early.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You are in the habit of being out late? A.
