ANN THOMAS. I live at Alfred-cottage, and know the prisoner. On 15th
Aug. the prisoner was opposite my window from five minutes to two till five
o'clock-he could not have stolen the coat-it is ten minutes' walk from
Little Chapel-street-he wore a cord jacket.
COURT. Q. What was he doing? A. Standing with two young men,
and at five minutes past four I went out to see a quarrel about the prisoner
striking a woman who came out of the public-house- 1 knew him by sight-
I was at work at my window.
WEBSTER COOPER. I was with the prisoner on the day he was taken, from
a quarter to two till just five o'clock-he could not have stolen a coat in
Chapel-street-he was not out of my sight-he wore a cord jacket, not
a coat.
COURT. Q. What are you? A. A turner-we are neighbours-we were
playing at marbles the whole time, in Windsor-place-we never left.
John LIMB. On the day the prisoner was taken, I was with him from
two o'clock till a quarter past four-he could not have stolen a coat during
that time in Little Chapel-street-he wore a cord jacket.
GEORGE LANGRIDGE. I live opposite Mrs. Thomas. On the day the pri-
soner was taken, he was before my window from half-past three till half-past
four o'clock, playing at marbles- I spoke to him-he wore a cord jacket.
JOHN SIMPSON. I live at Windsor-place, Westminster. On the day the
prisoner was taken, he was not out of my sight from half past three till hall
past four o'clock-he wore this cord jacket (produced).
SARAH Dsan. I am the prisoner's mother. On the day he was taken,

till a quarter to seven.
