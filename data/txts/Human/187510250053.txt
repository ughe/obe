I believed that statement most implicitly—on 28th September the prisoner gave me this cheque for 40 l .—he had incurred a further debt at that time, but I had stopped the carriage on 15th September—I told him I could not go on any further, as the amount was too heavy, and I refused to serve him for some time, and on the last Saturday in Sep tember he met me and asked me to remove him to 50, Cambridge Gardens, and I continued to serve him until the 1st October, because he said the money would be forthcoming about 26th September from his rents, & c.—he owed me 47 l . 4 s ., and two guineas afterwards made it 49 l . odd—I have had nothing but the 10 l . on the 28th August.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  After you gave me the first cheque I never received more than 10 l . in cash—you paid me on an average 3 l . a week for two months—you told me you had authorised your agent, Mr. Brown, to pay me 40 l . and he said you had not, you had no funds; your account was then 49 l . 15 s . and I declined serving you any longer.
