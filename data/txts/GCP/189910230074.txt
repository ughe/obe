Evidence for Waugh.
ELLEN MATTHEWS. My husband is a builder-I reside at 62, Clive
Street, Grangetown, Cardiff-Waugh and William Hayes came to Cardiff
to see me on October 12th last year---they asked my husband to lend them
£50—he gave them a bill for £50, as he had not the money-Hayes asked
that the bill might be made out to him, as Waugh was an undischarged
bankrupt-Hayes did say the money was for the purpose of helping you
out of difficulties--you asked me to lend you £5, to get back to London
--I told you I had no money in the house-Hayes asked my husband to
exchange cheques with him, he gave my husband a cheque for £6, and
my husband gave him one in return-that cheque was dishonoured.
By the Court. We had to pay the bill—we have been unable to find
Hayes since-it has nearly ruined us.
augh, in his delence, stated that he was in financial difficulties when he
was introduced to William Hayes, that Hayes suggested that if he (Wangh)
could get some good acceptances, Hayes could get them discounted; that
Waugh applied to his cousin, Mr. Matthews, for help, and if Hayes had
handed over the £50 Mr. Matthews adranced, he (Waugh) could have got
out of all his difficulties. He denied that he had conspired with any of the
prisoners.
Hayes' Defence : I had no intention of defrauding any body. I did not
know that Waugh was an undischarged bankrupt until some time after we
started business together.
