 243.  EDWARD CLARKE and GEORGE GRAVES were indicted for feloniously assaulting William Scott , on the 4th of November , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 hat, value 1s.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 1 shilling, and three halfpence, his property.
 WILLIAM SCOTT. I am a  labourer. About twelve o'clock at night, on the 4th of November, I was in Bishopsgate-street, near Sun-street, going home to Angel-alley- I had been drinking, but was not so drunk as not to know what I was about; the prisoners came up, and Clarke asked if I would give them something to drink - I knew them before by sight - I have drank with Clarke; he said, "Scott, give us some gin;" I said I had no money to spare - they both seemed much intoxicated; I walked on, they followed me, and just within a few yards of my house, Clarke put his foot before me, and I fell down on my face; Graves put his hand into my right-hand pocket, and took out one shilling and three halfpence - he asked Clarke if he had got a knife; I was on my face, but I think it was his voice; Clarke said, No; I sung out for the watchman - they went away with my hat and handkerchief; I got up at seven o'clock in the morning, and gave information at a wine-vaults.
   RICHARD MORRIS. I am an officer, about seven o'clock in the morning I went into the Black Raven public-house, and found Scott, who told me of his loss; I found the prisoners in bed at a house in Angel-alley; when Clarke put on his clothes they were muddy. I said he had been in the mud: - he said he was so drunk he did not know where he had been. Graves said the same.
     NOT GUILTY.
 244.  GEORGE MASON and JAMES COOK were indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of George Deane, the younger , about four o'clock in the afternoon, on the 13th of November (he and others being therein), and stealing 3 metal boxes, value 4s., the goods of George Deane and George Deane, the younger.
   WILLIAM TURNER. I am keeper of the Monument. On the 13th of November, about four o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the two prisoners in company for some time - they at last went up to Mr. Deane's window, at the corner of Monument-yard; I saw Cook remove part of a pane of glass, and take it out; Mason stood at his side; they watched for a few minutes, and then I saw Mason put his hand in, and take three boxes out - they went up Fish-street-hill together; I went and informed Mr. Deane, then pursued up Cannon-street and took them, running in Gracechurch-street, and brought them back - Mason had the boxes.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you always say that Cook cut the glass? A. Yes - I do not know what he did it with.
 GEORGE DEANE, JUN. This is my dwelling-house - it belongs to the firm; I am in partnership with my father; he was not at home; these boxes are ours, and were near a pane of glass, which had been mended that morning - it was quite secure twenty minutes before; they must have cut it, I think - they are worth 4s.
 MASON's Defence. I was going home when he took me.
 Several witnesses gave the prisoners a good character.
 MASON - GUILTY. Aged 13.
 COOK - GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Of stealing only. - Transported for Seven Years.
 245.  ROBERT ROSAMOND was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of October , 1 watch, value 36s.; 1 seal, value 4s., and 1 string, value 1d., the goods of William Bowie , from his person.
 WILLIAM BOWIE. I am  employed at the East-India Company's warehouse, and live in Rudduck's-buildings, Shoreditch. On Saturday, the 28th of October, about six o'clock in the evening, I was in Long-lane, Smithfield, and was stopped by three or four persons, which I at first considered accidental - one of them attempted to take the wall, and as I gave way for him, I was pushed forward, and my watch very forcibly snatched from my fob - I was hustled a little; the one who took it ran down Three-fox-court, to a dark place; I did not sufficiently notice him so as to swear to him - I found the prisoner in custody, with my watch, next night; I ran down the alley, but could not see the person.
   EZRA GARDINER. I live at the corner of Three-fox-court, in Long-lane. I was in my shop, and saw three men hustling a man at the corner of my door - I and my brother rushed out; my brother and the prosecutor followed the prisoner in the direction they ran - I had seen three of them run down there; I observed one of them, and firmly believe him to be the prisoner; I went down Long-lane to the pig-market, to meet them at the other end of the court, but before I got there I met the prisoner, who was the first person running; in fact, I saw no other person running - I cannot say whether he came out of the court, as I was then about twenty yards from it. I seized him, and said, "You are the person who robbed the man of his watch" - he said, "You are mistaken, I have done nothing;" my belief is that he is the man.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it not dark? A. Yes; the gas was lighted.
   WILLIAM FAULKNER. I was coming up Long-lane, at a few minutes after six o'clock, and saw Gardiner and the prisoner scuffling in the road; they got on to the pavement; the prisoner tried to get away, but could not - I saw him take something from his coat-pocket, and throw it into the passage of a house there - I called to the landlord, and said, whatever the man had got, he had thrown into the passage; he called for a light, and this watch and case were picked up in his passage, in two different places.
   JOHN DELL. I live at a shop, No. 62, Long-lane - Faulkner called out to me, that the prisoner had thrown something into my passage - I was giving a girl change, and saw the prisoner at the door in custody - I called for a light, and found the watch and case.
   JOHN GODFREY. I took him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
