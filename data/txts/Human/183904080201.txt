GEORGE ANGUS. I work for Mrs. Nash. On Tuesday, the 9th of April, about twelve o'clock, I was in the field minding the sheep, and saw the two prisoners in the road, looking over the hedge into the field where the sheep were—I counted the sheep with Cockerell, in the even ing, and there were thirty-four—I missed one next day. NELSON SMITH. I am a policeman. I was sent for on this occasion—I went into the field and saw footsteps of two persons, which lead to the end of Sweetbriar-walk, about three quarters of a mile from the field—I obtained a search-warrant, and went to Julph's house—he lives with his mother, and Price lodges there occasionally—I found the two prisoners in bed together, at least Julph was in bed, and Price at the foot of the bed, partly dressed—I searched the premises, and found in a box which was locked, two shoulders, two legs, and part of a loin of mutton—I have compared that with the skin, and am certain it belonged to it—I compared the prisoners' shoes with the footmarks in the field, and they quite matched—they are both very peculiar shoes—there was blood on the pair belong ing to Price—when I took them, Price attempted to get out of window, but a man I had placed outside prevented him.
   Julph. Q.  Are you certain the shoes you had belonged to me? A.  Yes, they are the shoes you took off—I did not order you to put them on, you had them on—Price had not his shoes on, but he put them on without my asking him to do so.
   Julph's Defence.  The shoes did not belong to me—I did not steal the sheep.
   Prices Defence.  I am innocent of it. JOSEPH ALDER. I produce a certificate of both the prisoners' former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoners are the persons—they were convicted together, and sentenced to be transported for life.
   JULPH— GUILTY. Aged 18.
   PRICE— GUILTY, Aged 18.
