This woman never came into the house all, but she is the woman who gave me the character of the pri soner; and as to gentlemen, I have never had any gentleman in my house, I think, for these six months—I never came home at three o'clock—there is not a more domesticated lady than myself—every word she has said is false—that girl has never seen a gentleman in my house since she has been in my service, and I have numbers of witnesses to prove that I live respectably, I have lived in the house fourteen years—I wear a dressing-gown in the morning, because I see to my household affairs before I dress.

SARAH BROOKS was again  indicted for stealing 1 pair of stays, value 25 s  .; 1 night-gown, 5 s  .; 1 pinafore, 1 s  .; 1 pair of stockings, 5 s  .; 1 night-cap, 2 s  .; 1 neck-tie, 6 d  .; 1 skein of silk, 6 d  .; 2 handkerchiefs, 2 s  .; 2 printed books, 10 d  .; and 1/4 lb. weight of soap, 2 d  .; the goods of Charlotte Sophia Clifton , her mistress. CHARLOTTE SOPHIA CLIFTON. When the prisoner's box was searched, she gave me the key to open it—I found in the box a pair of stays, a new night-gown, a nightcap, a pincloth , a pair of stockings, a neck-tie, two books, and two pocket-handkerchiefs—I have a set of the books—they are all my pro perty—the maker's name is on the stays, on a printed card, where I bought them—the prisoner had not the charge of my things—she did not keep her box in the room we both slept in, but down stairs—I leave my things in my dressing-room—she could not have put the things into her box, out of the way—I had put the stays into a basket, to be unburned, to be prepared for cleaning—the night-gown was in the wardrobe up stairs, that was unlocked—the stockings were put into a basket, to be ready for mending—she might have got the books out of my parlour, where I slept—the stays I gave two guiness for, but I value them at 1 l . 5 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALF. Q.  Was her box kept locked or un locked? A.  Always locked, I have the key at home—she gave it me out of her pocket—it was her own trunk—she trembled very much when I opened her box, and I said, "You no need to tremble, Sarah, if you are honest"—I took the property out before several witnesses—there of them are here now. LOUISA FELLOWERS HARDY. I was not present when the prisoner's box was opened, but I was when the things were found—I was there on visit, and I went down stairs in the morning, to bid Mrs. Clifton good-bye, and found her and Mrs. Bradley searching the box—I saw the things all taken out of the box.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Why do you call her Mrs. Clifton? A.  I know the lady by that name—I believe she is married—I have not known her long enough to tell you that.
