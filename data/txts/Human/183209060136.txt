 2056.  JACOB BARNET was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 1 cloak, value 20s., and 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s., the goods of James Beckley , from his person.
   JAMES BECKLEY. I am a plasterer. I went to Fairlop-fair on a Sunday in July, and on my way there I found a cloak and two handkerchiefs - I went round the fair, and asked several persons if they owned them, but nobody did - next day the prisoner came up to me at the corner of Winfield-street, and said, "What have you got there?" I said, "A cloak;" he said, "Come with me, and let me look at it;" he took me into a public-house, and said,"What will you take for this cloak?" I said 30s. - he did not say what he would give; I went out - he called me in again, and said, "Let me have a look at it;" another man came in - they look it from under my arm; the other held it up, and ran out at the door, saying, "I will take it up and show it to him," and when I wanted to go out after him, the prisoner went from one side of the door to the other, and prevented me going out - at last he let me go; I said, "Where is he gone?" he said, "Where the hell do I know;" he then went, and pretended to make water; he kept hitting me on the head with a stick and said, "If you say I took it I will break your b-y head;" I gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. What day did you go to the fair? A. On Sunday; I went for the purpose of going to the fair - I was in the employ of Mr. Williams, of Finch-lane, and am so still; I worked for him at Whittington's alms-houses - I live with my father in Horse Shoe-alley, Bishopsgate-street - I am going on for fifteen years old; I was never in custody - I was once accused of stealing bacon, and was discharged - I was once taken to Worship-street about some wood; I never told Chiddy and Smith that I myself had stolen the cloak and handkerchiefs from a gentleman's chaise at Fairlop-fair.
     NOT GUILTY.

 2057.  ANN NICKLESS was indicted for wilful and corrupt perjury.
 MESSRS. ADOLPHUS and PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE TODD. I am a clerk in the registery of the Consistory Court, Doctors'-commons. I produce some minutes, which were taken from the registery - Dr. Daubeny and Dr. Matcham were surrogates of the Court at that time, and are so still; I produce a citation in the cause of Ellen Kenrick against Thomas Kenrick in a suit of divorce and separation, by reason of cruelty and adultery - with the allegations on behalf of Thomas Kenrick - the deposition of Ann Nickless, on behalf of the defendant, on the eighth article - the interrogatories to the witness examined on the eighth article - and the deposition of Nickless to the twelfth article.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Have you a book, in which you enter proceedings in causes? A. Yes, it is here; the citation is the first proceeding - all the documents produced are originals; I have the depositions of five witnesses in support of the suit; they are Thomas and  Sarah Burge, James Ashley, Charles Sinclair Bedford, and John Wright- I have depositions, on the other side, of William Kenrick, Ann Nickless, Caroline Paris, Stephen Moray, Jane Mintle, Elizabeth Sullivan, Elizabeth White, Elizabeth Mizen, and Martha Moore.
 MR.  WILLIAM PRITCHARD. I am one of the examiners of the Court, and took the depositions of the defendant; they are attested by me - here is a deposition taken on the 3rd of June; it was signed by the defendant, after being read over by me - she was not sworn by me; she has signed it as having been sworn - it was read over to her as having been sworn, and she signed it afterwards; she had been sworn to speak the truth - I then examined her, and took the examination myself; the examination appears to have been resumed on the 17th, as a cross-examination on the part of the promoter of the suit; Dr. Matcham's name is signed at the end of the two examinations, when she was repeated; on the completion of the examination, she deelared that the signature was hers, and that the contents of the examinations were true, by virtue of her oath; and I have attested that repetition - that acknowledgement extended to the examination of the 3rd, as well as the 17th of June; I believe the defendant to be the person.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. The party first goes before the surrogate, and is sworn, then to you to be examined, and after that goes again to a surrogate, and affirm the truth of the subsequent examination? A. Just so; I could not, of my own knowledge, know she was sworn; the proctor is responsible for that, and the registrar certifies that she is sworn; it is Mr. Shephard's duty to attend the swearing of the witnesses, or somebody for him, and the proctors on both sides, but not myself.
