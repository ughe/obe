635. WILLIAM WILSON and JOHN SOUTHGATE were indicted for
a burglary in the dwelling-house of William Spencer, about two o'clock in the
night of the 10th of Jan., and stealing therein, 1 ham, value 78.; 3lbs. weight
of pork, 2s.; and 2 dead fowls, 3s. 6d.; his goods; to which
WILSON pleaded Guilty of Larceny.--Confined Six Months.
WILLIAM SPENCER. I am a butcher, and live in Brierley-place, Wands-
worth, in the parish of Lambeth, and keep the house. On the i0th of Jan. I
fastened up my house at night-I went into my back garden, between nine
and ten o'clock in the morning, and saw the cellar flap was removed from its ori-
ginal situation-I had heard a rumbling noise in the night, which awoke me-I
rose in my bed and listened-my wife thought it was the children-I laid
down, and heard a second noise-I went down stairs-all was quiet-I went
up again, but on seeing the cellar flap I thought I had been robbed-the flap
had been nailed down, and had a window inside it—that was raised, and taken
away, the whole flap and frame together—they drew the ends of it out of the
wall by prizing it, I imagine-I missed two dead fowls, some mutton chops,
some pork, a ham, and part of a plum-pudding-the fowls were kept in the
house, under the cellar stairs.
Cross-examined by Mr. HUDDLESTONE. Q. Is there any tenant in the
house but yourself? A. No-tbe flap had been fastened into the wall-the
cellar door was fastened.
JOHN MAY (police-constable V 281.) I apprehended Southgate- I told him
what I took him for--(I did not say Wilson was in custody)- he asked me if
the other one had split - I said, "No"-on the way to the station I told him
Wilson said he took the shovel down, which the cellar flap was ripped up with
-- he said that was wrong, the shovel was laying on the dung-hill when they
went down-after they got to the station I told him I must have his boots off
to try to some footmarks at the back of the premises-he said they were not
the boots he had on on the Saturday night-I had found these boots at his
father's, down stairs- I received a pair of bools from his father, and compared
them with several footmarks in Mr. Spencer's garden-it appeared to me they
would make similar marks on the ground-I did not make any marks, but
tried them on those which had been made.
Cross-examined. Q. Was it true or false that Wilson had not split? A.
Why, it was false-1 was not aware at the time that he knew Wilson was in
custody- I knew Wilson had said something-I told him a falsehood-I bad
no object in view-1 intended to state here what he said—I produce the
boots.
Joan DEMPSEY ( police-constable V 268.) I was at the station when South-
gate was brought in-no threat or promise was held out to him, I did not
tell him that what he said would be used, for or against him--he said it was no
use for him to deny it now, as the other one had told all about it; be might as
well tell what he knew about it-he said, “He wants to throw it all on me, and
he has done as much of it as I have; he pulled his shoes off, not to leave any
marks in the place, and he found the shovel what I opened the place with."
Cross-examined. Q. Had you no conversation at all with him? 4. Not
a word-no remark came from me at all-nobody had been speaking about it
at all-May had taken him into custody, and left him at the station before I
came there.
WILLIAM SPENCER re-examined. Q. Is there a communication from the
parlour to the passage in which the things hung? 4. Yes-there was furni-
ture in the parlour-that was not displaced.
