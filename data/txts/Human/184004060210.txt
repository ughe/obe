   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Do you know Jarvis's cottage? A.  Yes—I knew Mrs. Jarvis and the prisoner—I saw him at Mrs. Jarvis's once, sitting by the fire—I do not know whether he was smoking—it it two or three months ago.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you happen to know a man named Henry Wright? A.  Yes—I believe he is a pot-man—he lodged at Allen's at the same time as Gould lodged there—that is a good while ago, I should think about last Christmas—Wright was out of employ then—I do not know where he is pot-man—when I first knew him he was at Mr. Stroud's, at the King's Arm's public-house, Liverpool-road—that is twelve months ago, I believe—it is about a quarter of a mile from Pocock's-fields—he was pot-man there—I think it was after that that he went to lodge at Allen's—I cannot recollect distinctly the time he lodged there—I am not sure whether it was before Christmas or after—I do not know how long he lived there—he very seldom frequented my beer-shop, he did once now and then to take a glass of porter—I do not know any other place besides Stroud's where he lived—he was a good bit out of employ, I know, but how long I could not positively tell—I have not seen him about Pocock's-fields lately with beer—I did at the time he lived at Stroud's—he was not living there at the time I heard this about the money, it was before that—I do not know whe ther he was living at Allen's at that time—I cannot say.
   MR. JONES . Q.  You say Wright and the prisoner lodged together at Allen's. A.  Yes—I do not know whether they were intimate friends—I do not recollect that I ever saw them together away from Allen's. JOSEPH MUSTOW. I live at a cottage at the back of Mr. Temple-man's, close to his—I do not know the prisoner, but I have seen him about with beer from the Barnsbury Castle public-house where he was pot-man—I cannot say how long it is since he left there—I know I took beer of him before Christmas, but I do not think I have since—I do not know whether he supplied Mr. Templeman with beer—I have seen him about the cottages supplying people with beer—I knew Mr. Templeman very well—I have heard that he was in the habit of talking to people about his money, and he has showed it to me several times—he has paid me small sums of money—he generally took it out of his pocket—he took out more than he wanted—he appeared fond of showing his money—on the Tuesday before he was murdered he showed me this 50 l . note—it was doubled up so that I could just see the amount at the corner—it was in his room—he went to the drawer and fetched it out to show it me—I did not mention the circumstance to any body till after his death—I have seen the prisoner at Mrs. Jarvis's, when he lived at the Barnsbury Castle public-house—I cannot say that I have seen him there since, or in the neighbour hood—I have two dogs—I keep one at the end of my house where I keep my pigs—that is just at the back of Mr. Templeman'a cottage—it is always out at night chained up—he is a sort of lurcher—any body he knows may do any thing with him, but to strangers he is not so civil—I kept that dog there while the prisoner was at the Barnsbury Castle—the prisoner was not acquainted with the dog—I do not think he knew him—he might have seen him, but not to be near him—I do not think the prisoner was ever on my premises.
