I do not know that I stood on any particular programme advocating the strictest pos  sible regulations for securing impartiality and purity in contracting. The house, No. 5, St. Helen's Terrace, was acquired from me on lease in 1903 at a yearly rent of £60. I think it was in good re pair at that time. I have heard since that £97 was spent upon it the first year. I do not know what it was spent for. I should think for ordinary wear and tear £15 would have been sufficient. It was let at £65 before I let it to the Guardians. I am not aware that it was inspected by Knight before I completed my arrangement with the Guardians. I do not remember a resolution in September, 1900, to the effect that no house should be taken from a member of this Board or a member's wife, or any relation of a member of this Board. I do not think I should have supported a resolution of that kind. My personal policy was to restrict the acceptance of tenders to those who lived within the Borough. Mr. W. A. Barker's works were in the Borough. He carries on business at his fathers place; I believe he lives at Forest Gate. I believe he built a temporary hospital for small-pox patients in 1904, but I do not remember whether I advo cated the acceptance of his tender. I was Chairman, of the meeting. It was done as an emergency on account of the small-pox epidemic; young Barker was doing another contract on the premises, and we asked him to tender for it, and it was got on with immediately. Mr. George Barker is a neighbour of mine at Leytonstone, and he was a member of the Board. If the son had a contract and his father, being a Guardian, lent him the plant, I should not see any harm in that. I do not recollect having any conversation with Barker, sen., upon the question of giving the contracts to his son. Barker, jun., also had a contract under the Board under which he was paid £7, 000 or £8, 000.
 Mr. Vachell objected to the cross-examination as being on totally new ground, which was not put before the magistrate.
 The Solicitor-General said it could not be put before the magistrate because Mr. Hirst did not there offer himself as a witness.
 Mr. Justice Phillimore. In abstaining from offering himself as a witness before the magistrate he was quite within his rights; but when he starts the case here, which was not expected by the Crown, he must be prepared to meet the cross-examination.
 The tender of William Barker for the old school buildings was £9, 447; there were six lower tenders, and the highest was £14, 000 I supported Barker because I knew that if he had got this tender he would employ local labour, and at that time there was a lot of distress about. I do not recollect that I moved on January 12, 1905, that additional works should be executed by Barker to the extent of £3, 746. (The minute was read.) There is no doubt that is correct. The whole of this work would be under the supervision of Knight. I may have voted on June 30, 1904, for another £530 in connection with the old school buildings.
