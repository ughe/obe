The prisoner Webb, in his defence, on oath, stated that he paid for the
goods he received ; that the nails, iron, and corrugated zinc were sent to him
to sell by a man named Perry, or Darkey, or Woodman; and that he sold
part of them; that he had never been to Shoe Lang in his life, and
could never have received any goods from there; that as to the 19 inter-
ceptors, he went to Mr. Skaife's with an order in an envelope, and told
him to take them to Leytonstone; and that he told Arthur Gibbs, the car-
man, he would meet him there; that he got there just as Gibbs pulled
up, and then went to Skaife's and paid Holder for the goods; that he was
spoken to by Neal and Haywood about the pans two or three days before
they were stolen, but refused to have anything to do with it, and never had
them at all: that he had had a different lot of furnace pans from Ben
Taylor, who traded somewhere in Bermondsey, but that he did not ask Mr.
Young to take care of some furnace pans which he paid Gibson to take
there, and that they were removed from Neal's van (Mr. Skaife's carman)
to Gibson's van, and were his own goods.
WEBB-GUILTY on the second count (Stealing the iron.) Recommended
to mercy by the prosecutor. MR. KERSHAW stated that the amount of pro
perty stolen from McArthur's premises during one year was considerably
over £1,000.--Five years' penal servitude. FULLER-Eight months'
hard labour. HOLDER and NEAL-Six months' hard labour.
