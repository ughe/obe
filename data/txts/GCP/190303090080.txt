SAMUEL, Mayor.
WILLIAM HENRY DAVIDSON. I am a chemist, and now live at 49.
Upper Lewes Road, Brighton--in 1897 I was living at 66, High Street,
Hastings, and carrying on the business of a chemist there--I have now
retired -- was at Hastings for about eighteen years, and while there
I used frequently to go to a barber's shop to get shaved--the prisoner
was employed there, and he shaved me on many occasions--one day
he bought several things from me, and asked me to let him have a
particular poison-he came to my shop and purchased one ounce of
tartar emetic---We have to keep a copy of the sale of poisons--this is
my poison register book (Produced)--there is an entry here of the tartar
emetic sold to the prisoner; it is "April 3rd, 1897, name of purchaser,
Mr. G. Chapman, 1 ounce of tartar emetic."--that is in my writing--then
there is "purpose for which it is required," but I cannot make out what
is written here, it is in the prisoner's writing-it looks like "take"
or " teke "-he wrote that in my presence after he signed G. Chapman-
I knew bim as Chapman-. I do not recollect his Christian name -I gave
him the ounce of tartar emetic--it was in a white powder--this label
is in my writing--it is the one I put on the bottle containing the tartar
emetic (Read)-" W. H. Davidson, dispensing chemist. Poison, tartar
emetic. Dose: grain to t grain : to be used with caution. 66, High
Street, Hastings. The word " poison" is printed--I used a red label
to indicate poison--I also sold the prisoner two or three medical books--
they were old editions--I have no doubt these are the ones (Producell) -
I cannot find any other sale of tartar emetie in my book--I saw in the
newspaper an account of a woman named Chapman having died of this
poison, and I went to the police and gave such information as I had.
Cross-examined. I am a registered qualified chemist--the prisoner was
not a stranger to me- I cannot recollect having sold so large a quantity
of tartar emetic as this in any one year-if I had sold it, it would be entered
in the book-it is very seldom used during my professional life I have
several times sold as large a quantity as this I cannot say that it was a
very unusual request to have made to me because the prisoner had had
several transactions with me-- I think he did have some other poisons, but
they would not come under Schedule I.-I do not know if I demurred at
his wanting so large a quantity-I cannot say what was passing through
my mind at the time, but from my conversation with him I knew he had
a good knowledge of medicines, and so I let him have it-I knew he
was not practising as a medical man--no doubt he gave a cogent reason
why he wanted it, but I cannot say what it was now --two grains of
tartar emetie might be a fatal dose, and twelve certainly would be --
I conformed to the law by answering the questions in the book and by
knowing the man well-no doubt I asked him what he required the
poison for I do not know now what the word "take" means-
I cautioned him I entered it in the book-I labelled it poison-I could
not do any more. I also put the dose on the label—some years ago I
sold a man 1 lb. of tartar emetic, that was for horses--I did not know if
the prisoner kept horses-Ioannot give the slightest information at this
