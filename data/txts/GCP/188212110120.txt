This being the case for the prosecution, MR. BESLEY submitted that there was
no false pretence; at the most it merely amounted to an exaggeration of the value
of the articles. He pointed out that every article was really in pledge for the
amount stated on the ticket; that ample opportunity had been given the prosecutors
to ascertain the true value for themselves, and that they had never sued on the
promissory notes. Ho referred to the decision of Chief Baron Pollock (Cox's
Criminal Cases Vol. 9, in Q. v. Evans) and to that of Lord Campbell (Deersloy
and Bell p. 265, Elkington's case). MR. MATHEWS having replied, the COURT
considered the case turned entirely upon the point of law, that it was merely a
question of exaggeration of valus, and that if he were to let it go to the Jury he
should be overruling the decisions in the cases cited. He therefore directed a
verdict of
Not GUILTY.
