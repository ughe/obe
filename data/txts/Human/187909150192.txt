(  A diagram was here put in showing the situation of the wound, and this part of the evidence was unfit for publication. ) The inspector showed me the small blade of a knife which fitted the wound exactly, but it might have been done by any penknife—I made a post-mortem examination next day with Dr. Cyril Jones—the wound could have been inflicted while she was lying on the bed, but I do not think it could have been done if she was sitting flat on a chair—her clothes were very scanty—her age was 59; the brain was healthy, but almost bloodless, and the body was very empty of blood—there was a slight bruise on the right temple, and a deal of bruising under the scalp and extravasated blood—if she was sitting on a low chair and in front of the edge it might have been inflicted sitting, but I did not notice the height of the chair—I noticed a patch of blood on the hearth, it appeared to be bright arterial blood, which would be the first to flow from such a wound—my opinion is that it was inflicted near the hearth; it was the only clotted blood, the ordinary blood was soaked into the bedclothes—when the wound was first inflicted the blood would flow more freely, and being in larger quantity it would not be absorbed by the clothes—there would probably not be time for blood to be on the knife—that is the result of an experiment I made that night—my idea is that the knife was inserted under the clothes—the whole-handled knife which fell from the woman's body would inflict the wound; there would be very little difference—the blood would certainly spurt from the arteries very quickly.
   Cross-examined.  If she had been drinking heavily she might sleep very heavily, and the insertion of the knife without violence might not wake her, but it might have been done by a stab.**********
   Re-examined.  I think the wound might have been done in any position if there had been a struggle—supposing she was sitting on the edge of the chair it could be done in that position.
   By the COURT . It is very unlikely that it was done by a stab, but if it was she must have been sitting on the edge of the chair—the prisoner was in a maudlin state when I saw him; he was sitting on a chair for the first 20 minutes, but he walked out of the room pretty well—his hand was steady enough to take the knife out of his pocket—I do not think it would require any remarkable steadiness of hand to inflict such a wound. CYRIL LLOYD JONES, M. D., M. R. C. S. I live in Blackfriars road—I assisted Mr. Wilson at the post-mortem examination—I do not know that I differ from him in any material particular, but I do not think it possible to say whether it was done in the way he suggests or by a sudden push and cut up—I do not think it likely that it was done by stabbing through the clothes.
