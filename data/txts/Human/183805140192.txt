I am superintendent of the horse establishment at the Birmingham and Grand Junction Railway. I was at Newcastle at the last election—I was living at Mr. Cork's yard, at the Castle hotel—I was head ostler there—I remember the Saturday, the day of the nomina tion, perfectly well—I remember seeing Mr. Miller and Mr. De Horses there that day—they dined there—I assisted in carrying up and waiting at dinner—Mr. Miller, Mr. De Horsey, and others, went away after dinner—I was placed that evening at the bottom of the stairs—I took my sta tion there about seven o'clock, and remained there till past twelve o'clock, nearly-Baddely and Down were placed at the top of the stairs—I am sure Baddely was there—I did not leave the bottom of; the stairs from seven till a little after twelve o'clock—Mr. Miller left dinner at half-past six o'clock, and I never saw him afterwards—I being at the bottom of the stairs, he could not have gone up without my seeing him-at ten o'clock several persons came—there was Wall, and several more that I do not know—I have often seen the defendant Gallimore—I really cannot say whether he was one of the persons—they were all tipsy-a constable was called, and one came and took Wall out-no speeches were made that night out of the window of the inn—there were many hundreds of persons in front of the Castle from seven to eight o'clock.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you remember the canvas sers coming back? A.  Yes, about ten o'clock—there are two staircases to the house—I am not a voter—I am sorry to say, I was not paid for waiting at the bottom of the stairs—my master, Mr. Cork, put me they—I was to keep the rabble from going up stairs—I was placed at the principal staircase, where all the people went up—the back stairs, are within sight of the front—I cannot say positively whether any one was placed at the foot of the back stairs.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Do both the stairs come on the same landing? A.  Yes.
   Q.  Have you any reason for particularly remembering that there were no speeches made out of the windows that night? A.  Many people said Mr. Badnall had given the members enough that night without any speeches—that was the remark of the crowd.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you inside the house, or outside? A.  Inside the house.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did Mr. De Horsey return alone? A.  I believe he did.
   MR. CLAJRKSON . Q.  Did you see him? A.  I saw him and spoke to him-when I spoke to him he was alone—I cannot say whether five or six people came in with him; there were scores in the house—the crowd staid two hours before the hotel door—I heard them cry out "The canvassers are coming"—that was an hour before Mr. De Horsey returned—Mr. De Horsey's servant was in the house all the time he was gone.
