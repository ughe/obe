   MR. BOYS . I am one of the firm of Hale, Boys, and Austen, solici tors, in Ely-place. In the autumn of 1841, we were employed by Mr. and Miss Blackwell, as their solicitors—we were to get some papers out of the possession of Sutcliffe and Birch—we afterwards got those papers out of their hands—their bill was paid before the papers were delivered—I saw Miss Constable after that—she subsequently executed this deed in my presence—( looking at the confirmation deed )—I am not the attesting witness to it, Mr. Martin is the attesting witness—I see Miss Constable's signature—I saw her three times on the subject pre viously—the prisoner was with her on those occasions—at the time I first saw her on the subject of preparing this deed, I had seen the other deed, where there are blanks—that deed was adverted to on the first occasion—I am not sure it was produced, but either the deed or a copy was there—the title was the sub ject of discussion, and this blank deed, and the effect of it, was one point under consideration—I have an original memorandum of my own—this was on the 10th of Dec., 1841—the opinion of a conveyancer had been taken previous to that, and here is the case—when I saw Miss Constable, the question that we were considering with Mr. Black well and Miss Constable was the perfecting the title of this estate, in consequence of the deed in blank having been obtained from Sutcliffe and Birch—the case, which had been laid before the convey ancer, was before me, and under discussion and consideration, and I have here the minutes which I made, when the prisoner and Miss Constable were present—this was on the first occasion—the prisoner brought the case that I had before taken the opinion of counsel on—I had given it to him, if I recollect right, the day before—I explained to Miss Constable the effect of the title—I cannot state the exact words I said—I called her attention to it—two other deeds had been prepared by Sutcliffe and Birch—they were settlements, apparently on her part, of this property, and the effect of these I explained to her as well as this, and she expressed regret that the title was in that state by means of the blank deed—she fully understood that a deed was necessary—I explained to her the parties who were interested under the trust, sup posing the blanks for the sums had been filled in—Stanley's name was men tioned to her, as being a party interested—I mentioned it to her—the first part of the minute that I made here is the amount of the debts due, which were to be the consideration for the deed to be prepared—I explained to her that there were certain blanks in the deed, for debts to be entered to certain parties, and I explained to her the effect of the trusts, and that Stanley was the trustee—Stanley was then dead, but was represented by the prisoner, who claimed the interest which he had under his will—we were not her solicitors, but the Blackwells'—that was the first time I had seen her, but she was so satisfied with Blackwell, that she instructed us afterwards, at a subsequent interview, to prepare this confirmation deed—we were engaged for both parties, and had the drafts for counsel.
