CHARLES BEWLEY. I am a member of the Royal College of Surgeons-
on Sunday, 27th January, I was called to the house of Moseley, and saw the
deceased man Tabraham-that was about 20 minutes past 3 o'clock-
I found him perfectly insensible, in a state of collapse-I examined his
body-I found his head slightly bruised ; different protuberances about the
head; and one rather severe blow under the chin-he was perfectly insensible,
not to be roused up-he died that evening, about a little after 8 o'clock-I
made a post mortem examination in conjunction with Mr. Duplex-
examined the head and state of the brain-I found the brain generally
healthy; there was some amount of extravasation in the left hemisphere-
on the front portion of the brain, on the left side, there was an escape of
blood ; about a teaspoonful-I found one external mark, a bruise, corres
ponding to that-tbat extravasation of the blood was the cause of death.

COURT. Q. Is it not more likely that the fall would cause it, than the

GEORGE DUPLEx. I reside at 60, Torrington-square, and am a member
of the Royal College of Physicians, at Edinburgh-by order of the coroner
I made a post mortem examination of the deceased man-I heard the last
witness's evidence--there was a very considerable bruise on the left temple
which had disorganised the temporal muscle, completely lacerated it; aud
undernenth the skull, corresponding with that locality, I found a quantity
of blood extravasated, from the rupture of the vessel-death unquestionably
arose from that extravasation-the organs of the body generally were
healthy-I infer, from what I saw, that the death of the man was occasioned
by a fall.
Cross-examined by Mr. Best. Q. Would such a fall as a senseless body
being lifted into a cart, and then dropped down, would that canse death
like this? A. I should think that it would want a heavier blow than
that.

head might have been knocked against any portion of the cart, would that
have caused concussion A. That would not have caused extravasation-
have no doubt at all myself as to the death being caused by the fall, and not
