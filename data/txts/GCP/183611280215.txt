THOMAS BAYLBY. I am a plumber. On the 11th of November,
s bout 5 o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my shop with some
pipe he asked if I would buy it-I asked him if it was his owoche
said yes, and he wanted to sell it, as his wife had given herself up to
drink lately, and had made away with some of it, and be thouglit he had
better turn the rest into money-he said he had had it by him some timeand
that he had been at work for a former master who gave him this lead instead
of wages, as he was thinking of having water laid on at a future time
he told me where he lived, and that he worked for Mr. Oliver, on Black
heath-hill-on the Wednesday following I heard Mr. Oliver had lost
some lead, and as it was similar to this I gave information,
Cross-examined, Q. Did you hear any thing of the reward , 4, I
did, I read the bill that was how I learned that Mr. Oliver had lost
lead, the reward made no impression or me-- it is twelve months
yesterday since I was here I know the Hospital at Greenwich had some
lead stolen from it-Mr. Edward Bayley is my uncle I was placed in the
dock, but discharged without the leaststain on my character I can be trusted
now for a thousand pounds--I have not the least doubt that I shall get the
reward if the man is convicted, but not if he is acquitted I believe my
brother James was in the shop when the prisoner came in, but I cannot be
certain--we have not conversed about the evidence we were to give-wecame

mained in the shop till half past six o'clock-I believe I told the Magi-

Wednesday evening following this was not reported currently through the
village-I have known the prisoner I suppose ten years, or else I should
not have bought the lead of bim-I have bought lead once of him before
not more to my knowledge.
