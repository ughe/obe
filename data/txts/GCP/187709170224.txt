ALEXANDER ONSEY. I am manager to the prosecutor-on 11th June I
was in the warehouse, and saw the property which was stolen on 29th or
30th May--the prisoner Harris' father came to me on June 11th and offered
me some patterns of silk-I saw at once that they were the goods we bad
lost, and made arrangements to see him again the following morning-he
gave me this card. (M. Harris, wholesale shirt manufacturer and ware-
huuseman, 8, Great Prescott Street, London.) I saw the bulk at his place of
business the next morning at 11 o'clock, in Great Prescott Street-he showed
me a piece at a time, till I saw the whole of them; there were eleven pieces
-be brought them from under the counter, where he was writing-he said
the price would be 18, 2d. per yard all round, and it amounted to 681. 58 -
the value of the white silk is 28. 2d. to 28. 6d. a yard; the white sargnet,
38. 3d, to 38. 60.; the coloured crimson, 38. 3d. to 38. 9d; the Italian cloth,
18. 10d. I got a cab and said "Will you put them in the cab i we may
as well take them to the warehouse, and Mr. Spiers can decide the
purchase "--they were put into the cab, and we drove a certain distance-
when we went down Great Prescott Street the prisoner Harris got into the
cab--we stopped at a public-house where the police had arranged to meet
me, and from there the police took Harris and the silk to the station-this
is the memorandum of the quantities shown to me by Harris-he did not
write it in my presence.
