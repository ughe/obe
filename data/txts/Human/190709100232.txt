Re-examined. Prisoner gave Eustace the address of 24, Halton Road.
 Detective-sergeant  EUSTACE . K Division. On August 14 I went to 417, Holloway Road, and saw prisoner in the front room with Mr. Bryce. I saw prisoner writing a receipt (produced). After telling him the charge, prisoner said, in answer to questions. "I bought it from a man on the 9th inst. He asked me £16 for it; I gave him £13. I was standing in the Holloway Road when I saw the man with it. He was driving it. He took the horse out of the cart and I pulled it round to my stable, a few yards from the address I have given you. I do not know the man I bought it from; I never saw him before; I haven't seen him since, and I have no idea where he may be seen. I do not know nobody that saw me buy it, and I cannot give you any further information." He gave his name as Henry Jones, 24, Halton Road, Islington. I took him to the sta tion; subsequently to West Ham. He made no reply when charged I went to the address given; prisoner was not known there, and there was no stable near the house. I asked for White, as well as Jones.
 Cross-examined. Halton Road would be about two miles from Caledonian Market. There is no mews in Halton Road. I went to No. 29 as well as 24.
 Prisoner's statement at the police court: "All I have got to say is I appeal against the charge of having received the trap. I acknowledge I bought the trap and sold it to the gentleman. The other things I know nothing of."
 (Defence.) HENRY WHITE(prisoner, on oath). I was going up the market—we call it Smithfield—on Friday, August 9; I was standing outside the public-house in Holloway Road, near the railway arch, about quarter to one, when a man drove up in a horse and cart. He says, "Will you buy that lot?" I says, "No." "Buy the cart then." He asked £16; I said, "No, I will give you £10." He finally ac cepted £13. I said, "I have nowhere to put it; I have no horse; where can I put it?" He said, "There is a mews down here is Halton Road; go and put it down there and leave it." He directed me the way down and I went, took the trap, and left it there. I paid for the trap at the time I bought it. On the Sunday following I went to Mr. Bryce, having been told he wanted to buy a trap. I went to his stables first, asked his chap if it was true Mr. Bryce wanted a trap; he said, "Yes," and told me to take mine up to his guv'nor. He lent me the cob, and I went to Halton Road to fetch the trap, the boy coming with me. We then drove back to the stable, and from thence to Mr. Bryce's house. What passed be tween us was substantially what he had said; also on the Wednesday when the detective came. I generally sign receipts, "Henry Jones."
