 755. FREDERICK TIEDIMAN and CHARLES WHEELER were again indicted for stealing 10 paving stones, value 5 s  .; 10 other stones, 5 s  .; 10 flags, 5 s  .; 10 feet of stone, 5 s  .; the goods of the Commissioners for carry ing into execution certain acts of Parliament. WILLIAM WELCH. I am a goldsmith and live in Baker's-row. The pave ment was being laid down near my shop, and my attention was attracted to it—on the evening of Monday, the 22nd of Jan., after it was dark, I saw Tiedi man there—(he had been engaged there as a labourer in putting down the new pavement)—I saw a truck there—Tiediman filled it with paving stones—I did not see Wheeler at the time to know him—I saw the truck go away—I do not know who took it. ARTHUR KEEFE. I was engaged in repairing Baker's-row. On the 22nd of Jan. I saw Wheeler and another young man bring a track—Wheeler and the other labourers put some of the stones into it—the other mason told me to assist in taking the truck, and I went with it to the top of Baker's-row.
 (  Charles Waugh and George Andrews deposed as in the former case. ) WILLIAM FREDERICK EAST. I am surveyor to the Commissioners under these Local acts—I spoke to Tiediman on the 24th of Jan.—I said the atones did not appear to increase, and requested him to relay them immediately—I meant that they appeared to decrease—Tiediman felt inclined to be impudent, and asked if I supposed he had taken them. NOT GUILTY.
 756. WILLIAM THOMPSON was indicted for stealing 2 reams of paper, value 3 l  .;, the goods of John Hodge and another. JOHN HODGE, stationer, Drury-lane.  I have one partner. About three months ago I sent a quantity of paper to Mr. M'Gowan, in Windmill-street, Haymarket, by my own cart—these two reams of paper are mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you put it into the cart? A.  No—a porter in my establishment sent it—there is no particular mark on this paper—it was made to order for a particular person—I made more than a hundred reams of it—I sent it all to Mr. M'Gowan, as it was made for as order, and not required. DOUGAL M'GOWAN. I received some paper from Messrs. Hodge about three months ago—I am in the habit of receiving a great quantity from them to warehouse—it was of the size of this—I cannot tell whether this is any of it or not—I cannot tell the number of reams I received. JOHN KINSMAN, stationer, Common-street.  On the 8th of Jan. the prisoner came and offered me this paper—I asked how he became possessed of it—he laid he had bought it, to sell again—I bad suspicion, and stopped him. NOT GUILTY.
