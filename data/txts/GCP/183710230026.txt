2206. MARY SMITH and SARAH SMITH were indicted for steal
ing, on the 27th of September, 1 pair of boots, value 4s. 6d., the goods of
John Aaron.
WILLIAM BURNELL. I was shopman to Mr. John Aaron, a pawnbroker,
in Whitechapel-road, on the 27th of September ; I have left him since.
On the evening of that day I saw the prisoner, Mary Smith, in the shop-
she offered to pawn a pillow, which our foreman refused to take in, and
when she was gone I missed a pair of boots directly I did not see Sarah
in the shop.
John GERRARD. I am a policeman; I live in New-road, Cannon
street. In consequence of information I received, I went to look for the
prisoners- I looked into Mr. Burnell's shop and saw the prisoner, Mary
Smith, who had passed me before-she had a pillow in a bundle-
stopped till she came out, and then asked what she had got-she said,
"Nothing but an old pillow"-I asked her if it was black or white-she
said I knew the colour of a pillow as well as her-I asked to look at it
she was very loath to give it me, and the moment I laid hold of it the
boots dropped from the middle of it-I said she must go with me--Sara
Smith was about a yard from her, and directly followed us up towards
Mr. Aaron's shop-I had a pillow in one hand and a pair of boots in
other-Sarah Smith laid hold of her arm and of my arm to try to rescue
her from me- I told her to be quiet and let her alone, but she still kep
hold of ber-I called Aaron's shopman out, and told him to take som
of her.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
Mary Smith. Sarah Smith was not near me till I got to Brick-Jane,
when I was in custody, and asked what was the matter with me.
MARY SMITH-GUILTY. Aged 17.-Transported for Seven lears
SARAH SMITH-NOT GUILTY.
