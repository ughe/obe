170. RICHARD MILLS was indicted for stealing 2 groats, the monies
of George Wetton Jordan; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
GEORGE WETTON JORDAN. I am a baker, and live in Bedford-row, Wal-
worth. On the 3rd of Nov., between twelve and one o'clock, I was in my
parlour-a customer came into the shop- I went, and found the prisoner
behind the counter, coming on his hands and knees from the place where the
till was- I took hold of him, and found the till open, and two 4d. pieces,
which were there a few minutes before, were gone-there was some copper
in the till, which was not gone-I had left the till shut-I searched the pri.
soner, but could not find the two pieces--I sent for the officer, who put his
fingers into the prisoner's mouth, and took the two 4d. pieces out.
HENRY Scott (police-constable P 241.) The prisoner was given to me
for stealing two 4d. pieces-he said he had not taken them-I saw them in
his mouth, and took them out-the prisoner cried.
James Baker (police-constable P 68.) I produce a certificate of the pri-
soner's former conviction (read, Convicted 12th May, 1845, and confined
three days, and whipped)—the prisoner is the person.
Guilty. Aged 11.-Confined Four Months.

172. WILLIAM BENBOW was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief,
value 38., the goods of William Evans, from his person; and that he had
been before convicted of felony.
WILLIAM EVANS. On the 20th of Nov. I was close to the Elephant and
Castle-1 got half-way across the road-a man ran past me-1 crossed, and
turned, and saw the prisoner between two officers-one of them had my
handkerchief-I claimed it-it had been in my pocket just before-this is it.
CHARLES WRIGHT (police-constable N 304.) I saw the prisoner take this
handkerchief from Mr. Evans's coat-pocket-lie was in the act of banding it
to another who was in company with him-I seized the prisoner-ano-
ther officer came up went to try to find the other who had run off,
but I could not-- I turned back, and saw the other officer had got the pri-
soner and handkerchief-there was a crowd of ten or a dozen persons--some
carriages were passing--the prosecutor paused for a moment, and then it was
done.
