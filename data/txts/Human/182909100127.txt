 1794.  JOHN MACKLIN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 4 loaves of bread, value 2s., the goods of Jabez Thurgood , his master.
   JABEZ THURGOOD. I am a baker, and live in Old-street - the prisoner was in my employ for three weeks or a month. On the 8th of September my brother sent him out with a lot of bread.
   JOHN THURGOOD. I am the prosecutor's brother. On the 8th of September, I gave the prisoner sixteen loaves to take to Mr. Selwin, in Golden-lane; I went the next morning, carried the bread myself, and found but twelve booked - they keep the book, but the prisoner books the bread; I know this to be the prisoner's writing - I do not know what became of the other four: he knew the book would be seen - he was discharged the next day.
   JABEZ THURGOOD. I discharged the prisoner for another offence; I did not speak to him on this subject.
   JAMES HAWTHORN. I was applied to, and took the prisoner; he said the reason he kept the loaves, was his master not paying his wages.
 Prisoner's Defence. I deny it; I met a man the night before, who said Mr. Thurgood had been accusing me of stealing eight loaves - I went home, and asked Mr. Thurgood if he had said such a thing; he said No: I went on the 8th of September to the shop, and was to take sixteen loaves, but we had not much bread, and I took but twelve, and booked twelve in the shop.
   JABEZ THURGOOD. I have known him eight or nine years - he bore a good character; I paid him all up on the Tuesday evening - he did come back, and said he understood he was accused of robbing me.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1795.  JOHN MACKLIN was again indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 4 loaves of bread, value 2s., the goods of Jabez Thurgood.
   JABEZ THURGOOD. On the afternoon of the 8th of September, I put sixteen loaves of bread on the board, and sent the prisoner to the same place; he only delivered twelve - he came back intoxicated; I paid him and discharged him in about two hours; here are only twelve entered in Mr. Selwin's book, and I am sure I gave him sixteen: I am sure he took sixteen, and took them all at once - he was not to go to any other customer, and brought none back.
 Prisoner's Defence. My master put down sixteen - I delivered them on the shelves; I did not count the bread.
   SAMUEL SELWIN. I keep a chandler's shop. On the 8th of September the prisoner brought me twelve loaves, and no more - I saw him write the number in my book.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined One Month.
 1796.  THOMAS SIMPKINS and CHARLES SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 1 saddle, value 30s., the goods of George Houghton; and STEPHEN SERJER was indicted for feloniously receiving the same goods, well knowing the same to have been stolen; against the Statute.
 SIMPKINS pleaded GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
   GEORGE HOUGHTON. I live in Harford-street, Mayfair. On the 3rd of September, Smith, the prisoner, came to me, and said he came from John Watson(whom I knew) to borrow a saddle - I objected to let him have it; he returned in ten minutes, and said if I would lend it to Watson he would use it for his own riding - I let him have it for Watson; it was to be returned by twelve o'clock the same day - I saw Watson the next day, and asked him why he had not returned the saddle; he said he had never borrowed it - Watson is not here.
   WILLIAM SPINDLER. I am a general-dealer in saddles and harness. On Friday week a message was left for me to go and look at a saddle at No. 9, Hart-street - I met Webster, and said "I am going to look at a saddle, there was one lost out of the park, perhaps it is it;" I went there and asked the man in the shop if there was not a man who had a saddle to sell, he said go to the first-floor- I went up, and the woman said "My husband is gone out and has got the saddle;" I went back to my house and heard that it had been there - I went to the Duke of Wellington public-house, and saw Serjer, and another man who had the saddle; Serjer said, "It does not belong to me, it balongs to a man named Smith - I want 36s. for it;" I told Webster.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I am an officer. On the 4th of September I went and saw Serjer with the saddle - he said he bought it of a man named Smith, but he did not know where to find him; I took him - he was examined the next day; some of his friends looked out for Smith, and said he was at a public-house - I went and said, "Is your name Smith?" he said "Yes - I gave the saddle to Serjer, and I received it from Simpkins;" I took him to the office - Simpkins said he was the person who applied for it under false pretences.
 Smith's Defence. I never knew where the prosecutor's shop was; I met Simpkin, who asked me if I knew a person who would buy an old saddle which he had cleaned up, as it had got mouldy - I said I knew Serjer, and took him to him.    NOT GUILTY.
