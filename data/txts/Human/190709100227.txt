 (Defence.) JOHN FRIEND(prisoner, on oath). On Bank Holiday night (August 5) I was in Lea Bridge Road, accompanied by my brother and my sister-in-law, when I met a man named Lovell leading a harnessed horse with a rug over it. He said, "My trap has broken down up the road and I have left it. Do you know where I can borrow one?" I said, "No, I do not." Outside the "Shop Aground" I met Riley with Gardner. I said to Riley, "Do you know anybody that can lend a chap a trap." Riley said, "My landlord has got one. He will lend it to you for a bit of silver." Meanwhile we went to Nesbit Street, where Head lives, and dragged the trap round to the "Stag" public-house in Brooksby's Walk, where Lovell said he would meet me with the horse and harness. Lovell put the horse in the trap and away he went, saying to me, "Do you know anybody that will buy the old cob. I am a bit short of money and want to go to the market to-morrow." On the following morning I met him in Old Ford Road, and he asked me, "Did I know anybody who buy his horse?" I told him "Yes." He can said, "Here it is; do what you like with it. I want £10 down for it or you can have an exchange for another one, as I want some money." I got up into the trap, and going along the Old Ford Road I met Gardner and Riley. I said, "Halloa, would you like to go for a ride?" They said they did not mind and got up and accompanied me to Droking. When I got to Pixham Lane I left Riley and Gardner at the top of the lane while I went down to where I was going to sell the horse. I am perfectly innocent of having stolen the horse. I had seen Lovell on the Thursday before at Spitalfields Market with some bananas on his cart with a horse exactly like the one he gave me to sell, and I thought it was the same horse when I saw it one the Monday night.
 Cross-examined. After I had sold the horse I intended to put the cart on the rail and send it up to London Bridge. I think I had 10 1/2 d. in my pocket. That would not have paid for sending back the cart, but I should have had money after I had sold the horse. It did not strike me to ask any questions about the harness or the horse because I had seen Lovell with it on the previous Thursday. He keeps a fruit stall somewhere in the Bethnal Green Road. When I was arrested at Dorking they asked me who this Lovell was, and I told the detective as far as lay in my power. I did not know Lovell very well, only through having seen him up and down. Lovell promised me a bit of gold if I sold the horse. I was to meet Lovell again after I had sold the cob at the corner of Brick Lane, Bethnal Green Road the same night. I have never seen Lovell since as, of course, I have been in custody, whereas if I had been out on bail I might have had a chance to look about for him.
