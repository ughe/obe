   COURT . Q.  You had been in the habit of going to that coffee shop many times? A.  Yes—I live some way from it—I had seen Raymond before at the coffee shop when I have walked through—there is convenience at the coffee shop for persons of my calling—I have been there a great many times.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Was there a crowd outside? A.  No—at the time I was knocked down I only saw Wren and Raymond there—there was nobody else there—I was knocked down directly I got out-side—I do not know how many persons were in the coffee shop—there was a young girl with me—she was in the private room, and there was a young man who was having supper with her—not a single soul saw me knocked down—Wren is not the owner of the coffee house, he is the waiter—Raymond has nothing to do with the house that I know of—I have seen him there as I passed through sometimes—I have not said that he struck me on the forehead with his crutch or with his hand—I said that he held me down, with his hand on my forehead, but did not knock me hard—that was when I was knocked down, and while he was taking the money from me he hit me with his open hand—Wren was on the step of the door—the girl was inside finishing her supper—the door was not open—she could not see—there are two doors—Wren served in the shop, and Mrs. Smith and the servant girl were serving in the bar, which is through the shop—I did not notice any customers in the shop when I went out, but there were some when I first went in—two men were sitting by the fire when I first went in—it was on the step of the door that Wren asked me to lend him some money—the girl desired me not to pay a sovereign in the shop, but before that I paid down a sovereign and thought it was a shilling—it was before that that Wren asked me to lend him five shillings—I was then outside the door on the step—I had changed the sovereign before that—the other girl came from the Caledonian-road—I went into the shop alone at first, that was to have my supper—I gave Wren the half crown on the step of the door just as the other girl was coming up—the rest of my money was in my purse at that time—I do not know the young man's name—I had seen him before—I am not an acquaintance of hia, but I have seen him with my friend—I do not know whether he is a witness here—I have not seen him since—he was in the coffee shop at the time I was knocked down—he did not call out, but when I called out he came out—he said that he did not hear me—when I first went in with the other girl I do not know whether the two men were there, but they were when I first went in—I brought my money out because I was going to see some girls whom I knew, and I might spend some of it—I did not think anything about losing it or I should not have taken it—I did not show it to the other girl—she knew that I had it, because she lived with me, and knew that I took it out—she left the house with me—I did not see her first in the coffee shop, I left with her at 8 o'clock in the even ing, and took my money in my purse—I did not tell her the reason I was taking it—she told me to leave the money at home, and
