 867.  GEORGE CLARKE, JAMES CLARKE, and THOMAS BRANSCOMB were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , 2 live tame ducks, price 5s., the property of John Dixon.
 JAMES DIXON. I am a  glass-cutter, and live in Park-place, Hare-street-fields. On Saturday last I had two live ducks, which slept under the stairs at night, and they run out in the morning - I saw them safe at half-past six o'clock in the morning; I received information, and about one o'clock in the day I saw the prisoners eating some pastry, and supposed they had sold the ducks, and got the money to buy it; I asked George Clarke about them, and he said he had not seen them - he was a neighbour; I looked into his basket, and saw the dung of the duck - he had seen me before I came up to him, and told James Clarke to run away; James afterwards told me he had sold them to a Mr. Clarke, in Montague-street; I went there and got them - I know them to be mine.
   CHARLES BARRY. I am a butcher. I was near the prosecutor's house on Saturday last, about seven o'clock in the morning, and saw four boys pinning the ducks up in a corner against the house - George Clarke then caught one and put it into a basket - one of the others caught the other, but I cannot say which - I told a woman of it.
   ISAAC CLARK. I am a poulterer, and live in Old Montague-street. On Saturday morning, about eight o'clock, George Clarke brought the ducks to me, and said they were his own, and that he lived opposite Mr. Wells, the farrier - I gave him half a crown for them; I put them into my yard till the prosecutor came.
   ISAIAH BANBURY. I was coming from my own home a little before eight o'clock, and I saw George Clarke with two ducks - and a man, whom the last witness employs at times looked at them, and the witness asked, out at the window, what price they were, and the boy said, "Half a Crown, "and that he lived at No. 5, Charles-street, opposite Mr. Wells, the farrier; I afterwards saw Joseph Clarke, near the door of the Compasses, public-house, with some silver in his hand, and a fourth boy was near him; they went to several places to get change - I saw some halfpence in their hands, and they were gone in a moment.
   ROBERT WOOD. I am an officer. I bring the ducks, which I got from Clarke's - he would not give them up till I went to get a summons - then he gave them up.
 BRANSCOMB's Defence. I was asleep on Saturday morning, and George came and called me about half-past six o'clock, and I went with him.
 GEORGE CLARKE - GUILTY. Aged 15. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Whipped and Discharged.
 JOSEPH CLARKE. -    NOT GUILTY.
 BRANSCOMB. -    NOT GUILTY.

 868.  JAMES PAYNE alias    JOHN BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of February , 1 wrapper, value 6d., and 20 yards of woollen cloth, value 15l., the goods Zachary Langton and others, his partners.
   HENRY BATTEN. I am clerk in the employ of Messers. Pickford and Co. I received a truss from the Angel Inn, Fleet-market, on Thursday, the 23d of February, directed to Joshua Lever, Bolton, Lancashire; we were to convey it to Manchester.
   JAMES POWNER. I am clerk to Messrs. Pickford. On the evening of the 23d of February I was in the office, at the Castle Inn, Wood-street, and saw the prisoner come off the shafts of a waggon, with a truss on his shoulder - it was going to the City basin, and was directed to James Lever, Bolton. I followed him, and stopped him in Wood-street; the truss fell and I fell - he ran away; I followed, and called Stop thief! I saw him in a few minutes, and have no doubt of him.
   THOMAS DAVIS. I am in the employ of Messrs. Pickford and Co. I loaded a truss on their waggon in the yard on the 23d of February, about half-past ten o'clock; on the fore part of it - it was directed to Joshua Lever, Bolton, Lancashire.
   JAMES GREIVES. On this evening I was at my door, opposite the gateway. I heard the cry of Stop thief! ran out, and saw the truss in the street; I took it up - the witness ran after the prisoner, down Love-lane, and in a minute afterwards I saw him brought back.
   JAMES HARRISON. I work for Messrs. Pickford. I saw Grieves take the truss up, and brought it to the office while the prisoner was in the yard.
   JOHN BARNES. I am a constable. I received the prisoner and this truss.
   JAMES POWNER. This is the truss - it is the property of Messrs. Pickford and Co - Zachary Langton is one of their partners.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
