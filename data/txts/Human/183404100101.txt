 718.  PETER BARTLETT was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of February , 10 pair of gloves, value 1l. 5s., and 6 pair of unfinished gloves, value 12s., the goods of George Arnold , his master.
 GEORGE ARNOLD. I live in  Berners-street, and am a glove-manufacturer. The prisoner had been my journeyman three or four months - I missed gloves, which induced me to mark two or three dozen, both finished and unfinished - I put these on the counter on the 27th of February, in the morning - the prisoner came in and stood at that counter - I missed half-a-dozen pairs - the officer stood at the door, and followed him when he went out at dinner-time.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I am an officer. I was waiting at the prosecutor's door, and followed the prisoner to Tabernacle-walk - he went into No. 5, and in four or five minutes he came out, and went into a public-house - I took him in charge, and took six pair of gloves out of each of his coat pockets, and this dozen from his hat - he said they were his own.
 MR. ARNOLD. These are my gloves - they are what I marked.
 Cross-examined by MR. JONES. Q. What is the mark? A. This is it; "six not welted" - I never received any gloves of the prisoner in my life - he said two or three times that he had two duplicates of gloves, which he bought of a little manufacturer, who had broken, and I purchased them of him - I went into a public-house with the prisoner on the morning he was taken, on our road home - we had some porter, but no gin - I said to him, "Bartlett, I am sorry for you" - my house has not been searched for stolen goods.
   CHARLES LONGMAN. I am journeyman to the prosecutor. The prisoner worked there - he told me if I would take some gloves, he could sell them, and he could put me up to more than I knew - he came on the Monday morning to work, and took two skins - I told my master, and he told me to hold on with him, and try if I could pick out where he sold them - he came again on the Tuesday, and took a pair of gloves which were finished; and on Wednesday morning he took three pair, which were not finished, and said he was going to sell them at dinner-time.
 Cross-examined. Q. He told you he could put you up to something? A. Yes, on the Saturday night - I had only been there a few days - I had before lived at Yeovill with my brother-in-law, and worked for  Mr. Wilmington - there was something occurred between us; but he gave me a very good character, and gave me up to Mr. Cook - I was not discharged for having received money for goods, and entering them in the book as not paid for - I received the money, and gave it to my master's daughter - I was then called down to tea, and was told I had received half-a-crown more than I had paid; but I had not.(The prisoner put in a written defence, declaring his innocence.)
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Confined for Six Months.
 719.  JOHN WILLS was indicted for receiving, on the 26th of February , 4 pair of gloves, value 10s., the goods of George Arnold , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 GEORGE ARNOLD. This prisoner lived in Tabernacle-walk - I followed the last prisoner to his house; and after we took Bartlett, we went to this prisoner's house, and found four pair of gloves on the table and in the drawer, which are mine - the prisoner is a glove-manufacturer - he said he bought those gloves of Bartlett.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I am an officer. I went with Mr. Arnold to the prisoner's house - I said to the prisoner, "I believe you had Bartlett here yesterday?" - he said, "No"- I said, "You have to-day?" - he said "No" - I said,"I am witness that he came here to-day" - Mr. Arnold then found four pair of gloves, and said, "These four pair I had in my hand yesterday - I can swear to them" - the prisoner then said, "I had them of Peter Bartlett."
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. He keeps a glove shop? A. Yes; some of these were in his window - I do not know whether he was at home when Bartlett went to his house.    NOT GUILTY.
