I CONE
FRANCIS COTTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have one shop in Shoreditch,
and one in Hackney-road on the 7th of January I was in my shop
in Hackney-road-about the middle of the day the two prisoners came
in and brought a piece of flannel, but I did not take it in-it was in
flat folds-I forget the sum they asked for it, but it was 91. or 101.-I asked
what quantity there was, and they said the quantity was marked on it,
187, pointing to a red cbalk mark on the outer end-my young man was
attending to the prisoners, and I asked him if he had measured it-he said,
"No"-I said I could not tell what there was without they said they
would not have it measured on any account, as it would put it out of con-
dition, and those marks on the flannel were made in the country by author-
ised registered men-they showed me a ticket which my young man had
given them, and that of Mr. Fleming's.
Williams's Defence. They were my own goods, and I meant no harm
---my step-son merely carried a piece or two-he knows nothing about it.
(The prisoners received good characters.)
WILLIAMS-GUILTY-Confined Three Months.
BAYNTON-GUILTY-Confined One Month.
