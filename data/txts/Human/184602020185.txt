GEORGE THOMAS EMMETT was indicted for stealing 1 pair of spur-leathers, value 1 s  ., 6 d  .; 1 leather strap, 1 s  .; 1 bridle, 2 s  . 6 d  .; 3 hearing hooks, 2 s  . 9 d  .; 1 whip, 10 s  .; 1 brush, 2 s  . 6 d  .; 1 box. 1 s  .; 1 snuff-box, 6 d  .; 1 stop-cock, 6 d  .; and 2lbs. of brass, 1 s  .; the goods of Samuel Hammond .—2nd COUNT  of Allen Miles . SAMUEL HAMMOND. I am messenger's assistant to the Court of Bank ruptcy—on the 12th of Nov. I was put in possession of the goods and pre mises of a person named Davis, in High-street, Borough—the prisoner was employed by the broker to lot the goods—he came there on the 16th of Dec.—he had a blue bag in his hand—I afterwards saw him leave the premises with a bag in his hand, which appeared to be the same he had brought—I have examined the brass-work produced—It is such at is used by harness makers—I did not deliver to the prisoner, or suffer to be taken by him, any of this brass.
   Prisoner.  The property produced is in the inventory. Witness.  We have not missed anything from the inventory, but I am not able to say whether these were in it or not—the sale had not taken place. GEORGE WILD( police-constable, M  94.) I saw the prisoner in a marine store dealer's shop, in Union-street, on the 16th of Dec., with a blue bag in his hand, and a riding-whip in his right hand—he placed the bag on the counter, and took from it a brown paper, containing these rings and this brass-work—I went to him, told him I was an officer, and asked if they were his—he said, "Yes"—I asked where he worked—he declined telling me—he said he lived in May-fair—I said, "I must take possession of this, and take you, except you can tell me where you have been at work to-day"—on the road to the station, he said if I would go to No. 100, High-street, there was a person named Hammond, in possession under the Bankruptcy Court, and he would make it all right, for he gave him the brass, and told him to sell it to buy some porter for to-morrow—as we were going to the station, I missed the whip—I said, "Where is your whip?"—he said, "I have dropped it, but that is of no consequence"—I took him to the station, and was ordered to search him—he said I need not, he had nothing about him—I found this strap, this brass box, and these other things in his coat pocket—I said, "You said you had nothing"—he said, "Mr. Hammond gave me these; It is of no consequence."
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you say I offered them for sale? A.  You placed them on the counter—the dealer referred to his book, and asked for your name and your card—I stated before the Magistrate that you said you had dropped the whip. GEORGE DAVIS. I am a harness-maker, and live at No. 100, High-street, Borough.
