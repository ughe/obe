CATHERINE BRYAN. I live at No. 7, Royal Kent-court, Kent-street, and am single. This bonnet and ribbons are mine—my mother had given the prisoner a lodging, on the 14th of February, and something to eat, and allowed her to share my bed—she was to pay nothing for it—I had my bonnet in a bonnet-box—I got up at seven o'clock, and left the prisoner in—ghe thanked me for the night's lodging, and went away—I after wards went up stairs and missed my bonnet—she mentioned Woolwich as the place she came from—I went to Deptford, and there found her in custody, and saw this bonnet in the officer's bands.
   Primer.  My father is a sergeant in the artillery, he is in America, and al lovi my mother 35 s . a month to support her—she drew 5 l . 5 s . four months ago, and went away, and left me at Mr. Richardson's, No. 69, New-road, Woolwich, a grocer, with nothing to support me—I was informed she was in London, and I came to London in search of her, in a place called Pye-street, where there is a lodging-house—I went there—she had not been there—I went to White chapel, and, not finding her there, I was crying in the street—I came to this young girl's mother's—she asked what I was crying for—she took me in and gave me a night's lodging—the next morning I got up and took this bonnet, on purpose that I might get into some place for relief—it was not a lodging-house where I lived—it was a grocer's shop—my mother lodged in the house for two years—I used to go about to the neighbours, and they used to give me a little victuals—my father will be home again in the Spring. CATHERINE BRYAN re-examined.  That is the account she gave when she was taken in by my mother. JOHN LEDGER re-examined.  She told me the same story—there was a young man before the Magistrate, who knows the bouse where the prisoner and her mother had been, and she stated the name of the person—on the day before 1 took her I saw her going up towards London, which just cor responds with what she says now. GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Six Days.
 894. EDWARD DOCURA was indicted for embezzlement. GEORGE DRUCE. I am a baker, and live in Commercial-road, Lambeth. The prisoner was in my employ, to carry out bread and to receive money, for which he was to account to me every evening—on Tuesday, the 24th of January, I gave him a bill of 2 s . 3 d . for Mrs. Clark, Chester Cottage, kennington—on the Sunday morning I asked him if he had received that bill—he said he had not—he has put down in my book (which he had no business to touch) 2 s . 6 d ., which is three pence over—I did not discover that entry till I had been to Kennington, to see whether he had received it—this is not my writing. HENRIETTA HOPE. I am a customer of Mr. Druce. On the 30th of January I saw my aunt, Mary Ann Clark, pay the prisoner 2 s . 6 d .—the bill was 2 s . 3 d ., but he had no copper—I saw him receipt the bill—here it is it was delivered on the 24th, and paid on the 30th.
