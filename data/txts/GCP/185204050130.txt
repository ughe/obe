I paid the money to Mr. Stowell, I counted it on the table, there was
111. or 121. in silver, and he said, "Dear me, bow am I to take this silver?"
- lent him a chamois leather bag to take it away in-it was stated at the
time that 201. was for the money borrowed in Jan., and 30s. was for the
interest-it was between 1 and 2 o'clock in the afternoon; Mr. Stowell
dined at our house that day-we had a shoulder of mutton and onion sauce
on my oath I paid bim the money for the purpose I have stated, in the
presence of my husband I was acquainted with Mr. Stowell, and had been
some time previously-my maiden name was Maslin-Miss Maslin is my
sister--at the time this money was paid, Mr. Stowell was on friendly terms
with Mr. Kennett, particularly so, and be had been so some time previously
when I paid him the money, I asked him for the TOU; he said he had
left his pocket book at home, and the 10 U was in it, but he would send it
over-I was not present at the payment of the ten guineas, but I counted
the money and gave it to my husband-I was examined at the County
Court-I remember, about 18th or 19th March my husband going out with
some money ; Mr. Alfred Younger, who is Calvert's broker, left our house
with him.
