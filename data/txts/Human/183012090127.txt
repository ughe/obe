   JAMES FOGG. I am a Police-officer. On the 10th of December, I went with Deverell to the prisoner's house, in William-street - I saw the prisoner's wife there, and I found in a drawer, a new gown of Norwich crape; a new table-cloth, a piece of linen, and two silver spoons, under the bed; the table-cloth was not made, and a new jacket and trousers which had not been worn - I went to the prisoner and told him his wife said he had taken these things out of pawn, and they appeared to be tarnished - he said they had been out; I asked him where, but he would not tell me - he said he supposed his wife had told me; he said he had paid his rent that day.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he in custody when you went to his house? A. Yes.
   WILLIAM BIDDULPH. I am a slop-seller. Some months ago the prisoner ordered a frock and a pair of trousers at my shop - they were made in about a fortnight, but he did not call for about a month, and he said he would not take them then, he would call soon; on Tuesday, the 7th of December, he came in the morning, and requested to have 2s. back, of 4s. which he had left as earnest - I let him have the 2s., and in the evening he came again and paid me 12s., which was the balance that was due to me; that was about seven o'clock in the evening, and he had called in the morning between nine and eleven.
 Cross-examined. Q. You will not swear it might not have been eleven o'clock? A. I will not swear one way or the other - I had to go out of town, and to be in Oxford-street at twelve o'clock; I started about eleven from home - I know he called before eleven.
   JOHN DEVERELL. I am headborough of Shadwell. On the 8th of December I took the prisoner, in the prosecutor's shop - the prosecutor charged him with stealing the bag; I took him to the watch-house - when we were there, he said he had taken the 6d. and 1 1/2d.; I put some questions about the pawnbroker, which he declined to answer.
 Cross-examined. Q. I believe you found him working at the docks? A. Yes, occasionally - I was loath to lock him up, and I left him with a friend or two while I went to make further inquiries - I let him go that night, and he came to the office next day of his own accord; before we parted that night I took a share of a pot of porter, and I believe there was a quartern of gin between six of us - Mr. Lamonte was present; the prisoner said Mr. Hows had given him trouble enough, and if he did not pay for it he would - I let him out because I had been to Mr. Hows, and he had received information that Samuel Heatley was the person who had committed the robbery: I knew where to find him, but Mr. Hows then came and said he was not the man, it was the prisoner he thought; we then parted- I went to the prisoner's house, and his wife said he had been to the shop, and I advised Mr. Hows not to press the charge - I then went to the public-house where the prisoner and his friends were, and I said, if he and his friends would answer in the sum of 20l. I would let him go; I took no regular bail - he came and delivered himself up the next morning; I ascertained that he was well known.
   JAMES FOGG. I saw this examination, written by the clerk, and signed by the magistrate, Captain Richbell . - read.
 "On the Tuesday morning in question I went into Mr. How's shop; before that I took home a pair of shoes and a boot to Mr. Dowding, and Dowding paid me 1s. 6d.; I asked for 1 lb. of sausages, no one was in the shop when I went in - Hows was in the back-place, with a pen counting up a book or something- he had a great many halfpence on the counter counted up, and I said to him, "Mr. Hows, how easy I could have a sweep of these in my hat;" Mr. Hows said he was not mistrustful that any one would take them - he put the sausages in the scale, and they were thin ones; he weighed them, and I gave him 1s.; while he was giving me the change a woman came in and began speaking to Mr. Hows - I took my change up and tied the sausages in a handkerchief; there were three pieces of copper with the sixpence - I went from there to the London-dock with the sausages in my hat - I went home, and my wife and I had the sausages' and went to work till half-past twelve o'clock at night; I had not 6d, to get my breakfast when I went out in the morning; the Norwich crape my wife bought at Mr. Hunt's at Shoreditch - he is a tally-man; it might be two years ago. As for the linen it belongs to Mrs. Smith (my wife's sister) - the other piece of linen my wife bought one day this week, I think; the other gown piece was brought while my wife was from home yesterday morning, about nine o'clock, and left with me for my wife to make; I never saw the person who brought it before - she left no name; my wife bought the table-cloths yesterday or the day before - I gave her two sovereigus the day before yesterday; I lost a sovereign out of my pocket yesterday - I sold a Scotch cap and a pair of boots for 1l. 10s. on Tuesday, and I sold some other things. On Tuesday morning last I went and fetched the jacket and trousers from a clothes-shop opposite Gould's-hill - he measured me for them three or four months ago, and I paid him 4s. off, and on Tuesday morning I asked him to let me have 2s. off the 4s., and he did so - when I had sold the things I have mentioned I went and paid him for the the jacket and trousers; I paid him 12s. - his name is Dowditch, or some such name; he knows my name, and has it in his book.  JOHN WAPSHERE."
 Taken before me on the 9th of Dec, 1830. T. Richbell.
 Prisoner's Defence. I gave an account at the Police-office who the things belonged to - my wife is a dress-maker; the officer might have found a dozen dresses - the officer had a receipt of the Norwich crape, which my wife gave him off the file.
   JAMES FOGG. I had it, but I do not know that I brought it here; it was merely the bill - I put it in the drawer at the office; I went to Mr. Hunt's, and found it was all right - she had bought it there.
 COURT. Q. On what day did you ascertain that it had been bought? A. On the Thursday I went to his house; it had been bought eighteen months ago, at a linen-draper's, in Shoreditch.
