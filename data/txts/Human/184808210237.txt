   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE SIMPSON. On 1st July, between four and five o'clock in the morning, I was with my mother in the Borough-market—the prisoners came up and bought eighteen gallons of raspberries, and paid 8 s . 3 d . for them—Lawrence gave me four half-crowns, which I gave to my mother—she gave me 2 s . 3 d . change, which I gave to him—Barrett was standing by his side, and had joined in the bargain—I took the raspberries to the opposite side of the market—they were put into one of the prisoner's baskets in a barrow—the pri soners returned in half an hour, and Barrett bought six gallons of raspberries—Barrett paid roe balf-a-crown and a shilling, which I gave to my mother—the prisoners went away—they came back again with a man and woman—the prisoners persuaded them to buy some raspberries, for which they paid three half-crowns—I gave them to my mother—I saw the prisoners together in the Borough-market again on 8th July, and gave them into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Had you seen them in the mean time? A.  No—I do not know that they have attended the market four or fire years—I found out that these half-crowns were bad about half an hour after the man and woman left, which was about six o'clock—I had not sold many goods that morning—Keys had not paid me any half-crowns—I did not sell fruit to other persons afterwards—Mr. Fricker has a stand near my mother's. HANNAH SIMPSON. I am the mother of George Simpson. I was in the Borough-market selling fruit, on 1st July—I saw the prisoners come for eighteen gallons of raspberries—I saw Lawrence pay my son four half-crowns—he gave them to me, I put them in my pocket—the prisoners went away they returned, and Barrett bought some raspberries which came to 3 s . 6 d .—I saw him pay a half-crown and a shilling to my son—he brought it to me—I put it in the same pocket with the others—no one had been to pur chase fruit in the meantime—the prisoners went away, and came back with a man and woman—they bought some raspberries—I cannot tell what money my son gave me, but I put it into my pocket—no one had been to deal in the meantime—I had no money in my pocket but what I had from my son—after I had sold the last lot, something was said, and I took eight half-crowns and two shillings out of my pocket.
