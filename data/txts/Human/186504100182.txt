 470. JAMES RICHARD CRAMPTON  (33), was indicted for bigamy.
   MR. LANGFORD conducted the Prosecution. JOHN HACKING( Policeman, K  216). On the 22d of March, at half-past 10 at night, I was on duty at Ilford—the prisoner came to me accompanied by a young woman—he said he had come to give himself up for bigamy—I asked him why he did so, and he said he was "hard up," and had had nothing to eat that day; he had been to New York, and could get no em ployment—I asked him where his wives lived, and he said one lived at 26, Richardson-street, Borough, and went to the church at St. John's, Waterloo on the 3d of March—I got a certificate of marriage, which I compared with the books in the church—this is a true copy ( produced. ) ALICE HARRIS. I have a daughter named Fanny Louisa—on 3d July, 1853, I was at the church of St. John's, Waterloo, and saw my daughter married to the prisoner—my daughter is alive—I saw her last night.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not your daughter go and live in adultery with another man? A.  Yes, when you deserted her.
   COURT . Q.  Had she any children by the other man? A.  No—the pri soner went to the Crimea in 1855, and he returned and claimed his wife in 1862—my daughter did not give him into custody—she, does not wish to have any claim upon him. LOUISA ROYSTON HILL. I live at 4, Frederick-place, Little Ilford—on the 6th of March, 1864, I was married to the prisoner—I had a certificate—I lived with him up to the 14th of November, when he left, and went to New York—he came back again on the 3d of last March.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you recollect going to Richardson-street to see my first wife? A.  I do; I said that I loved you, and would marry you again if you could get a divorce—you were always a good husband to me, and I would marry you again. GUILTY  .— Recommended to mercy. — Confined One week .
