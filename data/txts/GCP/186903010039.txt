344. FREDERICK TOOMEY (20), and CHARLES CHRISTOPHER
(26), Barglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Mark
Girschen, and stealing thirty-three pieces of cloth, his property.
Mr. Moody conducted the Prosecution; and Mr. ROWLAND defended Toomey,
and MR. GRIFFITHS defended Christopher.
Mark GIRSCHEN. I live at No. 10, Artillery Passage, Bishopsgate Street,
and am a tailor-about 2 o'clock on 17th February, I was called up, and
missed several pieces of unfinished work, cut trowsers, and pieces of cloth-
the value of the things stolen was about 51.- I have some of them here-I
shut up about 11 o'clock the night before--when I woke up, the first floor
window was right open—the window was taken out, the ropes had been cut
through-it was safe when I went to bed.

STEPHEN BIRD. I live at No. 8, Artillery Passage-on the morning of
17th February, between 2 and 3 o'clock, I heard some kind of cracking
noise, as if someone was trying to break & window sash-I got up and
looked ont-I saw a man standing on the lead work over the shop front of
Mr. Girschen's-he succeeded in getting the window open-I then went in
and came back with a bundle, which he threw out of the window--I gave
the alarm--the man jumped down and ran away-I called, "Policel" and a
constable came up in about ten minutes I can't say who the man was.
HENRY MILLER (Policeman H 120). On this morning, I was on duty in
Dorset Street-I heard a cry of "Stop thief I"-I saw Toomey coming from
the direction of Artillery Passage, walking along with his hands in his
pockets-it was about thirty yards from Artillery Passage--I made a catch
to get hold of him-he said, "I am d i f you shall ontoh me" -he ran
down Dorset Street-I ran after him, but I was thrown down by a female,
and lost sight of him-I went back to Artillery Passage, and as I turned
out of Union Street I saw Christopher coming down—he was running, and
constable 98 after him—he was caught about 100 yards from Artillery
Passage.
Cross-examined by MR. GRIFFITHS. Q. Was he taken in Union Street
4. No, Commercial Street-I said I came down Union Street-he fell
down-he was not knocked down—I went up to him before he got up-he
said, “I did not do anything, I have done nothing, what do you want with
me t-after he got up he said, “Let me stop for my cap" -he had no cap
on-I did not let him stop.

JOHN Wicks (Policeman H 201). I heard cries of " Police l' and saw
Toomey running, in Dorset Street, from Artillery Passage--I ran after him
to Spitalfields Market-there he fell down, and I fell on him-I asked him
why he was running, and he never said anything, he was too much exc
hausted-he was very violent-I took him to the station, and charged him
with breaking into this house he did not say anything-he was running
as fast as he could-I chased him about 150 yards before I caught him, I
should think.
JOHN CRUDGE (Policeman H 90). I heard cries, and saw two men
running, in Dorset Street-I followed, with the other constable, and caught
Toomey-he kicked and struggled very violently--we were too exhausted to
say anything.
