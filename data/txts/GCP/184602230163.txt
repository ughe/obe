188. FREDERICK MUNTON was indicted for feloniously assaulting
Johanna Munton, and cutting and wounding ber in and upon her neck, with
intent to murder her.
FOR ANY MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD Vasey. I am a cabinet-maker and joiner, and live at No. 13,
Crosier-street, Lambeth. The prisoner is married to my sister--they live
at No. 3, Halford-terrace, Walworth—I lodged in their house from Jan, to
Feb.-on Sunday, the 25th of Jan., in the morning, as I was lying in bed I
heard them quarrelling below-it was about half-past seven o'clock, or day-
break--I judge that was the time we had no clock or watch in the house
my mother came into my bed-room-I heard them quarrelling and a severe
struggling between Mr. and Mrs. Munton--when the struggling was all over, I
heard a voice at the foot of the stairs I sent my mother down, and told her
to call me if I was wanted-my mother went down, and about two minutes
afterwards she called me I went down and saw the prisoner stark naked and
corered with blood-I asked him what was the matter--he made no answer
I saw my sister, and I asked her who did it-she was lying on the ground
with her throat cut in the centre, and she feebly pointed to him I went
to the street door and found it double locked and the key taken away. I on
that asked the prisoner for the key-"I do not know" was his reply " You
have concealed the key" I said to him—"I do not know " was his reply
again-I asked him to lend me a hand to take down the board in front of the
parlour window that he was in the habit of cutting out his cloth upon--the
white roller blind was down and marks of blood on it. I pulled it off the
roller altogether and threw it into the corner of the room-we took the wire
blind down, he raised the sash and I got out of the window and called the
policeman-Dr. Otway was sent for-he came and attended her for the
wound-there was blood on the panels of the folding doors as if squirted
upon, and marks of fingers, and likewise round the room, on the looking-glass
and on some tea and sugar that laid on the mantel-piece--they have latterly
lived on quarrelling terms, anything but happiness I have heard him uso
threatening language to her heard him go out on the Thursday night pre-
vions and return home about one o'clock on Friday morning-when he came
home I heard a quarrelling and a knock as if somebody was knocked down,
and I went and demanded admittance at the parlour door-it was locked-I heard
him knock her down-I went to the street door to call " Police," when my
sister came out and put her hand before my mouth-I asked her if he had
struck ber-I asked her that twice, and she answered “No no, never mind,
Never mind"-I then went up stairs into my own bed-room, and then I
came down and listened to them, and he wanted her to take a false oath
against me-she said, "I cannot, I will not take a false oath against my
brother," and he replied, " Then I will do for you"-there had been some
misunderstanding between me and the prisoner previous to this-my mother
was examined before the Magistrate-she has died since-she died on the
oth of Feb. -- I can repeat several other former things.
