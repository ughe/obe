WILLIAM SMITH. I am shopman to the prosecutor. I saw the prisoner
go to the counter-she asked for some butter, and while my mistress went to
give her change she put the cheese into ber basket-I told my mistress.
Prisoner's Defence. I went in for half a pound of butter; I threw a sove-
reign down, and asked for change; I took the butter off the block and laid it
on my right hand, where were some pieces of cheese, but not of the size or
weight of that produced against me; I got my change, put the butter in the
basket, and went outside to the prosecutor ; I bought a rabbit, and opened
the lid of my basket and let him put the rabbit and a piece of pork in:
went to the next shop, got my grocery, and the prosecutor came and wanted
to see what was in my basket; I gave him the basket, and he said to me.
"Where did you get that cheese?" I said I never knew I had any cheese: I
do not know whether it was taken out of my basket; I never saw any cheese
of that weight and size. GUILTY. Aged 21.-Confined One Month.

937. CHRISTIAN RYLE, stealing 1001bs. weight of flour, value 16.
the goods of Isaac Blackbourne Hurst, bis master; and CATHARINE
LOUISA RYLE, feloniously receiving the same.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
Isaac BLACKBOURNE HURST. I am a baker, at Royal-hill, Greenwich-
Christian Ryle was my servant for about a year and eight or ten months
In consequence of a communication made to me, my attention was directed
to him in the bakehouse-I called in an officer, about nine or ten o'clock in
the evening, and searched about the flour-room-I found about twenty-five
empty bags, which had had flour in them, concealed behind the flour-sachs
-next day I searched for them again, and they were gone-no one had
access to the flour but him--I have discovered very great deficiencies in my
stock of flour for the last eighteen months-after he was in custody he se
for me, and I went to him-he asked me to forgive him, as Mr. Baxendale
had forgiven his man-he said he would come and live near me, and nothing
of the kind should happen again, or he would leave the country.

to cut his throat ? A. No, it is not true I did not take up a dough-knife
and threaten to cut him in quarters-it was on a Tuesday night that I di
covered this; I cannot tell the date-it was the night I received an anoby
mous letter by the post-I did not ask him if he had any enemies I did no
go to his house and ask for him to return to his work, and all should be
forgotten.
