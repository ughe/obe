JOHN LAWSON. I am a hatter. In the month of November last I lived Upper-street, Islington—I have been arrested for debt, and confined in Horsemonger-lane prison—I am there still—I know the prisoner—he called my shop at Islington on a Thursday in November—I cannot tell the day—I had seen him frequently before, and considered him a respectable person—he is a coach-proprietor—he said the brokers were in his house, and he wished to raise some money, and asked me if I would buy a silver snuff box—I believe the box produced to be the same—I ultimately bought for 12 s .—some time after, being in want of money, I pawned it at Mr. cerkins's, King's-road, Chelsea, for 12 s ., in the name of Cooper.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were not before the Magistrate, I believe? A.  No—I was arrested at that time—I know Munyard by seeing him some into the place where I am confined—he came there to see a man a named Densham, who was confined there—I never told any one that Mun-yard got up this prosecution, nor any thing of the kind—I said I thought lanyard was the principal party, from a reason I had.

FREDERICK AUGUSTUS WILLIAM MURTHWAITE HELPS. The prisoner lived with me as groom. He left me on Saturday the 7th of April, and I missed these articles—this is my livery suit—he had no right to take it—these other articles are also my property. JOHN HUGH SWEETING( police-sergeant C  2.) I went on board the Exeter Express packet—I found the prisoner there, and found this livery suit in a bundle which he claimed—the other things were in a box on board. GUILTY. Aged 21.
