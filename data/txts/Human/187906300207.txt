By the COURT . I was not asked by any one to prosecute—I wrote to Mr. Fullagar when I saw it in the paper. ALBERT SPIEGELBATER.—I am a watchmaker at 62, The Mount, White Chapel Road, Mr. Kersten is a friend of mine—I remember being in his house about the middle of August, when May, in my presence, asked him for 7 l ., and said he had engaged Mr. Besley, and had to pay him the money—no receipt was given.
   Cross-examined.  I was there till you went away, sitting opposite you in the bar parlour—I came through the bar—I did not see you write the receipt produced, and nothing more was said than what I have stated—you did not remain long after you had the money—I was first asked the week before last to come forward as a witness—I did not think anything more of it, but I recollected well enough after I was told—I was surprised to see in the papers that criminal proceedings were being taken against you, but I did not think anything of it then. HENRY WILLIAM LOCKYER( Re-examined ). I did not receive 7 l . or any money in respect of an action against Kersten in the Lord Mayor's Court, and Mr. Besley was not instructed in the matter at all.
   Cross-examined.  You had cases with me about that time—I should have recollected if you had said, 'I may want you for other cases in the Mayor's Court," but I should not recollect if you said, "I shall want you for another case shortly."
   Witnesses for the Defence. HENRY HAMBRO. I am cashier to Messrs. Cross, Sons, and Riley, Waterloo Place—on 3rd April I-called on Mr. Fullagar and gave him instructions to proceed with a fresh indictment in Lytton's case—he said he had not been very well and had got a gentleman attending to his busi ness who would conduct the case in his absence, and he called you in and introduced you—Mr. Purver was present, and it was arranged that I should put myself in your hands, and you were to conduct the case—it was an Ordinary case; I cannot say what interest our client took in it—it was necessary, the time being short, for us to go about every night for witnesses, and we went about a good deal to gather materials for the case—we were going about and spending money—you made pencil notes which 1 believed to be items of expenditure in the case, but I did not examine them, and during the whole time we were together you paid all the expenses—I had not to pay; it was not a matter I could expend money in—you attended diligently to the case, and to the best of my recollection when you left the case you had all the witnesses before the Grand Jury in the second indictment in Davis's case, when Mr. Fullagar came up in a rage at the Court the day the case was coming on—I cannot remember how many adjournments had taken place—I was present on the 23rd when you handed Mr. Fullagar the papers—Mr. Fullagar appeared to say as little as he could—he was certainly out of temper at the Sessions House but not then—I heard you offer to make out an account, which you did in the adjoining room—I have tried but cannot remember complete sentences of what was said—I recollect him saying something to this effect: "Even those figures would show that you have not accounted for about 12 l .," and I think you said "Very well;" and I remember you saying there would be some remuneration due to you for what you had done for him.
