   Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 839. JOHN WILSON and HENRY SMALLBONES were indicted for stealing 2 chairs, value 15 s  ., the goods of Robert Lake ; and that Wilson had been before convicted of felony; to which
   WILSON  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Six Months . WILLIAM MORTON( police-sergeant, P  7.) I was in company with another policeman, about five o'clock in the evening of the 14th of Jan., in Old Kent-road—I saw the two prisoners together—I followed them till about half-past six o'clock—they passed and re-passed several shops—I saw them come to Mr. Lake's, a furniture-dealers, by the Bricklayers' Arms—they passed and re-passed—they were still together—I then saw an arm reach down two chairs, which were resting on a couch parlty inside—Smith, the officer, ran across the road, and the prisoner, Wilson, ran towards me—I caught him in my arms, and he fell under the belly of a horse—he had nothing with him—he began to kick me over the legs and thighs—I saw that he ran from Mr. Lake's shop—I charged him with stealing two chairs—he made no reply—I then asked if he understood what I said—he said he did not understand it at all.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Where were you? A.  Near the Swan—I was perhaps fifty yards distance from the prisoner, across the road—it was dark—I saw the prisoners together about a minute before the chairs were taken. HENRY DUFF( police-constable P  136.) I was with Morton. I saw the prisoners together for one hour and twenty minutes—I saw them pass and re-pass Mr. Lake's shop for about a quarter of an hour—I saw Wilson with one or two chairs in his arms—Smith ran to him, and Wilson ran from him into the arms of Morton—I ran and saw Smallbones about 200 yards off, looking back to see if anybody was following him—I said to him, "Come back to your mate"—he said, "You have not seen me do anything—it was not me that took the chairs"—I had not said anything to him about chairs.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When did you first say you saw Smallbones looking back? A.  I said so in my evidence before the Magistrate—this is my signa-ture to this deposition—I said, "I ran in the direction I thought Smallbones was gone, and overtook him—he was walking when I overtook him." WILLIAM SMITH( police-constable P  302.) I was in the Kent-road—Morton called my attention to the two prisoners—I saw them together for ten minutes or a quarter of an hour—I saw Wilson take the two chairs off a couch or sofa—he walked away with them towards New Cross—I went towards him—he threw the chairs on my feet, and in stepping to catch him, I stepped on one of the chairs, and broke it—Wilson ran into Morton's arms—I saw Smallbones with Wilson together a minute before he took the chairs. JOHN DANIEL BLAIN. I am shop-boy to Mr. Robert Lake—he is a furniture-dealer, and lives in the Old Kent-road. On the 14th of Jan. I saw Wilson take the chairs off the sofa, and walk away—I ran after him, and stopped him—I did not see Smallbones at that time—I had seen them together about five minutes before—these are my master's chairs.
   SMALLBONES  — NOT GUILTY.
