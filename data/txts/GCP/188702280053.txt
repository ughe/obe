him--the Kensington branch shop was never opened to my knowledge;

he said “I have altered my plans; I intend to take a shop there”-in

this reply: "July 21-86-Sir, I am surprised at your letter, and if you

a full letter to-morrow. If I am injured by your indiscretion, talking
about my business, how can you expect me to give you so large an income
as I am giving you? However, I can easily satisfy your claim if we come
to terms.-8. H. SMITH.” Next day I received this letter: "Dear Sir,
You are a strange man. I will reply fully to yours to-morrow, also enclose
cheque for your salary, which you will beceive on Saturday morning.
Don't fear losing anything by me, much less fear losing it yourself. You
are too excited." I received my salary up to November 27th, but I had
stayed away from the business for two months, in consequence of this
letter. (Dated Clapham, October 11, 1886, and stating ; "It is no use your
