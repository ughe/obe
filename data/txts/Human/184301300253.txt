I am a general dealer. On the 31st of Jan., the pri soner was coming down Cow-cross, on the mare, which was very lame indeed—I asked him whether it was for sale—he said, "Yes"—I said, "Who does it belong to?"—he said, "My master"—he told me who his master was—I asked him where his master lived—he said, up by Sadler's Wells—he was a green-grocer—I asked him the price—he said be wanted 30 s .—I said, "Is that the lowest?"—he said, "No, master says I may take 1 l . "—I said, "I will go along with you, and see whether it is all correct"—I gave him the 1 l ., and went with him towards Sadler's Wells, to see if his master lived there—before we got there, he ran away from me—I ran, caught him, and asked what made him run away—he said he was going to leave bis master, and did not wish to give him the money—I asked him for the money back—he gave it to me, and told me he had stolen it, and who he brought it from—I took him to the Post-office—the mare has been left in Sharp's-alley, in care of my wife.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you mean to swear he told you he had stolen it? A.  He said him and another had stolen it from Mr. Bolton's place—that was not in Seabrook's presence—it was before he came up—he said no further about his master, but that he was going to leave him that night—that he should stick his wages to the money—he told the Magistrate he had stolen it—that was taken down in writing, as far as I know—I heard my deposition read over to me—I am no scholar, but this is my mark—I heard him say to Seabrook that another boy stole it, and he sold it for him, but that is not what he told me—the stables the horse is supposed to come from are over the water.
 (  The witness's depositions being read, omitted to state that the prisoner said he had stolen the mare. ) WILLIAM GIBBS. I said so at the office—what I said there was nothing but the truth. HANNAH WHITE. I am the wife of William White, foreman at Mr. Bolton's premises, at Lambeth. I have seen the prisoner—I know a boy named Morgan—I saw him come into the yard, put the halter on the horse, and lead it out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was he alone when he did that? A.  Yes, quite—I saw him take it out of the yard—I did not see him take it any distance—there was nobody in the yard but me saw him come in, and put the halter on the mare—I did not see the prisoner or anybody else there. NOT GUILTY.
