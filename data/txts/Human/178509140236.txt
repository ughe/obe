The first witness called is Christian Goodman, a young looking lad, and his evidence is, that Lockell and Harvey came to the door of his father's house, and knocked at the door; that his mother opened the window, and asked what was the matter; and upon their telling her they wanted Peter, but not what for, she came down, and opened the door for them, and that after going into the house, they went away again; and that when they did go away he himself knowing where Peter was gone to, went to tell him that these people had been after him to take him up, and to advise him to keep out of the way, that he met Newberry a short way from the house and he gave him that advice, but he trusting in his own innocence would go home; the witness went home with him, Lockell and Harvey were returned to the house, and took Newberry as soon as he came into the house, so that by his account they were twice in the house, and had gone away in the intermediate time, and he had taken the opportunity to go and apprize Newberry that they were in pursuit of him; in order to fist the matter more fully, he was much further cross examined, and the first enquiry will be how far the account he gave on his first examination is confirmed or rendered probable by his own testimony on cross-examination; the next enquiry will be how far it is consistent with or contradictory to the evidence of the other witnesses, on the part of the prosecution, he said the door of the house was quite open, and added, their door always stood always open; he describes himself as going out to look for Peter, and his meeting him within not more than the length of this Court off his own door, they not stopping or going anywhere else, but returning directly back, but he says that Harvey and Lockell came twice, and that they went after the first search to a public house, and were come down again and near the bottom of the stairs when he came back with Peter Newbery; now it is scarcely possible that this account can in all these circumstances be correctly true, for if they came twice, and went up stairs with a view of searching for them, and his father went with them to the public house their enquiries there, their return, their going up stairs again to make a second search, and coming down again, all must have passed according to his account, within the time that the witness would be walking the length of this Court: when he first said the door stood always open, I dare say it would not suggest to your minds that it was latched, and if it stood always open Lockell must have known it, and therefore would not knock at a door which he knew was always open. The evidence of Stones does not very materially differ from the account given by the boy, he speaks only to the last time of Lockell and Harvey going into the house, he rather increases the time, he says on his cross-examination that Peter came home by himself, and shoved the door open with his left hand; Goodman the father swears that the boy was up stairs till he came back from the Pink, and then he went down stairs, and was absent a few minutes; it is very remarkable that the evidence of the father, whose knowledge if they were relating a true fact, would be more likely to coincide with the son than other witnesses, is more directly contrary to him than any other witness, he describes the time between the first and second search very considerable, he makes it from a quarter of an hour to twenty minutes between the first time of their coming and the time of their apprehending Newberry; but he directly contradicts the evidence of the boy.
