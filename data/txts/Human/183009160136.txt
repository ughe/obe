 Q. Was he so drunk that an honest man would not like to make a bargain with him? A. No, I should not - he staggered about the street when we first came out; Jones investigated who I was before he gave me money - he did not inquire if any money was due to me; Lewis recollected about owing Smith the shoes, drunk as he was.
 COURT. Q. Did you tell him he was too drunk to make a bargain? A. No, nor did Smith say so - he was sober.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not, on the pay-day at the India-house, say to Jones, "I hope you don't think I had a hand in the business?" A. I did not; Jones said,"Smith, I don't want you, I think you are an honest man."
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did Smith, in your hearing, call"Lewis Morgan,"? A. I did not hear him; I was present when he introduced him - he did not use any name.
 Lewis. Q. Did not both you and Smith call me by my own name while Jones was at dinner? A. Both of us did.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Six Months.
 LEWIS -    GUILTY. Aged 38. Transported for Seven Years.
 NEW COURT. THURSDAY, SEPTEMBER 23.
 Third Middlesex Jury. - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1796.  JOHN SMITH and WILLIAM HALL were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of September , I handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of David Lewis , from his person.
 DAVID LEWIS. I am a  medical man. On the 16th of September, as I was in St. John's-square, Clerkenwell, I felt something pull at my pocket; I turned and saw Smith putting my handkerchief into Hall's bosom - I had had it half a minute before; I seized Hall, and Smith ran away - I called Stop thief! he was brought back in about a minute; I had lost sight of him, but I am sure he is the person.
 Hall. Q. Can you swear I was with Smith? A. Yes; the handkerchief was put into your bosom - I seized you, and took it from you - you wanted me to let you go, and said you knew nothing about it.
   CHARLES DENNIS. I was coming through Jerusalem-passage, and saw the two prisoners walking just a-head of me; Mr. Lewis was walking with two others - when he got to a broad part, Smith took the handkerchief, and put it into Hall's bosom; Lewis seized Hall - Smith ran off; I pursued, cried Stop thief! and he was stopped without getting out of my sight - I had seen them together for one hundred yards.
 Hall. Q. Can you say I was with Smith? A. Yes, and when I came up to you, you said, "You did not see me take the handkerchief."
   WILLIAM HILL. I stopped Smith, who was coming towards me.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Hall's Defence. it was thrown at me, it lodged between my coat and waistcoat; I know nothing of Smith.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 HALL -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1797.  WILLIAM WINDRED was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , 1 watch, value 1l.; 2 seals, value 4s.; 1 chain, value 2d., and 1 key, value 1d., the goods of John Snell; and SARAH MYERS, was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   MARIA SNELL. I am the wife of John Snell. On the 26th of August I lost a watch from a drawer, in the room behind my shop, at the corner of Spital-street, Spitalfields- I had seen it safe twenty minutes after nine o'clock, and missed it a quarter before ten, when I went to the drawer to look for a bill; I had been out for about five minutes, to carry half-a-bushel of coals to the next door, and in that time Windred came in, and left a bag which I had sent with coals to his mother the day before - I did not see him in my shop.
   ELEANOR TAYLOR. I was cleaning the step of our door, which is next to the prosecutor's - I saw Windred in Snell's shop, he said to me, "Ellen, tell Mrs. Snell I have brought the coal-bag home;" he went into the shop, and might be there five minutes, but I went in doors, and did not see him come out.
   WILLIAM NELSON. I work with my mother. On the 26th of August, I saw Windred opposite the prosecutor's door, at the corner of the street, at half-past ten o'clock - he said he had a watch, and asked me if I knew where to sell it - I said No - I went with him down Petticoat-lane, where we met a man, who told us where Myers lived; we went to her house and saw her - he asked her if she would buy it - she said Yes; he asked her 1l. for it, and she gave him 8s., - she gave us each 4s.; we left the watch and came away - when he came out he saw his mother, and ran away; at night the officer came for me, but I was not at home - Mr. Brown came to me, and I took him to Myers.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you mean to say that Mrs. Myers ever gave one farthing for the watch? A. Yes - she did not ask him where he got it, nor did he say his father gave it to him - I did not take it to Myers; I am sure we were together - she did not stop the watch for him to bring his father; she bought it as soon as we took it - I carried the money about me, and chucked it away; there was no other woman in the room; but there was when I went back with my mother to the house; I would not have done any thing in this business for the world, if I had thought it was wrong - I forget how often I have been taken up; I was kept to hard labour in the House of Correction - it is about six months ago; I was there three months - I am now twelve years old; I was sent there because I went to a chandler's shop to buy some liquorice, and the man swore I went to steal his till - I was never committed for stealing a watch; Mr. Cobley accused me of stealing one; there were two other boys with me - we were not taken up.
 COURT. Q. Did Mr. Cobley get his watch? A. Yes, and then he said we did not steal it.
 Windred. He took the watch himself, put it into his sidepocket, sold it himself, and gave me 4s. Witness. I could not take it - two boys came and called me down from my work; I then met him, and he told me of it.
