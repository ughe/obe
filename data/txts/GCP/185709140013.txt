RICHARD A. BROOMAN. I am a patent agent. I assisted Mr. Bauwen to
make out a specification of his patent, particularly the one of the 10th April
1854—the still was worked differently, but the other apparatus are the
same as I described in that specification. I have examined this modelin
April, 1854, there was the same kind of division between the fire and the
pipes as now, but I cannot say what it consisted of-I visited the premises
in the latter part of 1856, and at the commencement of this year, and the
apparatus was precisely the same as regards the furnace, but the still was
quite different there was no alteration whatever in the mode of conveying
the heat through the pipeg- the action of the fire ont wrought iron pipes
would be much greater than on cast iron-it would wear them out, and if
they got red hot they would get leaky in the joints, I imagine, but I never
tried, and therefore cannot speak from actual experience-if they were to
be red hot, cast iron would be the best material--this pipo (produced) is
one-eighth of an inch thick-it is an ordinary gas tube--if the object of
Mr. Bauwen was to keep the steam below the temperature of the boiling fat,
and to keep the pipe from getting red hot, this pipe would be decidedly
adapted to that purpose I have seen the apparatus at work-it seemed to
work satisfactorily.
