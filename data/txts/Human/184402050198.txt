   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Does it appear by this document that this was from the luggage train? A.  No, it appears merely as excess fares—this is the paper given to me by the prisoner on the 29th, and at that time he paid over 4 s .—in the book here is a sum of 4 s . received for one man from Chelmsford, by the goods train, on the 29th, he having no ticket. WILLIAM STEVENSON M'INTYRE. I am inspector of the Eastern Counties Railway. It is the duty of the inspector to receive excess fares in case of the sickness of the porter or the policeman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know any thing of a man coming by the goods train from Chelmsford, on the morning of the 29th of Dec.? A.  No. NOT GUILTY.
 768. JOHN CANNON was again  indicted for embezzlement.
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. JAMES PARR. I was acting as porter at the Railway on the 1st of Jan., when the five o'clock train came to town. I received 4 s . for excess fares from Mr. Salter's guard from Chelmsford—I either gave it the prisoner or laid it on the desk. NOT GUILTY.
