   WILLIAM HOLLINSHED. I am a cabinet-maker, in King-street, Seven-dials. On the 28th of July we had been pulling down part of our premises; this lead was packed up, and laid in a stable - I had seen it safe the day before, and remarked that somebody had been moving it; this piece of lead fits the place it came from exactly.
   GEORGE MOGFORD. I am servant to Mr. Hollinshed. There was lead in the stable - I met the prisoner that morning with a bundle, and watched him into an old ironshop; I went and told the prosecutor; we went to the house, and asked what he had brought; the man said it was lead - I did not know what it was; I thought it was a bundle of tiles - it was wrapped up in a bundle; when I saw him first he was not above ten yards from the stable; he was in the back yard, with the bundle on his shoulder; it was about a quarter before nine o'clock in the morning - I found the bag that the lead had been in at the old ironshop, but the lead was out, and the bag laid upon it - it was about a quarter of an hour after I saw the prisoner that I saw the lead again at the shop; he is a bricklayer's labourer, and had been at work there some time.
   DANIEL DURALL. I live at the iron-shop. The prisoner came and asked if we bought lead; I said we did not; I said, "Very likely you have not got it in an honest manner;" he said, "Will you let it be here a few minutes;" I made him no answer; but he pushed me aside, put it down, and in a few minutes after the prosecutor came in - he had the same lead.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw some rubbish and some shavings; I kicked the shavings aside, and took up this lead; I took it to the shop, and was coming back to tell the boy if any body came who owned the lead, I would give it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 1748.  GEORGE WINGROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of June , 2 hearth-rugs, value 60s., the goods of Samuel Hollingsworth.
 MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the prosecution.
   THOMAS BEESTON. I have an hearth-rug pawned at Mr. Jones, Broad-street, Bloomsbury, on the 1st of June, I have every reason to believe by the prisoner - he answers the description of the person.
   JOSEPH LYALL. I am shopman to Mr. Aldridge, pawnbroker. I have an hearth-rug pawned for 9s., on the 1st of June, to the best of my recollection, by the prisoner.
   GEORGE HEDWORTH. I am a constable. I took the prisoner at Petersham, near Kingston, on the 2nd of September.
   ANN HEATH. The prisoner and his wife lodged near me in Paine-street, White Conduit-fields; I do not recollect when I saw him last, but his wife came to me one day, the officer came the same day, and I saw the prisoner come from his own house, and go to the bottom of the street; it was some time in August, the day we appeared at Hatton-garden.
   SAMUEL HOLLINGSWORTH. The prisoner lived as a porter with me, at No. 6, Bedford-street, Bedford-row- he left me about the end of April; his wife was taken on the 30th of July - these two rugs are mine, and were part of my stock; I never sold any thing to him.
 Prisoner. Q. Were there not other persons in your employ about my height, who used to frequent your shop when you were out? A. I never heard of any person coming to my shop in my absence, except you and your wife.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am entirely innocent of taking the rugs, nor did I ever pawn them. (See the case of  Thomas Hollingsworth, page 826.)
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Transported for Seven Years.
 1744.  RICHARD HARMER was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 1 chair and stand, value 12s, the goods of William Redfarn.
   WILLIAM REDFARN. I am a chair-maker, and live in Bethnal-green. On the 9th of September I missed a child's chair, which was tied to a stove outside my door - it was safe about eight o'clock in the morning; I went in search of it, and found it in Wilson-street, Moorfields, at a broker's shop.
   GEORGE MATTHEW MILLER. I keep a broker's shop in Wilson-street. On the 9th of September, about ten o'clock in the morning, I bought this chair of the priprisoner, for 10s.
   JOSEPH WALTON. I am an officer. I was sent for, and took the prisoner.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming down Shoreditch on that morning - a young man came and told me to go and ask 11s. for it, and they would not give it - then he told me to go on further, and I went to this house - he gave me 10s. for it - we changed half a sovereign and had half a pint of beer; I told them to go there, but I do not know whether they went or not.
   GEORGE MATTHEW MILLER. I offered him 10s. - he went home with me, I gave him half a sovereign - he said he understood it was half a guinea - he went away, and came again and said it was a great sacrifice, but he would let me have it.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Judgment Respited.
 1745.  ELEANOR FITZGERALD was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 1 half sieve, value 6d., and one peck of pears, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Samuel Ellis.
   SAMUEL ELLIS. I am a salesman in Covent-garden. On the 10th of September I lost a half sieve and about a peck of pears in it - I knew the prisoner, and found the pears on her; she had just got out of the market, within twenty rods of my stand.
   WILLIAM COOPER. I saw a woman take the sieve of pears from the stand, but I cannot say it was the prisoner - we found the prisoner in about ten minutes; she is like the woman.
 Prisoner's Defence. I can safely take my oath I bought them - I gave 2s. for the pears, and 6d. for the half sieve; I did not run away with them, nor hide them- I said, "I will take you to the man;" when I got there the countryman was gone.
