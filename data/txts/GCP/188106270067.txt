638. MICHAEL DONOVAN (44), Wilful and corrupt perjury.
Mr. WAITE Prosecuted ; MR. J. P. GRAIN Defended.
ROBERT K. ARCHIBADD GREEN. I am a clerk in the Probate Office-I
produce this affidavit originally sworn on the 26th April, and re-sworn on
the 28th.
JAMES CONNOR. I live at 107, Parker Street, St. George's-in-the-East,
and am first cousin to Mary Donovan, deceased-I have known the
prisoner for two years; I saw him at the deceased's house in April last.
and I asked him if his friends knew anything about the death of Mary
Donovan-he said they were all dead-I told him I was first cousin to the
deceased he said I was nobody_I told him there was an uncle and a
sister in New York-I bade him good night and came away: I also told
him she had a brother living in Ireland I have seen the deceased's brother
- he came about eight days after she died when he was written for
Cross-examined. I am a labourer in the employ of Deane and Grant
I am no relation to the prisoner; I do not know whether he can read or
write I have nothing to do with the Probate suit the claimant has the
brother: I do not claim any part of the deceased's property I did not
assist in the burial; I believe the prisoner buried her I know he works
on board ship; I do not know that he is a relation of the deceased's the
detective and the brother and the brother's wife took me to Bow Street
to charge the prisoner with perjury-there was no solicitor with us. I do
not know Mr. Leathey-I do not know the parish priest of the neighbour
hood whero Mary Donovan died-I saw the solicitor at Bow Street: I did
not know who he was I do not know whether he is here I had not told
the prisoner of the brother before her death I said so at Bow Street
I was never in Ireland.
Dit for
JOHN HARLOW. I am a solicitor and a commissioner for taking onths,
of 39, Southampton Row-the prisoner was brought to me on the 28th
April last-he swore this affidavit; the words * died intestate and
"next of kin to the deceased " were in the affidavit when he gwore it
also the words "to the best of my knowledge, information, and belief
-I very carefully explained it to him as he was a marksman.
Cross-examined. The affidavit contained the words: «Died intestate
widow without children or parent, uncle or aunt, nephew or niece, child
or grandchild, and that I am the lawful cousin german and next of kin
of the deceased," and it ends up "to the best of my knowledge, informa-
tion, and belief."
PATRICK O'CONNOR, I live at 16, Green Arbour Court, Golden Lane,
St. Luke's, and am a labourer-I knew Mary Donovan ; she was my
niece-I last saw her alive about two months ago in Old Gravel Lane, where
she lived I was afterwards told that she died on a Tuesday night, and I
never knew anything of it till Friday evening, and I went into the house
where she was lying dead-I saw the prisoner there; he asked me who
I was—I told him I was her uncle, and that there were two brothers of
hers in Ireland; he made no answer he went down to the clergyman's
house, and I went out after him--when he came out of the clergyman's
I asked him what the clergyman said, and he asked me what I wanted of
the clergyman-I went to the clergyman, and told him I was her uncle.
and that she had two brothers in Ireland, and if there was anything the
brothers ought to have it.
