JAMES HINES( police-constable R  95.) On the day in question I went to the prisoner's house, and took possession of six pieces of sheet copper, which I produce—they weigh 105lbs.—Ingram is a plumber, and does not deal in copper. THOMAS OLDHAM. I am a labourer, and was employed in September in stripping the copper off the roof of the infirmary at Greenwich Hospi tal—I lapped  the copper up, and put it by the side of the dormer to throw down to take it to the store-room—I saw the copper found on Ingram's premises at the office—It is the same description of copper—It is something like it, that is, about the same colour, but I cannot swear it is the same.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  I suppose there are hundreds and thousands of pieces of the same colour as this? A.  Yes, there is a great deal. WILLIAM THOMAS. I am inspector of the R division of police. In con sequence of information, I went to Ingram's house, on Tuesday, the 8th of September, after the copper had been removed—the house is at the corner of a road, leading to the water side, called Bishop's-place—the shop is being fitted up for a plumber and glazier—there was a quantity of glass and things in the front shop—there is a door in Thames-street, in the usual way—I went down Bishop's-place, and found a side entrance—that is a very wide door, large enough to admit a cart—when you look at the door, at first it appears to be all one door; but on examining the side of the posts, I discovered a bit of brass, and on pressing my foot on that I found part of the door flew open, so as to admit a man—without that there did not appear a small door in the large one—on going in at that door, I found myself under a shed, with earth for the floor—I afterwards searched under the earth—I went into the workshops adjoining the shed, and removed a box or drawer which stood there with heavy tools in it, and found there was a trap-door—It is in the room adjoining the shed—the trap-door slanted down to a brick vault under the shed, woth boards at the top, and earth over the boards—there is no means of getting to that vault, but through the shoot I have described—the shoot is in the workshop behind the front shop—there was a furnace in the shop—I searched all over the premises, and found property whitch forms the subject of another indict ment—the maleprisoner gave himself into my custody on Sunday week last, at Deptford—he said he had had the advice of his solicitor, who ad vised him to surrender, and his doing so he hoped would be the means of saving his wife, as she was perfectly innocent.
   COURT to WILLIAM SIVELL . Q.  How closely did you fellow in after these people? A.  Immediately; I did not lose sight of them for half a minute—they went through the side door—I did not see them press against the brass, but that was the door they entered at—the property was found within a yard of the spot; but there is a small party-wall which concealed it from me—I went in at the door in the wall, which Mrs. Ingram put her back against. NOT GUILTY.
