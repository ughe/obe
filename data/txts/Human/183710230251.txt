RICHARD JAMES THOMPSON DAVIS. I was at the bar on that day, and heard the prisoner call for the peppermint—I looked at the shilling, and saw it was bad—my wife asked him where he took it—he said he took it at Croydon, with some other silver, he hardly knew where—after he left I followed him, and gave information to Alfred Smith to inform the police—I still followed him—I went to the station-house, and was there when he was searched—he said he had thrown the shilling away.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you follow me out of the public-house? A.  Yes—I did not keep my eye upon you long. ANN BRIDGE. I am the daughter of Mr. Bridge, a beer-seller. On the 9th of October the prisoner came to our house, between five and six o'clock, and asked for half a pint of beer—I served him—he paid me a bad shilling—I examined it at the time and found it bad, by biting it—I re fused it—he gave me 1 d . and went off.
   Prisoner.  I was never in your shop. Witness,  Yes—he had the same handkerchief and shirt as he has now on. SARAH BLAKE. I live in Tooting Graveny, my husband is a baker. The prisoner came to my shop, on the 9th of October, at ten minutes before six o'clock—he bought a penny-loaf, and put a shilling into my hand—I rang it on the counter, and laid it in the till, on a five-shilling piece, not liking the appearance of it—there were other shillings, but I laid this separate—I gave him a sixpence and 5 d .—I then saw Alfred Smith, and he said something to me—I gave him the shilling—he looked at it between the lights, and could not discern whether it was bad or not—I then bit it, and called my husband—he bent it, and pursued the prisoner.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you put it into the till? A.  Yes, I did not throw it in—I laid it on a five-shilling-pieces—I took it out when Alfred Smith came to the door. THOMAS JOHN PAGE( police-constable V.  77.) On the afternoon of the 9th of October I saw the prisoner, and Mr. Blake, and Smith, in High-street, Tooting Graveny—Mr. Blake gave me a shilling, and said the pri soner had been passing it to his wife—he gave him in charge—I searched him—he put his hand into his pocket, while I put my hand into another, he took something out and put into his mouth—I caught him by the throat, and held him between five and ten minutes, to prevent his swal lowing it—Smith put his fingers into his mouth, and said he could feel something in the shape of a coin, but it was not got out—I then found he had something in his hand—I forced it out, and it was good money—I found on him one shilling and seven good sixpences, and 2 d . in copper, and this box of composition.
