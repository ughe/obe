• What are they for, Mr. Calcutt?" He said, "Just a little Christ-
mas present." I said, "I don't want them," and he said that he
would be very pleased if I would accept them. I said I would much
rather not, as I really didn't want them. However, he pressed me,
and I said, “ Very well, Mr. Calcutt, I won't be rude to you; I will
accept them and I will give them to one of my boys." Nothing was
said with reference to my action or the Board. In January, 1905, he gave
me an umbrella at Mile End; he left it at my Mile End Road house.
Some days after that he came in one evening and said, "I left you a
little Christmas present, Mr. Hirst," and I said, "Whyt I don't
want an umbrella, I have a dozen of them now." And so I had.
Then he gave me a tie pin at Christmas, 1906. That was brought
down to the "Eagle" at about 11 o'clock. I should think it was worth
about 30s. I was not told that it belonged to the late Lord Angle-
sey. I called on Calcutt and told him I thought he was very silly to
give me these Christmas presents, and he said, "If you will accept
it. I shall be very pleased," so I accepted it and put it in my coat,
where it remained until I put it in a safe at my private house, where
It remained until the time of the Inquiry. At the time I was arrested
I did not know where the pin was, but one Sunday afternoon I
found it and took it to the solicitor, who told me to hand it over to
Mr. Kane. Then at Christmas, 1906, he sent me a travelling rug
to the "Eagle." I never saw that until I saw it in Court. Calcutt
never gave me a gold penknife. I thought he gave me these
Christmas presents on account of the work he was doing for me; I
never thought they had any connection with my action on the
Board. With reference to the accounts for the work that he did
for me, the repairs amount to £476 4s, 5d. Apart from that he
built a house on Lord Tredegar's estate in Harley Street for £478 88.
The next account is one of £43 in 1904 for making good some
damage done by fire; then in December, 1907, there is an account of
£1 15s. for work done at Bancroft Road, and in August, 1907, £56
for work done partly at the "Freemasons' Arms," and partly at
Hertford Street. The amount of work done for me altogether by
Calcutt was £1,055 7s. 5d., which has all been paid for. I never
knew he was doing work for me at exceptionally low prices. I sent
him to do work wherever I wanted a job done; he did it, and sent
in the account, and I never once disputed it with him. With regard
to the work done at Bancroft Road, I never had a bill for it, but
only & receipt when I paid it. At that time he owed me some money
in respect of the Herne Bay houses, and I wrote £30 off that. It is
true that Calcutt has bought a substantial number of turkeys frame
me, and he has also bought wines and spirits at my house. At the

gave 3 considerable order to one of my managers. I said to the
