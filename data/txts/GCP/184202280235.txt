1117. JOSEPH CHILD and THOMAS ROACH were indicted for
stealing, on the 20th of February, 15lbs. weight of copper, value 78.; 5
copper bolts, value 58.; 2 staples, value 18.; 16lbs, weight of iron, value
Is; and 1 iron wedge, value 6d.; the goods of Thomas Beech and
another, in a certain vessel on the navigable river Thames.
HENRY PEACHY. I am foreman to Thomas Beech and Son. We had
1 ship being broken up at Mr. Sedger Wade's, at Rotherhithe -- there was
some copper and bolts there, about the 20th of February--we have lost
such things as these produced - I believe these to be the property of Beech
and Son.
HENRY HARDING MIDDLETON (police-constable M 163.) About nine
o'clock on Monday morning, the 20th of February, I went to Sedger Wade's
- I saw the two prisoners putting property from a tug-boat into their boat
-I went into their boat-Roach pusbed me back into the other boat, and
got away-Child and I were shoved into the river-Child said he would
go quietly if I would allow him to take the oars, which I did.
Child. He took them out of the tug-boat, and swore he took them out
of my boat. Witness. I threw them on the tug-boat first, because I could
not get up the wharf-I picked up this wedge on the wharf-these three
pieces were all I found in their boat, and these others
Court. Q. How far from Sedger Wade's was their boat? A. Fifteen
or sixteen feet-to get property on their boat it was necessary to get first
on the lag-boat.
GEORGE THOMAS MYERS. I found this copper in the bottom of the
boat-I know this piece is Mr. Beech's.
Child. You did not know it at the police court. Witness. I swore
to this one at the police court, and to this iron bolt also.
WILLIAM NOAKES (police-constable M 104.) I went in search of Roach,
and found him secreted in a hen-coop on the adjoining premises.
NOT GUILTY.
