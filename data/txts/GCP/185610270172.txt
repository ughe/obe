dicted for unlawfully having counterfeit coin in their possession, to which

1066. ANN SULLIVAN was indicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit
coin.
MR. W. J. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
EMILY ADDYLEZAR SAUNDERS. I am barmaid at a tavern in Union-
street, Southwark. On 15th Sept. the prisoner came there about half past
3 o'clock for a pint of porter-she gave me a shilling-I kept it in my hand
bent it, and said it was bad-she said, "I got it from a lady at Blackheath
- she wanted it back, but I would not give it to her she went away, and
did not pay for the beer-I laid the shilling on a back shelf, under a wide
glass--I afterwards gave it to David Morris.
Prisoner. 6. Did you not give it to a lady at the bottom of the counter
A. No, I never moved—I did not lose sight of it.
Prisoner. It was a King William shilling; she gave me the change
before she said it was bad.
HENRY LEESON. I am barman at the Henry the Eighth, in Union-street.
On 15th Sept. the prisoner came about a quarter before 4 o'clock-she asked
for a pint of porter-she paid me a bad shilling-I told her it was bad, and
bent it on the counter-she said she did not know it was bad, it was all
the money she had about her, and she got it in Blackfriars-road-I gave her
into custody, and gave the shilling to Morris.
UR
MARY ANN MORRIS. I am the widow of David Morris (policeman, I
.49)—he died 15th Oct.--this is his writing to this deposition.
HENRY LEESON re-examined. I heard Morris examined before the
Magistrate-his deposition was taken in the room, and read before the
Magistrate-he said that it was true, in the prisoner's presence, and she
was asked if she had any question to put to him-she said, "No."
The deposition was here read as follows : “David Morris saith, I am a
police constable, M 49—I took the prisoner into custody at the Henry the
Eighth, in Union-street-I received this counterfeit shilling at the time-I
also received from the first witness & counterfeit shilling which I also
produce."
MARY ANN MORRIS re-examined. My husband brought home two
shillings wrapped in paper, and gave them to me to take care of--after
his death I gave them to his brother to take to the station.
ROBERT MORRIS. Mary Ann Morris gave me two shillings in a paper-I
took them to the police station, and gave them to sergeant Davis.
WILLIAM Davis (police sergeant, M 15). I received these two shillings
from the last witness.
HENRY LEESON re-examined. This is the shilling I took from the prisoner
-I marked it at the station, and know it again.
EMILY ADDYLEZAR SAUNDERS re-examined. This is the shilling I took of
her-I marked it at the station.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint--these
are both bad.
