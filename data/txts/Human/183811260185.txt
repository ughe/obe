ELIZABETH WELLS. I am sister of last witness. I went up stairs to Mrs. Campbell, when I found out the disagreeable taste in the coffee, and told her of it, but she had drank of it then—she only had about two table spoonfuls—she was in a delicate state of health before. JAMES CAMPBELL. I keep the Crown. On the morning of the 16th I was with my wife when she took the coffee—it made her ill, and I had the prisoner taken into custody—he said he had given it out of a lark . HENRY HADLOW. I am a surgeon in Jury-street, Aldgate. I was called in to attend Mrs. Campbell, on the morning of the 16th of Novem ber—I saw the coffee, and there was cantharides in it sufficient to produce an injurious effect to any one taking it—Mrs. Campbell was very seriously affected by it for twenty hours, and is an invalid still.
   Cross-examined. Q.  She was an invalid before? A.  She was—I could not tell what quantity there was-twopenny worth mixed in half-a-pint of coffee would produce most serious consequences, if all taken by one person-Mary Ann Wells and Mrs. Campbell were both ill, and it was necessary to apply powerful remedies—Mrs. Campbell labours under a stomachic disease, not connected at all with the occasion of my being called in to attend her in this case, but it was aggravated by this—she is now under my care.
