   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Have you known Aaron Bray some time? A.  Yes—he has known me, and has had oppurtunities of seeing the way in which I have conducted myself through life. JOHN WRIGHT. I am a grocer, and live at the corner of Rath bone-place, Oxford-street. I have been in the habit, before and sinse the 8th of June, of observing the conduct of the omnibuses coming up and down Oxford-street—before Aaron Bray commenced, the omnibuses were perfectly quiet—they would stop at my door and put down, and do every thing quiet—after Bray came, there has been a con stant confusion—If an omnibus belonging to one of the proprietors drew up, Bray would drive up very fast behind them, or come in front of them—If an omnibus were to stop, Bray would stop—he would always wait for the other—there appeared no object for him to stop, but he always did stop—when the others have stopped to take up passengers, or set them down, Bray has always stopped, whether he had any one to take up or not—I have observed this conduct for four or five months—I am not interested for either of the proprietors—I have come forward without being subpœnaed. EDWARD MOORE. I am an undertaker, and live in Rathbone-place, close to Oxford-street, the door is at the corner—I have had my attention called to the omnibuses, going from Paddington to the Bank, up and down Oxford-street, and I have observed the conduct of Mr. Aaron Bray's—the omnibuses were going regularly before he came—after he came, when the omnibuses drew up, Mr. Aaron Bray's drew across them, and prevented the passengers going in them—that has been, if not from the first, very soon after—I observed it in June—I have observed this conduct of Bray's more than twenty times—If Bray has been on the right side, he has tried to come in between them and the pavement—at other times he has gone and drawn across the crossing, to prevent the passengers getting in—I have no interest whatever in the issue of this inquiry—I came without any subpœna.
