FREDERICK WABCOTT. I live at Leytonstone-I am agent for the
County Fire Office-I know the prisonerat Michaelmas, 1880, ho pro-
posed to insure his furniture from what he told me I filled up this
proposal form-he was insured for 1001.- the renewal premium was
paid on 7th Ootober last by his wife-she brought the money to my
house.
JAMES GODWIN. I am a brickmaker and builder at Leyton LT
have known the prisoner several years and have supplied him with
building materials-some few months back he owed me 621.- he paid me
a portion by a three months' bill, leaving a balance of 277. 148. 6d..he
gave me a second bill for that, which became due on 4th January last,
and was dishonoured—I had a writ out against him-I met him on the
Friday before, 13th January—he said the writ becoming due on the
following day what could he do-I advised him to get the money and
discharge it-he said he would see Mr. Johnson, a gentleman he was
connected with in financial matters, and get him to settle it- he asked
what would be the consequence of its not being attended to–I told him
that judgment would be obtained and his goods would be seized.
FREDERICK TAYLOR. I am foreman to Mr. Henson, & plumber and
glazier at Leytonstone-about five weeks ago the prisoner called at the
shop about some money that he owed Mr. Henson-he said he was going
to town, and he expected some money on the job he was doing, and if he
could get it we should have 101.- the amount due was about 202.he
called again and said he did not get it, but he expected it the next day,
Saturday-I have not seen him since.
GUILTY.—Ten Years' Penal Servitude.

269. JOHN ALDOUS (24), ALFRED NOTTAGE (28), JAMES
BESCHINO (50), and JAMES NORMAN, Stealing five sacks of
forage, of Edwin Fardell and another.
MR. MORTEN Prosecuted; Mr. GEOHEGAN appeared for Aldous and Nottage,
MR. WILLES for Beschino, and MR. M. WILLIAMS for Norman.
SAMUEL KING. I am yard foreman to Messrs. Fardell on 11th January
I superintended the packing of Aldous's van, and gave him an account
of what was put in it, 64 sacks of forage, which he had to take to the
Victoria Docks-8 man named West assisted him-I saw him start-I
can identify five of the bags. (At the suggestion of MR. GEOHEGAN Aldone
and Nottage here stated that they were GUILTY.) The forage was composed
of bruised oats, cut hay, and cut clover-I had cut the hay myself thero
was no straw, beans, or peas--this is the same forage.

of bran-he left about 8 o'clock.
