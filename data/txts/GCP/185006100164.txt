1210. WILLIAM GROVES, burglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of James Linfield, with intent to steal.
Mr. Payne conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH SARAH LINFIELD. I am the wife of James Linfield, a grocer
and broom-dealer, at 54, Tiverton-street, Newington. On Wednesday morning,
22nd May, I was awoke, about a quarter or twenty minutes past three o'clock,
by the noise of the shop shutters breaking- I looked out of the bedroom
window and saw a bat go in at the shop window-I got a light, and in about
five minutes went down-stairs into the shop-I found the window and shutters
open, which I had left safe about eleven over night, and saw the prisoner
lying down behind the counter-I said, “Man, what do you do here?"-he
made no answer, and never moved-he was very tipsy indeed- I called my
husband, who got a policeman, and gave him into custody.
Cross-examined by MR. BIRNIE. Q. Did you miss anything? A. No;
I had 30s. in the till--not a thing was disturbed - I put my foot against
him when I asked what he did there he was led to the station by my
husband and a policeman-he seemed to have no legs to walk upon-1 had
never seen him before--the shutters were very old and rotten, the least touch
might have broken them.
THOMAS GRANVILLE (policeman, M 251). I was called, and received the
prisoner in charge-he was very tipsy-I could make nothing of him-he
was not able to walk very well.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Nor GUILTY.
