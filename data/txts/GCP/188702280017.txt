295. FREDERICK ADAMS (26), Feloniously forging and uttering
an order for the payment of 21. 58. with intent to defraud.
MR. CRANSTOUN Prosecuted.
CHARLES BURTON. I am a carpenter—the prisoner lodged at my house
-on the 18th December he asked me to cash this note for him. (A sailor's
advance note for 21. 58., dated December 18th, 1886, and signed "J. Sampson,"
master of the Grantully Castle.) I advanced 11. 58. 6d., and he owed me
198. 6d. for rent-I saw him write "F. Adams” on the back-I asked
him whether it was a right note, and he said “Yes."
MATTHEW BURTON. I am the son of the last witness -I got this note
from my father to get cashed -I took it to Messrs. Donald Currie's office
on 7th February-I was unable to get it cashed.
WILLIAM MOLELLAN. I am a purser for Messrs. Donald Currie and
Co., of Fenchurch Street, agents for the Grantully Castle—there is no
master named “J. Sampson” in our service--the master of the Grantully
Castle is Captain Young-this note is not genuine-I know nothing of it
-I do not know the prisoner or his writing.
JEREMIAU MCARTHY (Policeman 448 G). I took the prisoner into
custody on 12th February-I charged him with obtaining money under
false pretences-Mrs. Burton was present-he said, "All right, Mrs.
Burton, I intended to pay you this afternoon."
The Prisoner in his defence said that he went to the East India Dock to ship,
and saw a man he believed was the mate of the Grantully Castle, who advanced
him the note, to get clothes ; his friends objected to his going to sea and leaving
his mother; he had given Mrs. Burton a sovereign on the Saturday himself,
and all the money had been repaid.

GUILTY. Strongly recommonded to mercy.--Two Months' Hard Labour.
