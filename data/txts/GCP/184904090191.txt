SARAN KENFELL. I am the wife of William Kenfell, and live in High-
street, Poplar. On 8th March, Jane Elliott came to my house---she said,
"Here are some things which belonged to my father ; be is dead, and they
are of no use to my brother, because they are old-fashioned" - I bought these
trowsers, and shirt, and waistcoat of her, for half-a-crown-when the officer
came I gave them up to him-these trowsers are very old-fashioned; be
owns himself that they were made three years before King William died-
this shirt I valued at 6d.-this waistcoat has a piece put in the back of it-
she had the coat with her, but she told me she was not going to sell it, but
to take it on board ship to her brother.
WILLIAM LOVELL. I am a dealer. Jane Elliott came to me, and offered
to sell the coat for 1s. 60.--I said I would not buy it of her at all-she
then said her mother would come, and she did come, but I would not
have anything to do with it - the mother said her husband gave it her to do
what they could with it-it is a uniform coat, with the buttons cut off-I
would not buy it.
JOHN WHITE (policeman, R 180). I took the prisoners, and told them
they were charged with stealing a jacket and other articles from M'Sweeney
--they said they knew nothing about it-I asked the daughter where she
had put the jacket that she offered for sale to the marine-store dealershe
said she gave it to the man she had it from the mother said, “You had
better tell the truth"-she then said it was up-stairs- I found it, and found
the buttons that came off the coat-I took the daughter to the station-she
then told me the other clothes were at Poplar the mother's husband is a
sailor, and is at sea-she has two or three other small children.
Isabella Elliott's Defence. I came home, and my daughter told me she
had the coat given ber; she said she had sold the other things.
ISABELLA ELLIOTT-Not GUILTY.

· 1039. WILLIAM LEWIS and FREDERICK ALLEN, stealing 1 pair
of trowsers, value 68. 9d. ; the goods of James Francis ; Allen having been
before convicted.
Joun WHITE ( policeman, R 180). On 7th March I was in the Broadway,
at Deptford, between three and four o'clock-I saw the two prisoners in
company with another boy, who is not in custody- I watched them for about
half an hour, over to Church-street, Greenwich, to Mr. Francis's shop,
saw the prisoners loiter about there for half an hour-I then saw Allen take
a pair of trowsers, and give them to Lewis - I took the prisoners-Lewis
threw the trowsers down, and said he had not got them.
John CARPENTER (police-sergeant, R 38). I was with White-I saw the
prisoners- I watched them some time, but finding they knew me I got out
of the way, got to the back of the shop, and ran round-I saw Lewis bringing
this bundle under his arm-he turned back, ran away, and threw it into the
gutter; it was this pair of trowsers, with a ticket of 68. 9d. on them; they
were inside the shop.
