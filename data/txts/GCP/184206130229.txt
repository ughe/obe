ROBERT REEVE. I am waiter at the Prince of Wales, at Woolwich I
saw the prisoner and prosecutor-the prosecutor wanted to stay at my
house till nine o'clock--the prisoner told him he should go then to the
barracks-he dragged him to the bar-be made a disturbance-1 put him
out, and told him to go to the barracks, if the man was a soldier.
Prisoner. Q. Did you hear him refuse to come to the barracks with
met 4. Yes I told you to go and get the policeman.
CHARLES HIERONS (police-constable R 228.) I saw the prosecutor
and the prisoner on Sunday night, about a quarter to nine o'clock, near
the rope-yard rails, in a road which leads to a wood, or to the barracks--
the prosecutor was following the prisoner, and trying to get something
from him, but I did not notice it on the next day he went to the
barracks, and the commanding officer sent the prisoner to the station.
Prisoner. I am in the 73rd Regiment; it is my duty to enlist any
man-I never received the half-crown or shilling of him; he refused going
to the barracks with me; there was an old man with me, but I did not
say, he was my father; he gave me a drop of beer; I took it, and
said, "Good health, fatber;" the prosecutor said he could not go till mine
o'clock the next day. I said if I waited for him I should get put into the
guard-house; that was what I said.
GUILTY. Aged 23.-Judgment Respited.
