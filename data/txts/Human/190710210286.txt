I did not write a circular and cannot say whether what was true in 1904 was also true in 1905. I altered the date, but it does not follow that it was ever used. The body of the circular shows that the current price and the quotation it altered. The date being altered, the other alterations would be consequential. As to whether it was an honest and truthful thing to say in either circular "as we write we have what we believe to be special and important information regarding Brighton 'A' stock," the first thing you have to do is to ask the person who wrote it, and secondly, you have got to find out whether it was used the year afterwards, and finally to ask the person who used it whether it was right or wrong. Supposing it was sent it might be true. How can you say it would be a pure fluke if it were? I do not know how Brighton "A" was in 1904, nor in 1905 for that matter. I will go so far as to say that all my alterations have not been accepted. I do not see anything in it likely to be dishonest at all. There is no reason why I should not have special and important information in 1905, but it does not affect me because I did not write the circular. With reference to Chalmers and Cox's circular of De cember 12, 1903, calling attention to the position of Louisville Rail-road shares, it may be a singular coincidence that exactly the same ad vice should be given in the following year with regard to Union Pacifics, but very likely the position was the same. I appear from the circular to have struck out Union Pacifics and put in Louisvilles, and to have made the necessary consequential alterations. A similar circular has been altered to make the observations originally applied to Louisvilles apply to Brighton "A" stock, but you cannot go so far as to say that if that were printed anew it would appear to be a cir cular advising investment in Brighton "A" stock. You do not know what may happen before it is printed. The alterations here are to show the difference in the dividend between Brighton "A" and Louis villes. I have not altered the circular except by altering the name of the railway and the price of the stock. As to whether it strikes me as a singular coincidence that observations originally written about an American railway should be exactly applicable to an English railway you are assuming that it is used in that form. I do not as sume that at all. The only alteration I have made is to give the proper price and proper dividend of Brighton "A." I was probably asked to do it by Hawkes. He wanted to know what the dividend was in Brighton "A" and the price of the stock, and I have put it on here. There is no alteration whatever in the body of the circular. I do not countenance or discountenance what is in the body of the circular. I have been asked to give the quotation of Brighton "A" in the latter year, and I have put it there.
