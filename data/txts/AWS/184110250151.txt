NAOMI SIMPSON. I am the wife of Theophilus Simpson, a carpenter;
I live next door to Mr. King. I heard Mrs. Hedges speak to the prisoner
on the Tuesday, and I spoke to him-I am quite sure the prisoner is the
man-I was sitting with my front door openI came to the door, and
said, 44 Mr. King is at home' -the prisoner said, 44 Poor man, let him have
his nap out, I will come again in half-an-hour"- sat with my door open,
and he did not come-on the Thursday night I saw him again, about nine
heard the clock strike-I had been at home twenty-five minutes
or half-an-hour when Mrs. Hedges knocked at my doorI brought the
candle, and I saw the prisonerI held the candle in his face-he came out
of Mr. King's door-I saw him come out, and he said 44 Good night"-]
swear he is the same person I saw on the Tuesday-he passed so close to
me on Thursday night that I could have touched him, and I knew then
that he was the man I saw on Tuesday-he went up the street to Mr.
Matthews's, and then turned round to see whether we proceeded any fur-
ther-we were then knocking at the door, and the prisoner went away
the door easily opened, and the things were tied up as has been described.
Prisoner. Q. Where were you standing when the person you say was
