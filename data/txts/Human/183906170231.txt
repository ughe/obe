Q.  You say Mrs. Abrahams said the gold was at the house of a man named Jessop—did she say Jessop kept his bed all day there? A.  She did—I do not know whether that was was to take care of the gold—she merely stated to me, that it was singular that he kept his bed all that day.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHOS . Q.  Now when you went to this house in Vinegar-yard, you found Mr. Moses and his daughter, Mrs. Abrahams, there? A.  I do not know whether she was there—I do not recollect having seen her there—she was alone at the house when I had this conversation with her—I did not know her at all before this—she entered into this conversation with me of her own accord, and told me all I have stated—we got into conversation, and that was How it occurred—this was long before I went as a witness to Lambeth-street—I have no recol lection when I was first examined at Lambeth-street—I was only ex amined there once, and my deposition was taken before the Magistrate in a private room—I was examined publicly and privately—I was not examined publicly twice.
   Q.  Who examined you privately? A.  I went into a private room, and my deposition was read over to me, I suppose by the clerk—there were some gentlemen there—I do not know who they were—I suppose they were the Magistrates—I saw them afterwards sitting as Magistrates—that was the way in which I was examined—a written paper was pro duced and read over to me as my statement, and I signed it—I did not swear to it there, it was taken into the office afterwards, and I swore to it there on the same day—I do not know whether it was drawn up by Mr. Abrahams—Mr. Abrahams took my statement as to facts, and that was the statement that was read to me—I signed it at the private ex amination, not before—I am sure, to the best of my recollection, that I was not examined a second time at the office.
   Q.  Did you, in the first statement you made in the private room, in clude every thing you have said to-day? A.  No, I did not—I have no recollection of the date on which I was examined—I have now a recol lection that I was examined a second time publicly, respecting my having gone to Money  Moses's on the Monday evening—I had not said any thing about that at the private examination—I had mentioned about my going to his house before, but not about my going on Monday evening the last time—I did not mention before my second examination about Mrs. Abrahams saying she must have the bars, and nothing but the bars—that had taken place before I was examined at all—it was while I was in the Strand, before I had absconded—I did not say any thing about baring seen Mrs. Abrahams on Monday evening, before my second examination—I do not think my second examination was so long as twelve or thirteen days after the first—I do not remember How long it was—it was not the next day—I do not know whether it was within a week—I have no re collection at all—whether one or two Sabbaths passed in the mean time I do not know—I might have seen Mr. Abrahams two or three times in the mean time—I did not see him every day—I was not doing any thing while living in the Haymarket—I lived there about three weeks—I took money of my own there with me—I am in my father's employment—my brother and Dean had money likewise—I had to pay various expenses—I never inquired what they had—I paid my share—I have no recollection that I paid all—it was all paid—I did not pay it—it was sent to the parties—I do not know who paid it—I did not pay any—I did not pay my share—I
