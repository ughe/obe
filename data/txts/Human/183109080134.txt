 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you know the prisoner before? A. No; the officers were standing in a line, about eight yards apart - the pressure was so great that the gentleman could not hear me; he was carried away with the crowd - I was struck the very moment I caught hold of the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. Are you able to say whether the prisoner himself struck you? A. He struck me several times, but not at that moment - he and I had a fight up as far as St. Martin's church.
   JAMES STYLES. I was on duty, and saw Lancaster engaged with the prisoner and another; I went up, and Lancaster said, "Secure that hand, he has got some money in it" - I seized the prisoner's hand, which was then up, and found 8s. in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far were you off? A. Not two yards; I did not see the person who lost the purse, the confusion was so great, and we were so ill-used.
 Prisoner's Defence. The property belonged to me - I believe I have people here to prove it; there was 8s. 6d. in the purse, and I took it out to lend a young man sixpence- the Policeman took hold of it; I said, "You shall not have it;" he said he would - I said, "Mind, there is 8s. 6d."
   CAROLINE DAVIS. My husband is a broker. I have known the prisoner about twenty years; on the day the bridge was opened, I had seen him from about twelve o'clock to between six and half-past six - he then went out, and did not return; I know he had a dark red purse, with some silver, as he had been taking shillings and sixpences for allowing people to see the sight on the water.
 COURT. Q. You do not live with him? A. No; my husband is a furniture-broker - I went to Arundel-street, where the prisoner lives, to see the sight; the prisoner has been employed by my husband these three months, to carry furniture; I saw the purse in his hand that evening, and saw him put money into it - I have seen the purse before, but not to notice it; it is a silk purse.
   CHARLOTTE READ. My husband was an outrider, and lives at Hampstead. On the 1st of August I was in Arundel-street, at a house which the prisoner's mother has the care of; I saw the prisoner receive several shillings from persons who came to see the procession - I did not see any purse.

 1837.  MARY CROSS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , 1 sovereign, the monies of John Hood , from his person.
 JOHN HOOD. I am a  seaman; I came home in the Cambridge. I put into the hands of my landlady three sovereigns and a half, on the 24th of August; next day I went there, and called for a glass of liquor, asked for the money, and gave them the card they had given me; they gave me the three sovereigns; I said, "Change the halfsovereign, and take for the liquor;" the prisoner came in, put her hand into my hand, and took out a sovereign - I turned round, and said, "Give me that sovereign;" she put her hand to her mouth, and said, "I have not your sovereign, I have not seen it;" she said she wanted a pint of beer - the landlady took her backwards, and overhauled her; but the sovereign has never been found - I had never spoken to her before; I do not know that I had seen her.
   MARY ELIZABETH PERKINS. My father keeps the White-hart public-house, Shadwell. The prosecutor left three sovereigns and a half with us; he came and had the three sovereigns, and wanted change for the halfsovereign; I saw the prisoner take a sovereign out of his hand - he mentioned it directly, and she was searched, but it has never been found.
   JOHN MITCHELL. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner, but found nothing on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to get a pint of beer; there were several young women there, and one of them, whom I knew, asked me to have a glass of liquor, which I did; I was coming out, and the prosecutor said I had taken his sovereign - I had never seen it.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1838.  JAMES CARTWRIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 5 umbrellas, value 30s., the goods of George Mason Isaacs.
 GEORGE MASON ISAACS. I live at  Goodman's-style, and am an umbrellas-maker. I lost these umbrellas on the 29th of August, from the window.
   ESTHER ISAACS. I am the prosecutor's sister. The prisoner came to the house, and I opened the door - he rushed in before I could get the door open; he went to the window, and took the umbrellas - I hanged the door too, and set my back against it to prevent his getting out of the shop - I had no one in the house, but I called out Uncle! thinking that would frighten the prisoner - I saw the Policeman go by in about five minutes; I called him in, and gave charge of him.
   THOMAS DAULBY. I am an officer. I was called in about twelve o'clock - I found the prisoner with his back against the counter, and five umbrellas lying on his left side, which Isaacs charged him with stealing from the window - I asked him his reason for doing so; he said that would be known by-and-by; he supposed they sold them; I said Yes, but they were not to be stolen.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I shall leave it to the mercy of the Court.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Transported for Seven Years.
