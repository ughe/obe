vember, I met Sturges in Stephen-street-in consequence of what he said,
I went down the street, returned, and saw the prisoner standing with a
basket in his hand, a coat on his arm, and a hat in his hand-I asked what
he had there-he said it was nothing to me, and, while I was examining
the basket, he ran away up James-street, and was stopped by Smith on

109. MATTHEW MCCULLOUGH was again indicted for stealing, on
the 24th of November, 2 pairs of shoes, value 58., the goods of Charles Smith,
CHARLES SMITH. I am a shoemaker, and live in Chapel-street, Lisson-
grove. On the 24th of November, between four and five o'clock in the
afternoon, I missed two pairs of shoes I did not see the prisoner.-1940
AMBROSE CHANNER (police-constable S 137.) I stopped the prisoner
in Stephen-street-- he was standing still, not runninghe had a basket, a
coat on his arm, and a hat in his hand while I was examining the basket the
coat fell on the pavementberan away-I ran after him he was secured
-I found in the basket two hats and two pairs of shoes he was about
five minutes' walk from the prosecutor's he said they were given to him to
mind by a person I saw nobody who could have given them to him
saw nobody in shirt sleeves.
HENRY STURGESS. I saw a man walk across the road in his shirt
sleeves, and a pipe in his mouth the man who stole the hats had not a
pipe.
WILLIAM SMITH. I took the prisoner in James's-street-he had been
running away from the policeman in Stafford-street.

-he then threw down the coat, and ran away- I took the basket-he said
