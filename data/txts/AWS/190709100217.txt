FRANK WILLIAX Brown, Fouy Farm, Folly Lane, Chingford I
bad a bay mare at grass on July 11 in a field opposite my
house On the morning of the 12th, at 4.30, the mare was not there.
It was daylight then. I hunted all round the field, and not finding
it reported it missing A chestaut gelding belonging to Mr Richard
Keen Was also missing On August 3 I went to Dorking, where I
SAW the chestnut gelding in Luek's van. Luek himself was driving
the horse. I waited about two hours to see if I could see the other
one. I afterwards spoke to Luck as he WaS coming ont of A puolie-
house and asked him where the mare Wa8. I said to him, You
bad two horses down here on July 121" He said, Yes." I said,
"I have seen one of the horses in you van this afternoon. Can you
tell me where the other one is 1" He wanted to know who I Was,
and I told him that the two horses which came down on the 12th
were stolen, and I was the owner. After I had satisfied him AS to
who I was he told me he had sold it to a bakery company. I askad
him if it was anywhere in the town, and he said, No; it is at
Bromley, in Kent." He told me he had sold the mare for E35, and
that a man named Smith had sold the animals to him. He said he
did not know Smith's address just then, but he could find him, and
that he had given E9 for the mare and gelding and two ponies in
exchange On August 4 I went to Bromley with Deteetive-sergeant
Phipps and Mr. Keen and identified the mare. I had no doubt
about her whatever. After that we went to Dorking, where Mr. Keen
identified his horse.
Cross-examined. Keen's was a striking-looking horse, easily re-
cognisable When I first saw it at Dorking it was being driven in
a baker's van with Luek's name upon it. The police had aseertained
that two horses were consigned from London Bridge to Dorking on
July 12. Luck made no concealment about it. The first I knew of
the whereabouts of my horse was from information given me by Luok
himself. I do not suggest that he refused to give me Smith's address.
Luck offered to go to the police station with me. There was nothing
the matter with the horse when I lost it.
