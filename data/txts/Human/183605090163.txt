 1293. WILLIAM GRIFFIN was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of April , 1 waist coat, value 2 s  ., the goods of Robert Johnson . ROBERT JOHNSON. I am a porter at a shoe-warehouse—the prisoner is a shoe-maker—we lodged together, and slept in the same bed for about ten months—on the 25th of April I went to bed first, and hung my waistcoat behind the door in the rook—I did not see him at home that night—it was taken while I was at work in the day-time—I came home about half-past eight o'clock on the night of the 26th—I never allowed him to pawn my things at any time. WILLIAM HENRY KELL. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Bethnal-green-road. I have a waistcoat which was pawned by the prisoner on the 26th of April.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .)
   Prisoner's Defence . When I took the waistcoat I did not think about thieving—I merely took it to get a few halfpence—I meant to redeem it again—I went next morning to get it, and my landlord had sent for it. GUILTY.* Aged 23.— Confined Six Months.
 1294. HENRY BALL and JOHN BEAVIS was indicted for steal ing, on the   29th of April , 1 chair, value 4 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of James Crosby . GEORGE KENT. I am a policeman. On the 29th of April I was on duty at Mr. Crosby's, a carpenter, in Kingsland-road—I watched the two prisoner for an hour, loitering about the shop of Mr. Crosby, talking to each other, backwards and forwards—I saw Ball stand on one side the shop, and the other on the other side—I saw Ball take the chair from the shop and walk away with it—I took him into custody—the other walked off—he was close to Ball when he took the property—I secured them both—this is the chair. JAMES CROSBY. I am a carpenter, and live in Kingsland-road. The chair is mine—I know nothing of the prisoners.
 (The prisoner Ball put in a written defence pleading distress. Richard Barker, gun-maker, Commercial-road, gave him a good character.)
   BALL— GUILTY. Aged 23.
   BEAVIS— GUILTY.—Aged 20. Confined One Month.
