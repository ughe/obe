the 19th of Oct.—I was not present at her death—she was dead when I called that day—I made a  post mortem  examination on the morning of the inquest, the Monday following, and the day previous—it occupied many hours—we took a portion one day, and a portion the next—Mr. Saunders was pre sent with me on the first day, and Mr. Fisher, Mr. Saunders, and myself on the second day, the day of the inquest—the father of the deceased was not present—I believe he was in the house a portion of the time, but not in the room—( referring to his minutes )—I first opened' the skull—on removing the skull, I saw nothing of any importance on the outer surface of the brain—there was an effusion of about two ounces and a half of serum in the arachnoid sac, which is one of the cavities of the brain—that was more than the ordinary quantity—there were no external marks of injury on the head—the vessels of the brain were in a turgid state, more particularly on the left side, where there was a slight degree of adhesion of the membranes of the brain—there was about two drachms of serum in the ventricle—that was not material—that was all I saw the first day—on the second day's examination, I saw external marks of sloughing over the sacrum , which I have spoken of as having seen when she was alive—the vessels of the spinal cord, or the marrow, were congested, having patches of red on the top—there were coagula of blood at different portions of the spine, that extended, in different places, from the last dorsal to the cervical ver tebree, that is, from the bone of the back to the bone of the neck—then were two dark marks under each knee—the spinal marrow was in a state of inflammation—the symptoms I have mentioned would indicate that inflam mation, mation, and others, which showed themselves previously to death, tender ness ness progressing up the spine, and pain, evidenced by flinching on pres sure, up the spine externally—I first discovered that tenderness at the sacrum , and some portion of the os nominata , the bones which form the inside of the hip, or the smaller bones of the pelvis—it extended then up to the first dorsal bone of the back—on further examination I found that it extended further up the dorsal—the sacrum  bone was exceedingly tender, so that she could not bear pressure on it, and flinched whenever it was touched—inflammation was the cause of that tenderness—the bone was also enlarged—I attribute that, also, to the inflammation—after death I found that the surface of the bone, immediately under the slough, was dark, as if it had lost its vitality, as if it had been mortified during life—there was a degree of cares , or softening of the bone immediately under it—that state of the bone was decidedly connected with the wound that I had observed externally—they acknowledged the same cause—I attribute the death to inflammation, or irritation of the spinal cord—I presume when it extended as far as the origin of the phrenic nerve, the nerve by which we breathe that that was the immediate cause of death—there had evidently been pressure on the spinal marrow from the coagula which was formed there—when the phrenic nerve is affected, life will very soon become extinct—if the phrenic nerve was paralyzed it would cease to act on the diaphragm, and then the diaphragm losing its power, instant death would happen—I suppose that was the cause of death.
