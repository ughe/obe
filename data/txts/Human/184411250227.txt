I am a dairyman, and live at Lower Tooting, On the 16th of Nov., in the evening, I saw Taylor go into a shop—I went afterwards, and made inquiries—in consequence of what I was told I watched Taylor, and saw her join company with Wilson—they were in company side by side, and talking—I then saw Taylor go into Mr. Pickctt's shop—Wilson was on the opposite side—when Taylor came out she crossed, and joined him—I went into Mr. Pickett's, and received the half-crown which I marked—it was afterwards given to the officer—when I came out I saw the two prisoners again—Wilson was a little in advance of Taylor—she then went into two or three other shops, and when she came oat of them she joined Wilson, and they went on—I followed, and saw Taylor go into Wood's shop, and come out—I crossed over to Wilson, who I thought was watching me, and offered to sell him a little spaniel dog that I had—he said he did not want it—I walked a little way on, and saw he was standing waiting for Taylor—she joined him, and they walked up Balham-hill together—I met my brother-in-law, Passfield, and we looked out for a constable—we went to the Bedford Arms—the prisoners came up and I took Wilson, Passfield took Taylor.
   Wilson. Q.  Were we together when you first saw us? A.  No—you joined company when Taylor came out of a house—you kept on together from Lower to Upper Tooting. WILLIAM PASSFIELD. I am brother-in-law to Mr. Barnett. On the 16th of Nov. I met him on Balham-hill—I assisted him in watching the prisoners—I saw them together, and heard them in conversation—I could hear them talking, and it seemed to me that they were wrangling—I took Taylor—they were together at the time I took her.
