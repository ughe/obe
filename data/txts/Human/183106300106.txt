 1397.  JOHN REGAN was indicted for bigamy.
 MESSRS. PHILLIPS and CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES GROMWELL. I was clerk to the Reverend Father O'Flynn, parish priest of Adair in the county of Limerick. The prisoner and Mary Murphy were married by him on the first Sunday after Easter, in the fifty-fifth year of the reign of King George  the Third; they were married in the priest's parlour - there was a candle lighted, and I attended the ceremony; they were married with a silver ring, which was lent by Mrs. Fitzgibbons, who was a Carmelite, and they wear silver rings - I did not get any remuneration; they dined in the priest's kitchen, had bacon and cabbage, and some cyder or ale; I gave them the drink by Father O'Flynn's direction - I did not see Father O'Flynn receive any money for this marriage, and I never knew a marriage before without; I saw Mary Murphy on Wednesday last, alive in London - I waited on the prisoner the whole of the day of the wedding, and know him very well - his hair was cut short, which is not usual; he had been a soldier.
 COURT. Q. Was it the usual marriage ceremony, according to the Catholic church? A. Yes; Father O'Flynn was a regular priest, and acting as such - I saw the prisoner in the same village some years after; I know he is the same man.
 Prisoner. Q. Is not Father O'Flynn deranged? A. He was not at the time; he has been since.
   JAMES PALMER. I am assistant clerk of St. Martin's in the Field's. In January last an application was made to us, by a person describing herself as Mary Regan; in consequence of what she said, we got a warrant for the apprehension of the prisoner - the prisoner saw the same person at Bow-street; he then made an affidavit that she was his wife, that he was born in Limerick, in Ireland, and had obtained no other settlement; he was ordered, with his wife and children, to be passed to Ireland; this is the affidavit - it was read and explained to him, and I saw him sign it.
 - SAWYER. I am parish clerk of St. Ann's, Soho. I remember the prisoner being married, but I cannot say in what month - I have the register here of the marriage of  John Regan and Jane Nicklin, on the 8th of February last, but the banns were published in the name of Nicholin.
   JANE NICKLIN. I married the prisoner in February, at St. Ann's, Soho; I did not then know he had a wife.
 Prisoner. Q. How long was I away before I returned to you, after I was taken on a charge of deserting my other wife and children? A. I did not know of his being taken on that charge; I never sowed any money up in the collar of his coat, to bring him back to England - I did not know of his being removed to Ireland.
 COURT. Q. Had you been married before? A. Yes, I was a widow; here is the certificate of my first marriage - I had no reason to believe the prisoner had been married before; I keep a house in Naseby-court - I take in lodgers.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was the prisoner sent to prison for his cruelty to you? A. Yes, about a fortnight after we were married; he was kept there ten days - he was a Policeman.
 Prisoner's Defence. Her house is of the worst description - there is a room in it for***; I went to get a warrant against it, and she said I was spoiling the house.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1398.  JAMES LOVELESS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 fork, value 6d.; 4 knifes, value 3s., and 2 spoons, value 1s., the goods of Hannah Laing.
   JOHN KING. I am in the service of Mrs. Hannah Laing; she keeps an hotel in Leicester-place. The prisoner was in the habit of bringing porter to the house, and he used to fetch the pots away; some small knives, spoons, and a fork were missed.
   THOMAS RICHMOND. I am a Police-constable. I went with Mrs. Laing to the Bell public-house, in Leicester-street, where the prisoner lived as pot-boy; I searched a box in his sleeping-room, which was pointed out by his master, in his presence - he was desired to give the key, that it might be searched; he opened it - these articles and a pair of scissors were found in it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found these things wrapped in a paper in one of the cans - I did not know whose they were, and I put them on one side till they were asked for; I found them in my master's kitchen.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Six Months.
 1399.  MARGARET MULDOON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of April , 2 handkerchiefs, value 4s., the goods of Jeremiah Dismon, her master.
   JEREMIAH DISMON. I keep the  King's Arms, in Slater-street, Bethnal-green; the prisoner was my servant of all work. On the 15th of April I got up at eight o'clock in the morning; I missed her - she had been with me a month; I missed two handkerchiefs which had been in my private sitting-room the last thing over night - I went to Crown-court, Milton-street, and found the prisoner in a room with an old woman; I found this handkerchief on the bed - it is mine.
   CHARLES KNAPP. I live in Chiswell-street. I have a handkerchief, pawned by the prisoner.
   JAMES HANLEY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and produce the handkerchief.
   MARY RIGGS. The prisoner came to me when she left her place, and brought this handkerchief; I have known her some time - she had a good character. GUILTY. Aged 22.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Seven Days.
