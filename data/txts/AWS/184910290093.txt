MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN SHIRLEY. I am errand-boy, in the employ of Mr. Slinn, boot
and shoemaker, 103, High Holborn. On Monday, 15th Oet., about nine
o'clock at night, I left my master's shop to take t.some work-I left the
door quite secure and locked- left no one in the shop- returned about a
quarter to ten, and found the door open-I pushed it wide open, and two
young men came out-the prisoner is one-he said, Is your master at
home said, up with his hand, hit me a blow in the chest,
and ran up Hatton-garden-the other man ran down Holborn-hill-[ ran
after the prisoner towards Hatton-garden, trying out, Stop thief! and
Williams came up and stopped him about 100 yards from my master's shop
I then went back to the shop with Cagford, and found this black bag (pro-
duced), with boots in it, just inside the shop door-I then went to the station,
returned again, and found this white bag produced) very nearly under the
window, inside the shop-the bags were opened at the station-the black one
contained twelve pairs of boots and one odd one, eight pairs of shoes and one
odd one; and in the white bag there were eleven pairs of boots and one odd
one-they all belong to the master-when I left the shop the window was full
of boots, and when I came back it was all cleared out-] found a parcel on the
counter containing leather, of the same kind as my master had, and which was
banging on the nails when I went out-I had no light-there are lights in
the street, and next door there is a public-house where there are two lamps.
JOHN WILLIAMS I am a labourer. On 15th Oet. I was four or five yards
from Mr. Slinn's shop, and saw the two men come out of the shop-I am sure the
prisoner is one of them- did not see him strike Shirley-he ran up Hatton-
garden-I ran after him, stopped him, and Adams came up immediately.
Cross-ezamined by MR. HORRY. Q. How far up Hatton-garden was it?
A. It might have been twenty-five yards up-I did no lose sight of him from
the time he came out of the house.
