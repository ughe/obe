CLARENCE BARBER. I am nephew to the prosecutor, and am in his employ. I produce two orders, marked "A" and "B"—I received them at our counting-house.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  The order produced by the witness, is it not a common order for the delivery of butter? A.  Yes, when we know the party.
   COURT . Q.  It that in a shape in which the proprietor of any butter would send for it? A.  It is generally the case that we have this sort.
   JURY . Q.  Would a delivery clerk deliver butter on an order of that kind? A.  Yes.
   COURT . Q.  What, without any request to deliver, but merely having the marks, and the name of the firm? A.  Yes—if we knew the butters marked so belong to the firm, we should deliver them—when we know the parties are in the habit of sending their carts down for goods—it is not in my department to deliver the goods—I am not aware whether Mr. Barber has made good the deficiency in the butter. THOMAS NESBITT. I am in partnership with John Nesbitt. We were the consignees of a cargo of butter by the Regina  —this bill of lading reached me by post—in consequence of that I made an arrangement to land a portion of the cargo at Brewer's Quay, at Mr. Barber's warehouse—that portion did not include what was marked I M R—I sold some of that portion to Samuel Rice and Son, and ordered the bill of lading to be endorsed to Rice—I ordered them to endorse an assignment of the portion of the cargo named in the bill of lading—that included the forty-six casks marked I M R.
 (The bill of lading produced consigned the butters in question, with others, by the ship  Regina,  to Messrs. John and Thomas Nesbitt," or to their assigns"—upon the back was endorsed, "Deliver to the order of Messrs. Samuel Rice and Son, I C M 46, I M R 46, say 92 casks of the within butter for J. and T. Nesbitt. WM. GEO. LEGG.") WILLIAM GEORGE LEGG. The indorsement on this bill of lading is in my hand-writing—I did it by direction of my employers, Messrs. Nesbitt—it was left at Mr. Barber's wharf. JOHN RICE. I am in the butter business. I have no partner—I purchased of Messrs. Nesbitt some casks of butter, including forty-six marked I M R—they were landed at Brewer's Quay to our order, and remained there, subject to my order.
   COURT . Q.  Did you draw any of them from there yourself? A.  Yes;—this paper is not written by myself, or any of my servants—I never authorised it.
   JURY . Q.  IS that the usual form of sending an order? A.  Yes; we merely put the marks, and the ship the casks come by—( The paper was here read as follows:—"I M R, Regina,  1/4 20 Cks. RICE & Co.")
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Have not you seen other casks of butter marked in the same way? A.  I never saw the mark before on any cask—I have no partner—my father died twelve months ago—since that, I have carried on the business alone. CHARLES PITTOCK. I am principal warehouseman to Mr. Joseph Barber, in the butter department. I have been fifteen years in his service—I ordered this butter to be delivered—I have authority to deliver goods to nobody but the person to whom they are consigned
