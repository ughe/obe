 611. JAMES WALLIS and JOSEPH FIELD were indicted for steal ing, on the   30th of October , 10 lbs. of onion-seed, value 16 s  ., the goods of John Biggs , their master. JOHN PAYNE( police-constable V  11.) In consequence of information, on the 1st of January, I went to Mr. Draper's a gardener, in the parish of Richmond, and he produced 10 lbs . of onion-seed, which I now have in my possession—I took the prisoner Wallis into custody on the 2nd of January—I said I wanted him—he said, "You want me for that onion-seed"—I had not said any thing to him about onion-seed.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Where does Mr. Draper live? A.  Between Kew and Richmond. JOSEPH BEACH. I am foreman to Mr. John Biggs. In the middle of October I had some onion-seed of Mr. Biggs—it was in the drying-house—I missed some at the time, but I cannot precisely say what quantity—the drying-house cannot be shut up—on the 2nd of November last I had some onion-seed in the barn—I lost some at that time—I was cleaning it—I fastened the barn door by the hog yard—I attached the chain on the staple, fastened it with the pin, and hit it in with a stick—I seedbeds it with a rope, and took the key away in my pocket—when I came back at one o'clock, and unlocked the front barn door, which is in the yard, I found some persons during my absence had broken the door open by the hog yard—I missed a quantity of onion-seed—It was precisely the same as this—it resembled it exactly.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This took plate in November? A.  I missed some once in October from the drying-house, and once in November, of the same quality—I can notsay that other hardeners have not the same seed. THOMAS STREET. I am a market-gardener, living at Richmond.
