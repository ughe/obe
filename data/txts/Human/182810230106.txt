 - SUPTON. The prisoner works under me at the shop; I recollect his being there on Monday week - he left at one o'clock, and told me he returned at half-past - I saw him again three minutes before two; I did not see him asleep - he is an honest hard-working lad; the loft the watch was in, is an open loft - trades-people pass through it.
   ROBERT SHORT. I work in the same shop; I recollect seeing the prisoner on Monday week; he returned from dinner at twenty-five minutes past one o'clock - he goes to Old Cavendish-street with his father to dine, as it is too far to go to Camden-town; I should think it would take him half an hour to go to Cavendish-street, eat his dinner, and return; I think I may venture to say he could not have gone to Wardour-street and eaten his dinner and returned; I heard no offer made to him about this.
 COURT. Q. What day was it that he returned at twenty-five minutes past one o'clock? A. Last Monday week; it is my duty to attend the front-shop while the men go to dinner, to watch when they return - I have my dinner at two o'clock; two other men came between one and two - they were Green and Burn; Green came first of these two - there was no particular reason why I should notice the time the prisoner returned; but he generally does come about that time - he never takes his full hour, not one time in ten.
   - SCOTT . I am the prisoner's father, and work for the same master, but not in the same factory. On Monday week I recollect his dining with me and my other son; we all dine together regularly - it takes us about twenty minutes to dine; I did not notice the time he left that day, but I think it wanted about five minutes of the half hour when he went away - he, being the youngest, generally puts away the dinner things; I recollect his being at home to tea at half-past five o'clock; and at night I went home a quarter-past nine, and he was at home before me - my wife said he had been in bed with a head-ache half an hour; I will take an oath he did not go out after I went home - he could not without my knowing it; I heard of the circumstance, and I said to him, "If you go to the pawnbroker and he swears to you, you know you will be prosecuted; but if you are guilty you have no occasion to go, the master won't prosecute you" - he said, "No, I am not guilty; I would sooner do any thing than confess a thing I am not guilty of."
   JOHN SCOTT. I am the prisoner's brother. I recollect his dining with me and my father that day - he went away about the usual time; no sooner than usual - when I went home at night he was in bed.
   MARY ANN SCOTT. I was at home at my father's on Monday week. The prisoner came home about twenty minutes to nine o'clock - he went to bed with a very bad head-ache, he had no supper - he continued in bed till my father came home; I suppose my father's house is half an hour's walk from Wardour-street.
   - SUPTON . I remember his having a bad headache that afternoon; when he returned he could not do his work - I finished it for him.
   MR. BEAUMONT . I am one of the proprietors of the factory. The prisoner has worked there four years - he is very sober, honest, and industrious; the pawnbroker came and picked him out - I pointed out the consequences to him, and told him if he were guilty to own it, and Mr. Taylor would not prosecute him, and we would employ him still; he said he was innocent.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2197.  JAMES WATSON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of October , 1 coat, value 40s., the goods of William Wiggins.
 WILLIAM WIGGINS. I lost a coat on the 1st of October, from  Exmouth-street, Spafields. Martha Richards gave it to the prisoner; I knew him by working for my father - I had seen the coat safe at nine o'clock that morning in my drawer.
   MARTHA RICHARDS. I was at the prosecutor's house on the 1st of October, when the prisoner came and asked for a dark green great coat for Mr. Wiggins, which I gave him; he did not say where it was, but I knew, and gave it him.
   SIMON GILES. This coat was brought to me for sale on the 30th of September, by a young man resembling the prisoner - I never saw him before: I believe it to be him - I bought it for 18s.; he told me it was not his own, but a gentleman's, who had sent him, and if he did not approve of the price he should return before four o'clock.
   GEORGE WADDINGTON. The prisoner was given into my custody on the evening of the 30th of September - he told me he had been sent by two other persons for the coat, and sold it at Mr. Giles - I went there, and got it.
 COURT to WILLIAM WIGGINS. Q. What day was it? A. I think it was the 1st of October.
 MARTHA RICHARDS. I think it was the 1st of October.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. It is useless for me to make any defence - I am guilty. I had been out of employ five weeks, and was led into this by the prosecutor's own brother; I sold it to Mr. Giles, and the reason I made that agreement was to know if his brother would be satisfied. GUILTY. Aged 20.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor, believing him to have been in distress. Confined Two Months.
