628. MICHAEL CRAWLEY, stealing 1 watch, value 41., the goods of
James Alfred Cooper, from bis person.
MR. METCALFe conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES ALFRED COoPgR. I am a grainer, and live in Medway-atreet,
Horseferry-road; on 11th April, about 9 o'clock in the evening, I was
coming down on the right hand side of Holborn-hill, the side the church is-
I was nearly at the bottom of the hill, walking with Ann Davis-as I was
walking along I saw a number of persons--I cannot say how many-there
was a sham fight going on between two men-I believe, it to have been a
sham fight-the other persons were round them-in going through these
people Miss Davis said some one had hold of her dress, near the pocket did
not stop at all to look at the fighteI was surrounded, and could not get on
-I turned round. and there was a jerk at a silver guard chain which was
round my neck, and I missed my own wateh directly.iI turned round, and
saw my watch in the prisoner's band--I saw a portion of itthe ring and
the knob were out of his hand-it was a very large watch-I do not think a
man of his size could grasp it in his hand-I could not do it myselfthe
prisoner then ran away, and ran up a court, and some persons stopped me
from going up-he was let up the court, and I was not-when I lost my
wateh I was trying to get the female out of the crowd-] said, 44 It is only a
lark, take care of your pocket"when the prisoner entered the court I was
almoet within arm's length of bim, running close behind him-the court was
about as far from where I lost my wateh as to the corner of this court-I
believe the prisoner is the man who took the watch. (MR. PAYNE commenced
a cross-examination of this witness but the prisoner stating that he desired to
conduct his on defence, MR. PATNE returned his brief.)
Prisoner. Q. How far was the court from where you were robbed A.
About as far as to this door in the cornerI cannot say how far the court is

Come here" to look at you-I was looking at you for a minute or more-it

he said he had a man on suspicion of stealing my wateh-the officer first
asked who I was, and what my business was, and he asked if I had any wit-
nesses-I said no, and I was about to leave the place, and he called me back,
and you were then standing at the bat, and I said, 44 That is sufficient
will swear be did not say he had apprehended the prisoner-h did not say

COURT. Q. When did you see the prisoner again A. I think it was
two days after I lost the wateh, I saw him at the station house-I was busy
at the time, and got in disgrace for being out so long.
