PIRIE, Mayor.
Prisoner. I said I had got no money because I did not know what you
came about; I did not know who you was ; you did not say you had come
to take me or that you was a constable. Witness. He must have known
me-I was in plain clothes-I told him I apprehended him on suspicion of
being concerned, or having robbed Mr. Gurney's house-Mr. Gurney was
with me.
GEORGE GURNEY re-eramined. When I left the money in the basket in
the drawer there was a shilling with a mark on the head-I am certain this
is it, in consequence of a small T, and another T being on it, and a stamp
underneath the T-I am certain I noticed these three marks on it-I took
particular notice of the silver to see if any was bad, and am positive this
is one of the shillings-the silver would not be disturbed till night, and
then left in the bar as change for the morning.
Prisoner's Defence. The money I came by honestly; I do not see
why this shilling should not be changed at the house if any body came in
for change; I was in Mr. Gurney's house on Tuesday night near upon
eleven 'clock; I came home about five minutes after eleven my mother
had left the door ajar; I went to bed, got up between seven and eight,
came up to the Lion, stopped there most all day, and had all this money
from Monday morning in my pocket.
GEoRGe GURNEY re-examined. We always keep a bag of loose silver
besides-my son counts the money up-we count out 408. for change, the
rest is put into a drawer with a patent lock and locked up-when the
silver was gone for change, I should resort to that which was left in the
drawer-each day's change is put out for change on the following day,
and if there is not sufficient we resort to the bag-this shilling might have
been paid away if 11. worth of silver was wanted-i might be given in
exchange for gold, but nothing else.
THOMAs GARNeR re-examined. I had not given any change in the
morning, as all that was put there for change was gone.
DAVID EVEREST re-examined. I apprehended the prisoner at nine
o'clock on Wednesday night.
THOMAs GARNER re-examined. I had not examined the forty shillings to
see the marked one-we have a bag of loose silver besides for change-the
rest of the silver in the basket is left in the drawer locked up-it is counted
up in pounds in the morning, and put out for change for general use-it
gets into circulation in the course of the day-I saw the prisoner in the
house on Wednesday, but he never changed any money.
Prisoner. I had not worked for a master for nine months, but my
mother keeps a mangle; she keeps me, and gives me about 4s. a week for
elothes, besides having porter's jobs; I saved the thirty shillings up.
DaKEn. I am the prisoner's mother-I cannot say where my son
was on Tuesday night-I went to bed very tired, and ordered the door to
be left ajar-I do not know how he could have thirty shillings, but he
earned many a shilling of me-I have said. 44 You come home to-day and
I will give you 2d. or 3d.," and he saved this money up to buy clothes, I
firmlv believe.
