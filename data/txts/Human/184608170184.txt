1692. JOHN TOOMEY was indicted for stealing 3 wine-glasses, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 3 salt-cellars, 6 d  .; 2 tumblers, 1.; 1 brush, tray, and cover 1 s  .; 1 sauce-tureens and covers, 5 s  .; 2 tureen-stands 1 s  .; 2 vegetable dishes and covers, 3 s  .; 1 candlestick, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 7 plates, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 sugar box and cotcr,2 s  .; the goods of Lewis Woolf , his master. LEWIS WOOLF. I am a potter, and live in High-street, Wapping. The prisoner was in my service—I missed, about the 1st of Aug., these three wine-glasses, and other things—he was stopped in going from my wharf with a sack, and some things were found in it—his house was searched, and these things were found—they are mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL . Q.  How long has he been in your employ? A.  Constantly, upwards of twelve months, and on and off upwards of three years—I have had suspicion, and missed a great many things, but I never found him out till he was taken with some other things in a sack—he then said it was the first time—I have missed upwards of 300 l . worth of goods in the last twelve months.,
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Confined Nine Months .
 (There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
 1693. THOMAS WRIGHT was indicted for stealing 701bs. weight of rope, value 65., the goods of the Great Western Railway Company, his masters; and THOMAS WATKINS for feloniously receiving the same, "well knowing it to have been stolen; to which
   WRIGHT pleaded GUILTY  . Judgment respited.
   MESSRS. CLARKSON and O'BRIEN conducted the Prosecution. MARTIN O'HARA. I am a police-constable of the Great Western Railway Company. On the 21st of Aug. I saw Watkins loading his cart, at a quarter before eight in the morning—he was covering something over in the cart—I went and asked him what he was concealing in the cart—he said, "Nothing"—I stepped up and looked in the cart, and found a bag containing a quantity of rope, which I have here; some of it is new and some old—I asked How he came by it—he said one of the porters gave it him—I asked him to point out the man—in a few minutes Wright came up—Watkins said, "That is the man that gave it me"—he afterwards said he had purchased the rope of Wright at 5 s . a cwt., and paid him half-a-crown earnest—I asked Wright, and he said he thought they were his perquisites—Watkins knew that Wright was servant to the company—I weighed the rope, it was 70lbs.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL . Q.  What was Watkins concealing it with? A.  Some straw—it was a carrier's cart—he had a good arm-full of straw, about as much as carriers' carts usually have—the cart was loaded with goods underneath—this rope was on the top—I did not see that he had any need for the straw—when I asked him where he got it, he pointed oat Wright immediately—Watkins said he bought it at the rate of 5 s . a cwt., and I had given Wright half-a-crown earnest—Wright did not say anything to that—Watkins said, "The porter gave it to me"—I will swear those were the I words he made use of.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know whether there are any perquisites I there? A.  No, there are not.
