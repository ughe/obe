MUSGROVE, Mayor.
MARY SHIPP. I am matron of the Infant Orphan Asylum. On 16th
Oct. I returned to the Asylum, having been absent, and in consequence of
what I was told, I missed a silver teaspoon with " I. O. A." on the front
and "8" on the back of it-I believe these pieces produced to be part
of that spoon-there are the letters "1. O. A." on one piece and "8" on
another-I only missed one spoon then, I have missed one since--br
the counting of the spoons there are two missing-they are the property of
the Trustees of the Infant Orphan Asylum.
Cross-examined by Mr. M. PRENDERGAST. Q. When did you leave the
Asylum ? A. On 5th Aug.-1 cannot say I counted the spoons when I
left, but I believe they were right, they were reported to me as being
right-I did not miss any till the policeman produced the pieces--the pri
soner James has been in the habit of coming daily to the Asylum for the
last two years, to take away pigs' wash.
COURT. Q. Had you seen him on the premises before you went away!
A. Yes; and after I came back-he was not employed by the Asylum.
HENRY JOHN WAYLAND. I am a silversmith at Stratford. On 5th
Nov. the female prisoner and another woman came to my shop and asked
if I purchased old silver-I said, “Yes ;" and the prisoner produced these
pieces of silver spoons-I examined them, and told her I had no doubt
they had been stolen-sbe said her husband had found them in some wash
- I took the pieces and told them I must inquire further about them, and
they must call the next day-the part with the initials was so bent that I
could not see them-I bent it right, went after them, and gave them into
custody, and gave the pieces to Benton, who I had sent for-the female
prisoner gave the name of "Jury, of Chingford."
JOSEPH BENTON policeman K, 381). I was sent for to Mr. Wayland's
and found the female prisoner and another female there-they were given
into my custody, and I received the pieces of silver from Mr. Wayland-
the female prisoner said her husband had found them in some dung wash
--they were taken to the station and searched, and the female searcher
gave me a silver knife found on the other woman-1 afterwards went to
the Infant Orphan Asylum, saw the matron, and produced the pieces to
her-I afterwards apprehended James Manby, and told him it was on
suspicion of stealing two silver spoons-he said he found them in turning
some dung over in the Forest- asked him whether they were whole or
broken up-he said, "Whole"-I produce the Act of Parliament, which I
got from the secretary, Mr. Buckler.
Cross-examined. Q. Was any one present when he said they were
whole ? A. No-he did not say they were in pieces-the prisoners have
been out on bail in consequence of the female having a child.
(The prisoners received good characters.)
