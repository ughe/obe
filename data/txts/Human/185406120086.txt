GEORGE BOWLES. I live at No. 256, Blackfriars-road; I keep an oyster stall. On the evening of 11th May the prisoners came to my stall and had 1 d .—worth of oysters—while Gayham was taking her's I heard something fall—I said, "He has dropped a bottle"—a boy picked up a piece of glass, and I found it was a lid of a show glass of mine—I said I could not afford to lose it, and I would not open any more oysters; they must pay me, and Gayham gave me a shilling—there was a young man watching, and he came to me as soon as the shilling was put into my hand, and he said, "Hold it!"—I did so, and a constable came in a few minutes—I marked the shilling and gave it to him. WILLAIM WESTON( policeman, L  65). On the night of 11th May my attention was called to the prisoners in Blackfriars-road—I watched them—they were together, and in company with another female—they went to a public house or hotel—they left that and went to Bowles' stall—I saw them eating oysters—Bowles gave me this bad shilling—I took them into custody, and took them to Mrs. Sharp's, and received this shilling from Mrs. Sharp—I produce a third shilling, which I got from the searcher at the station.
   Gayham.  You gave the shilling to the searcher, and told her to say that she found it on me. Witness.  No, I did not; I found 2 s . 8 d . in good money in your hand. THOMAS PRESTON( policeman, L  78). I was with the last witness when the prisoners were taken—I found this counterfeit shilling on Williams. SARAH PEPIER. I searched Gayham at the station, and found a bad shil ling in her pocket—I marked it, and gave it to the police inspector.
   COURT to WILLIAM WESTON . Q.  You said you had the shilling given you? A.  It was given to the inspector, and he gave it me. WILLIAM WEBSTER. This shilling that was given to Bowles is bad; this one given to Sharp is bad; the others are bad, and all are from one mould.
   Williams's Defence.  I had been in company with several persons; I am not able to say who I received them of.
   WILLIAMS  — GUILTY. Aged 27.
   GAYHAM  — GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined Twelve Months .

I am the wife of John Seaward; he keeps a beer shop in Cross-street, Walworth. On 30th May the prisoner came in the evening, for a glass of stout—she gave me a bad shilling—I tried it with a magnet, and found it was an iron one—I told her, and she gave me a good florin; I gave her change, and she left; I sent a person after her—when I told her the shilling was bad, she said she knew where she got it—I said, "I dare say you do"—I gave it her back.
