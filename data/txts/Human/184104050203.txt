 1265. AUGUSTA LAY was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of March , 1 spoon, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; part of a brooch, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Ed ward Dutton , her master. EDWARD DUTTON. The prisoner was my servant—she came to me on the 2nd of March—I saw this spoon safe on the 28th of February, the day before she came—I missed it on the last day of February, or the 1st of March—I supposed the other girl had lost it—I saw nothing of the spoon till the 15th of March, when the policeman brought it—this brooch is also mine—it was safe seven or eight days before—it was found on her—I swear it is my property. ELIZABETH DUCKETT. I am the wife of Joseph Ducket, a policeman. I searched the prisoner one Monday—I do not know when—I found 9 s . 6 d . on her, and the brooch in her bosom. JAMES UPJOHN. The prisoner came to my shop on the 15th of March, about three o'clock in the afternoon—she offered this spoon for sale—I detained it and her. CHARLES JACKS( police-sergeant T  20.) I took the prisoner in charge. NOT GUILTY.
 1266. AUGUSTA LAY was again  indicted for stealing, on the   1st of March , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  .; 5 pieces of silk, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 pocket-book, value 1 s  .; 1 printed-book, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 fork, value 6 d  .; 1 brooch, value 6 d  .; the goods of John George , her master. JOHN GEORGE. I live at Richmond. The prisoner was in my service for about seven weeks—she left me on the 1st of March—on the 15th I was told something, and missed the things stated—these now produced are mine. CHARLES JACKS( police-sergeant F  20.) I searched the prisoner's box, and found these articles in it. GUILTY  . Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. — Confined Nine Days.
 1267. ROBERT LOOSELY was indicted for receiving, on the   31st of March , 21bs. weight of marjoram seed, value 14 s  ., the goods of James Richard Tyler , well knowing the same to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. JAMES RICHARD TYLER. I am a seeds-man, living at New Brixton. I bought 3lbs. of marjoram seed at Mr. Sangston's—I sold 1lb. of it, and lost the other from the seed room—it was in a paper bag—I believe this now produced to be mine. ALEXANDER ANDERSON LANGSTON. Here is my writing on this bag, the same as there was on the bag I sold to Mr. Tyler, and the name of its seed "knotted marjoram." EDWARD DENVER. I am a seedsman at Brixton. On the 31st of March, the prisoner came with this seed to sell—I asked him what he wanted for it—he said 4 s . or 5 s . a pound—I had known him as being in the habit of buying seed of me and others—I have always considered him very honest—I had the best opinion, of him—I told him the quantity was of no use to me.
