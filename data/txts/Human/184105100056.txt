have it examined, and see if it is worth more"—I took it out—Spinks said he would give me the 10 l . back again if it was disapproved of—I went, and found it was worth 14 l .—I said, "I have paid 10 l ., you may have 4 l . more"—he wanted 5 l .—I let him have 5 l ., which was 1 l . more than I understood it was worth—a few days after that he had got two diamond rings, and one mourning ring, in pawn at Spinks's—I have got one of the gold watches at home now, which I paid 10 l . for, in St. Martin's-lane, and that he has tried in all sorts of shapes in the world to get out of my hands, but I would not let him have it—that watch I gave him 15 l . on—he came to me, and said he was much in want of money, and got me to go and pawn it again for 10 l ., and let him have the money—I lent him some Act—it was at least that, and more than that, for what I know.
