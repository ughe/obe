FRANCES COBARN was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of November , 1 watch, value 2 l  .; 1 watch-guard, value 6 d  .; 1 watch-chain, value 6 d  .; 1 watch-key, value 1 d  .; 1 seal, value 2 s  .; 1 sovereign; and 6 shillings; the goods and monies of Charles Weightman , from his person. CHARLES WEIGHTMAN. I am servant to Lieutenant Saville, of the Royal Artillery; I am a private. On the 16th of November my master sent me to Woolwich—I was to return next morning—I got up between four and five o'clock on the 17th, and went to the Coach and Horses public-house, to get a pint of beer and a glass of gin, about half-past five o'clock—the pri soner came in while I was there, with another young woman, and two men, dressed as sailors—they had something to drink—I got into conversation with the sailors—they asked me to drink at different times, and I became quite insensible by drinking with them—when I awoke I found myself is another public-house, the Duke on Horseback, in a room on the ground-floor—my watch, seal, and guard, a sovereign, and 5 s . or 6 s . were gone—this is the watch I lost—it is my property—( looking at it .) MARY MARQUIS. I am single, and live in High-street, Woolwich. I went to the Coach and Horses on the morning in question—I saw the prosecutor there drunk—I remember the prisoner coming in with others—they drank together, and the prisoner left the house with the prosecutor, to go home together—I afterwards saw them at the Duke on Horseback—they had a quartern of rum there, and I partook of some there—the prosecutor pro duced a sovereign, and told me to pay for the rum—he laid it down, and before I could get hold of it, the prisoner took it up—she put it down at the bar to pay, and took the change herself—the prosecutor fell asleep there, and the prisoner tried to awake him—she took the guard off his neck, with the watch to it, and took it to the bar, to be taken care off till the man awoke—she was gone when he awoke—she did not give the prosecutor his change—he was in the parlour. SUSAN FOX. I was at the Coach and Horses, and saw the prisoner come in, with another young woman and two men—she asked the prosecutor to drink—I afterwards went to the Duke on Horseback, and saw them there—they had more liquor there—Weightman threw a sovereign down on the table to pay for the rum—the prisoner took it up to the bar, changed it, and put the change in her glove—I then went out with her—she had a basket in her hand—I saw nothing of the watch—I was not noticing any thing at the time—she asked me to go down the street—I said I had no shawl on my neck—she gave me a shilling, which I gave her back—she bought me a shawl in the street, and asked me to go back—Mr. Campion came, and took the watch out of her hand at his own door as we passed—she must have got it away from the bar, but I had not seen her do it—she had money of her own in the basket—I do not know whether the shawl was paid for with her own money or the prosecutor's.
