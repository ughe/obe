JOSEPH PEMBERTON( a prisoner. )I have been an errand-boy, and lodged with my mother, in East-street, Manchester-square. I have known Broad a little while—I bare only seen Holton twice or three times at most—he lives in Tom's-court, Thomas-street, Oxford-street—I know a per son named El wood. On Wednesday week, the 30th of January, between six and seven o'clock, Broad and El wood came to my house, and wanted me to come to Gilbert-street, Oxford-street, to get some lead—Mrs. Parry's stables are at the back of Gilbert-street—we went to the stable in Cock-yard—Elwood and Broad lifted me up on the wall—I had no knife—I pulled the lead off with my hands, and chucked  it down, and gave it to Elwood and Broad—there were two pieces—Elwood took the lead into the mews, left it there, and went and borrowed a sack of Holton—I got down from the wall into Gilbert-street, and went with Elwood into Holton's shop, with a piece of lead, for which he received 4 s .—he was to receive As.  3 d ., but the 3 d . was not paid—Holton put the lead underneath the counter—this was between six and seven o'clock—I afterwards met Broad in Duke-street, and he told me he had got 3 s . 6 d . for the other piece of lead—Elwood gave me half-a-crown—I did not see Mott at all that night—we went again the following night, and that night I saw him in Tom's-court—Broad was with me—Mott saw us together—nothing was said when we took the lead into Holton's the night before—Broad produced the lead—Holton said nothing, but put it into the scale and weighed it—he did not say what it weighed, but said it came to 7 s . 3 d . the two pieces—both the pieces were there—Broad took one, and Elwood the other, into the shop—when I went in with Elwood, his piece came to 4 s . 3 d .—Holton paid him 4 s .—I was not there when Broad took in the second piece—I did not hear Holton say it came to 7 s . 3 d ., but that was what it did come to—he asked no questions where it came from, but put it into the scale.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Why did you say at first he put it under the counter, instead of into the scales? A.  So he did put it under the coun ter—he weighed it—J did not think of that at first—I had no notion that they wanted to steal the lead till I got there—they would not let me go home—they hoisted me on the wall—I went up, not quite willingly—I could not help going up—he gave me the 2 s . 6 d .—he said I had better have my share of it—he pressed me to take it—I have just come out of Newgate now—I have been in Newgate before, that is about six months ago—I have been in this Court before, for stealing two straw bonnets—I was in Newgate a week after that, and then at the House of Correction for three months. SAMUIL LOFTING. I am employed at Mr. Mivart's stables, in Cock-yard, Davis-street. I took Pemberton into custody there on the landing place—I know nothing of this transaction. EDWARD PIGGOTT. I received charge of Pemberton in Gilbert-street, on the 1st of February, between 11 and 12 o'clock, and in consequence of information, I went to the Three Compasses in Silver-street, and found Broad—I told him I took him for stealing lead in company with Pember ton, in Cock-yard—he said it was not him, but Pemberton—I went next day to Holton's shop, in Tom's-court, but found no lead.
