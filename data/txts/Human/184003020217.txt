I saw a man go by in a boat, the prisoner beckoned to him, and he came as near as he could—he got across the bridge, and into the warehouse, alongside of a stack of rope—the prisoner took up two small bundles of rope, and put them into the scale—the man came in, the prisoner pointed down to the scale, and then he called his master to come and see it weighed—he did so, and the rope was put up again, and the man went away—at night the man came and received some money for it, then the prisoner was paid his wages, and I was paid—the prisoner told me to go over to the public-house—I saw the man sitting there, and some money pass between them—the prisoner said, "If you don't come up to the  scratch , I will cut  it altogether"—he paid me 3 s . that he owed me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How old are you? A.  Eighteen—I have been with the prosecutor a fortnight—I was up in the oakum loft at the time—I could see them through a hole—I was picking oakum—I saw the rope weighed—I told of this the next day—I was frightened to tell my master, I should have got knocked down—I received my wages of my master on the Saturday night—I have never been accused of any thing—I ran away from school at Morden twice—I did not tell Mr. Smith that I was punished for taking a pair of shoes from a public-house, and selling them for 2 s .—I was not at work at Mr. Branch's yard—I used to go there—I had loaded carts for the prisoner, and received 3 s . for it—I worked for Hayter and Reed before that.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  How long is it since you left school? A.  Seven or eight years—my wages at Mr. Bonnor's were 12 s . a week—the prisoner told me if I would hold my tongue they would be about 25 s .—I went home and told my mother, and she advised me to tell my master.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you mention about coming up to the scratch ? A.  Yes, at Union-hall—what I said was read over to me. GEORGE BANNER. I carry on business at Bermondsey. I remember the prisoner calling me down to see some rope weighed—he weighed it—I paid the man accordingly—the prisoner has been in my service two or three years—I had placed great confidence in him—Saturday night is a very busy night, and I am not open to any talk from my servants then.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were in the habit of leaving town? A.  Yes,—I found him always correct—I have frequently trusted him with large sums of money.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined Nine Months.
