JOHN PARKER. I am clerk to Tarling and Maides. I was in their
counting-house on the 25th of November, when Mr. Ashton was there !
heard Mr. Maides tell him that he allowed the prisoner a quarter of oats
and three ewt. of beans in a week, and that it was amply sufficient, and as
much as the horses could eat-Mr. Ashton said, that he thought it was
sufficient-I heard Mr. Maides offer to go with Mr. Ashton to see the
corn delivered at the prisoner's-he said he would search before the coru
was delivered, to see if there was any there-Mr. Ashton said, there was
no necessity for it, he was quite satisfied, and there were others he suspect-
ed more than the prisoner the cart came up, and one quarter of oats was
taken out-I did not see what was left in-Mr. Maides directed the car-
man, in Mr. Ashton's presence, to search the prisoner's premises earefully
and if he found any corn, besides what he took, to bring him a sample!
have known the prisoner for the last three years, and always considered
bím a very honest man.
