WILLIAM JAMES HOLLINGSWORTH. I am assistant to my father, an
auctioneer at 13, High Holborn-on 11th February we had this bill of
sale for 68l. on the household furniture and effects at 4, Newcomen Road,
Finchley, executed by the prisoner-it was registered on 12th February
and was in force on 24th June, when about 401. was owing; it is in
existence now-about 201. has been paid off.
EDWARD ELWIN DARVELL. I am a stockbroker; my offices are at the White
House, Telegraph Street, City-Mr. Terrell acted as my solicitor in this
matter, and all he did was with my sanction and authority-these two
cheques were given by me to Mr. Romilly on my solicitor's instructions
and advice-I have never been paid anything-1 instructed my solicitor
to take proceedings on the promissory note—the amount is still due.
Cross-examined. I know nothing of Romilly-I cannot say if he has
been subpoenaed-these are the cheques; they are payable to Romilly and
endorsed by him--one is 23rd and the other 24th June; the first was late
in the evening--the promissery note was for 241. 108.; the prisoner got
191. 108.--I don't think anybody has had the difference ; I have not had
it-I might have had it if the bill had been paid I suppose the difference
of 51. is for interest and contingencies—I paid 191. 108. for the bill and
am out of pocket--the 51. was for interest-I know nothing of the guinea
paid to Mr. Terrell.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. "I never read the draft
or the declaration.”
