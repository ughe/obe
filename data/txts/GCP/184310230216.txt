MR. CLARKSON. Q. Were you acquainted with him at all ? A. I have
known him for the last ten years, I think ever since he has been at West-street
chapel, and should know him when I met with him I went to Dover on the
6th of Sept. about half-past nine in the morning, and saw him on Ramsgate
pier about that time I returned the same afternoon-I do not know where
Mr. Bridgman was staying-1 was not applied to to become a witness, I came
forward voluntarily about a fortnight ago. e
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Was that from observing in the newspapers a report of
what was taking place before the Magistrate ? A. Yes- I was not applied
to by Mr. Bridgman---I more particularly remember seeing him on Thursday
morning, about twelve o'clock, on the sands I was going towards Broad-
stairs- I have not the slightest doubt about it-I was only there one week-
I went down on Saturday, the 2nd of Sept., and returned on the 9th-I only
went to Dover that one Wednesday, and it was as I was starting that I saw
him on the pier, and next morning my daughter and younger sister were
riding on donkeys on the sands, towards Broadstairs, and they almost ran
over Mr. Bridgman.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Is your daughter here? 4. No-she is only seven
years of age.
John MASON THOMPSON. I live at No. 9, Chancery-lane. I was at Rams-
gate in the early part of Sept., and saw Mr. Bridgman, whom I knew, there on
Thursday the 7th, as near as I can recollect, between twelve and one o'clock
-a particular circumstance enables me to speak accurately to the day-not
knowing how long he might continue at Ramsgate, I asked him to fix :
time to dine with me-be named the next day, but as I was engaged on
the next day to dine with some friends, I told him I was sorry I could not
receive him then, but should be glad if he would dine with me that day, and
after some little hesitation, he said, “Well, I did not promise to return to
my friends, and therefore I will do so," and accordingly he dined with me
on Thursday the 7th-but for that circumstance I certainly should not have
recollected the day. "
Court. Q. Why, did he not tell you he was going to Dover on
Friday? A. I think he said he was going to Dover in the afternoon of
the next day, Friday-I am not exactly certain with respect to the conver-
sation about Dover-I knew he was going to Dover, but whether it was on
the Friday or Saturday, I cannot say.
Mr. Bookin. Q. Are you able to state, on your oath, that he dined

slightest doubt about it-being an invalid, I dined at two o'clock, and that I
told him-it was rather later that day, in consequence of there being some
little addition made-I am sure I gave him the invitation the morning I mel

with me at my own house, in consequence of his preaching where I attend-
knowing he was to preach in the evening, I asked him to dine with me
never was in his chapel.
