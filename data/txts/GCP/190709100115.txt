BURLES THOMAS CORK, Palmer's Green. On July 6 I was staying
at St. Ive's Hotel, Broadstairs. Meggs and Quill were there. On
July 9 Meggs left, saying he was going to bring down a friend named
Pleydell Bouverie. He returned with Brown. On Saturday evening,
July 13, Moggs suggested playing cards, and after some persuasion
I agreed to play with the prisoners and a friend of theirs named
Oscar Obermeyer. Nap was suggested. I said I was not a card
player. Eventually Obermeyer stood out, and thó prisoners and
I started penny nap. I lost about 20s. The play varied, and we
paid as we went on. After a time I had exhausted my change, and
I think Brown suggested keeping a score; he did go on slips of paper.
The points were raised to 6d., and then to 1s. We played for 10 or
15 minutes at penny points and continued for about half hour at
6d. and 1s. points. For a time the prisoners paid their losses to each
other. I think Meggs paid about £30 to Brown in that way. At
one time there was as much as £l on the nap. At 9.15 we loft off
and the score-sheet was produced by Brown. He said the three of us
owed him certain sums- I owed him £60, Meggs £30 or £34, and
Quill a smaller amount, which I do not recollect. I saw the score
abeet at the end of the game, the figures were crossed out and altered
so that it was impossible to follow them. I afterwards said I would
prefer to leave it over until the following month. I had no money!
with me. They said that would be all right. On the next day,
Sunday, Quill said that as Bouverie was very particular as to the
payment of card debts, he would be willing to make me a loan for a
month or so, so that I could pay it. I said, "No." I remained at
the hotel till July 20, when I left, leaving the prisoners there. On
July 27 prisoners called on me and suggested they should take me
oul--that is, have dinner at the club and go to the theatre-the
following week. I understood Quill was a member of the Junior
Aimy and Navy Club, but the name of the club was not mentioned.
After they left Detective Saunders called on me. On Monday, July 29,
the prisoners called at my office and asked me to go out with thetu.
Brown suggested he should drive me to the Goodwood races in his
motor-car. They had come in a taximeter cab. At the hotel Brown
said he was related to the Earl of Radnor, who was the owner of the
bulk of the property in Folkestone, that his mother had property
iu Kent. He also carried a handkerchief with a coronet, which he
showed to most of the inhabitants of the hotel, and a ring with what
were supposed to be the Radnor arms or crest upon it. Quill said
he was a native of Ireland, that his father had a big estate of some
1,500 or 1,600 acres and a castle, and that he was inviting some
